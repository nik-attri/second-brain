Building payroll is hard. Proving it is correct is close to impossible. Looking at my last post, I almost feel that people at Zoho must be laughing at me. Even companies like eClerx, with 22,000+ employees, rely on third-party software to manage their employees and payroll systems.

And there is a reason for that. Payroll is not just about calculating salaries. You have to handle every possible scenario: new joiners, resignations, attendance corrections, unpaid leave, arrears, bonuses, reimbursements, loans, tax regimes, PF, professional tax, statutory changes, approvals, and full and final settlements.

A payroll app can look perfect and still be wrong. One wrong rupee, multiplied across every employee and every month, quietly becomes a compliance problem. Testing that properly has always meant months of work involving accountants, spreadsheets, parallel runs, and endless test cycles.

For testing every scenario without even a single tester, I went beyond all limits. If you think AI coding systems like Claude and Codex will make this easier for you, you are going to burn tokens like hell. I think you have to look beyond them.

If you are building something of this complexity in an individual capacity, it is almost impossible. But finally, I have configured a testing system that ran almost continuously for 48 hours. Still, I cannot say it simulated every possible scenario, but I can say it covered most of the scenarios I had planned, including testing everything in an actual browser.

This challenge pushed me into territory I could not have imagined just a week ago. I am glad it is finally complete after two tireless days.

I will release the first payroll demo with my Talking AI Assistant, Orbit, this week. I hope you are going to enjoy it.
