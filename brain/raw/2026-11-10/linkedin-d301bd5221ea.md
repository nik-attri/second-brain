If the lion’s share of your job is writing code, congratulations: you’re already unemployed. You just haven’t received the notice yet. ⚠️
The software developer profession as we know it is officially dead. We are all now Gru from Despicable Me.
While some proudly wear their Senior Engineer badge and spend a week polishing 100 lines of code, others deploy entire architectural modules in a couple of hours with just two prompts. The market will soon throw the former out on the street without an ounce of sentiment. Because the value of manually typing syntax is plummeting to absolute zero.
Business will no longer pay $3,000+ a month for what a multi-agent pipeline can do for $200—without burnout, arguing with clients, or endless approval cycles.
We are at the epicenter of three brutal paradoxes of this new reality:
1. Developers no longer write code. You are now the operator of a multi-agent factory. Your job is to delegate. Today, even the lowest-level position is effectively a Tech Lead for an endless crowd of hyperactive, stubborn, yet completely devoid-of-common-sense minions.
Your job is no longer to swing a shovel next to an excavator. It’s to build entire pipelines like factory conveyors. You need to write heuristics playbooks (approaches to invention), and define the product and company culture—just so your minions have a rough idea of where to go and how to get there.
2. The "Lost Generation" of Juniors. People spent 5 years studying algorithms, design patterns, and frameworks, and today all of that has depreciated. To survive now, they need a systemic product vision and managerial skills that no one taught them. The result? A crowd of disillusioned graduates will split in half: some will adapt through the pain, while others will simply give up and go work with their hands in the physical world—as mechanics, carpenters, or plumbers. And you know what? They’ll be needed more there.
3. Time has collapsed, and the feedback loop is broken. This is the scariest part. Previously, a product lifecycle was a year—you built it, measured the metrics, and confirmed your vector was right. Now, by the time you push a project to production using the "new way," AI models and approaches have mutated twice over. We are all transforming blindly, at hyper-speed, without time to validate if we are even flying in the right direction.
The bottom line is simple: There is no point in digging a trench next to an excavator. Either you learn to manage the conveyor of minions, or you move to the physical world—where a line of code can't tighten a nut. (The key word here is yet).
So, whose side are you on? Are you already running your AI factory, or are you still polishing pull requests and starting holy wars over whether if...else or switch...case is better? Let me know in the comments 👇
