I once watched a user interview where the founder spoke for almost the entire call.

The product introduction kept going. Then the roadmap. Then more context the user had not asked for.

The user had already mentally left. The founder was still presenting.

I told this story during the workshop because a bad user interview can sound exactly like a good investor pitch. The founder is energetic, the product sounds ambitious and the meeting feels busy. But we learn almost nothing about what the user actually does.

For an early product, I want the user to speak at least as much as the founder—usually more.

I also want the interview to leave the world of opinions as quickly as possible.

What do you use today? Show me. Where does the task begin? What happens after the first output? Which part do you still finish manually? Can you share the screen and try the product now?

The screen is useful because people are generous in conversation. They say something is clear while clicking the wrong button three times. They say a workflow is “fine” while opening another tool to complete the real job.

This is not dishonesty. It is the difference between what people remember and what they actually do.

I do not need the founder to become silent. Sometimes context is necessary. But if the user has become the audience, it is no longer a user interview.

I turned the interview structure into an open-source Skill:

`npx -y skills add Gingiris-1031/gingiris-user-interview`
