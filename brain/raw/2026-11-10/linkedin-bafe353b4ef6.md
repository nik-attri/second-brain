In my last post, I shared a view that not every problem needs AI, and not every AI use case is a good Decision Intelligence use case.

I think there’s another side to that conversation.

Getting AI to recommend an action might actually be the easy bit. Getting an organisation to act on it is much harder.

Take a fairly typical supply chain decision.

Imagine AI spots an emerging supply constraint and recommends rebalancing inventory between markets to protect supply where the potential impact is greatest.

Makes sense.

But then the real questions start.

Who actually owns that decision?

Do they trust the recommendation?

Can they make the call, or does someone else need to approve it?

Do the KPIs support the decision, or are different teams being measured against competing priorities?

And once we decide, can our systems actually make it happen?

This is where I think Decision Intelligence gets really interesting.

Because there’s a big difference between knowing what we should do and actually being able to do it.

We could build a brilliant model that identifies a potential supply issue weeks earlier.
But if responding still requires three meetings, four spreadsheets, six approvals and someone manually updating another system...

Have we really transformed the decision, or have we just spotted the problem earlier? 😄

The more time I spend around Decision Intelligence, the more I think the difficult bit isn’t always the intelligence.

It’s everything around the decision.

🎯 Who owns it?
⚖️ Are the right incentives and KPIs in place?
🤝 Do people trust the recommendation enough to act?
⚙️ Can we turn the decision into action?
🔄 And do we capture what happened so the next decision gets better?

That last one is particularly important to me.

Because this is where we start moving beyond AI that simply informs people towards Decision Intelligence that actually changes how a supply chain operates.

It also brings me back to something from my last post:

Sense → Decide → Act → Learn

For me, the goal isn’t to take people out of that loop.
It’s to be much more deliberate about where human judgement genuinely adds value, where machines can make better decisions at scale, and how we bring the two together.

Maybe the next big challenge in AI isn’t creating better recommendations.

Maybe it’s creating organisations that are actually able to act on them.

Interested in others’ experience here.

What have you found harder: getting to the right recommendation, or getting the organisation to act on it?
