Most teams building AI systems make the mistake of over-engineering autonomy.

Zapier co-founder Wade Foster recently shared a benchmark that reinforces the core architecture principle we build around:

Using AI strictly for judgment steps—rather than deploying end-to-end autonomous agents—cut workflow run costs by 71%.

His recommendation? Let LLMs assemble deterministic automations instead of trying to make an agent handle every API call and data pass.

At Alviti, our philosophy has always been simple: automate the mechanical, leverage AI for judgment, and give people the freedom to be creative, strategic, and profoundly human.

Here is what that looks like in production:

Deterministic pipes for data movement: Moving information between your CRM, inbox, and database doesn't need an LLM burning tokens and guessing schemas. Standard, deterministic logic is instant, rock-solid, and cost-efficient.

Targeted AI for human judgment: Where models actually belong is at qualitative inflection points—interpreting unstructured context, triaging intent, drafting nuanced responses, or handling exceptions.

Compounding reliability over compounding errors: When a multi-step agent guesses at each milestone, errors compound fast. When you anchor workflows in deterministic execution with isolated AI judgment nodes, you get enterprise-grade stability on day one.

The real competitive advantage in AI isn't finding ways to use it everywhere.

It’s engineering pragmatic systems that know precisely when to use code, when to invoke intelligence, and when to get out of the way.

How is your team balancing deterministic automation with LLM judgment in production?
