Standardise the Best and Stick to It.

At a medical device manufacturing operation we had considerable variation in production output between operators and shifts. Quality checks weren’t always being completed consistently either.

We needed a common standard, but I wasn’t interested in writing procedures from behind a desk. So I brought together four of our most consistently high performing operators and team leaders around a pallet in the warehouse, armed with some A3 paper.

Our mantra was simple: Standardise the best and stick to it.

Together, we mapped out what each operator should do during every shift. We established machine patrols, with inspection points clearly marked on the floor and equipment and Single Point Lessons showing what good looked like.

Regular quality checks, including elastic weld tension and visual inspection, were recorded manually so that any drift could be identified early. We also established a consistent packing standard at the end of each machine.

The procedures were developed in a single shift, then introduced across the operation, with everyone trained to the same standard.

The outcome was more consistent performance across shifts, clearer expectations and some healthy competition between shift teams and their leaders.

The important point?

We didn’t invent a new way of working. We identified what our best people were already doing well, captured it and made it repeatable.

Standardisation isn’t about settling for average. It’s about making excellence the starting point for everyone. And once you’ve established that standard, the discipline is in sticking to it.
