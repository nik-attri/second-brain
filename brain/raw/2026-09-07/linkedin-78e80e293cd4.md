Daniel’s Journey at Momentum Miles — Episode 2

The first pipeline I built changed everything.

In Episode 1, I shared how I joined Momentum Miles through a referral to clean over 200 million rows of contact data.

This episode is about the moment I stopped just cleaning data… and started engineering it.

The contact data was sitting in Dropbox, and every time we needed to work with it, there were manual steps involved. While learning AWS, I kept thinking:

There has to be a better way to move and manage this data.

So I built my first production-style data pipeline.

Dropbox → Amazon S3 → AWS Glue → Amazon Redshift.

That pipeline moved the contact data from Dropbox into an S3 bucket, transformed and prepared it with AWS Glue, cataloged it with Glue Crawlers, and loaded it into Amazon Redshift, where it became structured and ready for analytics.

Along the way, I started working with a much bigger AWS ecosystem than I ever imagined:

* Amazon S3 for storage.
* AWS Glue for ETL jobs and transformations.
* Glue Crawlers to catalog datasets.
* AWS Lambda to automate parts of the workflow.
* Amazon Athena to query data directly from S3.
* Amazon Redshift as the data warehouse powering our contact data.

I can’t even explain how that felt.

It was honestly a mind-blowing experience.

For the first time, I wasn’t just watching AWS tutorials or building practice projects. I was building and maintaining a cloud data pipeline that handled real production data used by the business.

Watching millions of records flow through services that I had connected together made everything click.

I realized Data Engineering wasn’t just about cleaning CSV files or writing SQL queries.

It was about building systems that move data reliably, automate workflows, and make data accessible for the people who need it.

That experience completely changed my confidence.

I went from cleaning contacts to thinking about storage, ETL, automation, data warehousing, and scalable cloud architecture.

And then something else happened.

I began asking myself a different question.

Not…

How do I manage this data better?

But…

What if I didn’t just manage the data?

What if I built the tools the business actually needed?

That single question completely changed the direction of my journey at Momentum Miles.

It was the moment I stopped thinking like someone responsible for data… and started thinking like an engineer who builds solutions.

And that’s where the real story begins.
