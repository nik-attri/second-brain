Recently I got asked “A lot of the AI conversation is still centred on tools, whereas you seem focused on redesigning workflows and operating models first.”
Then he asked whether AI engagements usually begin with process mapping or with use cases already identified.

In my experience most organisations come with a tool, a use case or a problem they want solved quickly. But we rarely start there. We start with the work processes. Because AI will not fix broken work. It exposes it.

If a process is unclear, AI makes the confusion faster. If the data is poor, AI produces poor outputs more confidently. If decision-making is slow, AI creates more options without improving accountability. If teams are already working around broken systems, AI may simply automate the workaround. This is where many organisations make the wrong first move.

They ask:
“Which AI tool should we use?”
Instead of:
“What part of our operating model is causing friction, delay or unnecessary cost?”

That second question is where the value is.
Before any serious AI implementation, leaders need to understand how work actually moves through the business. Only after that does AI become useful.

Not as a shiny add-on. Not as a disconnected experiment.
Not as another tool staff are expected to figure out alone.
But as part of a better way of working.

The organisations that win with AI will not be the ones with the longest list of tools. They will be the ones that understand their work deeply enough to know where AI belongs. 

Before asking what AI can do, ask what work needs to change.
That is where the real value starts.
