Who the hell is "Ron"!? Apparently, "Ron" - whoever that is - is good for the algorithm. I find this deeply perplexing.

This past weekend, I did an incredibly lawyer-ish thing: I took every Davis Law Group LinkedIn post I could get - 44 posts going back to 2020 - and had ChatGPT (using another thing called Buffer) analyze the hell out of them. You know... Word count. Topic. Time of day. Day of week. Opening style. Personal stories. Serious vs. ridiculous. Questions. Callbacks. Dirt. Death. Deals. Impressions. Engagement.

Basically... I did a home inspection on myself. And there were some interesting findings!

First, the old data is kinda wonky. Some of our 2024 posts supposedly had three impressions and a 66% engagement rate. Technically true. Also technically useless. So we focused primarily on the 19 more recent, mature posts with comparable data. And here's where it gets interesting.

Our four highest-impression posts were ALL: (1) Funny or irreverent; (2) More than 100 words; (3)Text only; and, (4) Substantive.

And they all ended with some sort of joke or callback instead of "CALL US TODAY!!!" Three of the four were about Dirt - aka real estate law. Personal stories also seem to have more breakout potential. Posts mentioning actual humans - Ron (again, who is this?), Oscar, Cassie, etc. — seem to have more breakout potential.

And short little lawyer blurbs!? Meh. Everyone hates lawyers. Who knew!?

Apparently people would rather read 200 words about rods, chains, killer clowns, crooked shelves, AI rascals, and Ron yabbering about contracts than read: "Here are three things you need to know about real estate law."

Perplexing.

The funniest part? The data basically told me to keep doing my thing, which is apparently: Tell a story; Be a little ridiculous; Explain the actual law; Don't sound like a law firm; And somehow circle all the nonsense back around to the legal point.

In other words, after analyzing 44 posts, building spreadsheets, sorting data, calculating medians, separating historical datasets, and interrogating engagement rates...

The conclusion appears to be: Keep letting "Ron" say weird stuff.

Damn it. And thank you for reading.
