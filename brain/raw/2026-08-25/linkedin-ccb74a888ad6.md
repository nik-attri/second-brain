I don’t think founders need better time management.


At least, not always.

Sometimes they need fewer things that require their attention.

I’ve noticed something about administrative work:

A founder can have a perfectly organised calendar, a beautiful task-management system and a colour-coded inbox…

…and still spend half the day doing work someone else could handle.

Because organisation doesn’t automatically create leverage.

You can organise 50 tasks beautifully and still be doing 50 tasks you shouldn’t be doing.

That’s why I don’t believe the answer to an overloaded founder is always:

“Let’s help you manage your time better.”

Sometimes the better question is:

“Why does this task need you?”

Does the founder really need to personally schedule every meeting?

Do they need to search through emails for that document?

Do they need to remember every follow-up?

Do they need to manually coordinate every moving part?

Or does the business simply need a better system and someone responsible for keeping it moving?

There’s a difference between being organised and being properly supported.

And I think we sometimes confuse the two.

A founder shouldn’t measure productivity by how many administrative tasks they managed to squeeze into their day.

They should be asking:

“What important work did my time make room for?”

Because sometimes the most productive thing you can do is stop doing the thing.

That’s not laziness.

That’s leverage.
