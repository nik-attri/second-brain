Before You Build an MVP, Validate the Problem.

One of the most expensive sentences in a startup is:

“I think customers will want this.”

Founders naturally become attached to their solutions.

But customers don’t buy solutions because they are innovative.

They buy because a problem is important enough to solve.

So before committing months of development and significant capital to an MVP, validate the problem first.

Here is a simple framework I recommend.

Step 1 — Define ONE customer

Avoid:

❌ “Our product is for SMEs.”

Instead:

✅ “Our first customers are manufacturing SMEs with 50–200 employees that still manage this process manually.”

Specificity makes validation easier.

Step 2 — Conduct 15–20 problem interviews

Don’t start by explaining your product.

Ask:

“How do you solve this problem today?”

Then explore:

→ How frequently does the problem occur?
→ What does it cost them?
→ What workaround are they currently using?
→ What frustrates them about the existing solution?
→ Who decides whether to purchase an alternative?

You are looking for evidence of pain, not compliments about your idea.

Step 3 — Look for behaviour, not enthusiasm

There is a big difference between:

“That’s a great idea!”

and

“When can I try it?”

Even stronger:

“How much will it cost?”

The closer the conversation gets to commitment, the stronger your validation becomes.

Step 4 — Identify your riskiest assumption

Every startup has one.

It could be:

Will customers pay?
Will businesses change their existing workflow?
Can we acquire customers economically?
Will users return frequently enough?

Your MVP should be designed primarily to test that assumption.

Step 5 — Define the validation target BEFORE development

For example:

20 customer interviews → 5 strong prospects → 3 willing pilot users → MVP

Now development begins with evidence rather than hope.
