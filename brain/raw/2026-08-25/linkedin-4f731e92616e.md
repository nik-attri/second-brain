I don’t think comments under ads are “raw VOC.”
⠀
They’re useful as hell.
⠀
But they’re reacting to something.
⠀
Run an ad talking about price and, unsurprisingly, people start talking about price.
⠀
Run one explaining the mechanism and you get:
⠀
“Does this actually work?”
⠀
Run a comparison ad and suddenly half the comment section is arguing about the competitor.
⠀
Those are real objections.
⠀
But the creative had a hand in deciding which objection showed up.
⠀
That changes how I research comments.
⠀
If I pull:
⠀
“Way too expensive.”
⠀
from underneath a price-led ad, I don’t immediately add:
⠀
PRICE = MAJOR MARKET OBJECTION
⠀
to the research doc.
⠀
Maybe it is.
⠀
Or maybe I just read 15 ads that spent the entire first 10 seconds making people think about price.
⠀
Comments are basically elicited data.
⠀
The stimulus matters.
⠀
So if I’m saving ad comments now, I want the ad beside them.
⠀
Angle.
⠀
Claim.
⠀
Offer.
⠀
What was said immediately before the customer responded.
⠀
Otherwise you can end up thinking you discovered something about the market when you actually discovered something about the ad you showed them.
⠀
Honestly, I think the comment and the creative belong in the same row of the spreadsheet.
