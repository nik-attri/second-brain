Multi-agent Computer Use Agents (CUAs) automate complex digital GUI workflows by splitting tasks across parallel virtual machines (VMs). But as these systems scale, they face a fundamental physical constraint where each subagents run in independent VMs and merging the live state of two running machines can be extremely challenging. Our intern, Mian Zhang, tackled this exact challenge in his new paper  "Spine-Branch Coordination for Multi-agent Computer Use."  and achieved new SOTA. Instead of relying on ad-hoc workarounds, Spine-Branch models this VM-state constraint from the ground up:
🧠 Spine: A main chain of workers linked by VM cloning that carries continuous, persistent task state. 
🌿 Branches: Parallel workers that gather data, hand off artifacts to the spine, and shut down-ensuring live VMs never need to merge.

Key Results (across 200 long-horizon CUA tasks):
🎯 Higher Success: Increased overall task success across three LLM backbones (50.5% to 67.0%). 
💰 Massive Efficiency: Slashed per-task execution costs by 34% to 70%. 
⚡ Smarter Scaling: Cut manager coordination overhead by ~5× with zero runtime replanning.
