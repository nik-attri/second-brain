Your SOP Isn’t the Problem. Your Process Might Be.
Quality Systems & Supplier Quality Insights — Part 5
                                                                                                                                📊 MasterControl’s 2026 Regulatory Readiness Handbook for Life Sciences reports that data integrity accounted for an estimated 61% of recently issued FDA Warning Letters.
That statistic caught my attention because it raises a bigger question:
❓ Are we addressing data integrity problems at the source?
When issues arise, the default response is often more training and tighter procedures.
💻 But consider a process where employees manually transfer data between paper records, spreadsheets, and electronic systems. Every handoff creates another opportunity for error.
I call this the “Human API” problem: relying on people to connect systems and compensate for gaps in process design.
Training matters. SOPs matter. But neither can eliminate risks created by disconnected systems, manual data entry, version control issues, or after-the-fact documentation.
The better question is:
🎯 Did we design the process so doing the right thing is the easiest thing to do?
That is where Quality Systems design becomes a compliance strategy.
                                                                                                                              Here is a simple readiness test:
🔍 If an inspector asked for the complete record of a batch activity from last Tuesday, could your team produce it quickly and confidently, or would someone have to reconstruct the story from multiple systems?
That answer may reveal more about your inspection readiness than another SOP review.
💬 Where does your QMS experience the biggest data bottleneck: manual entry, disconnected systems, version control, or after-the-fact documentation?
