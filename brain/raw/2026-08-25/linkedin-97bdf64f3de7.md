Everyone talks about AI replacing jobs.
Almost nobody talks about what it could build in a country like mine.
I run a small tech company in Bangladesh. Every day I see the same pattern: incredibly talented people, solving real problems, held back by one thing — leverage. A developer here can write code as good as anyone in San Francisco. But they spend 60% of their week on work that shouldn't need a human at all. Writing the same boilerplate. Answering the same support questions. Copying data from one place to another.
AI changes that math completely.
For the first time in history, the cost of "thinking work" is collapsing. And that's not a threat to Bangladesh — it's the biggest opportunity we've ever had. Here's why:
1. We don't have legacy systems to protect.
Companies in the West are spending billions retrofitting AI into 20-year-old processes. We can build AI-native from day one. Our small size is our advantage.
2. The talent is already here.
Bangladesh produces tens of thousands of CS graduates every year. What's been missing isn't skill — it's tools. AI is the great equalizer: a solo developer with good AI workflows now ships what used to take a team of five.
3. Our market problems are real, not hypothetical.
While Silicon Valley debates whether AI should write poetry, businesses here still manage inventory on paper and lose days to manual paperwork. Applying existing AI to these boring, unsexy problems? That's where actual value gets created. That's most of what we do at Sohojogi.
But I'll be honest about the hard parts too.
Electricity and internet reliability still bite. Access to paid AI tools is difficult when your card doesn't work internationally. And global clients still default to hiring from places they've heard of. These are real friction points, and pretending otherwise helps nobody.
What would actually move the needle:
→ Practical AI training focused on using tools, not just theory
→ Easier payment access for Bangladeshi developers and founders
→ More local founders sharing what works publicly, instead of gatekeeping
I don't know exactly where this goes. But I know this: countries that adopted mobile payments early leapfrogged entire banking generations. AI feels like that moment again — except this time, the barrier to entry isn't infrastructure. It's just the willingness to learn fast and build.
We're not waiting. Are you?
What do you think — is Bangladesh positioned to punch above its weight in AI, or are we about to miss the window again?
