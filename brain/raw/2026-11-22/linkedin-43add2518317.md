AI is making junior engineers productive much earlier in their careers.

That’s a good thing.

But I’ve been thinking about something else.

If AI starts solving the problems through which engineers traditionally learn, how will the next generation develop the experience needed to become great engineers?

Think about it.

A lot of our engineering experience came from debugging for hours, understanding unfamiliar code, making mistakes and figuring things out ourselves.

Today, AI can do much of that work in minutes.

We can’t ignore the speed the market demands, nor should we go back to doing everything manually.

But perhaps we need to rethink how junior engineers learn.

Are we using AI to help engineers become better, or just helping them deliver faster?
