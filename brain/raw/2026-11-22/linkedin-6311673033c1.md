🚀 Nobody tells you what the number on Musk's balance sheet actually costs him, and the bill is worse than the headline.

I wouldn't trade places with Elon Musk for anything, and I say that as someone who has spent a career trying to build wealth, chasing the kind of scale that looks good on a cap table but says nothing about whether you'd actually want the life attached to it.

Richest man alive, and here's what that actually buys:

1. A massive legal team on permanent retainer, because the SEC complaints and Congressional investigations don't stop and someone has to keep explaining the tweets after the fact. Once Trump is no longer able to provide cover, dozens of federal agencies could be expected to spin back up all the investigations that Donnie quashed. 

2. A PR team working the next shift, cleaning up whatever the lawyers couldn't spin. That's a full-time staff whose entire job is damage control on one person's impulses. And army of paid influencers flood all social media channels with manufactured propaganda to make him look like a benevolent godlike figure on Earth, and smarter than Einstein.

3. A security detail large enough to keep away the people who think he's worth threatening. That kind of money buys a perimeter.

4. Customers walking away. Sometimes whole countries deciding they'd rather not do business with his companies at all because of what he says or does. That's lost revenue as a direct consequence of his own choices, and no legal team fixes that one.

5. Renown for his drug abuse. That never comes without physical and mental health complications. from NPD to autism, megalomania and paranoia, he's got a full buffet of issues to deal with - and so does everyone else around him. Among many things I'm sure will come out in more detail in the new four-hour documentary of his life coming out on HBO shortly.

6. A fortune that isn't even stable. He was a trillionaire for a few wonderful days, then watched hundreds of billions evaporate. I'd want to know what that does to a person who's spent his whole life being told the number is the scoreboard.

7. A personal life that reads like a spreadsheet. Children with multiple partners, reportedly tied to significant payments rather than anything resembling partnership. He'll never know true love. And as the kids get older and figure out what their daddy is all about, they'll need the writing team from Succession, The Godfather, and Cheaper By The Dozen to script the TV series. 

I've spent four decades around founders who chase scale at any cost. Most of them learn eventually that the number on the balance sheet was never the point. Some don't learn it until it's too late to matter.

Richest man in the world is a title. I'll take a quiet Tuesday and people who actually like me.

What would it actually take to pay you enough to live his life instead of yours?
