The global menopause market for supplements and over-the-counter pharmaceutical products is estimated at $19.7 billion in 2026.

Between 2021 and 2023, the number of menopause-focused consumer-health product variants tracked in the US grew by 39%.

Yet in a September 2024 Vypr poll for The Grocer, 41% of women aged 55 and older surveyed said they didn’t trust claims made about menopause products.

Think about that for a second.

Brands see a growing market, identify a target audience, develop products, build campaigns, and start competing for attention.

But consumers have a different perspective.

They've spent years living with their bodies, navigating changes, trying different solutions, and developing a pretty good bullshit detector along the way.

And there's another interesting psychological layer here.

For decades, women's health has been marketed around life stages. 

Fertility, pregnancy, postpartum, menopause. Each with its own messaging, products, and consumer profile.

But human identity doesn't reset every time someone enters a new demographic category.

A woman turning 50 doesn't suddenly become a completely different person with a brand-new set of needs, fears, expectations, and purchasing behaviors.

And that's where I think brands are missing something pretty fundamental.

When you build your entire strategy around a demographic milestone, you risk understanding the market better than you understand the people living in it.

And consumers can feel that disconnect.

The brands that figure out how to understand women beyond the categories they've been assigned could have a serious competitive advantage.

You can identify a billion-dollar market on a spreadsheet. But earning the trust of the people inside it takes a whole different kind of intelligence.
