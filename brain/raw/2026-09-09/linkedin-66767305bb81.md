Day 3 has left me thinking about where the time we save with AI actually goes?

I also managed to spend a lot of time speaking with vendors in the expo hall. Those conversations gave me some practical ideas to bring back, particularly around improving our implementation workflows, how we assess problems where AI could help and how we roll things out safely and sustainably.

One thing I'll be looking at more closely is what happens after the AI has done its part.

We can analyse a report faster, draft more emails and get through our own task list sooner. But if the result is longer reports, more messages for colleagues to read or more work for someone else to check, we may have just moved the workload.

That distinction matters when we talk about productivity. A task taking less time is useful. Whether it improves the whole process is a separate question.

For me it changes what we should be asking before we automate something:

• Does this remove work from the process or pass it to someone else?
• Will the person receiving the output find it easier to make a decision?
• Have we included the time needed to check, correct and maintain it?
• What do we want our people to do with the capacity we free up?

That last question deserves more attention. We can use the time to clear a backlog, improve a service or give people more room to work with customers and colleagues. Those benefits need a deliberate decision from us as leaders. They won't happen just because we've bought the tools.

There is also a limit to how much checking we can push onto people. As AI starts taking actions rather than preparing drafts, “someone will review it” needs to become a workable process. We need clear permissions, a person who remains accountable and a way to stop things when they go wrong.

I'm coming away with ideas for our workflows but also a more demanding test for AI adoption: follow the work through to the person who is supposed to benefit.

Before we call the next pilot a success, we should be able to explain what got better for them not just what got faster for us...
