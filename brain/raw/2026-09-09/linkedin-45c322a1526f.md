I’m truly fascinated by how split people managers are on letting junior employees use AI.

A recent CNBC/SurveyMonkey survey found:

➡️ 35% think junior employees shouldn’t be allowed to use AI at all.

➡️ 42% say yes, but with some rules.

➡️ Only 9% are basically like…give them ChatGPT and let them live their life. 😂

And I can actually understand both sides of this.

How are junior employees supposed to get good at their jobs if AI is doing too much of the thinking for them?

Because there are some things you just have to learn by doing…and getting humbled in the process (we’ve all been there).

You have to write the terrible first draft or you have to sit there and stare at the spreadsheet wondering what the hell you’re looking at.

You have to get something wrong and figure out why.

That’s how you build judgment…along with some character. 🤣

HOWEVER.

And this is a big however.

Telling someone entering the workforce in 2026 that they can’t use AI at all also sounds wild.

They’re going to be working with this technology for their entire careers.

And I think figuring out that balance is about to become a pretty big part of being a good manager.

When do you let someone struggle because the struggle is actually teaching them?

When does AI help them learn faster?

And when is AI doing the learning for them?

“Just don’t use AI” seems like it’s going to get harder and harder to defend.

So I’m curious: if you manage early-career employees, what are you actually doing right now?
