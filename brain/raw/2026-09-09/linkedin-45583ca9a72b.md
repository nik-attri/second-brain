It might be a little controversial to say this publicly, but we use AI to help us write grant applications at René House.

Here's what that actually means, because it's not what people usually picture.

I built a system that knows our own data intimately: our statistics, our case studies, what we're actually eligible for. It does a deep dive on funding opportunities and filters out the ones we have no real chance of winning, so we stop wasting time on applications that were never going anywhere.

About a year ago, for the first time, we hired an actual bid writer. A human. Up until then it had always been me, doing every application manually, with the odd bit of paid support when I needed extra capacity.

Now that bid writer has their own writing augmented and assisted by a system that knows this organisation better in some ways than I do. It reviews their drafts. It makes connections I wouldn't have made myself, even having built this organisation from the ground up.

That's the part I think gets missed in the 'AI replacing jobs' conversation. This didn't replace anyone. It means that the people we do have spend more time doing the most valuable elements of the job we hired them for.

If you're a small business or charity wondering whether something like this is realistic for you, I'm happy to talk through what we built and how.

What's the one repetitive task in your work that's an obvious candidate to hand over to an agent, if you haven't already?
