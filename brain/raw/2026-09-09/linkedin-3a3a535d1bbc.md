Today Facebook reminded me that it’s Amanda Beard-Neilson birthday. 

We first met many years ago at Quantum publishing where we worked in media sales, selling space in The Publican Newspaper and Flavour Magazine along with 🌳 Lisa Rabone. 

The three of us became firm friends and our friendship lasted long after our ‘Quantum Days’ despite our careers evolving in different directions and life throwing up all the sh&t that life throws up. 

Today I thought about the fact that only Amanda could get diagnosed with the big C and decide that she was going to write a blog and associate the stages with project management. 

Only Amanda would present her oncologist with a fully formed spreadsheet of how it would be managed to fit in with her life! 

Only Amanda would go hell for leather with Salesforce  London Calling.

Only Amanda would have the Foo Fighters lyrics read out at her funeral. 

I share all this for several reasons..

- Sometimes it’s best not to rely on your first impression … as someone who is naturally a bit more reserved, (less so now than then), my first real experience of meeting Amanda was at a company away session at Camber Sands, (beach games, BBQ, you get the picture) and Amanda walking around with a magnum of Bollinger saying’ ‘anyone for Bolly’ .. I thought she was a bit much. Well she was more than much, she was epic! 

- Don’t ever underestimate the difference you can make… just flick through Amanda’s LinkedIn… read the blog posts… but the most humbling experience was being invited to the memorial session Amanda’s Saleforce colleagues/friends held where one by one they stood up and shared stories of how Amanda had positively impacted their lives and careers 

- You will never know when it is the last time you will see someone… in the September, I met up with Lisa and Amanda for one of our lunches in London. 
I nearly didn’t go. 
I was tired and thought about re-arranging. 
Thank God I didn’t. 
During the lunch Amanda told us how relieved she had been to recently get the all clear. 
November would bring a shocking, sudden end. 
Our lunch was the last time I would ever see her. 

And so, today, I think of my friend fondly.  

I invite you to think about your friendships and all of those people that have had a positive impact on your life, and I ask you to consider the impact you can have on others. 

And, if nothing else, I will, today of all days to try and remember to be ‘more Amanda’
