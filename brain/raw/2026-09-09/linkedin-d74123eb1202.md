How a Shrimp Farm in Ecuador Stopped Losing $40,000 a Night — With One Hybrid Microgrid Decision

Ecuador's shrimp export industry is booming. But behind the growth is a fragile reality: most farms run on unreliable grid power, backed up by diesel generators that must start within minutes when the grid fails.

For one 200-hectare farm outside Guayaquil, a single overnight power failure meant more than spoiled harvests. It meant aerators stopping. Oxygen levels crashing. And in high-density shrimp ponds, mortality begins within 30 minutes.

The farm manager told us: "We don't lose power often. But when we do, we lose everything in one night."

The incident that changed everything:
A grid failure at 2 a.m. The backup generator didn't auto-start. By the time staff noticed and manually switched over, oxygen levels had dropped critically. 

Estimated loss: $40,000 in a single night. Not counting the stress on the team and the buyers waiting on delivery schedules.

What we installed:
A hybrid microgrid built around one standard 200kW/400kWh liquid-cooled BESS, integrated with the farm's existing diesel generator and grid connection. 

The EMS was programmed with one non-negotiable priority: the aerators never stop.

Here's the logic:
→ Grid powers the farm during normal operation
→ The instant the grid fails, the BESS takes over in under 15 milliseconds
→ The diesel generator auto-starts only if the outage extends beyond the battery's capacity
→ When the grid returns, the system re-synchronizes automatically

The result in the first 6 months:
→ Zero aerator downtime — not a single second
→ Diesel runtime reduced by 74%
→ Monthly fuel bill: $9,700 → $2,500
→ The $40,000 overnight loss scenario? Eliminated
→ Projected payback: 2.6 years

Why this matters beyond shrimp farming:
Any operation where power failure means product loss — cold storage, dairy, poultry, food processing — faces the same equation. The cost of a hybrid microgrid is measured in months of payback. The cost of one bad night is measured in tens of thousands of dollars.

The farm manager's words: "I used to sleep with my phone next to my pillow, waiting for the call. Now the phone doesn't ring. That alone was worth it."
