🚀 Building StreameoAir(1) — My Own Take on the 2000s MP3 Player
What if we brought back the dedicated MP3 player from the 2000s, but gave it the technology we have today?

That’s the idea behind StreameoAir(1), my current side/hobby project.
StreameoAir(1) is an ESP32-powered portable MP3 player designed around the nostalgic experience of dedicated music players, while incorporating modern features such as a touchscreen, full-colour TFT display, wireless connectivity, and a rechargeable battery.


🎵 Some of the features:

📡 Wireless Connectivity
Built around the ESP32, allowing StreameoAir(1) to communicate wirelessly with other devices.

🎧 EasyNearBy Connect
A Bluetooth auto-connect feature designed to make pairing simpler. Just bring your Bluetooth headset, earbuds, or speaker close to StreameoAir(1), and it can automatically connect without having to manually search through a device list.

📁 AirSync
A wireless file-management system that allows users to transfer and manage audio files, photos, and other content without plugging the player into a laptop or computer.

📷 Camera
Currently under development — coming soon!


🔩 Designing my own ESP32 board:

One of the biggest reasons I started this project was to challenge myself beyond software development.

Instead of simply using an off-the-shelf ESP32 development board, I decided to design my own custom ESP32-based PCB.

I’m using Espressif's ESP32 datasheets and hardware design guidelines as references while designing the board, alongside tutorials and other learning resources to understand the practical aspects of embedded hardware development.


The custom board includes:
🔋 Rechargeable Li-Po battery support
💾 TF/microSD card slot
📡 ESP32 wireless connectivity
🖥️ TFT display interface
🔌 Power management circuitry
🔧 Custom peripheral connections

For me, this project is more than just building an MP3 player.

It’s an opportunity to bring together embedded programming, PCB design, electronics, wireless communication, hardware development, and product design into one project.

There’s still a long way to go, and plenty of things I want to improve — but that’s exactly what makes building it from scratch so interesting.
