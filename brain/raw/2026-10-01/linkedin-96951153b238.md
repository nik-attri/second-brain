New UK data suggests AI is denting job prospects for computer science and economics graduates, with entry-level technical hiring falling. Graduate schemes were never the only route into technical work, and in a city this size they are the least available one.

Five alternatives worth working through:

1. Early-stage local companies, before they advertise. Small teams hire when the work appears, not when a recruitment cycle opens. Follow what they are building and send something specific about it.

2. Contract work into local employers. Plenty of organisations around Lancaster have a single piece of data or software work that never justifies a permanent post.

3. Research-adjacent roles. Research software engineering, data tooling, analysis. These posts exist within universities and the projects attached to them, and they often go to people already in the room.

4. A side project somebody pays for. One paying user is evidence, and it interviews better than a tutorial build.

5. Turning up where hiring actually happens. In a small ecosystem, a lot of roles get filled through someone who already knows you can do the work.

None of these is quicker than a graduate scheme. They are just more available here.
