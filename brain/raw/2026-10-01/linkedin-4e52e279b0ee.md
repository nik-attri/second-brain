Finance & AI #01: Before You Hire Another Accountant, Look at These 3 Processes.
Not every finance workload problem requires another person.
Sometimes, it requires a better process.
In many organisations, finance professionals spend hours every month performing repetitive tasks that could be simplified, standardised or partially automated.
The result?
Less time for analysis, more time spent consolidating spreadsheets, and delayed financial information for decision-makers.

Here are 3 finance processes I'd review before recommending additional headcount:
1️⃣ Bank and balance sheet reconciliations
If your team manually compares transactions, identifies differences and follows up on outstanding items every month, consider reconciliation tools, standardised templates and automated matching rules.
The goal isn't to eliminate review. It's to reduce repetitive work and help the team focus on exceptions.
2️⃣ Budget versus actual reporting
If preparing a management report means collecting spreadsheets from multiple departments, copying figures and manually updating tables, there's an opportunity to improve the process.
A standard reporting template, structured data and tools such as Power Query or Power BI can help streamline consolidation and make variances easier to investigate.
3️⃣ Financial data collection and reporting
Repeatedly chasing departments for expenditure updates, outstanding advances, supporting documents and budget forecasts consumes valuable time.
Standardised submission templates, automated reminders and well-designed reporting workflows can improve timeliness, consistency and accountability.

But here's the important part.
Automation is not simply about doing the same work faster.
Before automating a process, ask:
Is the process necessary?
Are the underlying data accurate?
Are roles and responsibilities clear?
What controls must remain in place?
How will we review exceptions and prevent errors?
Automating a broken process can simply make the problem happen faster.
As finance professionals, we should aim to spend less time processing information and more time interpreting it, managing risks and supporting better business decisions.
The question isn't always, “How many more people do we need?”
Sometimes, the better question is:
“How can we enable our existing team to deliver more value?”
