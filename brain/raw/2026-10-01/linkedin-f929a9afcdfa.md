Stop selling businesses automation.
They don't want automation.
They want the problem gone.

A founder doesn't wake up thinking:
“I need three API integrations and an AI agent today.”

They wake up thinking:
“Why are my team still doing this manually?”

“Why are leads going cold?”

“Why are customers waiting for answers?”

“Why are my systems not talking to each other?”

“Why am I paying people to do work that should happen automatically?”

Those are the actual problems.

Automation is simply one possible answer.
Sometimes the answer is AI.
Sometimes it's a simple integration.
Sometimes it's a better database.
Sometimes it's a redesigned process.
Sometimes it's a proper SOP.

And occasionally, the right answer is:
Don't automate it.

This is something I'm becoming increasingly intentional about in the systems I build.
I don't want to become the person who looks at every business problem and reaches for an AI tool.

I want to become the person who can look at a messy process and understand:
Where information enters.
Where it gets stuck.
Where people waste time.
Where decisions happen.
Where customers experience friction.
Where technology can genuinely remove unnecessary work.

Then build the simplest reliable system that solves it.
Because impressive technology is easy to demonstrate.

Useful technology is harder.

And that's the distinction I care about.
I'm not interested in building automation for the sake of saying I built automation.
I'm interested in making businesses simpler, faster and more intelligently operated.
If you run a business and there's one process your team keeps saying:
“That's just how we do it.”

Send me “PROCESS”.

Tell me what the process is.
I'll tell you where I'd start looking for the bottleneck.
And if automation isn't the answer, I'll tell you that too.

What is one process in your business you'd happily never have to do manually again?
