One thing I’ve learned from spending a lot of time in manufacturing facilities is that you can learn a hell of a lot about a company’s QMS just by spending 30 minutes on the shop floor.

Ask an operator where they find their latest work instruction. Ask what happens when a part fails inspection. Ask how they know the gage they’re using is calibrated, or what they do when a drawing revision changes.

You’ll figure out pretty quickly how well the system actually works.

And I don’t mean that as a knock on operators. In my experience, operators are usually the ones who can tell you exactly where the system is making their job harder than it needs to be.

The procedure says one thing, the ERP says another, the traveler has slightly different information, and Joe, who’s been running the machine for 17 years, tells you, “Yeah, we don’t really do it that way anymore.” 😂

That’s where things get interesting.

Because technically you have a documented QMS, but in reality there’s another version of that QMS living on the shop floor through tribal knowledge, workarounds, old habits, spreadsheets, and things people have just figured out over the years.

That doesn’t automatically mean people are doing something wrong. Sometimes it means the documented system hasn’t kept up with the people actually doing the work.

This is one of the reasons I think Quality Managers need to spend time on the floor and just talk to people. Not always with a checklist. Not always because there’s an audit coming. Sometimes just ask, “What about our system makes your job harder than it needs to be?”

You might be surprised by what you hear.

And you’ll probably walk back to your desk with a few things worth looking into.

For the quality people in my network, what’s something you’ve learned from an operator that you never would have found sitting at your desk?
