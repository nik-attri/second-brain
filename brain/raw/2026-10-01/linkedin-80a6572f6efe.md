Gen Z is hitting burnout roughly 17 years earlier than prior generations.

Not because they’re less resilient. And not because they can’t handle hard work.

The explanation may have more to do with the way we’ve designed work, and our growing obsession with making everything easier, faster, and frictionless.

As AI removes more of the uncomfortable moments from our days, there’s a bigger question worth asking: What happens when we also remove the experiences that build judgment, emotional intelligence, and real human connection?

In her latest article, Maggie Sass, Ph.D., PCC, connects Gen Z burnout, workplace emotion, AI, and a compelling case for why some friction at work may be essential to developing the skills we’ll need most.
