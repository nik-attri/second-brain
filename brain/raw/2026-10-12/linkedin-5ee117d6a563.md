Someone asked me during an exploratory call once: "What happens to my team if you automate all of this?"

Fair question. And I understand why they ask it. The concern is that a robot comes in, runs 100 tests in an hour that would've taken their team a couple of days, and suddenly there's less need for the people doing the testing.

But that's not how I see automation changing the role of QA teams.

What typically happens is that the QA engineer spends less time manually executing tests and more time analysing the results. They can look more closely at the flows, understand what the test cases are actually covering, and get involved earlier in development to think about what needs to be tested in the first place.

That's a better use of their skills. And I think it's a more interesting job, too.

The time they get back can go into expanding test coverage, designing better test cases, and catching issues earlier in the development cycle instead of at the end, when they're more expensive to fix.

The concern is understandable. But something I've noticed is that once people see the robot run through a full regression cycle, the conversation often starts to change.

When you see 100 tests execute in an hour with consistent execution and detailed results, it becomes easier to imagine what your team could do with that extra time.

And that's really how I think we should be looking at automation. Not as a way to replace the expertise of testers, but as a way to make better use of it.

What's your experience been when automation is introduced to a QA team? Does the initial concern fade once people see it in action, or does it take something more to build that confidence?
