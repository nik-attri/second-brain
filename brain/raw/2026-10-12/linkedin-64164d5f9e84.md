I wish I had discovered NotebookLM during my master’s. 😂😭

During my MSc research, I spent quite some time manually organising themes, connecting ideas, and turning qualitative findings into mind maps that helped me make sense of the bigger picture.

So, imagine my excitement when I discovered that NotebookLM could generate mind maps. 👀

Of course, I had to try it!

I explored its mind-mapping feature using material on coastal resilience and the DPSIR framework. Then, I looked at the result alongside some of the thematic maps I had manually developed during my own research.

And my reaction was basically… “Wait, we can do this now?” 😂

But beyond the excitement, this got me thinking about how AI tools could support researchers in:

→ Organising complex information
→ Exploring relationships between concepts
→ Developing research summaries and plans
→ Visualising complicated ideas
→ Communicating research findings more effectively.

Now, let me be clear: a beautiful mind map does not automatically mean sound analysis. 😅

The researcher still has to interrogate the output, verify the sources, assess whether the connections make sense, and apply their own understanding of the subject.

That distinction matters to me, particularly as someone working at the intersection of environmental research, climate resilience, and environmental management.

I’m currently exploring the practical applications of AI through the Google AI Specialization, and Course 3, AI for Research and Insight, has been one of my favourite discoveries so far.

And honestly… I’m only getting started. 😂

Researchers, have you tried NotebookLM’s mind-mapping feature? I’d love to hear what you think about using AI to support research workflows.
