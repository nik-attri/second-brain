When did we become so afraid of losing our jobs that we forgot how much power we actually have?

Seriously. Think about that for a second.

I just came across a post about Nike reportedly announcing layoffs while leaving employees uncertain about who will lose their jobs until 2027.

Imagine being told your livelihood might disappear, but you’re expected to show up tomorrow, smile, meet deadlines, hit your targets, and act like everything is fine.

What the hell are we doing?

These are people’s lives we’re talking about.

Parents raising children. Families paying mortgages. People trying to put food on the table and build some kind of financial security.

And somehow, corporations have convinced us that living with this level of uncertainty is just part of having a career.

I’m tired of seeing it.

I’m tired of watching talented, hardworking people treated like they’re disposable.

I’m tired of seeing companies talk about culture, loyalty, and family while making decisions that leave actual families wondering how they’re going to survive.

And I’m starting to wonder when we’re going to stop accepting it.

Because here’s something we seem to have forgotten:

Corporations don’t build themselves.

WE build them.

The designers. The engineers. The managers. The people answering phones, solving problems, creating products, and keeping everything moving.

Without us, there is no company.

Yet somehow, we’ve allowed the balance of power to shift so far that we’re afraid to even question the people making these decisions.

I’m not against businesses making difficult decisions. I’m against treating human beings like numbers on a spreadsheet.

We deserve transparency. We deserve respect. We deserve stability. And when stability isn’t possible, we deserve to be treated with dignity.

We need to take our power back.

Together. Through solidarity, speaking up, supporting one another, and demanding accountability.

Because the more we stay silent, the more this becomes normal.

And I refuse to believe this is the best we can do.

So here’s my question:

When are we, the people who actually keep these companies running, going to realize that we have more power together than we ever will individually?

Because something needs to change.

And maybe that change starts with us.
