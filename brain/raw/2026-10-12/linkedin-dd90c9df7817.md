Understanding Before Automation 
I've worked on enough process improvement and reporting initiatives to notice a recurring pattern: Organizations often focus on automating a process before fully understanding it. 

Automation creates efficiency. Understanding creates capability. 

Some of the most valuable lessons I've learned came not from dashboards, workflows, or automation, but from manually working through data and processes. That's where you discover: 
- exceptions, 
- process gaps, 
- ownership issues, 
- hidden assumptions, 
- data quality problems, 
- workarounds nobody documented. 

Automation should absolutely be the goal. But before asking: How do we automate this? it may be worth asking: Do we fully understand what we're automating? 

In my experience, the strongest automation solutions emerge from a deep understanding of the process, the people involved, and the purpose behind the work. 

Understanding first. Automation second. 

What has been your experience? Have you ever uncovered something important by manually working through a process before automating it?
