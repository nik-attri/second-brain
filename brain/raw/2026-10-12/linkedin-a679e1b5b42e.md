I'm Done With the Doomers Telling Our Young Generation There Will Be No More Jobs Because of AI

This week I read a post that finally pushed me over the edge.

An accountant, a chasid whom I respect, wrote a heartfelt lament about the future of junior accounting jobs. Why so worried? Because he now uses AI for everything. So who needs juniors anymore?

I read it twice to make sure it wasn't satire. It wasn't.

You've automated your own workday, and your conclusion is that the *junior* is in trouble? My friend, there's a lo yutzlach in this story, and it isn't the 22-year-old.

That post captures everything wrong with the doom crowd. They take their own lack of imagination and hand it to the next generation as a prophecy. And our kids are listening. "Why learn this? AI will do it." That's not caution. That's surrender dressed up as wisdom.

History says otherwise.
 Spreadsheets were supposed to wipe out accountants. Instead, accounting exploded, because when analysis got cheap, every business wanted more of it. Every time a tool makes work cheaper, demand grows, and the people who know the tool get hired first.

A junior isn't a human photocopier.
A junior is someone learning judgment: reading a client, spotting a number that smells wrong. AI doesn't replace that apprenticeship. It skips the drudgery and gets young people to the interesting part faster.

The hustlers always make it.
 Every wave of technology has the same plot twist. The incumbents complain, and the hungry kid with nothing to lose figures it out first.

Picture a young guy fresh out of BMG. No corner office, no ego invested in doing things the slow way. What he does have is a mind trained by years over a blatt Gemara, picking apart every argument, asking "why" until nothing's left to ask. You think he can't cross-examine an AI output and catch where it's wrong? He's been doing that to texts since he was fourteen.

Put these tools in his hands and he'll run circles around our worried accountant. He'll prompt faster, verify smarter, and take on twenty clients while the veteran is still posting about how scared he is. He doesn't see AI as a threat. He sees it as the staff he couldn't afford yet.

And the irony? Firms that stop hiring juniors because "AI does it" will wake up in ten years with no seniors. The firms that train young people to work *with* these tools will eat their lunch.

To the young people reading this: don't let anyone afraid of their own software tell you your future is cancelled. Learn the fundamentals. Learn the tools. Be the person who knows both.

And to my accountant friend: if AI really does everything you do, keep an eye on the kid from BMG. He's coming for your clients, and he's not worried at all.
