Is AI really closing the door on first jobs? The honest answer is that the experts disagree.

A widely cited Stanford University study of payroll data found that workers aged 22 to 25 in AI-exposed roles have seen a 16% relative decline in employment since late 2022. Other economists argue that remote work, not AI, explains most of the drop in junior hiring.

Here in South Africa, the official unemployment rate for people aged 15 to 34 is 47.4%.

Whatever the cause, the lesson for employers is the same.

If we stop hiring at entry level, we stop building the specialists and leaders we will need in five years. The pipeline does not refill itself.

From the recruitment side, this is what I would ask every hiring manager:
 1. Which roles in your business can still be a first job?
 2. Are we hiring for potential, or only for experience we can see on a CV?
 3. Who is responsible for developing the people we bring in?

Is your organisation still hiring at entry level? If not, who fills your senior roles in 2031?
