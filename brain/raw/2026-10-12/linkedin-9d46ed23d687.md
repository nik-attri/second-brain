The first Monday morning task I had as an AM in my first agency was updating budget burn trackers. (Not to give away my age, but this was at a time when there was also table football and beanbags on the ground floor...)

We all submitted timesheets on a Friday afternoon. Then, after a bit of Excel wizardry (by someone who I'm sure wasnt paid enough for it!), all us CS folk got an updated list of hours logged per team, ready to cut and paste into our project budgets.

I’m not pretending for a moment it was fun, nor was it the most exciting way to start the week...but my goodness, we did it. Because that’s how you knew where you were on spend. Without it, you were working blind.

Today, the cut-and-paste part is the easy bit. Most PM and time-tracking tools will show budget burn as hours are logged, and send an alert before a project hits its limit. Hell, some will even draft your team’s timesheets for them.

But they all need the same things my Monday spreadsheet did - time logged by the right people, against a budget broken down far enough to track against.

I'm not convinced this is being done. Timesheets go in late (if at all), or get filled in from memory at the end of the month. Budgets get sold as one big number and never split by team (sales teams aren’t famous for their love of a task-level breakdown), so there’s nothing to measure (let alone resource) against.

So the dashboard shows something...it just isn’t the truth. Which for me is worse than the Excel days. Back then, if the work wasn’t done, at least you knew you didn’t have the numbers. Now there’s a dashboard that looks like it’s working, and nobody realises it isn’t.

In that first agency, someone owned that process. It happened every Monday, and if it didn’t, people noticed. In most agencies I see now, that habit never got built, and better tools have made it easier not to notice.

(And yes, that applies even if you’re value pricing. Knowing what the work is costing you still matters, arguably more.)

Somewhere in your agency, a project is already over budget, but the dashboard says everything’s fine. And I don't think that's ok.
