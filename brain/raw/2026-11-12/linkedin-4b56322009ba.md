One thing I find interesting about working with data is how much information you can get from what looks like an ordinary spreadsheet.
I recently worked with a Coffee Shop Sales dataset in Microsoft Excel. Rather than just sorting through the records, I wanted to explore the data, create additional columns and see how different Excel functions could help me answer some business questions.
Here's a breakdown of what I worked on.

1. Data preparation and calculated columns
I started by going through the transaction records and creating additional columns that would make the analysis easier.
Created a Total Sales column by multiplying the quantity sold by the unit price.
Used TEXT() to extract the month and day name from the transaction dates.
Applied HOUR() to get the hour of each transaction.
Combined WEEKDAY() and IF() to group transactions into weekdays and weekends.
Used IFS() to classify sales into Low, Medium and High categories based on set conditions.

2. Using Excel functions to analyze the data
After creating the additional columns, I applied different Excel functions to explore the dataset.
SUM() – To calculate total revenue and quantity sold.
AVERAGE() – To calculate the average sales value per transaction.
MAX() and MIN() – To identify the highest and lowest transaction values.
COUNTA() – To count the transaction records.
COUNTIF() – To count transactions across product categories and store locations.
SUMIF() – To calculate revenue generated by individual product categories.
SUMIFS() – To analyze coffee sales across different store locations and compare weekday and weekend revenue.

3. Some figures from the analysis
Here are a few figures I got from working with the dataset:
Total Revenue: $698,812.33
Total Quantity Sold: 214,470 units
Total Transactions: 149,116
Average Sales Value: $4.69
Highest Transaction Value: $360
Lowest Transaction Value: $0.80

What the data showed
Coffee was the top category at about $270K (roughly 39% of revenue), followed by Tea at about $196K.
Weekdays generated about 72% of revenue ($503.6K vs $195.2K for weekends).
The three stores performed very closely. Hell’s Kitchen led with $236.5K, ahead of Astoria ($232.2K) and Lower Manhattan ($230.1K).

I also looked at how sales differed across product categories, store locations and days of the week.
I find it interesting that Excel functions can do much more than perform basic calculations. When you understand how and when to apply them, you can use them to explore data, identify patterns and answer questions that matter to a business. Working with this dataset gave me the opportunity to put several functions into practice and see how they can be used in a real business context.
