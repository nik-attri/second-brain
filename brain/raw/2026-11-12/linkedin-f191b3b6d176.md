Perhaps we've become so good at explaining what design does that we've forgotten how to talk about why we love it.

Someone can spend $20,000 on a beautifully designed chair. They might have saved for years, know everything about the designer or simply have fallen in love with it. Every time they walk into the room, they think, God, I love that thing. (still saving for mine ❤️)

Nobody's asking for a spreadsheet explaining how many extra hours of productive sitting it will generate. We understand that pleasure has value.

Yet when we talk about graphic design, particularly branding, everything becomes terribly serious. Strategy, conversion rates, brand recognition, return on investment. All important, of course. But when did we become so uncomfortable admitting that we might simply love something?

I still love handing someone a beautifully made business card. Mine is embossed, and yes, I'm still ridiculously pleased with it.

Apparently we're all supposed to exchange QR codes now. Where's the fun in that? 🙄 Sure, a QR code might get someone to your website faster. But I love that moment when someone takes your card, turns it over, runs their fingers across the embossing and asks about the paper. Suddenly you're having another conversation. Not because the card told them to visit your website, but because they were curious.

That's the bit I love. What happens after the design is finished.

I've spent 25 years working in design, and the projects I remember most aren't necessarily the ones with the biggest commercial results. They're the ones that made me feel something. The ideas I couldn't stop thinking about. The tiny details I became obsessed with. The moments when I looked at something we'd created and thought, bloody hell, I love this.

And I wonder whether, in our determination to prove that design is more than decoration, we've forgotten that making something people genuinely love is part of the point.

Of course design needs to work. But surely we're allowed to love it too? ✨
