On to the 2nd P: Price. The price of a product is not only monetary. It's what someone is willing to give in exchange for a problem solved. Inside a company, folks don't pay in dollars. They pay in time and effort. 

The old budget tracking process was expensive that way. Multiple Excel files, each with multiple tabs, all because people kept starting a new version when they couldn't find what they needed. The cost of time spend looking and deciphering was higher than simply making something new. Who wants to spend hours in Excel hell?

My new tracker cost less. One file. Two tabs - forecasting and actuals. 30 minutes live initial walkthru. Minutes bookmarking the one file. Everyone onboarded at their own pace, but within a month the "where do I find this?" had all but stopped and data mistakes were rare. 

A product has to cost less to acquire and use than the problem costs to live with. When it doesn't, people go back to their own spreadsheet, and that's how you end up with 50 million versions of the same file.
