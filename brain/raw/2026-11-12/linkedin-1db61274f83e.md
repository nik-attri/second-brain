Entry-level tech jobs are not disappearing. The easy ones are.

IBM is tripling its US entry-level hiring this year. Salesforce says it will take on 1,000 grads and interns.

Now the other side. WSJ data reported by KIRO 7 puts entry-level roles at 7.5% of tech postings and senior roles at 43%.

Both are true at once. Companies still hire juniors, but fewer of them, and for different work.

IBM says it rebuilt its junior roles around customer engagement and AI oversight. It has not said how many people it will hire.

I read that as a warning for any grad whose whole pitch is “I can write basic code” or “I can pull a report.” That is the work getting automated first.

What looks harder to automate: explaining why a system broke, checking AI output instead of trusting it, and talking to a non-technical person without losing them.

Uni taught me to build the thing. The jobs that are left seem to ask who can question it.

Hiring managers and recent grads: which is harder to find right now, someone who can build it or someone who can question it?
