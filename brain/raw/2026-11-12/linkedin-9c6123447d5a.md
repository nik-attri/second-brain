The dishwasher is about to become a robot. And this could be a much bigger business. What do you think?

We have spent decades making dishwashers more energy-efficient, quieter and smarter.
But we have barely challenged the biggest problem: A human still has to collect the dirty dishes, scrape food off them, sort them, load the machine, unload everything and put it away.
Why?
That is precisely the kind of repetitive, predictable work that robotics should eventually eliminate.
And the technology is starting to arrive.
Look at what is already happening:
 1. Armstrong Robotics — commercial kitchens
Raised $12 million to develop AI-powered kitchen robots.
Its robots are already deployed in a major restaurant chain.
The company reports more than 1 million dishes washed annually by its deployed systems.
Designed to operate 24/7 alongside conventional commercial dishwashers.
Uses 3D perception and neural networks to identify and manipulate dishes in messy environments.
 2. Sunday Robotics — the home kitchen
Its Memo robot demonstrates something even more interesting:
Clears dining tables.
Handles delicate wine glasses.
Collects plates and cutlery.
Loads dishwashers and starts washing cycles.
Can also make espresso and fold socks.
Sunday says a manually built Memo currently costs around $20,000, with a target to reduce manufacturing costs by at least 50%. Its beta programme is planned for late 2026.
3. The real breakthrough is AI, not the mechanical arm
Sunday's ACT-1 model demonstrated a table-to-dishwasher task involving:
33 unique dexterous interactions.
68 total interactions.
21 different objects.
More than 130 feet of autonomous navigation.
This is a very different level of complexity from a robot repeating the same movement on a factory production line.
Now consider the market opportunity.
Restaurants, hotels, hospitals, airports, corporate cafeterias and cruise ships process enormous volumes of dishes every day.
My five predictions:
Traditional dishwasher manufacturers will face competition from robotics companies, not just other appliance brands.
Commercial kitchens will increasingly buy dishwashing automation as a service rather than purchasing another appliance.
The next generation of home robots will be judged by how many hours of human labour they eliminate, not how many impressive tricks they can perform.
Robotic arms, computer vision and AI models could eventually turn the entire kitchen into an automated environment.
We could see a new $5,000–$10,000 household robotics category emerge as manufacturing scales and reliability improves. This is a potential price point, not a current market price.

I believe we are looking at the beginning of a new household robotics category.
The dishwasher was never the end product. Automating the human workflow around it is the real opportunity.
