This week is World Quality Week 2026, and one of the things our Quality Team challenged us to do was to write a poem or song to get us thinking about the importance of quality in our work. For our team, ensuring we collect and manage robust data has at times been a significant issue. Data lost in forgotten Teams folders and ancient archived directory structures, using multiple date formats in the same spreadsheet, ad hoc "data" sheets, or lost lab books, missing records & details, poor naming practices and a lack of consistency can all be issues, and proper training and building robust data handling practices are key to building a solid foundation for our science. Spreadsheet Hell is real, make sure your data doesn't end up there.
 
In science, our measurements are often all we have. You can’t go back and resample a bore from 2009. You can’t remeasure a water level that was never logged. If the calibration record is gone, it’s gone. A missing sample ID, an undated field sheet, or a file called “final_FINAL_v2.xlsx” can turn years of good fieldwork into guesswork.
 
Quality isn’t paperwork for its own sake. It’s what makes our science last:
 🔹 Calibrate your instruments, and record that you did
 🔹 Name samples consistently, in the field and in the file
 🔹 Keep the chain of custody intact
 🔹 Store data where the next person can find it, not in a Teams folder five levels deep
 
The people who rely on our data in 20 years can’t ask us what we meant. So:
 
Measure it. Record it. Verify. Or lose it all.
