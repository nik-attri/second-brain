🔄 Workflows, not departments.

Augmented Collaboration · 14/36 · The Engine
Organisations don't create value through departments. They create value through workflows.

This is one of the most important ideas in the Augmented Collaboration book — and one that our #badversion readers specifically asked us to explore.

Think about it.

A department is where people with similar skills sit. A workflow is how value actually gets created: from a customer's problem to a solution that makes them happy. Yet most organisations are still designed around departments, with separate teams, priorities, systems and processes. Customer insights get stuck in research. Product teams build on assumptions. Business model decisions happen somewhere else.
The result? Three critical workflows operating in isolation.

We call the alternative the Value Creation Engine. It connects three workflows in a continuous loop:
🔍 Customer Value – Understanding real customer needs and opportunities.
🛠️ Product Development – Turning those insights into products and services.
🚀 Business Model – Making sure the value can be delivered and captured sustainably.

One connected system, constantly learning and improving.
A great example comes from J.Crew Group, which introduced a concept that perfectly captures the problem: alignment latency.

⏱️ It's the time between when a decision is needed and when the right people can come together to make it.

Their response? A live work table where designers, merchants and suppliers collaborate in the same space.
Their insight says it all:
"The steps are the decisions, not the procedural sequence."
In other words, collaboration isn't something that happens after the work. It is the work.

💡 Try this Friday.
Bring together the people who actually execute one important workflow in your organisation.
Map it together:
🎯 What triggers the workflow?
🏁 What outcome are you trying to create?
👤 Who owns each stage?
🔗 Where are the handovers?
⚡ Where does work get delayed or information get lost?

Put the map on the wall. Date it. Compare it with reality.
Because the gap between how we think work happens and how it actually happens is where improvement begins. And with AI entering our organisations, this becomes even more important. We need to design workflows around how humans and AI create value together, not simply automate existing departmental processes.
