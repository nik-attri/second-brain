What if AI isn’t the thing that’s failing, and I t’s just exposing organisational dysfunction?

I’ve noticed something happening recently (especially on LinkedIn).

There’s a growing amount of:

“AI is crap.”

“Agents don’t work.”

“Look at the mistakes it’s making.”

“Humans can do this better.”

Some of that criticism is completely justified.

AI gets things wrong. In fact, unless kept in check, AI can be incredibly frustrating to use. 

But I wonder whether something else is starting to happen too.

AI might be exposing just how much of our organisations only works because human beings have learned how to compensate for them.

We know the process isn’t really the process.

We know who actually makes the decision.

We know which system not to trust.

We know which spreadsheet is the real one.

We know who to speak to when the official route doesn’t work.

We’ve spent years learning the workarounds.

Then we introduce an AI agent, give it the documented process, the available data and whatever enterprise context we’ve managed to pull together…

…and it F’s it up and the anti-AI folks say,

“See. AI doesn’t work.”

Maybe.

But there’s another possibility.

Maybe the AI agent has just exposed something we’ve become incredibly good at hiding.

The organisation doesn’t really know how it works in a way that can be consumed. 

Its value streams aren’t clear.

Its capabilities aren’t properly understood.

Its technology services and dependencies aren’t visible.

Ownership becomes fuzzy across the handoffs.

Context is fragmented, contradictory or out of date.

Humans can compensate for all of that.

AI agents can’t magically know the things we’ve never properly defined.

Here’s what worries me.

If we’re not careful, organisations will use early AI failure as evidence that humans were better at the old way of working, and use that as evidence to support inertia.  

When what it may actually prove is that humans became exceptionally good at surviving organisational dysfunction.

That would be one hell of an opportunity to squander.

Perhaps the question isn’t:

“Why did AI get it wrong?”

Perhaps the first question should be:

“What did AI just reveal about us?”

It’s time to Think Differently.
