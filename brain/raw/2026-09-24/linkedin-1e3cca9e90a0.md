Hardest Worker in the Room versus Smartest Worker in the Room:
I’m going to defend Excel.

For real estate asset management, Excel may be the industry’s best friend. It’s arguably the world’s best modeling tool. What it was never designed to be is the world’s best database. And yet, look at what has happened over the years.

One spreadsheet becomes two. Two become twenty. Twenty become dozens and dozens of spreadsheets that are being used to:
→ Store data
→ Track properties
→ Consolidate information
→ Normalize data
→ Calculate metrics
→ Feed reports
→ Share information
→ Maintain “the latest version”
→ And sometimes even act as the company’s database

That’s where Excel hell begins. And I don't think the answer is: “Get rid of Excel.”
Quite the opposite. The goal should be to reduce the Excel work that never should have been Excel work in the first place.

Let Excel do what it does incredibly well: Model. Analyze. Think.

Let a real data platform do what it does well: Ingest. Validate. Govern. Normalize. Integrate. Store. Report. Visualize.

Then connect the two. That’s the approach we take at Pereview. We’re not trying to take away the Excel models asset managers trust and have spent years building. We're trying to eliminate the dozens of spreadsheets that have slowly become a database.

So here's the question I want to throw out there: Is Excel really the problem in asset management?

Or is the problem that we've been over asking Excel to be something it was never designed to be?

Agree? Disagree? I’d genuinely like to hear it.
