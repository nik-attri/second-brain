AI cannot outrun a broken workflow.

This is one of the more awkward truths in business at the moment, mainly because broken workflows do not become less broken simply because someone has given them a chatbot and a nicer icon.

I understand the temptation. A new tool appears, everyone feels modern for approximately seven minutes, and the business gets to say it is “using AI,” which sounds much better than “we still approve things through a reply-all email chain that began during a different government.”

But after the novelty fades, the work underneath is often exactly where everyone left it.
The same approvals are slow. The same information is hard to find. The same reports are rebuilt manually. The same tasks depend on memory. The same process still needs three follow-ups, two screenshots and one person saying, “I think I saw that somewhere.”

AI may help an individual move faster, which is useful. I am not dismissing that. But if the workflow around that person is still wearing ankle weights, the business does not fly very far.

This is the difference between tool adoption and operational change.

Tool adoption is easy to announce. Operational change is harder because it means looking honestly at how work actually moves through the business. Where it gets stuck. Where people duplicate effort. Where information disappears. Where decisions wait. Where teams have quietly built survival rituals around systems that no longer fit.

That is the work I find more interesting.

Not “which AI tool did you add?” but “what did it actually change?” Did the process become clearer? Did the data become more useful? Did decisions move faster? Did people stop wasting time on things nobody should have been doing manually in the first place?

AI can be part of the answer. But it is rarely the whole answer.

The real value comes when intelligent tools are connected to better processes, clearer data, stronger workflows and systems designed around the way the business actually operates.

Otherwise, everyone gets a cape and the business still cannot take off.
