If a process needs to be done manually more than three times, I start asking: why haven't we automated this yet?

That is essentially what we sell at Chaser. So we should practice what we preach — especially me, as CEO.

When a repetitive task is taking a long time in a technology company, I get curious about the technology around it.

Could AI help? Could we automate part of it? Is there a tool already sitting on our desktops that we're not using properly? Could we change the process altogether?

I think everyone working in a technology company should have some level of techiness. Not everyone needs to code, but everyone should be curious about how technology can make them better and faster at what they do.

If it takes someone twelve hours a day to close five deals, I'll absolutely appreciate the effort and commitment.

But if someone figures out how to close the same five deals in five hours? That's where I get really excited.

Because I don't believe hours worked are a particularly interesting measure of performance. Outcomes are.

Some things genuinely take time. Good thinking takes time. Relationships take time. Difficult decisions take time.

But repetitive work shouldn't take a long time simply because that's how we've always done it.

For a technology company, efficiency isn't about asking people to work faster.

It's about constantly asking why the work needs to take that long in the first place.
