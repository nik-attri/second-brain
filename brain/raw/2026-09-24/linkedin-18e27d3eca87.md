We have spent years trying to stop bots from making payments.

Now payment networks are building infrastructure to let the right bots pay.

That is a fascinating shift.

Mastercard and Alchemy have announced an integration between Mastercard Agent Pay and Alchemy's AgentCard.

The idea:

You authorize an AI agent.
You define what it can spend.
You define where it can spend.
The agent can then complete eligible online purchases on your behalf.

Imagine saying:

"Find the best direct flight to Mumbai next Friday under AED 1,500 and book it."

The agent could potentially:

→ Search
→ Compare
→ Decide
→ Book
→ Pay

without requiring you to manually complete every step.

But the payment is actually the easy part.

The difficult question is TRUST.

How does a merchant know:

Is this a legitimate AI agent?

Who authorized it?

What exactly did the customer ask it to do?

What are its spending limits?

Has the instruction been altered?

Who is liable if something goes wrong?

This is why another recent development may be even more important.

Mastercard, Visa and Ant International are working on a common "Know Your Agent" framework.

Think of where this could eventually lead:

KYC → Know Your Customer

KYB → Know Your Business

KYA → Know Your Agent

Before an AI agent is allowed to transact, the ecosystem needs a way to identify and trust it.

And Mastercard is not stopping at payments.

Its Agent Suite for Merchants is also tackling product discovery, research and consumer-authorized purchases.

We are therefore moving from:

AI recommends → Human buys

to:

Human sets intent → AI discovers → AI decides → AI pays

That fundamentally changes digital commerce.

For banks, the opportunity is programmable permissions, agent identity and risk controls.

For merchants, the challenge becomes being discoverable and transactable by AI.

For payment networks, the opportunity may be becoming the trust layer between humans, agents and merchants.

The checkout button may not disappear tomorrow.

But for the first time, we can clearly see the infrastructure being built for a world where the customer may not be the one clicking it.

Would you trust an AI agent with a virtual card if you controlled its merchant access and spending limit?
