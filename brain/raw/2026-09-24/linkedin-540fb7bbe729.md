I think we might be asking the wrong question about AI.

Everyone keeps asking:

"Which jobs will AI replace?"

But I'm starting to think the much more interesting question is:

"Which parts of our jobs should AI replace?"

AI came up in a few conversations at an event at CodeSpace Academy in Málaga, and this is the question I kept coming back to.

Klarna is probably one of the best examples.

They went extremely hard on AI. Their workforce dropped significantly, they stopped hiring for a period, and they said their AI customer service agent was doing the work of hundreds of people.

Then they started correcting course.

Their CEO, Sebastian Siemiatkowski admitted they had probably focused too much on cost reduction. They started hiring humans again in customer service because, for some interactions, quality and human connection still mattered.

And I think there's something important in that.

Maybe the biggest opportunity for AI inside companies isn't:

❌ Replace the marketer
❌ Replace the salesperson
❌ Replace the finance team

Maybe it's:

→ Build the weekly marketing report automatically
→ Research and enrich accounts before sales calls
→ Keep the CRM clean
→ Summarise meetings and create follow-ups
→ Match transactions and flag anomalies
→ Find content opportunities from Search Console and competitor data
→ Turn all the boring operational work around a person into systems

Basically: automate the work people hate doing, not necessarily the people doing it.

This is actually becoming a big part of how we think about marketing at piquiyo.

We're spending more and more time building the systems around the marketing team: reporting, research, content operations, CRM workflows, monitoring, etc...

Not because we want AI to run marketing.

Because we want people to spend less time moving data between spreadsheets and more time actually thinking about marketing.

And I suspect this is where a lot of the real AI adoption inside companies will happen over the next few years.

Not "AI replaced 20% of our team."

But "AI removed 20% of the work nobody on our team wanted to do anyway."

Curious if others are seeing the same thing.
