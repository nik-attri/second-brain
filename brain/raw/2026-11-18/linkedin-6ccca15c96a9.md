Funny how the most useful AI projects are usually the most boring ones on paper

For example, I recently worked with a fast-growing company whose talent team was drowning in 4k+ applications a month with candidate data spread across different tools.

Instead of ripping out their stack, we built an AI layer on top of their setup that parses incoming CVs, evaluates fit against their specific role criteria, and pushes structured summaries and fit scores straight into their ATS

Hiring right now is stuck in a strange loop. Candidates use AI to apply for thousands of open roles, and companies use basic AI prompts to reject them. Good people can't get noticed, and recruiters still can't find the right fit

This project not only cut manual screening time by around 90% and made vetting way more consistent across the team, but it also changed how applications are evaluated

Now candidates are judged on what really matters to their leads (soft skills, real project ownership, etc etc) rather than the generic filters that default Claude or ChatGPT prompts usually care about (the candidate has 5 years with Node? Rejected. It's mentioned in the JD that you need 4+ years with Node.js, so the candidate is not qualified for this role)

Sometimes you don't need a massive R&D budget or tens of different AI SaaS subscriptions to make AI useful
