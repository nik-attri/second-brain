📊 Velocity in Scrum: How Do We Calculate It and Why Is It Important?

How does a Scrum Team decide how much work it can realistically take into the next Sprint?

One useful forecasting tool is Velocity.

📌 What is Velocity?
Velocity is the total number of story points completed by a Scrum Team during a Sprint, based on its Definition of Done.
📊 Let's understand with an example:
Consider the completed story points from the previous 5 Sprints:
🔹 Sprint 1 – 35 Story Points
🔹 Sprint 2 – 38 Story Points
🔹 Sprint 3 – 40 Story Points
🔹 Sprint 4 – 38 Story Points
🔹 Sprint 5 – 35 Story Points
How do we calculate Average Velocity?
Average Velocity = Total Completed Story Points ÷ Number of Sprints
186 ÷ 5 = 37.2 Story Points
So, the team's average velocity is approximately 37 story points per Sprint.
🎯 Why is Velocity useful before Sprint Planning?
Teams can use historical velocity to:
✅ Forecast how much work they may complete in the upcoming Sprint.
✅ Make realistic Sprint plans.
✅ Avoid overcommitting.
✅ Improve delivery predictability.
✅ Understand delivery trends over multiple Sprints.
👩‍💻 What should a Scrum Master remember?
Velocity is only one input during Sprint Planning.
Also consider:
Team availability and capacity.
Leave and holidays.
Dependencies and external commitments.
Complexity and uncertainty of upcoming work.
💡 Velocity vs Capacity
🔹 Velocity tells us how many story points the team completed in previous Sprints.
🔹 Capacity helps the team understand how much working time is available for the upcoming Sprint.
Important Reminder:
Velocity is not a target, competition or individual performance measurement.
It is a team-level forecasting tool based on past delivery data. The team should use it to make informed decisions, not to force higher commitments.
