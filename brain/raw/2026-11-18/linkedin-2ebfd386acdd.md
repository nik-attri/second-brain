Everyone is working. Nothing is moving.

Oh, I’ve been here. Haven’t we all? 😂

Everyone’s busy. Calendars are bursting, Teams is pinging itself into an early grave, spreadsheets are multiplying and somewhere, someone is arranging a meeting to discuss the actions from the last meeting.

And yet, the actual problem we were all hired to solve is still sitting there, looking at us.

Now, I’m a programme person. I love a good plan, clear governance and a RAID log. Give me a well-run programme and I’m a happy woman.

But where I get twitchy is…

When did following the process become more important than achieving the outcome? Tell me that! 

When did we start measuring success by how many boxes we’ve ticked rather than what we’ve actually delivered?

And why, for the love of all things holy, do we keep throwing more people at problems that are clearly caused by the way we’re working?

I’ve spent a good chunk of my career in NHS digital transformation, and I’ve seen some truly brilliant people achieve extraordinary things DESPITE the processes around them.

Imagine what they could achieve if those processes actually helped them.🤨

I’m not suggesting we throw governance out of the window. Far from it. In healthcare especially, good governance matters. Patient safety matters. Accountability matters.

But good governance should help us make decisions, manage risk and move forward. It shouldn’t become an endurance sport.

So when things aren’t moving, I tend to ask a few awkward questions.

Who actually needs to make this decision?

What’s stopping them?

Does this step add value, manage a genuine risk or exist because we’ve always done it this way?

And my personal favourite…

If we stopped doing this tomorrow, would anyone notice? 😂
And honestly most often the answer is uncomfortable. I say good. That’s usually where the useful conversations start.

I don’t believe the answer is always more resources, another methodology or a shiny new system.

Sometimes we need to roll our sleeves up, challenge a few sacred cows, simplify what we’ve made complicated and remember what the hell we were trying to achieve in the first place.

And yes totally , that might mean having a difficult conversation. I’m quite comfortable with those. 😉

It’s also exactly the sort of thinking I bring to my work with XST. Helping organisations get past the noise, tackle the real blockers and turn a lot of hard work into actual progress.

Because we’re not paid to ‘look’ busy.

We’re paid to make things better.

So here’s my question. What’s the most ridiculous process you’ve ever encountered that everyone followed but nobody could explain why?

Go on. I bet there are some absolute crackers. 😂
