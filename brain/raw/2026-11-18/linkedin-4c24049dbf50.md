The spreadsheet from hell continues to haunt us.

I built it because I wanted to get the things I usually kept in my head into a system my team could actually use.

It maps work, dependencies, responsibilities, execution steps, checkpoints and all sorts of other details. There's a frankly unreasonable number of Excel tables involved, formulae do most of the heavy lifting, and the best bit, no AI required. 😄

It took a lot of iterations to get the logic right. Every time I thought I'd accounted for everything, something new would pop up.

I've found that the same thing happens when I look at an established manufacturing or contracting business.

A buyer might see a company profile, a website and a proposal. Behind those documents sits an enormous amount of operational knowledge: engineering capabilities, project records, certifications, safety procedures, specialist equipment, delivery experience and the people who know how everything works.

The challenge is working out which information matters to which buyer, where it belongs, and how the different pieces should support one another.

A technical buyer may need detailed specifications. Procurement may need qualification documents. A commercial decision-maker may want relevant project experience and a clear explanation of the value involved.

Those are different information needs, and the business has to be able to meet all of them.

This is the part of my work I enjoy most: understanding how all the pieces fit together, finding the gaps and working out what needs to happen next.

The spreadsheet has probably made me a little too comfortable with complicated systems.

My team would probably agree. 😄
