Carrier rates move. Surcharges show up in December. And most small shops ship every box the same way they did last spring.

Grok Bot gives you AI teammates you message like a coworker. Zachary Mitchell of Brainfinite AI has been working through a two-Bot shipping setup for owners who ship every week.

Every Tuesday at 7 AM, a Rate Check Bot quotes the business's three standard box sizes on UPS, FedEx and USPS for its main lanes (here, San Antonio to Dallas, Denver, Atlanta and New York), using the carrier accounts the owner signed into on the Bot's computer. It adds the numbers to an Excel rate history in OneDrive and flags any lane that jumped or picked up a peak surcharge.

Then it hands off to a Shipping Desk Bot, which looks at last week's Shopify orders and lists where a cheaper service would still have arrived on time. One table lands in the owner's inbox by 9 AM.

What it never does: buy labels, change shipping settings, or contact a carrier. The person who knows the customers still picks the carrier. A Bot can see that one service is cheaper to Atlanta. It can't see that a client paid for 2-day, or that the last few boxes to that address arrived damaged.

A quick reality check worth passing on: rates quoted on a carrier site can differ from the final invoice, so compare the first couple of weeks against real invoices. And with fake "missed delivery" messages going around, the setup tells the Bot to work only in accounts signed in on its own computer, not from links in emails.

The example uses Excel, OneDrive and Shopify. A team on Square, WooCommerce, ShipStation or a shared spreadsheet can run the same idea with what's already in place.

This is one early direction, not the full system. How it gets built depends a lot on what you ship and how often, and more complete versions are coming as the process matures.
