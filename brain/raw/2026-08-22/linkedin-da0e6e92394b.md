A coworker posted a copilot prompt:
DESCRIBE ME LIKE YOU'RE TRYING TO WARN SOMEONE BEFORE THEY MEET ME.

We found the results to be funny, and, on point:


Warning before meeting Kenneth:

Kenneth has the rare and unsettling ability to remember what was decided in meetings.  Not what was discussed. What was actually decided.

Three months after everyone else has moved on, he'll casually say, "Didn't we agree on Option B because Option A increased costs by 17%?" and suddenly the entire room starts checking old meeting notes.

He approaches broken processes the way an exorcist approaches a haunted house. If a workflow requires six approvals, four spreadsheets, and a weekly sacrifice to the printer gods, he will not rest until it has been automated, documented, and reduced to three clicks.

Be careful when using phrases like:
"Industry best practice..."
"We've always done it this way..."
"The data probably says..."
"It's only a temporary workaround..."

These phrases attract him.

Kenneth suffers from a condition known as Chronic Process Optimization Disorder. Symptoms include:
·      Reorganizing things that technically work but clearly shouldn't.
·      Asking, "What's our success metric?" before allowing a project to continue.
·      Becoming physically uncomfortable around poorly governed CMDBs.
·      Seeing a manual process and immediately calculating how to automate it.

In a crisis, while normal people panic, Kenneth becomes suspiciously calm. The bigger the outage, the calmer he gets. At some point you'll wonder whether he's solving the problem or feeding off the chaos.

His hobbies include:
·      Finding the one missing requirement nobody considered.
·      Translating executive buzzwords into actual work.
·      Turning "quick fixes" into sustainable solutions.
·      Politely destroying weak business cases with follow-up questions.

Do not challenge him to find inconsistencies in a document.

He will.
He won't even be trying.

Final warning: Kenneth is generally friendly, reasonable, and easy to work with. However, if you tell him you've improved service quality, reduced operational costs, increased customer satisfaction, and streamlined processes, he may ask for the metrics.

And unlike most people, he's willing to wait for the answer. 😄

Short version:
 "Imagine if a service desk manager, an auditor, a project manager, and a Process Improvement Black Belt were combined into one person and given just enough military discipline to become unstoppable."
