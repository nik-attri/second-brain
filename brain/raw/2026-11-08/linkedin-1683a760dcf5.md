I genuinely don’t know how recruiters and HR professionals do it.

I recently posted an open position on my team, and within 24 hours, I had hundreds of applications, a flood of new connection requests, InMails, and messages. It’s been a lot.

Not complaining, lots of talent out there, but two things really stood out.

First, the sheer number of ChatGPT- and Claude-generated resumes.

Look, I’m all for using AI. But if you’re applying for a Communications Manager position, I’d love to see how you communicate, not how an LLM communicates.

The least you can do is give your resume a personal touch. Rethink the wording, make it sound like you, and perhaps give the design a little attention.

And then, ironically, when that personal touch was added, the number of typos and grammatical errors I came across was astonishing.

Now, I’m the last person to complain about typos. I have fat fingers, type mostly on my phone, and make my fair share of mistakes. But this is your resume. For a job in COMMUNICATIONS.

Which brings me to the second part of my Sunday rant.

Can we talk about the difference between contributing to something and leading it?

I completely understand embellishing a resume and selling yourself. We all do it, and there’s absolutely nothing wrong with putting your best foot forward.

But let’s be real. If you have 5–8 years of experience, chances are you haven’t personally led every communications strategy, campaign, transformation, stakeholder engagement programme, and crisis response your organisation has ever delivered.

And that’s perfectly fine!

I’m not taking anything away from what I’m sure is valuable experience. But when you claim leadership of everything, you’re also taking credit away from the colleagues who worked alongside you, the leaders who guided you, and the mentors who helped you grow.

There’s a big difference between leading, managing, contributing to, and supporting a programme. And all four are valuable.

Especially in communications, where our channels, disciplines, and responsibilities continue to expand, no single communications professional does it all. That’s precisely why we have teams.

So perhaps we need to recalibrate how we talk about our individual achievements versus what we’ve accomplished as part of a wider team.

Anyway, thank you for attending my Sunday rant.

Now, back to reviewing resumes manually, because apparently everyone has mastered the art of hitting all the right keywords with Claude. 🙃
