This Diwali, the new flex isn’t how many people you wish. It’s how well you’ve leveraged AI to wish them all!

From personalised AI-generated greetings to automated WhatsApp replies and scheduled messages, we’ve reached a point where even festive wishes have entered the world of automation.

Somewhere, someone has probably built an entire Diwali wishing workflow while the rest of us are still deciding what to write!

Funny as it sounds, it’s also a fascinating reflection of how quickly the world is adapting to AI.

What started with experimenting with ChatGPT has quietly moved into our everyday lives. We’re using AI to write, create, communicate, plan, research and now, even remember to wish people.

And I love that.

But there’s another side to this evolution that got me thinking.

There was a time when Diwali meant waiting for your phone to ring.

Old friends, relatives, colleagues, schoolmates and people you hadn’t spoken to in months would call. Some conversations lasted two minutes, others twenty. Sometimes, an unexpected call from someone could make your entire day.

The wish was almost secondary. The fact that someone took the time to call was what mattered.

Today, our contact lists are bigger, our lives are busier and technology has made reaching anyone incredibly easy. Yet, somehow, we seem to have less time for genuine conversations.

We’ve gone from calling people to texting them, from texting them to forwarding wishes, and now to having AI write and schedule those wishes for us.

Perhaps that’s the most interesting paradox of the AI era.

We’re getting incredibly good at automating communication, but are we getting any better at connecting?

As someone building an AI company, I couldn’t be more bullish about what this technology can unlock.

But I also believe the real promise of AI is to give us more time for the things that technology cannot replace.

Let AI take care of the repetitive work. Let it make us more productive. Let it take mundane tasks off our plates.

But make that call to an old friend yourself. Spend that extra hour with your family. Visit someone. Have a conversation that doesn’t begin with a notification.

Because the real value of a Diwali wish was never in how beautifully it was written or how quickly it reached someone.

It was in the thought behind it.

This Diwali, let’s compete on how much work we can automate, not how little time we can spend on the people who matter.

Wishing everyone a very Happy Diwali! 🪔
