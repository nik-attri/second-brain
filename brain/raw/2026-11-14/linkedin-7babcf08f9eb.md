Some clients leave and you should be clapping.

That's a finance director we work with, and he wasn't entirely joking.

His leadership team had sat down with a spreadsheet of every client who'd left in the last couple of years, expecting a bit of a wake. What they got was an awkward moment of maths. Nobody knew the gross margin per client. Not for the ones who'd gone, and not for the ones still there.

One departed client, a small business they'd supported for free for years, licences included, had left them a few hundred a month better off. Then the service director asked the question nobody had asked in years: we've never said no to a customer, so at what point do we?

Put gross margin per client on the scorecard, with a red line. Any client below it comes up in the weekly meeting. Give the ideal client profile proper exclusion criteria, so sales have something to say no with before a contract is signed.

The first review is usually a bit uncomfortable. Expect a long-standing account or two in the red, quite possibly one everybody likes. Some get repriced. Some get a polite goodbye.

With renewals and year-end figures coming up: know what a client is worth while they're still a client. Finding out at the leaving do is an expensive way to learn arithmetic.
