Somewhere along the way, B2B marketing forgot how to make people give a crap about a brand.

And that's a pretty big problem. (understatement of the year, Shannon)

We became obsessed with attribution, MQLs, CAC, conversion rates, pipeline velocity, and proving every dollar generated seventeen dollars in return. All important stuff. I love a good spreadsheet as much as the next emotionally damaged marketer.

But somewhere in that obsession, we forgot something.
People have to know who the hell you are before they can decide to buy from you.

Brand awareness used to be a fundamental marketing discipline.

Now it's treated like the weird uncle at Thanksgiving. Everyone knows he exists, but nobody wants to give him a seat at the revenue table.

"What's the immediate ROI?"
"How many leads did that generate?"
"Can we attribute that impression to a closed-won opportunity?"

Meanwhile, we're spending a fortune chasing buyers who've never heard of us, don't trust us, and couldn't distinguish our positioning from six competitors.

It's like putting a pug on a trampoline and asking him to sell Pokémon cards.

Lots of activity. Impressive bounce rates. Marketing celebrates 47 MQLs. Sales asks why nobody's buying. The CFO wants to know why there's a dog on the payroll.
And nobody knows who the hell the pug is, why he's selling Pokémon cards, or why they should trust a salesman with absolutely no neck.

Congratulations. 
You've generated 47 MQLs, zero customers, and a deeply confused Pikachu.

Here's the thing.
Brand isn't the opposite of demand generation. It's what makes demand generation work better.

It makes your outbound recognizable, your campaigns credible, your sales conversations easier, and your company more likely to make the shortlist before a buyer ever fills out a form. I'm not advocating for expensive brand campaigns with no accountability. I'm saying we need to stop pretending that everything valuable in marketing can be measured by a last-touch attribution report.

You can't capture demand from people who don't know you exist. And you can't build a sustainable growth engine by only chasing people already looking to buy.

Marketing's job isn't just to capture demand. It's to create the awareness, trust, and preference that make demand possible.

Because if nobody knows your brand, you're not running a demand generation strategy.

You're just paying a pug to bounce harder.

And somewhere in Salesforce, that's being reported as pipeline velocity.
