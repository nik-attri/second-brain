Everyone made a dashboard this week!
Scrolling LinkedIn again and almost every post is the same one. I made a Corporate Finance Dashboard with Claude, I made an FP&A model in an afternoon. Fine, I believe you. 
We have been here before, haven’t we? An Excel dashboard was once an achievement worth announcing, then Power BI arrived and a report with slicers was a story. Nobody posts either today. When everyone can do a thing it becomes a commodity.
So the posts will stop, and soon. The consequences are only starting, and this is what I expect for enterprise applications.
1. The screen layer loses its pricing power. Vendors whose differentiation is reports and drag-and-drop configuration are exposed, and seat-based licensing with them. The main user of an enterprise application becomes an agent, and agents don’t buy seats.
2. Value moves down the stack. Those dashboards are the last 5% of the work. The rest is agreeing what EBITDA means, tying the number to the ledger, knowing who changed an assumption, why and when, surviving a reorganisation at monthend. None of that got cheaper because the front end did.
3. Shadow IT returns at 100x. Excel hell was thousands of spreadsheets, each with its own truth. Next is thousands of generated apps with no owner and nobody who remembers the prompt. Agents will not stop at building either, they will run the process. Traceability and auditability become as important as security, together with the evidence behind every decision an agent took. 
4. Build versus buy shifts at the edges. The long tail of point solutions gets built in-house. The core stays bought, companies buy accountability rather than software and somebody has to be liable when the number is wrong.
5. Implementation services take the hardest hit. Man-days of configuration compress faster than licence revenue does.
When everyone can build, building is no longer the skill. Knowing what should be built and being able to prove what was done is the scarce part.
