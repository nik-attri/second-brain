The AI productivity paradox

Are we becoming more productive, or are we simply getting better at doing more things?
AI has dramatically increased our ability to produce.
We can create content faster, analyze data quicker, automate workflows and accelerate execution across almost every business function.
But here's a question worth asking:
Are these productivity gains necessarily translating into better business outcomes?
There is a distinction between doing things faster and doing the right things better.
If AI enables a team to produce ten times more content, but none of it creates meaningful customer engagement, have we really improved productivity?
If automation accelerates a flawed process, have we created efficiency or simply scaled inefficiency?
The real opportunity with AI isn't just to increase output.
It's to fundamentally rethink how work gets done, where human intervention creates the greatest value, and how technology can improve the quality of outcomes.
Perhaps the next phase of AI maturity won't be measured by how much we produce, but by how much more value we create.
