𝗔𝗴𝗲𝗻𝘁𝗶𝗰 𝗔𝗜 𝗶𝘀 𝗺𝗼𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗳𝗿𝗼𝗺 𝗵𝘆𝗽𝗲 𝘁𝗼 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗶𝗻 𝗶𝗻𝘀𝘂𝗿𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗲.
In 2025–2026 we’re seeing the first wave of multi-agent systems actually handling work — not just summarizing documents.
𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗰 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗯𝗹𝗲𝗺𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗲𝘆’𝗿𝗲 𝘀𝗼𝗹𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴:
• Underwriters drowning in broker submissions
• Simple claims taking days instead of hours
• Product launches that still take months
• Legacy cores that make every modernization project painful
𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁’𝘀 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗶𝗻 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗱𝘂𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗿𝗶𝗴𝗵𝘁 𝗻𝗼𝘄:
𝗔𝗹𝗹𝗶𝗮𝗻𝘇 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗷𝗲𝗰𝘁 𝗡𝗲𝗺𝗼 – 7 specialized agents process low-value food spoilage claims end-to-end. ~80% faster settlement. Humans still approve every payout.
𝗭𝘂𝗿𝗶𝗰𝗵 + 𝗖𝘆𝘁𝗼𝗿𝗮 – Commercial intake agents cut manual triage from 75 minutes to 15 minutes. Straight-through rates jumped from 10% to 95% across five countries.
𝗔𝗜𝗚 (𝗖𝗹𝗮𝘂𝗱𝗲-𝗽𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗱) – Underwriters reviewing 30%+ more submissions and quoting 55% faster in key lines. Submission review time cut by more than 90% where deployed.
𝗛𝗗𝗜 𝗦𝗲𝗴𝘂𝗿𝗼𝘀 + 𝗶𝗻𝘀𝘂𝗿𝗲𝗠𝗢 – AI agents generating specs, APIs, and workflows from natural language. ~85% reduction in product development time.
𝗧𝗿𝗮𝘃𝗲𝗹𝗲𝗿𝘀, 𝗗𝘂𝗰𝗸 𝗖𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗸, 𝗚𝘂𝗶𝗱𝗲𝘄𝗶𝗿𝗲, 𝗦𝗵𝗶𝗳𝘁, 𝗖𝗙𝗖 and others rolling out agentic FNOL, renewal, and underwriting assistants.
𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀:
Agents handle the high-volume, rules-heavy, document-heavy work.
Humans keep authority on binding, payouts, and complex judgment.
Heavy governance, audit trails, and clear escalation paths are non-negotiable.
We’re still early. Most systems are selectively autonomous, not fully autonomous. But the productivity jumps on scoped use cases are already material.
