What do I, as a Financial Planner, actually do with my own money?

2) Investments

I started investing at 26, back in 2022 with £200, just dipping my toe in.
For the first three weeks I basically checked the app every day to make sure it hadn’t instantly gone to zero (which, let’s be honest, is how a lot of people in the UK think investing works).

Eventually I took the plunge and invested around £9,000. Not life‑changing money, but a decent chunk of my emergency fund at that age. It was a leap of faith, guided by a friend who was an advisor, and I used Nutmeg at the time (no longer exists, unfortunately).

It took 13 months before that investment made any money. I was in the red the entire time. And that’s exactly why I talk so much about avoiding emotional decisions. I only invested what I could afford to lose, and I knew I was in it for the long term, that took the stress out of seeing those negative numbers.

My strategy since then:

From 2023 to 2025, I invested £200 a month, consistently. Any lump sums I received went in too. Since becoming self‑employed, my rule is simple: I contribute something every month. Some months are bigger, some are smaller but the habit stays the same. You’ll notice a theme across these posts: consistency.

What am I invested in?
99% equities
87% developed markets
12% emerging markets
Actively managed

Results: Across that timeframe, I’ve seen 45% growth. It’s not life‑changing money, but it’s a hell of a lot better than a Cash ISA return.

I give myself more flexibility with this pot. It’s a long‑term retirement booster, but I’m under no illusion that I might use some of it before then. That flexibility comes at the cost of some tax relief I’d get through pension contributions, but this is the real world, not an Excel spreadsheet, and flexibility matters.

If you are unsure on investing, find it scary or just want to learn more, reach out.
