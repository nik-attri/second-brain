You can have every advantage in the world and still fail to make the most of it.

I was reminded of this during a 2-hour conversation with a friend Prakhar Saraswat who has been into tech for years and using AI since the GPT-2 days.

After my last post about wanting to understand AI beyond just using the tools, I decided to call him.

Honestly, I did hesitate.

Kisi ko phone karke bolna ki bhai, mujhe ye nahi aata, sikha do like admitting what you don't know isn't always easy.

But bhai, agar seekhna hai toh poochna toh padega hi.
So I called him.

“Bhai, mujhe ye samajhna hai. Batao kaise hota hai.”

And we ended up talking for two hours. The practical insights I got would probably have taken me days to gather through videos, articles and courses.

Here are five things that stayed with me ⬇️ 

1️⃣  Learn what matters to your work.

-> Knowing every new AI tool isn't the goal. Figure out where you can save time, simplify your work and deliver better results. That's where the real value is.

2️⃣  Stop chasing buzzwords.

Vibe coding, AI agents, prototypes like sab theek hai bhai, seekho.

-> But what are you building? 
-> What problem does it solve? 
-> Who gets value from it?

If there's no useful outcome, fancy terminology means nothing.

3️⃣  You're not late.

I told him I sometimes feel like technology has moved so fast that I've fallen behind.

He said:
“Bhai, duniya toh hamesha aage badhegi. Toh usse kya farak padta hai? Jo seekhna hai seekho. Apne pace pe cope up karo.”

Simple baat hai. There's always going to be something new to learn. Stop panicking about what you don't know and start learning what matters to you.

4️⃣  Not everything needs AI.

If doing something manually is faster and simpler, just do it manually.
Har jagah AI ghusa ke dimaag ka dahi karna optimisation nahi hai.

5️⃣  Become a better driver.

This was my favourite takeaway from the conversation 

He said bhai imagine a Mercedes and a WagonR. Now give both cars to a professional racer.

The cars have different capabilities, but the driver's skills still matter.
A skilled driver understands the machine, knows its limits and knows how to get the best out of it.

And that's the point.

Everyone is busy asking which AI tool is the best, but how many are actually working on becoming better at using these tools?

Models will change. New features will come. Tools will keep evolving.

You don't always need a better car. Sometimes, you need to become a better driver.

That's what I'm focusing on. Keep learning, keep experimenting and don't be afraid to ask questions.

Because nobody is going to learn it for you.
