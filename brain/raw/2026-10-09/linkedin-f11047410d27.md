„Hello?“, „Yes?“, „Speaking with …?“
If you have ever called outside of your own country, you may have noticed cultural differences in what you hear when someone picks up the phone.

The most commonly used greeting across Europe is “Hello”, which originated from a suggestion made by inventor Tomas Edison to David Trueman, president of the Central District and Printing Telegraph Company of Pittsburgh, in 1877. Edison considered “Hello” to be the most appropriate greeting when answering the phone and recommended to standardise it for telephone conversations.

In many countries, callers now use variations of hello in their native language, such as „Allô?“ in French or „Halo“ in Croatian. While the map shows the most common response when receiving a call from an unknown number, different variations exist within a country, depending on the language or dialect, the recipient’s age, or region they live in. In Belgium, for example, how you answer the phone depends on where you grew up. In the French-speaking part, people might say „Allô?“, while in the Dutch-speaking part, they might introduce themselves with „Met [name]“.

The way we greet on the phone is shaped by cultural and historical developments. Italians, for instance, likely adopted the greeting “Pronto!” (engl.: Ready) in the early days of telephone communication, when operators manually connected users, using the word „pronto“ to indicate that the connection had been established. Nowadays, making a phone call is fortunately much easier, but old habits have stuck with us to this day.

Consider this a reminder to call someone you haven’t spoken to in a long time, no matter where in the world they are located right now.

Note: The category 'Own name' refers to answering the phone by stating either one’s full name, first name or last name. Other common responses in Germany or the Netherlands include „Hallo“, in Spain „¿Diga/-me?“ and in Slovenia „Prosim?“.
