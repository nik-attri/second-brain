Most founders run a raise on zero principles. Here are the five principles that actually get you funded:
 
1. Maintain unshakable conviction
 
You are the product. Your startup is the evidence.
Investors buy your conviction and the clarity of your vision. Present an unavoidable future and they will buy into it.
What makes you unfundable: protecting some hypothetical plan you'll never achieve. Plans change. The future you've seen doesn't.
 
2. Run the process
 
You will put years into this company. The partner investing will put in hours. Investors are guests in your reality.
 
Make every investor meet you on your timeline. All of them in parallel, same start, same closing date. You decide who gets what, and when.
 
What makes you unfundable: following the VC's process. When the meeting runs on their rules they feel at home, and they pass.
 
3. Pitch in 60 seconds
 
Three lines open every meeting. What you do. Your traction. The opportunity. Then the demo.
 
Describe the mechanism without jargon: "we turn three hours of spreadsheet hell into 30 seconds."
 
Answer their questions in 30 to 60 seconds each. Then qualify them: typical check, checks written in the last six months, their process.
 
What makes you unfundable: a 15 minute monologue educating investors. They came to ask questions, not to hear a lecture.
 
4. Say no
 
No deck before the first meeting. "Here's a one pager. Give me 15 minutes and I'll walk you through it."
 
No meeting an associate. "Bring a partner and I'm there."
 
No data room before terms. "Once we agree on valuation, check size and timeline, you get access."
 
Saying yes kills more deals than no. No sparks curiosity and holds the frame.
 
What makes you unfundable: obeying their every whim. Sending the deck, pitching with the deck, meeting associates.
 
5. Close with options
 
A healthy raise is 40 to 50 partner meetings. Around 10% show real interest, and 20% of those offer a term sheet.
 
So you need options, and you need them at the same time.
 
Get 3 funds to 50/50. Then call each one: "I value transparency and your time, so I'm updating you. This deal could close within the next week." Ask for nothing.
 
There are only two ways they can respond. "Don't wait for us," which is a pass. Or "We're in," and they'll tell you exactly what they need to close.
 
What makes you unfundable: waiting for VCs to tell you they're investing. They decide alone, after the meeting, with no map. So you set the next step and the date before you leave the room.
 
100+ founders I've coached raised over $1.2B on these five.
