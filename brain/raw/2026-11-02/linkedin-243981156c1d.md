Only 45 out of 1,949 open Data Engineering roles explicitly signal an early-career level in the title.

That's 2.3%. Include internships, and it's still under 4%.

I analyzed a November 1 snapshot from Jobs Index, my data engineering project that collects job postings from company career pages using Workday, Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, SmartRecruiters, Gupy, and other hiring systems.

Here's what I found:
• 45 roles have titles indicating Junior, Associate, Data Engineer I, or Entry-level.
• 31 roles are internships.
• 646 roles (33%) don't specify a seniority level in the title at all.

That last group is what caught my attention.

A position titled simply "Data Engineer" could be suitable for someone with two years of experience. Or the company might already expect five.

The title alone doesn't tell candidates which.

Of course, this doesn't mean only 45 positions are actually open to junior engineers. Seniority requirements may appear in the job descriptions.

It's also a single-day snapshot, with coverage leaning toward larger companies.

But it raises an interesting question about how companies communicate hiring expectations.

Recruiters and hiring managers:

When you publish a role titled simply "Data Engineer," is that intentional flexibility to consider different experience levels?

Or is the seniority already defined internally, just not reflected in the title?
