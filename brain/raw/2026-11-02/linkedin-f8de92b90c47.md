Well, it's finally live. FutureDay is out there.

(After eight months locked away in my cave building this, the daylight’s taking a bit of getting used to.)

I've been out of work a few times over the years, and every time it was tough. 

It's only getting tougher, and hearing nothing back is pretty much the norm now.

I know from my own searches, and from talking to a lot of people going through it right now, that it really drains the life out of you. 

And the longer it drags on, the more it takes.

You're losing money, you're constantly fielding "how's the job hunt going?" from family and friends, and it starts to hit your health and your confidence.

The question I get asked most is "what am I doing wrong?" And the honest answer is, usually nothing.

People are doing what we've all been told to do: build a resume, apply on job boards, and wait.

That just doesn't work anymore.

The job search has changed.

What's always worked for me is getting hired through people, not job boards. 

Every job I've had bar one came that way.

And don't just take my word for it.

Ashby, the recruiting software company, looked at 38 million job applications. 40% of people who were referred got an interview.

Of those who applied cold, just 3% did.

But the problem for me, and for most of the experienced people I've been speaking with, was never knowing what to do.

It was keeping it all together and showing up every day.

I had contacts in a spreadsheet, drafts in ChatGPT, follow-ups in another app, and about five versions of my resume.

I'd lose track of who I'd messaged, and somewhere along the way I felt like I'd lost control and my confidence with it.

So I built FutureDay to give back what the job search takes out of you.

The visibility, the control and the confidence to get more conversations, more interviews, and get hired sooner.

It gets your resume and LinkedIn sorted, keeps track of the people you know and the companies you want to work for, and every morning gives you a short list of what to do next: who to reach out to and what to say.

You just rock up, do your five things, and come back tomorrow.

I'm not an extrovert. 

FutureDay is written for people like me: good at the job, not natural at selling themselves, and tired of working out what to do next.
