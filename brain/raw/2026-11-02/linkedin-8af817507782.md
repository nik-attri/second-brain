Buffett's buying checklist is in print. Few people use it.

Most of us choose by what's easy to measure.

Price, size and speed. Whatever fits in a spreadsheet column.

The first time I bought a business, I started with a spreadsheet of revenue, margins, price and industry. Transferability wasn't a column.

Berkshire Hathaway prints its buying criteria in its annual shareholder letters. They're free.

Warren Buffett looks at the harder things. 

Here are the six criteria, with what to check for each:
→ Consistent earnings.
↳ Berkshire looks for profit that repeats every year.
↳ Trust the last three years over the forecast.

→ Good returns on little debt.
↳ A strong business earns well without heavy borrowing.
↳ If it only works at maximum leverage, it doesn't work.

→ Management in place.
↳ The business keeps running when the founder leaves.
↳ Ask who runs it after the owner leaves.

→ A simple business.
↳ Berkshire sticks to businesses it can understand.
↳ If you can't explain it in two sentences, wait.

→ A clear price.
↳ Buffett knows what he'll pay before the talks start.
↳ Know your number and why, then stick to it.

→ Size that fits.
↳ Berkshire's list asks for purchases big enough to matter to Berkshire.
↳ Your first purchase should matter to you and still be small enough to run.

The one I'd tape to my monitor is management in place. A business that can't run without its owner is a job, not an asset.

Buffett hunts for businesses that keep working without him. That one idea runs through the whole checklist.

One line from his 2008 letter covers the rest:
"Price is what you pay; value is what you get."

Save this checklist for your next purchase.

Which criteria do most people skip?
