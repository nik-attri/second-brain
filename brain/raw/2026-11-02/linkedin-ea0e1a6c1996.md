The automation projects that stick have one thing in common: the client already mapped the broken process before we touched it.

Not a vague complaint. An actual map. Here's where the work enters, here's where it stalls, here's what someone does manually to push it through.

When that exists, we can build something that fits. When it doesn't, the first two weeks become an archaeology dig: interviewing three people who each describe a different version of the same workflow.

The technology is rarely the constraint. Understanding the process clearly enough to hand it to a system: that's the hard part.

We now ask one question on the first call: walk me through the last time this process broke, step by step. The answer tells us more than any written brief.

What question do you use to get a team thinking clearly about how their own work actually runs?
