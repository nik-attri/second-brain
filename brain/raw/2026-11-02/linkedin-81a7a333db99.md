The 4 questions we ask before touching any automation project. Take them.

Most ops automation fails not because the tools broke. It fails because nobody asked the right questions before starting.

We've mapped 137+ workflows. The ones that went wrong had one thing in common: we were handed a process description, not a process audit. Different thing.

Before we build anything, we ask:

1. Who actually owns this workflow right now?
(Not who it's assigned to -- who decides when it's done. If that's unclear, the automation will break on the edge cases they've been quietly managing by hand.)
2. What's the failure mode when it goes wrong?
(Every workflow fails. If the team can't describe the failure, they haven't run it enough to automate it.)
3. Is this workflow stable, or is it changing?
(Automating a moving target costs 3x what it should. We wait for stable, then build.)
4. What does the person doing this manually do with 10 hours a week back?
(If there's no answer, the automation won't get used. Someone will quietly keep doing it by hand "just to check.")

Four questions. Thirty minutes. Saves weeks of rework.
