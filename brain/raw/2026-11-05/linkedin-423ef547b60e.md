What if your next customer isn't a person — but an AI agent?

For years, Performance Marketing has been built around one fundamental assumption: We need to convince people to choose our products. 

We compete for attention. We optimize ads, improve landing pages, test creatives, audiences and conversion funnels. But what happens when people start delegating the shopping process to AI?

Imagine a customer asking an AI assistant: "Find me the best laptop for video editing. Under €1,500. Lightweight, good battery life, and available for delivery this week." The AI searches, compares specifications, checks prices, evaluates alternatives — and potentially makes the purchase. No browsing through ten websites. No scrolling through sponsored results. No comparing five different offers manually.

So where does Performance Marketing fit into this journey?
This is already becoming a real business discussion. On November 4, Reuters reported that Amazon and Walmart are taking very different approaches to AI shopping agents.
Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse from accessing its platform, while Walmart is enabling third-party agents to interact with its shopping ecosystem.

Why does this matter?

Because this is not just about technology. It's about who controls product discovery, customer relationships and, ultimately, the purchase decision. 
Morgan Stanley estimates that AI shopping agents could account for 10–20% of US e-commerce spending by 2030. That's a forecast, not today's reality. But it's worth paying attention to. And here's what I find particularly interesting from a marketing perspective.
If an AI agent is comparing products, what will influence its choice? Accurate and complete product data? Price and availability? Reviews and independent sources? Brand reputation and product differentiation? Delivery options and return policies?
Probably all of them.

But one thing is becoming increasingly important:
Having a great product is no longer enough if AI systems cannot properly understand, evaluate and recommend it.

And that raises some serious questions.
For PPC teams: What happens to paid placements when customers delegate product discovery to AI agents?
For SEO and Content teams: How do we make product information accessible and understandable to AI systems?
For E-commerce and Product teams: Are our product catalogues ready for this shift?
For CMOs: Who owns the customer relationship when an AI assistant becomes the primary shopping interface?

I don't think traditional advertising, branding or human creativity are going away.
But I do think we're approaching a change in how purchase decisions are influenced.

We spent years optimizing for the click. Then we started optimizing for visibility in AI answers.Now we may need to think about something else entirely: 

How do we make sure an AI agent chooses our product?
Are we ready to market to machines as well as people?
