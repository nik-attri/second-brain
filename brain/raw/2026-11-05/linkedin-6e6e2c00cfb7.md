You love chocolate. You love peanut butter (I hope, otherwise this will be a strained analogy). The Summit South is the Reese's Peanut Butter Cup of nursing home law. 



That's the best way I can describe it (this wasn't Claude's idea, it was mine). 

I went to The Summit in Denver and loved it. Here's why:

In a nursing home case, the numbers tell you WHAT happened. The staffing data, the cost reports, where money came into the building and where it went out.

But a jury doesn't decide a case on a spreadsheet. They decide it on the story of one person. Your client. The resident who got hurt.

This CLE builds the bridge between them.

Ernest Tosh is a mathematics wizard (long beard and all. no pointy cap though). He'll show you how to read the cost reports, follow the money, and turn PBJ staffing data into proof of short staffing a jury can actually feel.

Ken Connor, yes, THE Ken Connor, is a legendary trial lawyer. He'll show you how to take those numbers and make them part of your client's story, from jury selection through closing. You will be moved to tears in some if it. Bring tissues. 

Chocolate and peanut butter.

If you do nursing home abuse and neglect work, this is for you. And not just the lawyers. Paralegals, staff, even legal nurse consultants, you belong in this room too. You're often the ones digging through the staffing records first.

If you've ever wanted to really understand the staffing piece of these cases, this is the conference to attend.
