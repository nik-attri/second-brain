STOP TRYING TO SOLVE THE WHOLE PUZZLE. 🧩

One of the biggest lessons I’ve learned in business and leadership is that overwhelming problems rarely become easier by simply staring at the whole thing.

In fact, sometimes the size of the problem is what stops us from starting.

We look at the mountain of work ahead, the declining numbers, the difficult customer, the underperforming team, the cash-flow challenge, the major project or the uncertainty about what comes next…

…and our first reaction can be:

“Where the hell do I even start?”

I’ve been there.

But here’s what I’ve learned.

You don’t have to solve the entire problem today.

You have to solve the next piece.

Break the problem down.

What is actually happening?

What do we know?

What don’t we know?

What is within our control?

What is the single most important thing we can do right now to move forward?

Then do that.

And once that piece is solved, reassess the situation and tackle the next one.

That isn’t avoiding the big problem.

That’s how big problems get solved.

Great leaders understand this.

They don’t necessarily have all the answers when they walk into the room. They create clarity when everyone else is staring at complexity.

They take something that feels chaotic and turn it into a series of manageable decisions.

They give their people direction without pretending everything is certain.

They replace:

“How are we going to fix all of this?”

with:

“What’s the most important thing we need to fix first?”

That question can completely change the energy of a team.

Because progress creates confidence.

One decision leads to another.

One problem gets removed.

One obstacle gets overcome.

One small win creates momentum.

And suddenly, the problem that looked impossible yesterday doesn’t look quite so impossible anymore.

I’ve also learned that movement creates clarity.

You don’t always need another meeting, another spreadsheet, another strategy session or another hour thinking about what could go wrong.

Sometimes you need to take the first step.

Start.

Learn.

Adjust.

Keep moving.

Business isn’t about having a perfect roadmap before you begin.

Leadership certainly isn’t.

It’s about having enough clarity to take the next right step, then having the courage and discipline to take the one after that.

So if you’re facing something today that feels too big, don’t try to solve the whole puzzle.

Find the next piece.

Put it in place.

Then find the next one.

Because sometimes the fastest way to conquer an overwhelming challenge isn’t to work harder on the entire problem.

It’s to make the problem smaller.

One piece.

One decision.

One action.

One win at a time.

That’s how momentum is built.

That’s how businesses grow.

And that’s how leaders turn “impossible” into “done.”
