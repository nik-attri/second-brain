You want premium service, perfect schedules, zero warranty headaches—and then ask your best roofer to match the guy who can barely answer his fucking phone.

I see it constantly.

A roofing contractor comes in $500 or $1,000 cheaper and suddenly I’m being asked:

“Can you match this?”

Sometimes I can.

Sometimes I sharpen the pencil and take less margin because I value the relationship.

Sometimes we find a comparable product, change a specification, improve logistics, work with suppliers, or find another way to legitimately reduce the cost.

But sometimes the answer needs to be no.

Because we’re not comparing the same thing.

You’re comparing a number on a spreadsheet.

I’m looking at the entire job. Start to finish. 

Will they show up when the house is ready?

Do they have enough quality crews to support your production?

Will they answer the superintendent at 6:30 AM when something goes wrong?

Will they handle punch quickly?

Will they document the job?

Will they answer warranty calls two years from now?

Will they fix their mistakes without sending you another invoice?

Can they scale with you when you go from 20 houses a month to 60?

That’s what you’re actually buying.

And I understand why builders are pushing so hard.

This market is difficult.

You’re paying $20,000–$50,000+ in incentives and rate buydowns just to move inventory. Purchasing teams are under enormous pressure to find savings everywhere they can.

I don’t blame you for negotiating.

Hell, you should negotiate.

But there’s a difference between asking your partners to help you find savings and continuously forcing your best partners to compete with the lowest bidder in the market.

Because eventually that has consequences.

You make it harder for me to retain the best labor.

You reduce the resources I can dedicate to your account.

You make it harder for me to prioritize you over customers who value everything we bring to the table.

And eventually you squeeze so much out of the relationship that the contractor you actually wanted isn’t the contractor you’re left with.

Some of our best customers understand this.

They don’t call and say:

“Match it or we’re replacing you.”

They call and say:

“Devin, I need help. How do we get this number down?”

Now we’re fucking partners.

I’ll call suppliers.

I’ll look at products.

I’ll analyze waste.

I’ll work through specifications.

I’ll look at scheduling and logistics.

I’ll squeeze our own numbers where I reasonably can.

I’ll do everything possible to help that customer win.

And when that same customer calls me in a bind?

We’re moving mountains.

Those are the relationships you go to war for.

The cheapest roofer might save you $1,000 on the bid.

But the right roofer can save you from thousands of problems you never had to deal with in the first place.

There’s a lesson in that.
