I can't help myself. 

Now that the yard signs are coming down and our phones have finally stopped buzzing with text messages from candidates (fingers crossed), I’d like to borrow one of the most famous lines in campaign history. With full credit, and mild apologies, to James Carville.

Every company I talk to has someone who built something genuinely cool with AI this year. A custom assistant that drafts proposals. A project that summarizes every sales call. A spreadsheet agent held together by one person’s prompt and good intentions.

I love it. I’ve built more than a few myself, and they have changed how I work. Hell, they've changed how I operate. That said, a personal AI win is not an organizational capability, and the gap between the two is where almost all AI investment quietly goes to die.

What happens when the person who built the cool thing that was "adopted" goes on vacation? Or leaves? Whose account is the data sitting in? Did anyone check what got pasted into it? What security measures are built across the organization so the content isn't loose on the Internet? Can anyone else explain how it works?

Three things turn a clever personal project into something a business can actually run on:

1. Implementation. It’s wired into the systems people already use, with the right data access, real support, and someone to call when the workflow gets wonky at 2 a.m.

2. Change management. Workflows get redesigned around it, people get trained, and adoption gets measured. A tool nobody uses has an ROI of exactly zero.

3. Governance. Someone owns it, with a strong network and cybersecurity moat round implementation. It’s clear what data it can touch, who sees the outputs, and how it gets reviewed.

None of that is as fun as the demo. All of it is the difference between “look what I made” and “this is how we work now.”

So, to rework the slogan for the boardroom: "It’s the implementation, stupid."

What’s the best AI build in your organization that only one person knows how to use?
