"AI can inform. It must not decide."

That's Antoine Kanaan, who built HAQQ, a legal AI system, around one rule many AI products skip: the machine never gets the final call.

His reasoning is simple. A machine cannot be accountable. A lawyer can. So ethical calls, nuanced strategy and counselling clients in emotionally charged moments stay with people.

Three takeaways from his MasterMinds conversation:

1. Build into the whole workflow, not one task. Legal AI earns lasting use when it runs through a firm's entire process, not just drafting.
2. Governance is a feature. HAQQ slowed product work to ship human sign-off, audit logs and permissions, and it refuses to train on client data.
3. Automate the structured analysis. Keep the final judgement with the accountable lawyer.

Our read: in regulated industries across MENA, the AI products that win will be the ones a compliance team can sign off on, not the ones with the flashiest demo.
