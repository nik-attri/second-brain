What happens when we bring LLMs into qualitative research? And can we do so without compromising methodological rigor?

🎉 We are happy to share that our paper “Reproducible Workflows for LLM-Assisted Qualitative Coding and Text Classification in Systematic Literature Reviews” has just been published in the International Journal of Qualitative Methods.

Our key contributions include:
🔹 Reproducible workflows for LLM-supported coding tasks on different levels of complexity
🔹 A roadmap to efficient prompt and codebook refinement with LLMs
🔹 Systematic reliability assessment using different metrics
🔹 An interdisciplinary comparison of statistical performance measures and reliability indicators
🔹 A full open-access Python pipeline (“PACO”) that automates coding workflows, reliability testing with different metrics, logging, and evidence-line checks.
🔹 A collection of limitations and pitfalls we encountered, and how we mitigated them

➡️ Find links to the publication and the PACO pipeline in the comments below.
 
One central finding:
🔍 LLMs can help scale and accelerate coding, but their reliability depends on the complexity of the task. LLMs perform well in simple tasks, such as text classification or extraction. But when codes become complex, when we actually have to interpret nuanced meaning, substantially more alignment effort is required to achieve reliable results with an LLM. And even then, its performance always plateaued below human-human agreement.
