4-5 lakh lines of code. One engineer. Just two weeks.
Sounds impressive, right? But here is the part that got me thinking.

When Jigar Dafda, Chief Technology and Product Officer at Fynd, shared how AI is making this possible, my immediate reaction was:
Who is checking all that code?

Also:
• Can AI securely test software built by AI?
• What happens when a security “fix” breaks an important business function?
• How do companies protect sensitive customer and business data?
• And who remains accountable after the product goes live?
These are some of the questions we explored in the latest episode of Fynd Out with Ragini.

One example from our conversation really puts this into perspective:
Imagine an application with 5,000 different permissions. How would anyone manually test every possible scenario? That is where AI comes in. It can simulate thousands of users, test how those permissions work and spot security gaps that might otherwise go unnoticed.

But AI may also “solve” an issue by removing access to a feature the business genuinely needs.

That is why faster building cannot mean less human oversight.

At Fynd, we combine AI with years of hands-on operational experience to solve problems across retail, commerce, manufacturing and logistics. Because understanding how a business actually works, its existing systems, teams, exceptions and risks, is still the harder part.

We saw this while building Fynd Forge, our AI-powered Manufacturing Execution System (SME), now live at NeoLync’s Tirupati facility. Writing the software was only one part. Understanding the factory floor, its workflows and everything that could go wrong was where the real work happened.

My biggest takeaway for 2027 is simple:
Security cannot be the final step before launch.

It has to begin with the first decision: what data AI can access, which tools employees use, how the output is tested and who takes responsibility when something goes wrong.

This episode is not about another shiny AI model.

It is about the questions every founder, business leader and technology team should be asking before AI-generated software begins touching customers, revenue and real-world operations.

If you are figuring out how to use AI in your business or dealing with the challenges that come with it, this episode is worth a watch.
