Half a year and a hell lot of coffee!☕️ 

Almost 6 months and 6 days into my journey as a Management Trainee – Direct Business at Coffee Day Beverages, handling Gujarat, I’ve realised that the MBA classroom teaches you the fundamentals, but the real world has a rather interesting way of testing them.

A few things this journey has taught me:

1. Relationship management starts at the security gate.

From feeling intimidated by large corporate offices to building rapport with client admins, HR teams and other stakeholders, I’ve learnt that business relationships often begin well before the actual business discussion.

2. My call log belongs to a different person now.

I was once someone who called only my parents and a handful of close friends. Today, client follow-ups, service updates, operational escalations and internal coordination keep the phone busy. I’ve learnt that account management isn’t just about picking and dialling when things go right, but especially when they don’t.

3. Apparently, I’m a researcher now.

I’ve probably done more market intelligence in the last 3–4 months than in the rest of my life combined. Market mapping, competitor research, understanding client footprints and identifying opportunities have become part of the routine. Who knew spreadsheets could be this interesting?

4. I thought I was a traveller. Gujarat had other plans.

Having lived in 9 states and travelled to 22, I thought I’d seen enough. But travelling solo across Gujarat for client meetings, site inspections, demos and grievance resolution hits differently. Not every trip has a sightseeing itinerary. Sometimes it’s a long bike ride, a Tatkal ticket, a hurried bus ride and making sure a client’s problem doesn’t follow you home!

5. Winning an account is one thing. Retaining it is another.

The product may open the door, but service, CRM, responsiveness and consistent execution build lasting relationships. And representing a legacy brand like Coffee Day certainly adds to the pride.

Also, working around premium coffee beans has had an unfortunate side effect. Mention premix around me, and you might hear an involuntary “chee”. Occupational hazard, I suppose😂

One moment I’ll remember: an operations colleague once told me,

“Aapke kadam sone ke hain, aapke aane ke baad hamesha target achieve hi hua hai.”

I’ll take the compliment, smile and quietly add a touchwood. 😂

Beyond the humour, these six months have taught me about B2B sales, key account management, stakeholder relationships, market intelligence, territory management and cross-functional execution.

Most importantly, ownership isn’t just about the accounts you manage or targets you achieve. It’s about being someone people can count on when something needs to get done.

Grateful to my colleagues, seniors and mentors who’ve been part of this journey.

Six months down. Still learning, still brewing! ☕
