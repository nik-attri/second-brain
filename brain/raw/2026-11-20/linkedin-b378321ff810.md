The most expensive words in engineering might be:

“That’s how we’ve always done it.”

Not because the old way is necessarily wrong.

But because sometimes nobody remembers why we’re still doing it.

I’ve come across workflows where files are manually copied between systems, information is entered more than once, or a simple task requires five different steps.

And often, there’s a perfectly reasonable explanation.

Someone built a solution for a problem they had at the time.

Someone else found a workaround.

The workaround became a routine.

And eventually, the routine became the process.

🧠 Working across research and engineering at Lundbeck has made me appreciate something important:

Questioning a process doesn’t mean questioning the people who built it.

They probably solved the problem they had with the tools available to them.

But are we still solving the same problem today?

I’ve learned that improving a workflow doesn’t always require a new platform, a bigger budget, or the latest technology.

Sometimes it starts with a surprisingly simple question:

“Why do we still need this step?”

And sometimes, the best innovation is removing something rather than adding something new.

I’m curious: What’s one workplace process you think we should collectively retire? 👇
