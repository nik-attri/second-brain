The most expensive date in your business probably isn't in anyone's calendar.

It's the software contract that auto-renews at a higher price. The insurance certificate a client asks for the week after it lapsed. The supplier discount that expired because nobody reordered in time. The client agreement that rolled over on old terms because no one opened the renewal conversation.

None of these is a big decision. Each one is a small date with no owner. It sits in an email from eleven months ago, a PDF in a shared drive, or the memory of someone who has since changed roles.

What a missed date usually costs:
1. Money you didn't need to spend: list-price renewals, late fees, penalties.
2. Leverage you never used: the window to renegotiate closes quietly.
3. Trust you have to rebuild: a client or partner spots the gap before you do.

This isn't a discipline problem. It's an ownership problem.

When every contract, licence and deadline is read automatically, logged in one place and assigned to a named person, the surprises stop. The right owner gets a nudge 90, 30 and 7 days out, with the document attached. Renewals become decisions, not accidents.
