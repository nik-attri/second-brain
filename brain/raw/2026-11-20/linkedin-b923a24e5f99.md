DAY 12 OF 30 DAYS LEARNING CHALLENGE | PLANNING AN AUTOMATION PROJECT

Identifying a process to automate is one thing. Planning how to automate it is another.

In one of my previous learning activities, I explored the student admission and enrolment process in schools as a process that could benefit from automation.

I looked at the different steps involved, from a parent's initial enquiry and completion of an admission form to payment, collection of required items, registration and class assignment.

But yesterday's AI and Automation class introduced me to another important question:

How do you plan an automation project before you start building it?

I learnt that there are five important steps to consider.

Let's use the school admission and enrolment process to understand them.

1. Identify the Process

The first step is to identify a repetitive or time-consuming process that could benefit from automation.

In this example, we have the student admission and enrolment process, which involves several activities, people and stages.

2. Define the Goal and Expected Outcome

What exactly do we want to improve by automating this process?

For example, the goal could be to make admission processing more organised, reduce repeated manual data entry, improve communication with parents and make it easier to track each child's admission progress.

3. Map the Existing Workflow Manually

Before automating a process, we need to understand how it currently works.

For a typical school admission process, the workflow might look like this:

1. A parent makes an enquiry about admission.
2. The parent completes an admission form.
3. The school reviews the submitted information.
4. The parent receives details of the applicable fees and required items.
5. Payment is made and a receipt is issued.
6. The parent obtains the required uniforms, books and other supplies.
7. The parent returns to the school to confirm that the requirements have been met.
8. The school completes the registration and assigns the child to a class.

Mapping these steps helps us understand the flow of information, the people involved and where delays or repetitive tasks may occur.

It also helps us identify which activities can be automated and which still require human attention.

4. Choose the Right Automation Platform

After understanding the workflow and identifying what needs improvement, we can consider the right platform for building the automation.

Some platforms we explored in class include Zapier, Make, n8n, Power Automate and UiPath.

The choice depends on the process, the requirements and the capabilities of the platform.

5. Set Clear KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)

How will we know whether the automation is actually working?

We need measurable indicators.

For this admission process, we could consider:

- How long does it take to process an admission?
- Has repeated manual data entry reduced?
- Are parents receiving admission information more promptly?
- Can the school track the progress of each application more easily?

These measures would help us evaluate the effectiveness of the automation.

My takeaway:

One thing I am beginning to understand is that automation does not start with opening a platform and connecting applications.

It starts with understanding the problem, examining the existing process, defining what needs to change and planning how to achieve the desired outcome.

The tool comes after the planning.

A successful automation project is not just about automating tasks. It is about improving a process with a clear purpose.

Still learning, connecting the concepts and discovering how they can be applied to real-life situations.
