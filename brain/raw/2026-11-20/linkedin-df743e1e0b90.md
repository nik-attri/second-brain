We celebrated an 80% accuracy rate in 2023.
I almost cringe saying that out loud now.

We were building a Proptech platform that relied on user photos and videos. The internal ops team was drowning in manual moderation.

I championed building an in-house image classification model.
It was a slog. Two failed experiments. Whiteboard sessions late into the night.

When we finally hit 80% accuracy and integrated it into the posting flow, it felt like a major win. It boosted efficiency for our team, cleaned up the customer experience, and enriched the platform.

Fast forward 3.5 years.

That feature no longer needs an in-house model. It needs an API key, a good prompt, with Evals and Guardrails in place. In 2023, proprietary data plus a custom model was a moat. Now, moats are built on workflow orchestration, not raw model accuracy.

The lesson:
The emotional investment we make in a solution often blinds us to its shelf life.

I learned that being a product leader isn't about defending what you built. It's about being ruthlessly objective about when to replace it. The speed of iteration in AI has compressed the lifecycle of every feature.

Payoffs:
• Audit your roadmap for "sunk cost" features.
• Train your teams to architect for swap-ability, not permanence.
• Check your ego: your best work from 3 years ago might be obsolete today.

What is one thing you built in the past that you could ship in a weekend today?
