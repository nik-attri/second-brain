I've probably watched hundreds of videos titled:
"I built my second brain in an hour. Now my entire business runs on AI."
 
Most follow a similar recipe.
 
Put your notes in Markdown files. Store them in Notion or Obsidian. Ask Claude to build an entity and relationship graph. Connect an agent.
 
And suddenly, apparently, your agent knows your business, thinks like you and can run it for you.
 
I don't think that's how businesses actually work.
 
A personal knowledge base is useful. But running a business involves a lot more than remembering information.
 
I've been speaking with business owners, and the problems they're trying to solve are much more operational:
How do we make sure AI uses the same definition of revenue as our finance team?
How does an agent know which customers are overdue, which deals need attention, or which campaigns are underperforming?
How do we connect information across CRM, ERP, spreadsheets and other systems?
How do we make sure an agent follows business rules, respects permissions and gets approval before taking certain actions?
And how do we keep all of this current as the business changes?
A collection of Markdown files doesn't solve these problems by itself.
 
Neither does an entity graph or an MCP connection.
 
These are useful building blocks. But businesses need a maintained context layer underneath their AI systems.
 
One that connects business data, defines metrics and relationships, maintains current state, and makes rules and permissions available to agents.
 
That's what a context layer is for.
 
If you're trying to move beyond AI demos and automate real business workflows reliably, this is worth understanding.
