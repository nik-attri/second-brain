#REF! Remember that error? It meant your excel formula was pointing at something that wasn’t really there, and the AI economy just went public with one.

Anthropic listed this week, and coverage of its filing describes Amazon, Google and Microsoft as its “investor, customer, distribution partner and competitor.” The money goes in, buys the compute, comes back as revenue, and points at itself. Anyone who ever chased a circular reference in an Excel spreadsheet at 2 a.m. felt that one in their chest, trying to figure out what the hell went wrong.

This is also the company whose own CEO, Dario Amodei, has said there’s a 25% chance things go “really, really badly” with AI. He gives the other 75% to things going really well.....

And on the exact same day Anthropic started trading, Grand Theft Auto VI launched. A game where you rack up points by stealing cars, robbing people and shooting them, along with a whole lot of other sick stuff especially with women. 

So, in one Thursday a week before Thanksgiving we cheered for an AI company whose CEO gives one-in-four odds of disaster, and a game that rewards killing people. Happy Holidays everyone.......(really large eyeroll occurring right now).

Which way all of this breaks comes down to people. That’s what this week’s episode is about: the simple things we can actually control.

“Everybody rushed to FOMO. Nobody thought.” That’s what I told Tom Keenan on this week’s We’ve Got Trust Issues pod. 

AI is an incredible tool. But we haven’t used the best tool we already have, which is us. Looking at this in a way that matters to our organizations, our customers and our people, then building a change plan and following it through.

That plan needs 3 things most AI rollouts skipped:
- Governance: who decides how AI gets used, and who’s accountable when it gets something wrong.
- Ethics:  what it will never be used for, how people’s data is handled, and how you check it for bias.
- Efficacy:  proof it works for your people and your customers, measured after launch instead of assumed from the demo.

Almost nobody sat their people down and walked them through that. So Gallup has engagement at 29%, the lowest since 2011. 7 in 10 people are checked out. How low can you go?

Money can loop through the same 3 mega companies all day. Trust doesn’t loop. It only moves when you put something real in, and the simple things are what matter most.
