⚠️ WARNING: FRANK CILONA — PROCEED WITH CAUTION
You are about to meet a man who somehow has three full-time jobs, seventeen side projects, and still thinks he has time to “just put together one quick thing.”
Frank will initially seem friendly, approachable, and perfectly normal. This is how he gets you.
He is a professional networker with the dangerous ability to turn a five-minute conversation into:
a new partnership,
a radio spot,
a community program,
a PowerPoint presentation,
an Excel spreadsheet,
or somehow a jar of pasta sauce with a professionally designed label.
He has opinions. Strong ones. And if you tell him something can't be done, there is a very good chance his immediate response will be some variation of:
“Okay… but what if we did it THIS way?”
Do not make the mistake of assuming that because he's the CEO of the BBB, he's going to spend all day talking about business ethics. This man will go from consumer protection to high school football to the Ohio State Buckeyes to Italian food in approximately 14 seconds.
He also has a broadcaster's brain, which means he can turn almost anything into a story. Give him a microphone and put him near a high school football field and suddenly everyone within a 50-mile radius needs to know what just happened on third down.
Known weaknesses:
Ohio State
high school football
good Italian food
creative ideas
microphones
sarcasm
making things happen
saying “I've got an idea…”
Most dangerous phrase:
 “This shouldn't take long.”
It will take long.
Because Frank doesn't really do small ideas. He takes a simple thought and starts adding layers until suddenly there is a logo, a sponsorship package, a community partnership, a 30-second radio commercial, and a meeting scheduled for Tuesday.
And here's the biggest warning:
He'll probably make you laugh while he's doing it.
So if you're about to meet Frank, here's my advice:
Bring coffee. Bring patience. Be prepared to hear at least three ideas you didn't expect. And whatever you do, don't casually say, “You know what would be cool…”
Because six weeks later, you'll be running the program.
