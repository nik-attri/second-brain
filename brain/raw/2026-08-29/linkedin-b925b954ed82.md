Yesterday, I sat down for a one-on-one consulting session with a growing e-commerce business.

The founder reached out for the session. They’re processing about 40 orders a day, manually, across WhatsApp, Instagram DMs, and a spreadsheet and every time they try to scale past it, something breaks. 

It’s either orders get missed or follow-ups get forgotten. Their team is drowning, not growing.

When I asked what tools they were using, she said “mostly just us, remembering things.”

Yes, they have real demand.
Yes, they have a team hustling hard every single day.
But they wanted an external eye to see why hustle wasn’t turning into scale.

Here’s what we’re tweaking:

1. Their order intake: Right now it’s manual and scattered across 3 channels. Centralizing it into one system so works according to the right workflow and structure of the business. 

2. Their follow-up process: No automated reminders means warm customers go cold. We are building automated sequences that follow up without anyone lifting a finger.

3. Their team’s daily workflow: Too much of their day goes to repetitive admin instead of serving customers. We would be automating the repetitive 80% so they can focus on the 20% that needs a human.

4. Their visibility: Zero real-time view of what’s overdue, what’s stuck, or who owns what. We would be building a dashboard that shows the whole business at a glance.

In 30 days, they should be able to double their daily order capacity without doubling their headcount.

Automation is not about replacing people, it is about removing the friction that stops good people from doing great work.

Like I told my client yesterday , it is not “install a tool and hope, it is going deep into a business, finding where time and money are leaking, and building systems that quietly fix it, every single day, without anyone having to remember to do it”.

May we keep learning and building.
