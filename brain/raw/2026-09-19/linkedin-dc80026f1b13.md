I've just read something that has genuinely made me furious.

Apparently people with learning disabilities can die, on average, almost 20 years younger than the general population.

And I’m sorry…

HOW are we still talking about this?!

It’s 2026.

We have frameworks.

Policies.

Guidance.

Safeguarding procedures.

Health action plans.

Reasonable adjustments.

LeDeR reviews.

CQC.

Standards coming out of our ears.

We KNOW what good practice is supposed to look like.

So why the hell is there STILL such a massive gap between what’s written in a policy…

…and what actually happens to a person?

Because having a policy doesn’t keep someone safe.

Having a completed form doesn’t mean someone has had their health needs properly understood.

Having a process doesn’t mean the process is actually happening.

And a tick in a box definitely doesn’t mean someone got the right support.

This is the bit that frustrates me.

We keep creating more frameworks.

More guidance.

More processes.

More things for providers to evidence.

But maybe we need to spend a bit more time asking:

Is this actually happening?

Not;

“Have you got a policy?”

But;

“Show me what difference it made.”

“Show me what changed.”

“Show me how you know.”

“Show me what happened when something wasn’t right.”

Because ultimately…

we’re talking about PEOPLE.

Not paperwork.

Not compliance.

Not a spreadsheet.

People.

And when the consequence of the gap between policy and practice can be someone dying years earlier than they should…

that gap isn’t bloody theoretical.

It matters.
