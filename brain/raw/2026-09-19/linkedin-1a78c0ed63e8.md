One of the worst dudes you know is yapping about providing value right now.

“Provide value” has become one of those phrases in marketing that everyone repeats with the solemnity of a cardiologist discussing your arteries, despite the fact that nobody can seem to tell me what the hell “value” actually is.

Valuable to whom?

A spreadsheet can be valuable. So can a sentence that makes someone feel understood for the first time in six months, or a stupid joke someone screenshots and sends to the group chat because it 100% captures the essence of their boss.

I think we’ve confused value with utility to a concerning degree, and it has made a lot of content incredibly boring.

Once value became synonymous with teaching someone something they could immediately use, the internet filled up with checklists, frameworks, templates, “three things I learned,” and carousels containing information you could have Googled in 45 seconds.

Almost nothing else we willingly consume is held to this standard.

I have never finished a novel and demanded to know what I can implement by EOD. Nobody leaves a movie furious that there wasn't a downloadable worksheet. A Carly Rae Jepsen song can end and leave me wondering whether she miscategorized it by putting it on "Day" instead of "Night."

None of this improved my workflow. It made me curious, delighted, smarter, nosier, or gave my brain something to chew on.

If you think B2B content can only be valuable inside the box we’ve built around the word—teach something, solve something, hand over three actionable takeaways—you’re going to make boring content inside a very boring box.

B2B audiences are still people. They get bored, laugh, get curious, remember weird things, send screenshots to coworkers, and have taste.

A blog can teach me something I didn't know about an industry. A CEO can articulate a problem I've encountered 50 times but never had language for. A case study can contain an actual story instead of marching me from Challenge to Solution to Results like I'm touring a model home. A joke can make a technical idea stick. A strong opinion can make me reconsider one of my own.

None of those things require “5 actionable takeaways” to have done their job.

The minute you decide those responses don't count because they aren't “useful” enough, you've dramatically reduced the number of things your content is allowed to do.

Good content doesn't need to arrive carrying a tiny briefcase full of takeaways.

Value isn't a format. It’s what the person gets to keep after the content is over.
