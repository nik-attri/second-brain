Internships, contract and project roles made up 32% of entry-level openings on foundit so far in 2026, up from 12% in 2024.
The first job increasingly starts as an internship or a contract. Plan for it, rather than fight it.

The same foundit Insights Tracker (July 2026, based on its own platform) found that nearly six in ten entry-level postings expect prior internship experience.

There is a government route too. A PIB release of 22 Apr 2026 opened the PM Internship Scheme to final-year UG and PG students aged 18–25, with a No Objection Certificate that HoDs, Deans, Principals or TPOs can sign. The release sets a minimum stipend of ₹9,000 a month, says more than 300 companies took part in the pilot, and has companies posting round 3 openings on an ongoing basis.

Uptake is the gap. Government data reported by BusinessToday on 10 Aug 2026 shows round 3 had 1.22 lakh opportunities from 340 companies, but only 10,195 candidates had joined by 15 Jul 2026: about one joiner for every 12 opportunities.

And an internship is not yet a job. The Unstop Talent Report 2026, a vendor survey of 37,000+ students and 500+ HR leaders in Jan–Feb 2026, found 78% of firms run internships, but only 16% convert more than 80% of their interns into hires.

One honest caveat: foundit's numbers describe postings on its own platform, and Unstop's come from a vendor survey. Neither is a census of Indian hiring, so read them as direction, not precise measures.

How to plan for it:

→ Treat an internship as round one of the hiring process. The work is the interview.
→ Before you accept, ask how many interns became hires last year.
→ Build proof of work you can show: a project, a report, a result you can put a number on.
→ Final-year students: check the PM Internship Scheme, and ask for your NOC early.
→ TPOs: sign NOCs quickly, and track which companies actually convert interns.
