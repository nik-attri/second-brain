Everyone's talking about AI transformation. Most businesses haven't even automated the boring stuff yet they just hired a person to do it instead.

Here's what happens: someone's drowning in re-typing information from one system into another copying leads from a form into a spreadsheet, then into the CRM, then into a report. Nobody looks at that and thinks "this is exactly what AI and automation are built for." Leadership does the obvious thing instead and hires someone to keep up with it. Problem solved, on paper.

Except that new hire isn't adding capability. They're compensating for a workflow that AI-driven automation could've handled automatically from day one. You didn't grow the team. You just paid a salary to patch a gap that a properly designed automation would've closed for good without needing a coffee break or a raise next year.

Here's the tell: if a task is repetitive, rule-based, and doesn't require judgment, a person doing it isn't a resource they're a manual bridge between two systems that were never told to talk to each other. That's not a hiring gap. That's an automation gap wearing a job title.

Before your next hire, ask what workflow they're compensating for.

Curious where people land on this is there a case where hiring for a repetitive, rule-based task is actually still the smarter call?
