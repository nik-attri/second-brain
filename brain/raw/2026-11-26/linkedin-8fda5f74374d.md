Last week I was at the HCLSoftware #FactoryTourMilan in Milan and took part in the hackathon. Four and a half hours to build something, any idea you liked.

Congratulations to the winners, and to Javier Sánchez Oliva, my partner at Data101, who came third.

My own entry never reached the jury. Something went wrong with the upload and it simply wasn't there when they judged. A bit frustrating, but the idea is still good, so I'll share it here.

I called it AI Passport. The starting point is something I see with almost every customer right now: people are using AI tools and building small apps with AI much faster than IT, security or the data protection officer can follow. Nobody really knows what is in use, what data goes into it, or who said yes.

So the app is a single place where you ask for an AI tool or declare an app you built. You answer about ten simple questions (what data, how many users, does it decide things about people, who built it...) and you see the risk level straight away, with the reason behind each point. A tool that handles personal data for hiring goes to High, and the screen tells you why.

The risk level then decides who has to approve it. Something low risk only needs the manager. Something high risk also goes to security and the DPO, each with a deadline and the option to add conditions. Once approved, the tool gets a one-page card in a central register, with the owner, the data, the conditions and the next review date. And the CIO gets one screen showing everything in use, what is waiting too long, and what needs a review.
The rules are fixed and readable, loosely based on the EU AI Act and GDPR. It doesn't pretend to give legal advice, it just makes the decision visible.

The screenshots come from my own working version of the app, which I built separately from the hackathon entry.

If AI governance is on your list for next year, I'm happy to show it to you.
