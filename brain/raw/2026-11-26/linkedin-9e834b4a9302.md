It's 3:40 on a Thursday. The counter's busy, the phone's ringing, and a message comes in from one of your regular contractors.

It's a photo of a handwritten list, on the back of a delivery note, both sides.
"Can you price this up today? Need it for the morning."

So you start.

Line 1: "2.5 T+E x2 drums". Easy.
Line 4: "10 way CU dual RCD". Which brand? He didn't say. You pick the one he had last time, if you can find last time.
Line 12: a size that's either a 4 or a 9. You ring him. Voicemail.

Then it's every line, one by one. Find it in the catalogue. Look up his price, not the list price. Check the margin still works after his discount. Type it all into the system.

By the time you press send, it's nearly six.

And after all that, you don't know if he'll even take it. He might have sent the same photo to other wholesalers too. If one of them came back first, those two hours may have been for nothing.

This happens for real. Earlier this month a distributor told me a long contractor list takes them one to two hours to type in and price, and they get quite a few a week.

That's the job I'm building QuoteDesk to take off your desk.

Your team drops in the list the way it arrived: an email, a spreadsheet or that photo of the delivery note. They get back a draft quote:

• Every line matched to your own catalogue
• Every price from your own rules (contract price, customer price and so on), never made up
• A margin floor that holds: a line that would break it is held back, not quoted
• Anything it isn't sure about flagged amber, with the closest options for your team to pick
• Anything it can't read flagged too, never guessed

Your team checks it and sends it. Nothing goes to the customer without them.

So the contractor gets his price while he's still thinking about the job, not tomorrow morning. And your team spends the afternoon on the customers at the counter instead of typing.
