💡 𝐓𝐡𝐞 𝐪𝐮𝐞𝐬𝐭𝐢𝐨𝐧 𝐢𝐬 𝐧𝐨 𝐥𝐨𝐧𝐠𝐞𝐫 𝐡𝐨𝐰 𝐦𝐚𝐧𝐲 𝐩𝐞𝐨𝐩𝐥𝐞 𝐭𝐨 𝐡𝐢𝐫𝐞. 𝐈𝐭 𝐢𝐬 𝐰𝐡𝐚𝐭 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐧𝐞𝐱𝐭 𝐩𝐞𝐫𝐬𝐨𝐧 𝐧𝐞𝐞𝐝𝐬 𝐭𝐨 𝐛𝐞 𝐚𝐛𝐥𝐞 𝐭𝐨 𝐝𝐨.

More and more company leaders are sending the same message: AI is not mainly about replacing the people they already have. It is about changing who they hire next 👇

SAP is a good example. Its CEO has said he does not expect a smaller workforce, but a very different one. A memo reported by Bloomberg says new hiring would focus almost entirely on core AI roles. 

It is bigger than one company. PwC's 2026 study of job ads finds that AI is increasing the weight of human skills such as judgement and leadership, and that companies using AI best are expanding hiring faster than their peers. Even at entry level, jobs are asking for more senior skills sooner. 

What does that mean in practice? 🌍

🔹 The repetitive half of many jobs: such as typing in data, chasing updates, or building the same report every week ➡️ moves to tools
🔹 The half that needs judgement: understanding the business, deciding what matters, and owning the result ➡️ stays with people
🔹 People who learn to work with AI become more valuable, because they can do both halves well

You may have heard that people will not be replaced by AI, but by people who use AI. We would put it more kindly. Those who learn to work with it will have more options, not fewer 💡

For companies, before you post your next job, ask what the role should look like if a tool handles the repetitive part. The Job Description changes, and so does the profile you need 🤝

For professionals, the best time to learn how AI fits into your own work is now, while you can still choose how. Be ready to explain what you own, what you decide and how you use these tools 💪
