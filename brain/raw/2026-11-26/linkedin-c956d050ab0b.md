Fewer companies are hiring people whose only job is building dashboards.

A few years ago, Power BI or Tableau was enough to get hired as an analyst. The tool was the qualification. Today AI can produce a reasonable dashboard once the data model and metric definitions exist, so roles built purely around reporting are shrinking.

What hiring managers look for now:
- Business understanding. Knowing how revenue, customers and operations connect carries more weight than knowing one more tool.
- Ownership of a full process, from where the data comes in to the decision it supports.
- Clear reasoning. Interviews now spend as much time on how you approach a problem as on the SQL you write.

Predictions for the next 3 to 5 years:
- Analytics teams get smaller and more senior. Entry-level seats shrink, and juniors will be expected to work at a higher level from day one.
- Tool stacks consolidate. Fabric, Databricks and Snowflake already cover ingestion, modeling and reporting in one place, so analysts will need fewer tools and deeper skill in one.
- Stakeholders will ask an agent for answers instead of opening a dashboard. Analysts will spend most of their time building the semantic layer that makes those answers right.
- The analyst and analytics engineer roles merge. Modeling, testing and version control with tools like Git and dbt become standard analyst skills rather than a separate job.
- Industry specialists get hired ahead of tool generalists. An analyst who knows retail or healthcare inside out will beat a candidate with five certifications and no domain depth.

The analysts who do well over the next five years will define what the numbers mean, owning the semantic layer agents rely on and knowing the business well enough to spot when an answer is wrong. Dashboards are becoming a by-product, so build those skills now while most job descriptions still ask for Power BI and SQL.

Which of these predictions are you already seeing in your team?
