Do the work before you automate anything.

I’ve spent countless hours in the past building tools that never get used.

Recently, Elisha Bagnato our head of ops requested a finance dashboard to manage our day-to-day finance ops. So I built it….

The problem, 
what the heck was I building? I built what I wanted, not what she needed. 

I gave her a demo, handed over the reins. Then, nothing happened. 

Why? 
Because I made the mistake I teach our clients not to make. Automating a process we haven’t fully understood yet.

So, how did we solve it? 

We went back to the drawing board. Mapped every manual process step by step over a 3 month timespan until we had a complete understanding of where time was going.

We didn’t stop there. I could have easily, with this data, rebuilt the dashboard. But, we did something different. 

I taught Elisha my complete build process using Claude. Total sessions= 8x1hr over a 2 week period.

The result. 
Elisha built a tool that previously took 2 hrs a day, 10rs per week. Now has been reduced to just 20mins per day. That’s a net 83% increase in productivity.

The lesson
1. Have the people doing the work document the steps of a process currently being executed manually before building anything.
2. Understand exactly what happens and why. 
3. Map the process using flowchart tools like Miro or Whimsical.
4. Equation: Average cost per step = (total time in hours × average hourly rate) ÷ number of steps
5. Measure what to automate and why
