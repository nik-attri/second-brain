Next week, I’ll have an opportunity to spend some time with university students in Japan talking about AI.

As I’ve been preparing, one question has stayed with me.

As AI becomes more deeply embedded in the way we work, what should we still be responsible for deciding ourselves?

We often talk about which AI model is better, what it can predict, or how much work it can automate.

Those questions matter.

But once AI becomes part of an actual workflow, different questions begin to matter as well.

What information should it use?
What should it recognize as important?
How do we verify its conclusions?
Who makes the final decision?
And who takes responsibility for the result?

This is one of the ideas I want to explore with the students.

AI can help us see more, process more, and predict more.

But prediction and decision are not the same thing.

As AI becomes part of the infrastructure around us, I think our ability to design how decisions are made will become even more important.

I’m looking forward not only to sharing my perspective, but also to hearing how students who will begin their careers with AI already around them see this question.
