One of the strangest things about building Green Citation has been how difficult it has been to get people to answer really simple questions.

Not commercially sensitive questions.

Not "give me your client list".

Not "show me your secret methodology".

Not the f**king combination to the vault where you keep the gold bullion.

Things like:

How do you ask SMEs to report their emissions data to you?

What do they actually send back?

A spreadsheet? A PDF? A questionnaire? A collection of numbers in an email?

How many businesses or sites might you be collecting it from?

And what happens to all that information when it reaches you?

I asked these questions because I was trying to understand the problem properly.

Because how the hell am I supposed to build something useful for the people further up the chain if nobody will tell me what arrives on their desk?

So I've had to dig.

Read.

Watch conversations.

Ask more questions.

Build, test, rethink and occasionally wonder whether I'd completely misunderstood the problem.

And slowly, I've started seeing it.

The mess doesn't begin at the top.

By the time somebody is trying to consolidate information from 50 sites, 100 suppliers or 200 SMEs, the mess has already happened.

And this is the bit I've finally realised I should be shouting about.

I built the Green Citation Extraction Engine to deal with that mess further down.

The activity data is extracted from the original business records.

It's structured.

It's converted.

It has an audit trail back to the source.

And for multi-site organisations, it can be aggregated before all those separate pieces of information travel further up the chain.

So instead of somebody at the top receiving the mess and having to untangle it, they receive structured, aggregated emissions data with the evidence trail still intact.

That's what I built.

And weirdly, I don't think I fully appreciated just how valuable that part of it was.
