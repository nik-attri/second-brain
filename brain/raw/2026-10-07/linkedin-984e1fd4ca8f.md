2 out of 151.

That’s the number I want you to remember today.

During Dyslexia Awareness Week, we’ll see plenty of posts about raising awareness.

So let’s raise some.

According to the British Dyslexia Association, only 2 out of 151 local authorities can state how many dyslexic children are in their area.

Two.

Dyslexia affects around 10% of the population.

We’re talking about approximately one million schoolchildren.

Yet we have a system that, in most areas, doesn’t even know how many dyslexic children it is responsible for.

And then we wonder why children are missed.

Why parents have to fight.

Why diagnosis can depend on whether a family can afford to go private.

Why support varies depending on where you live.

And why the attainment figures look like this:

👉 Just 21% of dyslexic children meet expected standards in reading, writing and maths at Key Stage 2.

For their peers, it’s 74%.

👉 At GCSE, only 40% of dyslexic young people pass English and maths.

For their peers, it’s 72%.

And apparently 8 in 10 young people leave school without a formal dyslexia diagnosis.

At what point do we stop calling this an “awareness” problem?

This is a systems problem.

Because how the hell can you plan support…

train the right people…

fund the right services…

measure outcomes…

or hold anybody accountable…

when you don’t even count the children?

We don’t have a shortage of dyslexic children.

We have a shortage of visibility, identification and accountability.

And those children don’t disappear because the spreadsheet doesn’t contain them.

They sit in classrooms.

They struggle.

Some start believing they’re stupid.

Some disengage.

And eventually they become dyslexic adults carrying the consequences of a system that should have spotted them years earlier.

2 out of 151.

During Dyslexia Awareness Week, perhaps that’s the number we should actually be talking about.
