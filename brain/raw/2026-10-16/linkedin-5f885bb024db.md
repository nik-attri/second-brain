Millions of pounds were already earmarked to replace a failing manual ordering system with automation. The boardroom narrative was airtight: humans make mistakes, technology fixes them. Then a curious leader noticed an impossible glitch in the data. One lone branch, drowning in the same volume and using the exact same paper forms, had an error rate of zero.
The supervisor hadn’t upgraded a single piece of software. He had solved an enterprise-wide crisis by borrowing a simple evening routine from his mother: laying out his clothes the night before.

Coherence is comfort. Clarity exposes the real problem.

When a narrative fits the mental model already in our heads, the executive brain relaxes. The logic holds together, cognitive ease kicks in, and our instinct is to leap straight into execution.

We assume that because the story makes sense, we have line of sight.

That is how smart, highly capable teams walk right past the gorilla in the room:

The $60M system rewrite: 
Approved because the software was too hard to use. The real issue was entirely behavioural: nobody had shown frontline teams the purpose of the data or the value it created for their daily goals. They built it, launched it, and nothing changed.

The multi-million-pound sledgehammer: 
Capital was earmarked for an automated tracking build to eliminate human error—until a curious leader discovered one outlier unit had solved the breakdown - for free. A supervisor had simply redirected team attention to the right handover variable at the right time. He had recognised it as a behavioural challenge not a technology problem.

The 4-year green dashboard mirage: 
An executive team celebrated hitting sales targets year after year, completely blinded by a tidy narrative while their market share quietly evaporated.

Coherence proves the pieces fit your existing model.

Clarity asks what got swept onto the cutting room floor to make the story look that neat.

Leaders arent defective. 
We are human, and human intuition is hardwired to equate a smooth narrative with truth, to mistake confidence with competence.

To unmask the true challenge before unexamined assumptions compound into an eight-figure crisis, you dont need a lecture. 

You need an Attention Architecture—the deliberate discipline of directing focus away from comfortable narratives and into The Gray Area where real insight lives.

Slow is smooth, and smooth is fast.
