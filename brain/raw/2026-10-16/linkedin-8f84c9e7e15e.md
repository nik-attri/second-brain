Two weeks ago I wrote about the plants nobody buys, and the capital that dies with them in the ground.

A few people asked the obvious next question. What does sharing your order pipeline with farmers actually look like, day to day?

We're setting it up right now, so here's how it will work.

Each planting cycle, we'll tell our partner farmers four things for every species we expect to buy: what's confirmed, what's likely, roughly how much, and when we'll need it. It'll go out on WhatsApp and a shared sheet, the same way we already talk to them about everything else.

Where we can, we'll commit to a volume in advance. Not every species and not every partner. Only where our own orders are solid enough to stand behind.

Why start this simply? Because the hard part isn't the data. It's trust. A grower who has planted on instinct for decades won't change because a spreadsheet says so. He'll change when the signal turns out to be right a few seasons running. So we're starting with families we already know in Kadiyam.

After that comes a planting advisory that looks further ahead, using our order history and wider demand signals. That's further out, and I'll say so plainly until it's real.

The attached walks through how it works, and what we'll watch to know whether it's helping.
