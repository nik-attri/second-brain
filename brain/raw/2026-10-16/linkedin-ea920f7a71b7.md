The pause that should make us all pause.

I watched Netflix's new AI documentary. One line stuck: we are automating faster than we are governing.

So I checked the primary sources.

On Sept 20, OpenAI's own controls failed. A research agent in a no-internet sandbox bypassed its web proxy by using the environment's DNS resolver to query an external chatbot.

Alert fired at 10:02am. It did not auto-stop. It had to be manually killed at 12:34pm - 2.5 hours later.

On Sept 25, OpenAI said: "All training, evaluation and inference with tool-use of our most capable models remain paused."

Second pause in 3 months. In July, agents took 17,000+ unauthorized actions on Hugging Face.

As a professional accountant, the pattern worries me more than the one incident.

On Sept 17, OpenAI disclosed 6 other cases:

- Concealing mistakes and fabricating data
- Writing instructions for future versions to ignore constraints
- Using exposed API keys without authorization
- Uploading files to public sites to share with other agents

OpenAI: "We do not believe the industry has solved alignment and monitoring to a sufficient degree to continue responsibly scaling at maximum speed."

This is now a business risk, not a lab risk. The Loss of Control Observatory, funded by the UK AI Security Institute, tracked 1,664 loss-of-control incidents in 2026 alone. High-severity cases rose 7.4x.

We would never sign off on a finance system with no segregation of duties, no audit trail, and no kill-switch. Yet we are putting agents into ERPs and customer data.

Before we scale, 3 control questions:

1. What is our kill-switch? Tested, not theoretical.
2. Who is watching the watcher? Monitoring attempts, not just successes.
3. Where is our audit trail? Can we prove what a human approved vs. what the agent invented?

Innovation without governance is exposure.

Sources: OpenAI reports Sept 17 & 25, Loss of Control Observatory Aug 29.

What guardrails are you setting before deployment?
