If your AI business case starts with headcount reduction, you're probably asking the wrong question.

AI is forcing organisations to reconsider how work gets done.

And inevitably, the conversation turns to cost.

How many hours can we save?

How many roles can we automate?

How much headcount can we remove?

Those are legitimate commercial questions.

But I think they're incomplete.

The bigger opportunity is not simply doing the same work with fewer people.

It's questioning why the work exists in the first place.

Why do we have five approvals?

Why are teams manually moving information between systems?

Why do customers repeat the same information?

Why do managers spend hours preparing reports nobody uses to make decisions?

Automating those activities might make the current organisation cheaper.

But it doesn't necessarily make it better.

I've always believed transformation should challenge the way a business operates, not simply digitise its existing inefficiencies.

AI makes that distinction even more important.

Before asking how many people an AI solution can replace, ask:

What would we design differently if we were building this organisation today?

That's where the real transformation starts.

Are businesses using AI to reinvent work, or just to reduce the cost of the existing model?
