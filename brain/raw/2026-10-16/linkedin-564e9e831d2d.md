Is there a little gasp in the audience when I name folks who I FEEL did me wrong by their full given name when asked to speak about the FACTS of MY lived experience? 

Yes. There is also a felt sense of solidarity in the air. 

Because the number of women who approach me afterward to tell me their own story of being silenced, gaslighted, and otherwise treated poorly in the workplace says just how despicably cruel this capitalist system of work truly is. 

And we all know who runs it, what those folks look like, and just how comfortable they are with getting away with what it is that they do. Clock it. The best revenge is having the freedom to do whatever the hell I want, which involves naming people, spaces, and systems that uphold the status quo and hold back the urgency of progress.

We did exactly that on Blackweek's stage last week with my dear friend Amira K.S. Barger, MBA,CVA,CFRE.

Our live masterclass broke down our experiences practicing "nerve" and letting go of "nice," as we shared what it took to own our truth in a world that would prefer we be a compliant number on a spreadsheet, as opposed to diverse, values-driven individuals that we are. 

Amira asked how I knew when to speak up—at Reddit, for instance, I was flagged by the CMO for making a critical comment advocating for the interest of workers with physical disabilities 3 months into my new job. Facts. 

So, I share here what I shared on the stage, which seemed to have resonated with the audience and got some heartfelt applause in the moment. 

I always try to root myself in the intellectual training that I was proud to receive, and return to what my teachers in Black Studies taught me. Dr. Cornel West, building on the work of the prolific sociologist W.E.B. Du Bois, has said in his lectures that there are four moral questions that confront us:

1) Integrity: "How shall integrity face oppression?”
2) Honesty: “What does honesty do in the face of deception?”
3) Decency: “What does decency do in the face of insult?”
4) Courage: “How shall virtue meet brute force?”

"In the end," Dr. West says, "life is about being rich in things that money can't buy."

Sometimes, it is that simple.

Thank you, #BLACKWEEKLA.
