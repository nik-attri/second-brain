Prior authorization continues to be one of the biggest barriers patients and providers face when navigating healthcare.

This KFF study caught my attention: 69% of insured adults describe prior authorization as burdensome, and 34% identify it as the single biggest burden they face when accessing care beyond cost.

For those of us working in Patient Services and Patient Access, I think this reinforces an important point: improving access requires more than adding resources to manage increasingly complex processes.

We have to continue looking at how we redesign workflows, connect data more effectively, automate repetitive administrative work, and allow our teams to focus their time on the patients and providers who truly need human intervention.

Technology and automation can play a significant role, but the goal shouldn’t simply be efficiency.

The goal should be removing friction from the patient journey and helping patients get to therapy faster.

There is still a tremendous amount of opportunity ahead of us.
