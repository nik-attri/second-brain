LESSON 19.7 — A Pile of Wood Taught Me About Learning

This pile of wood taught me something about learning.

You do not always need a university degree to understand a business process.

Sometimes, you only need to observe.

Someone may be highly skilled at carving wood.

But after the wood is finished, what happens next?

How will it be counted?

How will it be loaded onto a truck?

How will it be unloaded at the warehouse?

What happens if there is no forklift or handling equipment?

When I saw this pile of wood, I did not only see wood.

I saw manual labor.

I saw time.

I saw transportation.

I saw warehouse handling.

And I saw an opportunity to improve the next process.

If the wood is simply thrown into a pile, workers may later spend hours counting, lifting, arranging, loading, and unloading it manually.

But perhaps the next time, it could be prepared differently.

10 pieces in one bundle.

20 pieces in one bundle.

Count it before loading.

Bundle it properly.

Think about how it will be moved before it reaches the warehouse.

A small improvement at the beginning can reduce a great amount of unnecessary work later.

That is why I believe learning should not stop at our own job description.

A skilled person may only focus on completing their own task.

But a person who understands operations asks:

What happens before my work?

What happens after my work?

And what can we improve next time?

We do not need to become experts in every field.

But we should learn enough to understand how the entire process connects.

Sometimes, the best lesson does not come from a classroom.

It comes from observing a problem, understanding why it happened, and remembering how to improve it next time.

You can learn from finance.

You can learn from logistics.

You can learn from sales.

You can learn from a warehouse.

And sometimes, you can learn from a pile of wood.

Because learning is not only about studying more.

It is about learning to see more.
