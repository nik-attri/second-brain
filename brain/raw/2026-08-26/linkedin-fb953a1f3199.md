The people who know your systems best may not know what your consultant needs to know.
The documentation says orders go from the ERP to the warehouse. Pretty straightforward.
Then you spend time with the people actually doing the work and discover that every afternoon somebody exports a spreadsheet, fixes a few things by hand, emails it to another department, and then the orders go to the warehouse.
Nobody hid anything. A lot of the people using these systems aren't technical resources—and they shouldn't have to be. They've done it this way for years, so to them, that's simply how the job gets done.
That's where I think there's real value in being on site. Get out of the conference room. Watch people work. Ask the stupid questions. Find the things nobody thought were worth mentioning.
Of course, time is money. Every hour a consultant spends learning your business is an hour somebody is paying for.
So where's the line?
How much discovery is enough before the cost of what you missed becomes a hell of a lot more expensive than the time you saved?
In my experience, some of the most important things I've learned about a customer's environment didn't come from a meeting or a document. They came from being there, watching how people actually work. And as an added benefit, you're already getting a head start on building the relationships that matter once the real work begins.
That's why I still believe "boots on the ground" is worth the time. 😜
