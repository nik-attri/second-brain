I was sitting on my flight to Boston the other week, reflecting this incredible summer. 

When the summer started, I had never shipped anything a stranger depended on. By August, I was watching people I had never met use what we built to do their job.

I spent the summer after high school as a Software Engineering Intern at Fraxia. I went in thinking the hard part would be the code. Most of what I actually learned was about the distance between something that runs and something people trust enough to rely on. The moment the work stopped being mine and became part of someone's job was the moment it got serious, and much harder.

Thank you to Mehr Anand, who helped me find the door in the first place.
Thank you to Kai Dunham, who trusted me with real autonomy and pulled me into the bigger projects so I could see how work actually gets scoped.
Thank you to Chadranhas Aroori, who mentored me through every stuck point and stayed patient while I worked it out.

This fall I am joining two teams at Northeastern.
Software Engineer at ACM, working on Cursive. Thank you to Ekam Bhatia for the advice that got me here and for the standard the team sets.
Data Analyst at IDEA, Northeastern's student-led venture accelerator. I will be building the data pipelines behind the portal our startups run on, so founders spend their time on their companies instead of wrangling spreadsheets. Thank you to Andrew Kuhn for walking me through the interview process.

Thank you also to Matthew Shi, who in the past few days has helped me get my bearings around Northeastern faster than I would have alone.
A year ago I was sitting in a high school classroom, still figuring out my future. Every name in this post is part of why. At some point, I want to be one of those names for someone else. 
And onto the next chapter!
