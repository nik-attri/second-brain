Two weeks into building agentic AI systems from scratch, and something shifted this week: I stopped writing every loop by hand and started trusting a framework to do it for me.
For the first two weeks, everything was manual: a ReAct agent loop built in plain Python, tool routing written line by line, no shortcuts. Then this week I moved to LangChain, and the confidence changed shape. It's no longer "I control every line", it's "I trust a tested workflow." That's a different kind of skill.

Then Week 3 opened with a problem I genuinely enjoyed sitting with: how do you let an LLM answer questions about a document too big to fit in its context window?

Keyword search doesn't cut it, "How did the business perform financially?" and "The company's revenue grew significantly" share zero exact words but mean the same thing. The real fix is converting text into vectors and comparing meaning using cosine similarity, the angle between two vectors, not their raw distance. And even then, there's a catch: embed a whole document as one vector, and it blurs into an average, drowning out the one paragraph you actually need. So you chunk first, embed each piece separately, then search.

Chunk -> embed -> store -> retrieve. That's the backbone of RAG.

Two weeks in, and the thing I keep noticing is: understanding the "why" behind a failure mode teaches you more than the working code ever does.

There's no one checking whether I open my laptop to work toward my commitment which I made with myself or not. No boss, no deadline, no one keeping score. But I do it anyway, because the version of me I'm trying to become is built in exactly these unwatched moments.

That quiet promise to myself is the one that's actually building who I'm becoming.
