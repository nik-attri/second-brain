A Bengaluru GCC. 
420 headcount
One HR desk. 

And the same 15 questions. Every single day.

Leave balance. Payslip errors. Onboarding paperwork. Policy doubts. 

None of it complicated. All of it repetitive. 
And repetition, at that volume, was quietly draining a team that was hired for far bigger work.

The usual fix is simple: hire another HR executive.

We tried something different.

Step one: we didn't hire. We built.

Instead of adding headcount, we deployed one AI HR agent, starting with onboarding, since that's where most repeat questions were coming from.

Not a generic chatbot. 

We trained it on the company's real policies, real SOPs, real language their own employees use every day.

Step two: the agent started teaching us something too.

Every question it couldn't answer confidently got logged automatically. 

Within weeks, that log became something unexpected, a clear map of exactly where the company's own HR policies were unclear or outdated. 

The agent wasn't just cutting workload. It was doing an audit nobody had time to do manually.

Step three: the team got its time back.

Response time dropped from days to seconds. 
Query volume fell fast. 
And HR stopped spending its week on repeat questions, freeing it up for the work that actually needed a human: performance conversations, retention, culture.

Why this matters beyond one company

This wasn't a one-off result. 
Across our AI HR Agent Suite deployments, we've consistently seen a 30–50% reduction in manual HR queries within 90 days, without a single additional hire.

The takeaway for any scaling company or GCC

You don't fix an overwhelmed HR function by adding more people to answer the same fifteen questions. 

You fix it by building systems that carry the repeatable work, so your people can focus on the work only people can do.
