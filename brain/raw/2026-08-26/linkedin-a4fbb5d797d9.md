A bandleader told me this week that starting anything new in August was a mistake, because during peak season there isn't a spare minute. He'll look at it properly in the winter.

He's right about August. I want to talk about the winter part.

Here's the loop I watched myself run for about six years. Spring, the enquiries start landing and you're behind before you've begun. Summer, you're playing four weekends out of four and the admin gets done in the gaps between load-outs. Fall, still going, still catching up. Then winter finally arrives, the calendar goes quiet, and the thing that was urgent in July isn't urgent anymore. There's no pain to point at. So it moves to the list, and the list rolls into spring, and spring is when the enquiries start landing.

The season never opens a window. That's not a scheduling problem you can solve by being more organised. It's the actual shape of the year.

And notice what the loop selects for. The moment you most need a system is the moment you have the least capacity to build one. The moment you have capacity is the moment you can't feel why you need it. Those two things never overlap, and they were never going to.

So the answer isn't waiting for a better week. There isn't one coming. The answer is picking the moment that costs you least and accepting that it will cost you something.

For most bandleaders I talk to, that's mid-season rather than off-season, which sounds backwards until you sit with it. In August the pain is right in front of you, so you know exactly what to fix and you'll actually finish. In January you're guessing at problems you can't quite remember.

One caveat worth saying out loud: this only works if the heavy part isn't yours to do. Nobody is migrating four years of bookings out of a spreadsheet in the middle of a wedding weekend. If the setup is on you, deferring genuinely is the right call. If it isn't, the busy season is the best time you'll get.

You're not behind because you lack discipline. You're behind because the year is built so the window never opens.
