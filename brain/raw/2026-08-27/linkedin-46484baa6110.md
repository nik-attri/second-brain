I made a classic mistake recently that turned into a surprisingly valuable realization about the tech industry.

I was working on a targeted talent shortlist for a high-level Product Manager position, specifically hunting for someone with deep expertise in Agentic AI, workflow automation, and 0-to-1 PLG growth. I had four stellar candidates vetted and ready to go.

Then my colleague sent me a quick text: "Double check what this client's platform actually does! lol"

I checked. The company I was pitching candidate sourcing services to? Their entire core product is an AI platform built to automate talent sourcing and candidate intelligence.

I had effectively tried to sell sourcing to the team building the software for automated sourcing.

Once the initial facepalm wore off, the moment sparked a bigger realization about where hiring is actually heading right now:
1. Automation speeds up discovery, but human context closes the gap Even companies building top-tier AI recruitment tools still rely on human alignment, relationship-building, and executive vetting when hiring critical leadership roles. AI can parse talent signals and track career trajectories in seconds, but it takes human judgment to evaluate drive, leadership nuance, and vision.

2. The demand for 'Agentic AI' skill sets is exploding The role we were discussing required candidates who deeply understand multi-step workflows and autonomous AI agents. Seeing an AI-native company aggressively hire for these exact skill sets shows just how fast the technical baseline for product leaders has shifted across the market.

3. The best hiring strategies are hybrid Software doesn't replace talent advisors, it supercharges them. The goal isn't to remove human intuition from hiring, but to eliminate the manual noise so leaders can spend more time having high-value conversations with the right candidates.
It was a funny, humbling reminder that no matter how sophisticated our tools become, hiring remains a deeply human endeavor.

How is your team balancing AI tools with the human touch in your talent strategy this year?
