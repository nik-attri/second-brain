The GTM Declutter: What Should We Stop Doing Without Breaking What Works?

One thing I've learned in Product Marketing and GTM: Adding a process is  easy. Removing one is leadership.

As B2B SaaS companies scale, processes multiply.
A weekly meeting becomes a dashboard.
The dashboard becomes a report.
The report becomes a presentation.
The presentation becomes another meeting.
Before long, teams are spending more time reporting on the work than doing the work.

I've seen this happen across Marketing, Sales and Product.

And the answer isn't to launch another "process optimization" initiative.
It's to systematically remove friction without removing control.

My 5-question GTM declutter qualification matrix:
1. What decision does this process enable? 
If nobody can explain the decision it supports, question it.
Not every recurring activity deserves to survive simply because "we've always done it."

2. Is this information already available somewhere else?
I've seen teams manually build reports that already exist in Salesforce, HubSpot, analytics platforms or product dashboards.
That's not diligence.
That's duplicated effort.

3. What happens if we stop doing it for 30 days?
This is my favorite test.
If the answer is "nothing material," you've probably found something worth eliminating.

4. Can technology handle the repetitive part?
Automate:
→ Data collection
→ Reporting
→ Notifications
→ Meeting reminders
→ Routine segmentation
→ Competitive monitoring
But don't automate judgment.
A dashboard can tell you what changed.
A PMM still needs to determine why it matters.

5. Who actually owns the outcome?
One of the biggest sources of GTM complexity is shared ownership without clear accountability.
If five people "own" something, often nobody does.
Simplify the workflow around the person accountable for the outcome.

But here's the part that matters most: Don't declutter blindly.

Before removing a process, identify: Purpose → Owner → Dependency → Risk → Outcome
Then test the change.

Keep what protects revenue, customers, compliance and decision quality.
Remove what creates administrative weight without creating business value.

Instead of:
Weekly Marketing → Sales → Product status meeting
→ 15-slide presentation
→ Multiple spreadsheets
→ 60 minutes of discussion
→ Follow-up meeting
Try:
One shared GTM dashboard
asynchronous updates
exception-based discussion
30-minute decision meeting only when needed.
Same visibility.
Less overhead.
More time for actual customer and market work.

After years in B2B SaaS, I've become increasingly convinced that GTM maturity isn't measured by how many processes an organization has.

It's measured by how effectively it knows which processes deserve to exist.
In a market where every team is being asked to do more with less, that's becoming a competitive advantage.

I'd genuinely love to hear how other PMMs and Product leaders are approaching this.
