We don’t build everything a client asks for.

In over 12 years in automation, I’ve realised one simple thing:
a good integrator shouldn’t agree to every client request.

Sometimes a client says:
‘We need to automate this process.’

We can take on the task, assess the timeline, develop a solution and put it into operation.
But first, we ask a different question:
‘Why does this process even exist in this form?’

Because practically anything can be automated.
You can automate Excel.
You can automate dozens of approval processes.
You can build yet another report.
You can add yet another step to the workflow.
And end up with perfectly automated chaos.

That’s why, on SYNTECH projects, we first get to the bottom of:
- where time is actually being wasted;
- where errors occur;
- which data is currently scattered;
- which operations can be eliminated rather than automated;
- what is really preventing the business from scaling up.
And only then do we design a solution.

Sometimes the result of our analysis is new automation.
And sometimes - a completely different answer:
‘You don’t need to automate this. It’s better to change or remove this process.’

For us, that’s a result too.

Because our aim isn’t to create more functionality for the client.
Our aim is to make their business more manageable, transparent and efficient.

And if that means building less – we’ll build less.

What do you think? Should we just agree and do everything the client asks for?
