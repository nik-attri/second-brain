Your biggest operational risk might be sitting inside someone's head.

Every Facilities Management team has experienced it.

"Ask Dave. He'll know."

That person knows where the electrical isolators are.

They know which access route contractors actually need.

They remember which assets were replaced five years ago.

They know where the old drawings are stored.

They understand the quirks of a building that have never made it into a spreadsheet, drawing or asset management system.

That knowledge is enormously valuable.

It's also vulnerable.

People retire.

They change roles.

They leave the organisation.

They're unavailable when an urgent question needs answering.

When critical building knowledge exists primarily in people's memories, organisations create a single point of failure.

This is one of the practical roles a Digital Twin can play within Facilities Management and Estates operations.

Asset locations, building documentation, maintenance information, access details and site-specific knowledge can be connected to the physical building and made accessible to the people who need it.

The objective isn't to replace experienced Facilities Managers.

Quite the opposite.

Their knowledge is valuable enough to preserve.

A Digital Twin can help turn individual knowledge into organisational knowledge.

Knowledge that remains accessible when teams change.

Knowledge that contractors can use before arriving on site.

Knowledge that new employees don't have to spend years rediscovering.

Knowledge that belongs to the building, rather than one person's memory.

A Digital Twin doesn't replace experience. It preserves it.

Your building shouldn't rely on someone's memory.

It should have one of its own.
