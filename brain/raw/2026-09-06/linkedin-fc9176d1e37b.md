Palo Alto Networks Just Bet That Security Analysts Will Become Agent Supervisors, Not Alert-Chasers

Palo Alto Networks has acquired Console, an AI-native startup built to automate enterprise workflows using autonomous agents — and it's folding the technology straight into Cortex, its flagship security platform.

The idea: instead of analysts manually triaging an endless queue of alerts, AI agents investigate context, connect dots across systems, prioritize incidents, and take remediation actions on their own. Terms of the deal weren't disclosed.

Here's what makes this more than a routine tuck-in acquisition. Security teams have spent years drowning in alerts generated by dozens of overlapping tools — more tools, more noise, more manual correlation work. Console's approach flips that: let the agent do the investigating, and let the human step in only when judgment calls or high-stakes actions are needed.

That trade-off cuts both ways, though. An AI agent with broad permissions across a company's security stack is powerful — but if it's compromised, misconfigured, or misdirected, it can do damage at the same speed and scale it was built to defend against. Giving software the authority to act, not just recommend, raises the stakes of getting the guardrails right.

My take: this acquisition is a signal, not just a feature update. Palo Alto is one of the biggest names in enterprise security, and it just put agent-based automation at the center of its roadmap rather than treating it as an add-on copilot. Expect competitors — CrowdStrike, SentinelOne, Microsoft Security — to move faster on similar bets, because once one major vendor makes "agents that act" the default, staying with "agents that only suggest" starts to look outdated.

The real test will be trust: will security teams actually hand over remediation authority to an AI agent, or will "human-in-the-loop" stay non-negotiable for anything beyond low-risk actions?

Where do you land — ready to let AI agents take action inside your security stack, or still want a human approving every move?
