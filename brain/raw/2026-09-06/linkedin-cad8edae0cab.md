I once made a positive hiring recommendation before the candidate answered a single question.

And I have eliminated candidates before the first answer too.

I know because I spent over two decades as a senior corporate leader managing 500+ staff — and a certified Behavioural Event Interviewing (BEI) practitioner who personally conducted hundreds of interviews from entry-level to director appointments.

Here is what was happening in those first 30 seconds:
→ How did they walk into the room? Did their posture signal confidence — or were they already apologizing for being there?
→ How did they greet the interviewer? Was the handshake firm, the eye contact direct, the introduction natural and clear?
→ How did they sit? Open and present — or closed, hunched, arms crossed?
→ What was their expression before the first question was asked? Did they look like someone who belongs in this room?

Before a single word is spoken, an interviewer is already forming an impression.
Not consciously. Not deliberately.

But the data is going in — and it influences everything that follows.

Here is the uncomfortable truth:
You can have the best answers in the room and still lose the interview in the first 30 seconds.

Because the body communicates something the words have not even started to say yet.
