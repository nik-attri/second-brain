🚀 Starting My AI Learning Journey — From Rules to LLMs
I’ve been using AI-powered tools for quite some time.
But recently, I realized something:
Using AI and understanding AI are two very different things.
I could use ChatGPT, hear terms like Machine Learning, Deep Learning, Transformers, LLMs, RAG, Generative AI, and Agentic AI — but I couldn’t clearly explain how all of these pieces connected.

So, I decided to go back to the fundamentals.
I’ve started learning AI through Akshay Saini 🚀 Namaste AI course, and I’ll be documenting my learning journey along the way. 📚

My first lecture was about The Evolution of AI, and it gave me a completely different perspective on where today’s AI actually came from.

Here’s the journey I understood:

🧠 1950s — Can machines think?
 Alan Turing introduced the idea of evaluating whether machines could show intelligent behavior.
🤖 1956 — AI becomes a field
 The Dartmouth workshop helped establish Artificial Intelligence as a formal field of study.
📜 Rule-Based AI
 We explicitly told machines what to do:
IF this happens → THEN do this
But the real world has far too many rules to define manually.
📊 Machine Learning
 Instead of writing every rule, we started giving machines data and letting them learn patterns.
🧠 Deep Learning
 Neural networks became capable of learning increasingly complex patterns and representations from large amounts of data.
⚡ 2017 — Transformers
 The Transformer architecture introduced a powerful way of understanding relationships between different parts of a sequence through attention.
💬 LLMs & Generative AI
 Transformers + massive amounts of data + compute paved the way for today's Large Language Models and Generative AI.
🌎 2022 — ChatGPT
 AI became something that millions of people could interact with through a simple conversation.
🤝 And now — Agentic AI
 The next evolution is moving beyond generating responses toward AI systems that can use tools, reason through tasks, and take actions.

My biggest takeaway?

AI didn't suddenly appear with ChatGPT.
What we see today is the result of decades of research, experiments, failures, breakthroughs, better algorithms, more data, and more computing power building on top of each other.

And honestly, understanding this evolution made many of the AI concepts I've been hearing about suddenly feel much more connected.
