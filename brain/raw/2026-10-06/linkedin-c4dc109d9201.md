🔐 SAME SIEM ALERT. TWO PATHS. 

Imagine this hits your SIEM at 09:00 AM: 
🚨 "Impossible Travel Detected." 
A user logged in from the UK. 30 minutes later, they are halfway across the globe.

 👉 Swipe through the images to see how two different SOCs handle this exact alert.

[IMAGE 1: THE MANUAL BURDEN]
In a traditional SOC, an analyst is drowning in data. They have to manually pivot between identity logs, VPN data, device info, and threat intel. It’s a slow, 10-step manual grind that leads to alert fatigue and burned-out teams.

[IMAGE 2: THE AI AUGMENTATION]
Now, hand that same alert to an AI Security Agent. In seconds, the AI correlates identity, geolocation, device hygiene, and threat intel to build the full story. 

Notice what happens next: The AI doesn't automatically lock the account. Instead, it serves up a high-confidence recommendation and pauses for **human approval**. 

The future of the SOC isn't AI replacing the analyst. 
It’s a partnership:
👨‍💻 Analyst = Judgment + Accountability
🤖 AI = Speed + Scale + Investigation

The workflow shifts: Alert ➔ AI Investigation ➔ Context ➔ Human Approval ➔ Automated Response.

As autonomous response evolves, where do you draw the line? Would you allow an AI agent to automatically contain a confirmed account compromise, or should a human ALWAYS approve the final action?
