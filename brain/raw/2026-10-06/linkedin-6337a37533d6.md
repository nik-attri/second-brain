Diary of a Wandering Entrepreneur - Vol. 3, Day 363 (1093)

This Friday, I took a small step for my studio, Lifer Games, and a giant leap for myself. Almost like Neil Armstrong, only upside down, and nothing like Louis. I had my first-ever call with a game development studio.

I've been getting offers on LinkedIn from outsourcing studios for quite a while - from graphics and mechanics to building an entire game from concept to finished product - but I automatically rejected them. Thanks, but it's too early: all I have is a heap of documents and spreadsheets with concepts, worldbuilding, mechanics, characters, stories and ideas, but nothing "real" to work with.

Then I got another message, from INFINITY UP, and thought: why the hell am I refusing a simple call? Half an hour of my life might bring me closer to actually making a game, and for some reason I'm "saving" it.

So, after a little work on myself, I agreed.

Fuck, was I nervous before that call))

I prepared for ages: what would they ask, what would I answer, how could I present myself and the game properly in just half an hour? Would they like it? Would they want to work with me?

Even though they already knew my game was still at the concept stage and said that was perfectly fine, they have no problem working with games in these stage of development.

The problem was me.

Almost my entire life, I've been a supplier of goods and services, not a client. I'm very used to understanding people's and companies' needs, finding where I can be useful, designing solutions and delivering results. I'm absolutely not used to being on the other end.

So I came to a conversation where I was actually supposed to be the buyer and they were supposed to impress me, feeling like I was at either a job interview or an exam - except I hadn't done my homework and had skipped half the lectures))

Luckily, the people on the other end were attentive, understanding and accepting. I genuinely enjoyed the conversation and felt that they were interested in what I'm creating and wanted to help me build it.

Super unusual. But I came out very happy.

And exhausted. And sweaty))

I promised to prepare a brief description - well, you know my "brief" - of the game and what I'd like to create here and now: something potential investors and players can see, hear, smell and touch. Within two weeks.

I ended up writing it today in an hour and a half)

Does that mean I've crossed the threshold from "writing a concept" to "creating the first actual samples"?

Not yet.

But I think I've started lifting my foot over it!
