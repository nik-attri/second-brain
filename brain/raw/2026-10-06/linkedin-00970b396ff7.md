SOME OF YOUR EMPLOYEES WILL FORGET THE WORK. THEY WON’T FORGET WHAT YOU TAUGHT THEM. 

Management is not only about getting work out of people. 
It’s about what you leave in people.

A friend shared a story about finding some old hats from their first job.

The hats brought back memories of their first Manger and unofficial Mentor (well call him) Kent - a man who had since passed away.

What stayed with me wasn't the hats.

It was this: They remembered the TIME the man gave to them.

Not his title. 
Not his management style. 
Not his productivity numbers.

His time.

That hit me. 

Because somewhere along the way, I think we've made management way too much about managing the work.

Deadlines. 
Deliverables. 
Performance.
All important. 

But youre not managing a spreadsheet.
You're managing somebody's career. Maybe even a piece of their life.

At GYA, we teach new managers the skills they need to lead people. But there's a part of management that can not be reduced to a checklist or framework.

BEING THERE!
Take the extra five minutes. Answer the question. Then explain why. Ask the employee “what they think” before giving them the answer.

Notice when they're struggling. Notice when they're ready for more.
Tell them when they're doing something well. Tell them when they're not.

Have the uncomfortable conversation because you care enough about their growth not to let them keep getting it wrong.

And every once in a while... 
Put the damn laptop down. 
Look at them. 
Listen.

That's MENTORSHIP!

And I don't think you need a formal mentoring program to do it. Sometimes you just need a manager who gives a damn.

AI is going to make us faster. 
It'll answer questions. 
Write reports. 
Analyze information. Save us hours.
Great. Use it. 
But don't confuse giving someone information with developing someone. Those aren't the same thing.

A GOOD MANAGER can answer your question. 
A GREAT MANAGER sometimes realizes the question you're asking isn't the question you actually need answered.
They know you. They've watched you. They know when to push.
When to teach. When to get out of the way.

And sometimes when to say: “You're ready. Go do it.”

Here's the part managers may NEVER get to see.

Years from now, that employee probably won't remember the project you were working on. 

They may not remember the deadline. They probably wont remember the quarterly numbers.

But they might remember you. Something you said. A chance you gave them. A mistake you let them learn from. The afternoon you stopped what you were doing because they needed help.

That's a hell of a responsibility. And an even bigger privilege.

So heres my question for managers: Are you just supervising someone's work... or are you helping build the person who will someday lead without you?

And for everyone else: Who was your Kent?
Tag them if you still can. 
Tell them. 
Don't wait until all you have left is an old hat and a memory.
