Last week, WKO/WIFI Niederösterreich ran a workshop on something I think we will see much more of: using AI to decide which export markets are worth exploring.*

And I absolutely think AI belongs in that process.

If you have 20 possible markets, AI can help you reduce them to five.

It can compare market size, growth, competitors, labour costs, regulation, customer segments and dozens of other variables much faster than a person could manually.

WKO’s workshop even included something I particularly liked, which is checking the quality of the sources behind the AI-generated research. 

But here is where I would draw the line.

Whereas I would absolutely let AI help me decide which five markets deserve investigation, I would never let AI be the reason I invest in one of those five.

Why? 'Cause the questions that often decide whether Serbia, Austria, Romania, Croatia or another market will actually work are much harder to answer from desk research:

- Who really buys this product?
- How long does the sales cycle *actually* take?
- Is the distributor everybody recommends genuinely good?
- How do customers negotiate?
- Who already owns the relationships you need?
- Will the price that looks competitive in a spreadsheet survive the first ten customer conversations?
- Does the market even behave the way the report says it should?

I have spent more than 20 years working between DACH and Balkan region, and I can confidently say that a good report can tell you where to look (and AI can now make that part significantly faster), but eventually somebody needs to speak to customers, partners, candidates, competitors and people who actually operate in the market.

And that's where assumptions either become a business case or fall apart.

How far would you personally trust AI in deciding which foreign market your company enters next?
