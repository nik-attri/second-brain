Last Thursday, every Colorado district found out what one day is worth.

October Count is the "official student headcount." Enrolled, scheduled, in attendance on a single date, and that count drives a big share of what a district is funded for the rest of the year. Districts spend weeks getting ready. Attendance calls. Schedule cleanup. Audit prep. There's a state portal, a deadline, and a resource guide for it.

Hell, they even send emails to the parents to make sure not to schedule a doctor's appointment for their kids that day.

That's a lot of organized energy aimed at one day.

Now think about the CTE students in those same buildings. The job shadows, the internships, the employer projects, the hours logged with real businesses. That work happens all year, and in a lot of districts it's documented in a spreadsheet, a binder, or a teacher's memory. Nobody runs an audit prep week for it. No guide, no portal, no deadline anyone feels.

I'm not knocking October Count. It matters, and districts are right to take it seriously. But if a district can mobilize this well around one day, imagine what that same discipline could do for the work students do every other day.

That's a big part of why we built CTEsoft. Count Day has structure. Work-based learning should too.

Tell me in the comments: at your school, which gets more attention, Count Day or Every Other Day?
