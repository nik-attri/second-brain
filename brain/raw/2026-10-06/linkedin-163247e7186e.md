If I was a founder planning my first launch video, here's what I'd obsess over:

1. The problem that made your product inevitable  
2. 2–3 product moments that visibly solve that problem  
3. The outcome the customer gets on the other side

That's the framework we used for Superflow AI's launch.

→ First, make the existing problem painfully familiar.

AI made building websites dramatically faster. It didn't do the same for reviewing them.
The person who used to review 9 pages a week could now be looking at 90. Production scaled, QA didn't. 

→ Second, show the product solving that exact problem.

For Superflow, that meant showing an agency's QA checklist turning into AI agents that review websites before the client does - finding issues and pinning them directly where they occur on the page. The product doing the work IS the story.

→ Third, make the outcome tangible.

The team doesn't have to spend hours manually checking every page. AI handles the repetitive first round, while humans stay in control of what gets approved and fixed; clients can then sign off directly from a link.

That's much more memorable than listing 10 features. Your launch video isn't a compressed product tour!!

When you're asking the market to understand something it hasn't seen yet, build a visual narrative around: Problem → Product → Outcome.
