AI is moving from answering questions to actually doing the work.

GPT-6 Astra caught my attention for one reason:

It isn't being presented simply as a better chatbot.

OpenAI says Astra is built for complex reasoning, software engineering, research, computer use and multi-step tasks.

And honestly, that changes how I think about AI.

Earlier, the workflow was mostly:

Human → Ask AI → Get an answer

Now we're moving toward:

Human → Define the goal → AI → Plan → Execute → Verify → Improve

That is a much bigger shift.

As a CSE student interested in AI and research, what excites me isn't just that AI can generate code faster.

It's the possibility of AI becoming a research and engineering partner.

Imagine giving an AI a problem and having it:

🔹 Understand the requirements
🔹 Research the problem
🔹 Write the code
🔹 Run experiments
🔹 Find errors
🔹 Test different approaches
🔹 Analyze the results
🔹 Help decide what to try next

But there is something I find even more important:

The better AI becomes, the more important human judgment becomes.

If AI can execute a task, I still need to know:

What problem am I actually solving?
Is the result correct?
Why does it work?
What are the risks?
Can I trust the result?

For me, that's where the future of software engineering gets interesting.

We may write less code manually.

But we may need to think more deeply about the problems we give to machines.

I'm still learning, experimenting and trying to understand where AI is heading.

And developments like GPT-6 Astra make me even more interested in learning AI, software engineering and AI research — not just using AI tools.

The future may not be humans vs AI.

It may be humans who know how to think + AI that knows how to execute. 🤖

What do you think?

Will AI agents become our next generation of software development tools — or something much bigger?
