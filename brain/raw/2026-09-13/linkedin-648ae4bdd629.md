Being busy is not the same as being productive.
“Work hard” is outdated advice.

Someone still walks files from desk to desk just to get a signature.
Personally, we still go through emails one by one, every day — as if that's just how work works.

It's not wrong.
It's just never been questioned.

That's what got me thinking:
Every task I repeat manually is a task I never bothered to design a system for.
Not because I'm lazy.

I just never stopped to ask:
“Does this really need a human every time?”
That's why I'm learning and practicing Zapier. You can apply the same idea with other automation tools, too.
Not to save five minutes — that's the boring reason.
I'm doing it because I'm tired of paying the same time tax on the same task, over and over again.

Automation isn't about doing less.
It's about deciding what actually deserves your attention.

Here's my key takeaway:
Hard work was never the goal. Useful work was.
Sometimes, what we call “hard work” is simply work we never stopped to redesign.

So, what's one task you still do manually — not because it needs you, but because no one has asked why?
