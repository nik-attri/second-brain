One thing I keep thinking about when we talk about CLM and legal-tech automation:
Are we trying to remove too much friction?
In CLM, we are constantly trying to make things smoother.
Can we reduce a step?
Can we automate an approval?
Can AI review this clause?
Can we move the contract from request → review → approval → signature with as little intervention as possible?
Makes sense.
Nobody wants people spending time on things technology can handle.
But then I started wondering - Is every interruption actually bad?

This thought came to me while watching something on Prime recently.
Growing up, watching a movie on TV meant watching advertisements in between.
Movie → commercial → movie.
You couldn't skip it.
And somehow, I still remember some of those advertisements.
The jingles. The dialogues. Sometimes the entire ad. :)
Today, if a one-minute advertisement comes while I'm watching something on Prime, I get irritated.
Prime used to be ad-free. Now, if you want to watch without advertisements, there is an additional subscription.
The funny part is, I don't think the one-minute advertisement is really the problem.
It's the interruption.
We have become so used to uninterrupted experiences that even a small pause feels like friction.

And that's where it gets interesting for legal tech.
A system interrupting me because I forgot to fill a field?
Unnecessary friction.
But an AI system stopping me because it noticed that an indemnity clause is significantly different from our usual position?
That's a useful interruption.
Maybe automation shouldn't be about making everything frictionless.
Maybe it's about removing the friction that doesn't add value, while keeping the interruptions that make us stop and think.
In legal work, sometimes 30 seconds of interruption can save hours later.
So while designing our AI/CLM workflows, perhaps we should ask one more question:
Not just "How can we make this process faster?"
But "Where should the system interrupt us?"
