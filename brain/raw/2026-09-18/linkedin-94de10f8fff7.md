The word process in our job is such a trap.

Usually, when working with agent - trying to automate a process (speaking in general, not about design) - you create workflows. Consecutive steps and hard rules to follow.

And for a lot of tasks in a lot of jobs, where there's a lot of repetitive things, where it's always A, B, C, D - that's perfect. Very simple for an agent to follow.

In design, however, it's far from the truth. It's actually something that cripples the quality of the work.

So I stopped thinking in terms of workflows. And I stopped thinking in terms of process.

It's about principles. It's about different modes of work - and the principles and judgement to decide which one to turn on.

And you figure that out from the essence of the task at hand, from the brief you have right now.

It's never step one, then step two. On the contrary - it's always different.

So it's not about stages. It's about modes.

Now we're in thinking and deciding mode - brainstorm. Then switch quickly to building-a-few-simple-prototypes mode. Then back to brainstorm, to decide. Etc.
