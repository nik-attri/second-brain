The more I look at how audit is evolving, the clearer one thing becomes: automation is not changing the importance of auditors. It is changing where their time and expertise matter most.

There is plenty that technology can do exceptionally well. Repetitive processes can be automated. Large volumes of data can be analysed faster. Exceptions can be identified earlier. Workflows can become more structured and consistent.

But identifying an exception is not the same as understanding its significance.
That is where professional judgement continues to matter.

The next phase of audit transformation is therefore not about choosing between automation and people. It is about bringing the two together in a way that allows each to do what it does best.

At Sama, our Analytics Wednesday sessions have explored how automation and analytics can streamline audit processes and strengthen controls. At the same time, the Caseware ecosystem is evolving around workflow, review and technology that supports professionals through the engagement.

The direction is interesting because it moves the conversation beyond simply doing audit work faster.

It is about creating more space for auditors to ask better questions, investigate what matters, challenge anomalies and bring context to what technology identifies.
The IIA has similarly highlighted the continuing importance of professional judgement, critical thinking and human capabilities as automation and AI become more embedded in audit work.

For me, that is where the real opportunity lies.
Automate the repeatable.
Analyse at scale.
Keep judgement human.
That combination could shape the next chapter of audit.
