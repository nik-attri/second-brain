When the model stops answering, can your customer get their own work back?

I raised this with a client while we were working through an AI-driven product. Their workflow was easy to follow while the model was available: the customer uploads material, receives an analysis, reviews it, makes corrections and continues working.

Then I asked what the customer could still do if the next response never arrived.

By that point, the customer has already contributed documents, decisions and time. A new analysis might have to pause. The surrounding flow still needs to preserve their state and give them a usable next route: open what they supplied, retrieve the version they approved, export the current record, complete a narrower step manually or resume safely when the model returns.

Not every product can offer every route. The team still has to choose which route exists. Leaving the customer unable to continue is also a product decision, even when an outside provider caused the interruption.

When I map an AI workflow, I follow the customer past the model call: what have they brought in, which version have they accepted, what can they still access, and how do they move forward if generation stops?

The model provider owns the model. If you are selling the workflow around it, you still own what happens to the customer's unfinished work. Adding a second model does not settle that issue either; a fallback may return an answer while behaving differently enough to change the result or break the workflow you promised.

I'd rather force those decisions early on while the product can still be changed than discover them with a customer already waiting for work they need to finish.
