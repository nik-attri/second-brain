Stop Planning in the Dark

Why Disconnected Planning & Scheduling Systems Break Your Production Plan — and How One Finite Scheduling System Fixes It

Root Cause: Two Systems That Were Never Meant to Talk

Most manufacturers are not missing data — they are missing one place where all the data meets a real constraint model. ERP and MRP answer "what do we need?" They were built for inventory, transactions, and order management, using infinite-capacity or rough-cut logic (RCCP) that treats every machine, tool, and person as always available. Scheduling has to answer a different question — "can we actually do this, and when?" — and that question requires a finite model of the plant: machines, tooling, labor and skills, materials, storage, changeovers, and maintenance calendars, all at once.

When those two questions live in two disconnected systems — an ERP/MRP planning layer and a manual or spreadsheet scheduling layer — nobody owns the handoff. The result is what one industry analysis calls "Excel Hell": exporting the plan, patching it by hand, and re-importing it, every single day, because the ERP's logic is too rigid to model the real plant and too disconnected from the floor to see what's actually happening.

From "Real Constraints" to a Schedulable Model

This is precisely the ground PlanetTogether APS is built to cover. Rather than treating capacity as a rough-cut assumption, PlanetTogether builds the schedule from the finite capacity actually available in the plant — resource by resource, constraint by constraint — so planners are working from a schedule that reflects reality, not an idealized plan.
