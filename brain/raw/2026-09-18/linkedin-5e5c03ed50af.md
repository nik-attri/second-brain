I’ve been selling plastic for nearly 25 years, and if there’s one thing I’ve learned, it’s this:

Sales isn’t about who can give the cheapest price.

If that’s all you bring to the table, you’re nothing more than a number on a spreadsheet. There will always be somebody willing to sell it cheaper.

What separates a salesperson from someone who simply takes orders is what happens before, during, and especially after the sale.

Do your damn homework.

Know your customer. Know their business. Understand what they’re trying to accomplish. Find the problem before you start throwing products at them. Talk to the people making the decisions. Know your products, know your capabilities, and know what you can actually deliver.

And for God’s sake, be genuine.

Don’t bullshit the customer. Don’t tell them what you think they want to hear just to get the order. If you can solve the problem, explain how. If you can’t, tell them. Then go find someone who can help.

I’ve never been afraid to say, “I don’t know, but I’ll find out.”

There’s a hell of a lot more credibility in finding the right answer than pretending you have one and getting it wrong.

Because here’s the truth:

Anybody can sell a product.

The real salesperson sells confidence, service, knowledge, and solutions.

The sale is just the beginning.

When something goes wrong, that’s when the relationship really gets tested. When the customer needs something six months later, a year later, or five years down the road, that’s when they remember who picked up the phone and who disappeared after getting the order.

I’ve built my career on relationships, not transactions.

I don’t want to be the guy who sold you a truckload of plastic.

I want to be the guy you call when you have a problem.

That’s where the real value is.

So don’t second-guess yourself when you’ve put in the work and know your business. Know your stuff. Be confident. Stand behind what you say.

And when you don’t know something?

Find the damn answer.

Because in the end, customers may forget the price you quoted them.

They won’t forget whether you were there when they needed you.

Nearly 25 years in this business has taught me that you don’t build a career by selling products.

You build it by earning trust, solving problems, and keeping your word.

That’s old-school sales.

And there’s still something to be said for that.

Because when the dust settles and the sale is long forgotten, your reputation is what’s left standing.

Sell the product once. Earn the trust, and you’ve earned the customer for the long haul.
