You are getting leads.
You are having sales calls.
People seem interested.

And yet… 
the business isn’t growing the way it should.

So naturally, the next question is:
“How do we get more leads?”
But sometimes, that’s the wrong question.

when you look at the revenue number, you are thinking:
“Where the hell are all these leads going?”

Because if the leads you already have aren’t converting, 
adding more leads just gives you… 
more leads that don’t convert.

Now you have a bigger spreadsheet to be disappointed by. 😄

The real problem could be somewhere else:
→ Your positioning isn’t clear enough, 
so the wrong people are entering the pipeline.

→ Qualification is weak, so your team is spending time 
with people who were never serious buyers.

→ The sales conversation is too focused on your product and 
not enough on the business problem.

→ You explain the value, 
but don’t connect it to revenue, cost, risk or time.

→ You leave the next step vague because 
you don’t want to sound pushy.

→ You follow up repeatedly instead of 
giving the buyer a reason to move the conversation forward.

→ Or the buyer simply doesn’t have enough certainty to make the decision.

And this is why I don't believe sales is about becoming a better “closer.”

Your job is not to convince people to buy.

Your job is to diagnose what is actually preventing the decision.
Think of it like a leaking bucket.

You can keep pouring more leads into the top.
But if the problem is happening somewhere in the middle of the sales process, 
more leads won't solve it.

Fix the leak before you increase the flow.

That’s how you build a predictable sales system 
not by constantly chasing the next lead.

If you are getting leads but struggling to convert them, 
look at the entire journey before blaming lead generation.

Sometimes the answer isn't “more.”
It’s better diagnosis!!
