🚨 Meta Ads Targeting Is Changing - and Marketers Need to Adapt
Since 2025, Meta has been gradually reducing the control advertisers have over manual audience targeting.

Here are seven important changes:

1️⃣ Detailed targeting exclusions are gone
Previously, we could exclude people based on interests and behaviours.
Now, exclusions are mainly possible through custom audiences, such as customer lists.

2️⃣ Detailed targeting is becoming a suggestion
Previously, the interests and behaviours we selected defined the audience.
Now, Meta may deliver ads beyond those selections if its system predicts better results. 
Even Ads Manager describes these choices as “Your suggestion.”

3️⃣ Some interests have been merged or removed
From January 15, 2026, several targeting interests were retired or combined.
Meta now blocks retired interests and recommends alternatives. Older ad sets using unsupported interests also stopped running after the cutoff.

4️⃣ Separate Advantage+ campaign types are disappearing
Previously, advertisers could select Advantage+ Shopping or Advantage+ App campaigns separately.
Now, Meta is moving toward one broader Advantage+ campaign structure.

5️⃣ Advantage+ Audience is enabled automatically
Previously, advertisers had to activate it manually.
Now, it is turned on by default unless you switch it off. This approach is also expanding to financial services, housing and employment ads.

6️⃣ Age and gender are becoming starting signals
Previously, if you selected women aged 25–44, the campaign stayed within that audience.
Now, Meta may go beyond those settings if its system predicts better performance.

7️⃣ Placement exclusions may no longer be completely fixed
Previously, excluding a placement meant no budget would be spent there.
Now, Meta may still expand delivery when its system believes another placement could improve results.

The bigger shift is clear
Manual targeting settings are increasingly becoming signals—not strict instructions.
The old approach was:
“I want to reach women aged 25–44 who are interested in X—and only those people.”
The new approach is:
“Here are the signals I believe are relevant. Now find the people most likely to complete my conversion goal.”
This means marketers need to focus less on building extremely narrow audiences and more on:

✅ Strong creative
✅ Clear messaging
✅ Reliable conversion data
✅ Proper tracking
✅ The right campaign objective
✅ Continuous creative testing

Meta’s algorithm is taking greater control of delivery.

Our job is to give it the right creative, data and conversion signals to make better decisions.
What do you think—will greater automation improve campaign performance, or are advertisers losing too much control?
