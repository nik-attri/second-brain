I keep hearing business owners say the same thing.

"We need another person."

Sales are growing. Everyone's busy. The admin is piling up. Managers are stretched. So the obvious answer looks like recruiting.

I want to challenge that.

Because before you add another thirty, forty or fifty thousand pounds to your overhead, there's a better question. Are you actually getting the best from the people you've already got?

And I don't mean working them harder.

God, no.

I mean giving them better skills, better systems and better technology.

I learned this in my own business. Whenever TNB grew, I assumed I needed more people. Sometimes I did. But I also had people spending hours doing things manually that could have been systemised. I had knowledge sitting in people's heads. I had processes that had evolved rather than been designed. And I had things that still came back to me because I'd never properly trained anybody else to own them.

That makes growth bloody expensive. Because at some point you're not recruiting people to create growth. You're recruiting people to deal with the inefficiency that growth created.

There's a difference.

So before you write the job advert, have a look at the team you've got. Where are they losing time? What frustrates them? What do they do over and over? What could better training let them take responsibility for?

Sometimes the biggest opportunity for growth is already sitting on your payroll.
