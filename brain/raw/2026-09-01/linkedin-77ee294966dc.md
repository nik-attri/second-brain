Nobody ever accused me of cheating with a calculator. Neither is AI.
I love Excel. I spent years getting good at it, and it's probably a dead skill now. In all that time, not one person looked at my spreadsheet and said a formula did the real work. Nobody asked me to disclose it. Nobody thought less of me.

So why is AI different? You may have seen the dust-up last week. A legendary investor published an op-ed that was obviously written with AI, and the internet demanded an apology. His answer: of course I used AI. Good. That should have ended the conversation.

Here's where I've landed after using it every single day.

The norm is AI now. Your coworkers use it. Your vendors use it. The person lecturing you about it uses it too. In two years, asking "did you use AI for this?" will sound like asking "did you use spellcheck?"

Normal still comes with judgment, though. Some things I hand to AI without a second thought. Some things I never will.

What gets AI at my desk:
• Research and legwork. It reads faster than I do.
• First drafts, summaries, meeting notes. The blank page belongs to the machine now.
• Basic responses. A two-line scheduling email? Why not. You will get no virtue signaling from me about typing those by hand.
• Cleaning up my own writing. Structure, length, typos.

My absolute hell-no list:
• Praise. If I tell you your work was excellent, every word is mine. Otherwise it means nothing.
• Genuine thanks. Same rule. A machine can't be grateful on my behalf.
• Anything where the thinking is the product. If AI did the thinking, what exactly did I contribute? Even then I sometimes ask. Only the Sith deal in absolutes!

One rule covers all of it: show some effort. If a reader can tell in three seconds that you never reread what the machine spit out, you just told them they weren't worth ten minutes of your time. That was true long before AI. AI only made it easier to do.

So go AI bananas. Be smart about it. Know when it's fine and when it isn't. Get good at it the same way you got good at Excel, back when Excel was a skill that mattered. Did AI help me with this? Of course it did. But I had to give it all the info.

P.S. Since you clicked this far, here's how to spot writing that was 100% machine and 0% human. A few of these together means nobody reread it:
You can revisit my Hell No of AI earlier article but here are the additions.,
1. "It's not X, it's Y." Once is a choice. Five times is a confession.
2. "Delve." No human delves.
3. "In today's fast-paced world..."
4. An em dash in every single sentence.
5. Everything arrives in threes: crisp, clean, and compelling.
6. Seamless. Robust. Leverage. Unlock. Elevate.
7. "Stands as a testament to."
8. "Whether you're a seasoned professional or just starting out."
9. Perfectly even paragraphs, zero typos, and no opinion anywhere.
10. "In conclusion." It announced its own ending because it didn't trust you to notice.
