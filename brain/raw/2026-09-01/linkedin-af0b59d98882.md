You can retire some of your Excel skills.

Maybe this sounds strange coming from someone who teaches Excel, but here's why...

A lot of things experienced users got good at were solutions to problems Excel now handles better: lookup gymnastics, copy-and-paste routines, giant formulas, brittle VBA scripts.

Those skills solved real problems. But Excel changed, and our habits don't always change with it.

Part of getting better now is deciding what not to keep getting better at.

Maybe the cleanup belongs in Power Query, the monster formula becomes a leaner dynamic array formula and you start integrating Python into your analysis.

That's a big part of my upcoming Modern Excel Intensive, a four-session live course on the modern Excel toolkit, including Power Query, Power Pivot, Python in Excel, Copilot, Office Scripts, and Power Automate.

The question running through the whole thing is:

What should you keep doing, what should you stop doing, and what should you replace it with?

If your Excel workflow has acquired a few barnacles, come scrape some off.
