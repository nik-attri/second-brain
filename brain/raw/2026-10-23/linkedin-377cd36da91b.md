The hardest conversation Senior PMs face is understanding why they aren’t getting promoted to Director, despite shipping features users love.

You are taught that the highest calling is to be the "voice of the user" inside the building. When you say that in an exec review, you think you sound principled. 

What your CEO actually hears is: "I only understand the expense side of the income statement."

Users will always ask for more capability, less friction, and lower prices. If you blindly optimize for user delight, you'll build a beloved 70 NPS product that quietly bankrupts the company.

The shift that separates feature owners from product leaders is realizing one thing - your software is not the product. The business is the product. Your software is just the mechanism that executes it.

Before you bring a spec or a roadmap into a leadership review, stop justifying it with user pain alone. User pain is just raw material. Show which of the 4 P&L levers solving that pain actually pulls:

1. Compressing CAC payback - does this get a prospect to the paywall faster so we recover acquisition cost in 2 quarters instead of 4?

2. Unassisted expansion - does this workflow naturally push an existing account into a higher pricing tier without a sales rep touching it?

3. Defends gross margin - does this automate something that currently costs us support hours, onboarding services, or heavy compute?

4. Workflow lock-in - does this lock our data or product so deeply into the customer's daily routine that ripping us out becomes too painful?

Now obviously this sits on a spectrum. If you're hunting for 0-to-1 PMF at a seed startup or building an early consumer viral loop, your only job is being the voice of the customer and ignoring CAC.

But once you cross into scale-up or enterprise territory, the rules flip. If you cant draw a straight line from a user's click to the income statement, you aren’t doing product strategy. You’re just doing philanthropy on the company's dime.
