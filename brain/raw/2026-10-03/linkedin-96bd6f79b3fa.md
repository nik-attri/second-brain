I sure as hell hope this isn’t someone’s idea of “helping me” showcase my Excel skills.

Or some exercise aimed at the third-party platforms that help small businesses manage their finances.

Because congratulations: 
Yes, I can process things manually. 

But that completely misses the point.

For the past financial year, I spent nine months meticulously reviewing, checking, and responsibly allocating every business expense for my accountant. 

That work has already been done.
I don’t need to prove I can build spreadsheets or manually key transactions. 

I need access to the nine months of financial data I already prepared.

Instead, because cash flow means I can’t immediately reopen a subscription, I’m left having to duplicate nine months of finished work on top of the three remaining months.

Those final three months were manageable. 

I had an 18-month period available to complete the work and could allocate those payments across that timeframe. 

The problem isn't the remaining quarter—it's losing access to nine months of 'paid' completed work prepared for my accountant, meaning I now have to duplicate it.

For small businesses with lower volume, Excel is a practical option. 
It’s also great for those who haven’t learned specialised platforms.

I have those skills too.
I can teach Excel, and I can help others process manually. 

But choosing to use dedicated software was about using tools I know and rely on to run my operations efficiently.

The issue is choice.

Being capable of doing something manually doesn't mean it should become a forced obligation. 

I should also be able to choose the system that works for my business.

And it better not, sure as hell, be some convoluted play by QuickBooks or MYOB to “steal me away” from Xero by locking one door so another looks appealing. 

Nor does it have anything to do with tarot, “twin flame” narrative, or someone else's narrative.

Whether I use Excel, Xero, QuickBooks, or MYOB should come down to what works for my business—not someone else’s agenda, competition, or experiment.

And if the goal is somehow pushing me back into bookkeeping employment, let’s be clear: my financial ability to continue studying in that direction was already cut off. 

Developing those skills further was also made less accessible. 

That doesn’t move me forward; it just circles me back to where I’ve already been.

All I am asking for is access to the nine months of financial data I already generated and meticulously prepared.

I shouldn’t have to rebuild three-quarters of a year's work simply because a subscription is paused. 

That doesn’t “help me discover my skills,” it doesn’t prove third-party tools are unnecessary, and it isn’t an open invitation to steer my direction.

It is my business. I get to choose how I run it.
