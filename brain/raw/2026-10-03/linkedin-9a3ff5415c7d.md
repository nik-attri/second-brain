The most expensive part of an AI project may be the 5% it can't handle.
Not the 95% that works beautifully in a demo.
The 5% that says:
“Something is different here.”
A customer gives an unusual answer.
A supplier changes a condition.
A document doesn't match the expected format.
A transaction falls outside the normal pattern.
An employee doesn't follow the redesigned workflow.
The AI pauses.
And suddenly a person gets involved.
Then another person.
Then an email.
Then an Excel sheet.
Then a meeting.
And eventually someone says:
“Why are we still doing this manually?”
That's the part of AI transformation I find most interesting.
Not the happy path.
The exception path.
Because that's where you discover whether the business actually redesigned the process — or simply placed AI on top of an old one.
Before automating a process, I now think it's worth asking:
What happens when everything goes right?
But also:
What happens when it doesn't?
Who decides?
Who gets the case?
What evidence do they receive?
What can the AI do next?
What must remain human?
And how does the exception get back into the process so the same problem isn't solved manually forever?
That's not just an AI question.
It's a business design question.
And sometimes the biggest AI opportunity isn't automating another 10% of the process.
It's eliminating the reason that 5% becomes an exception in the first place.
That's where some very interesting transformation work begins.
