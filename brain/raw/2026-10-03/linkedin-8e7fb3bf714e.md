Sometimes the best L&D fix isn't about teaching people something new, instead taking the wrong thing off their plate.

A few days ago, I tagged Momentum Partners Consultancy Services (PVT) LTD in a post about our recent engagement at Zahira College. But I wanted to go one layer deeper on why that project mattered to me personally.

When the leadership reached out, the ask wasn't "train our teachers better."It was narrower, more operational, and honestly, far more interesting:
 - The Masters-in-Charge (MICs) of co-curricular activities were drowning in                            administrative overload.
 - The constant churn of manual reporting, coordination, and admin friction was eating           directly into the part of the job that actually mattered, the students sitting in front of           them.

So we didn't jump in with a generic, off-the-shelf "time management" slide deck.   

We started with a proper Training Needs Analysis (TNA) to trace the friction points and understand exactly where those lost hours were going.   

The solution ended up being:
- Partly workflow automation to eliminate repetitive administrative steps.
- Partly accountability structures to protect focus hours.
- Only partly "training" in the traditional sense.   

That’s the part of Organizational Development I care about most. Capability building isn't about running classroom theatre or dumping more information on overwhelmed employees.   

Real enablement is understanding floor-level friction and paving it out of their way so high-value talent can actually perform.   

For the HR Directors, Principals, and L&D leads in my network:
- What is one administrative friction point currently eating up your team's most valuable       hours? 
Let's discuss and diagnose.....
