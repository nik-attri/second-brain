BIG ANNOUNCEMENT:

We just hired our 47th AI employee.

Please welcome Steve.

Steve monitors an inbox.

He doesn’t reply to anything.

But he does monitor the absolute hell out of it.

Steve joins our rapidly growing AI workforce:

Sarah — summarizes PDFs

Mike — moves information from one spreadsheet to another

Jessica — writes LinkedIn posts about how many AI agents we have

Brad — summarizes Jessica’s LinkedIn posts

Kevin — sends Slack notifications nobody reads

Amanda — checks whether Kevin sent the notification

And Greg.

We’re not entirely sure what Greg does.

But his agent dashboard is green, so we assume he’s crushing it.

Combined, our AI workforce operates 24/7, never takes PTO, and has increased the number of things happening inside our company by 847%.

Revenue impact:

Still calculating.

This is where the AI agent conversation is getting a little ridiculous.

We’ve somehow turned “number of agents” into a status symbol.

20 agents.

50 agents.

100 agents.

Okay.

What do they actually accomplish?

An AI workforce isn’t impressive because you created a bunch of agents.

It’s impressive when those agents can interact with real business systems, execute real workflows, operate within clear permissions, involve humans when necessary, and reliably produce measurable outcomes.

Otherwise you don’t have 47 AI employees.

You have 47 automations wearing name tags.

Don’t count agents.

Measure outcomes.

And somebody please figure out what Greg does.
