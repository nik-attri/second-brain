Same currency conversion, two different tax rules.

I'm preparing our Eagle app for initial customer preview, which includes calculations needed for US tax reporting. If you're an expat American, holding assets in the UK, "convert it to dollars" hides some traps. Now, I love dealing with complexity, but this isn't fun to do manually with just a set of bank statements...

FBAR (reporting foreign accounts): you find the highest balance each account held during the year, in its own currency. You then convert that one figure to US dollars using the Treasury's official exchange rate for 31 December.

Capital gains: here the rule runs the other way. Each trade is converted at the rate on its own transaction date, and the gain is calculated in dollars. You can't work out the gain in British pounds and convert the difference. Currency movement between buying and selling is part of the result.

Two rules, two methods, and the same pound can legitimately be worth different dollar amounts depending on the question you're answering.
