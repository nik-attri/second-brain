The sun doesn’t wear out, but the equipment sure as hell does! Just finished reading Wood Mackenzie September 2026 “Renewing Renewables” report. They forecast that more than 2.5 terawatts of wind and solar projects globally will face the decision to extend, repower, or retire in the 2040s.

These figures might sound mind-blowing to some, but after nearly two decades working through the many solar rushes, one thing has been very clear to me: eventually, this industry would have to face the music and take care of all this stuff we were installing.

We’re seeing more of it every day: failing equipment, hard-to-find replacement parts, and roofs that can’t wait.

Most of the owners we work with have one question in mind: Keep fixing it? Repower it? Take it down? And getting that answer right takes more than a spreadsheet. Somebody has to get on the roof, inspect the wiring, understand what can be reused, and plan the work without damaging the building, disrupting the business, or putting valuable existing utility agreements at risk.

The cool part is that this is work the next gen of tradespeople and renewable professionals can build meaningful, successful careers around.

All this to say, the renewable saga will continue well into the future, my friends... and those of us who’ve been around awhile have a responsibility to help the next gen get it right.
