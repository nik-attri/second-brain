In the video, the notable feature is the real-time adaptation: the person appears to modify how the heavy wheel is handled as the situation develops, rather than rigidly repeating the initial method. That's characteristic of adaptive intelligence.

An AI can exhibit adaptive reasoning, but is not inherently a continuous aircraft fly-by-wire controller. It normally receive information, reason about it, and respond. A fly-by-wire AI would continuously receive sensor data, detect changes, and immediately adjust an action.

The analogy is:

Adaptive AI:
observe → infer → adapt → recommend/act

Fly-by-wire AI:
sense → infer → adapt → act → re-sense → correct → repeat

That feedback loop is what gives the system a fly-by-wire character.

And this is where AI can differ from ordinary automation

Traditional automation often looks like:

> IF A → Do B.

Adaptive intelligence looks more like:

> IF A happens, determine what state the system is actually in, infer why it happened, and choose the best response available under the constraints.

For example:

Traditional:
“If temperature > 80°C, turn fan to maximum.”

Adaptive:
“Temperature is rising unusually fast despite increased cooling. The airflow sensor shows obstruction, so increasing fan speed further is unlikely to help. Reduce load and issue an obstruction warning.”

That second behavior is much closer to what you were calling spontaneous adaptive intelligence.

An AI system could potentially sit between the human and the machine, much like a very sophisticated fly-by-wire layer:

Human:
“Keep the machine stable.”

AI controller:
continuously interprets sensor information and decides how to maintain stability.

The human specifies the goal; the AI handles many of the micro-adjustments.

That's a better description of "adaptive intelligence" than simply saying the AI is intelligent.

And there is an even deeper version: the AI could identify a situation it has never seen before but is structurally similar to previous situations and transfer an appropriate response immediately. That's where adaptive intelligence starts approaching what humans often call intuition.

In fact, if the above video is showing the technician struggling to position that large wheel, an adaptive AI watching the scene could potentially recognize:

"Instead of fighting the heavy wheel, change the relative geometry."

They could potentially adjust the hydraulic lift height by a small amount rather than focusing on the person supporting and positioning the wheel manually.
That's a form of mechanical problem-solving through changing the reference frame.
That's an example of the distinction we were discussing: the intelligent response isn't necessarily "move the object harder"; it's "change the conditions so the problem becomes easier."
