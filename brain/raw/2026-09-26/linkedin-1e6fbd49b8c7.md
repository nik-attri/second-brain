AI is making campaign management easier.
But it’s also making bad inputs more dangerous.

As advertising platforms become increasingly automated, the Campaign Manager’s job is changing.

It’s no longer mainly about:
→ choosing keywords
→ adjusting bids
→ creating audiences
→ manually optimizing campaigns

AI can increasingly handle many of these tasks.

The real competitive advantage is becoming the quality of the signals we give the system.

Think about what happens when:

• Your conversion tracking measures leads, but not qualified leads or actual sales
• Offline revenue never gets connected back to the ad platform
• Conversion values don’t reflect real business value
• CRM data is disconnected from advertising platforms
• Attribution is treated as proof of incrementality
• Campaign objectives are optimized around what is easiest to measure, rather than what matters to the business

In that situation, AI doesn’t necessarily solve the problem.

It can simply optimize faster against the wrong signal.

That’s why I think the Campaign Manager of the AI era needs to understand much more than campaign settings.

They need to understand:

💡Data → Tracking → Conversion quality → Business value → Measurement → Experimentation → Automation✅️

Google is already putting more emphasis on first party data, data strength, offline signals and incrementality as part of its measurement ecosystem.

And with AI Max expanding across Search, the direction is clear: platforms are taking over more of the tactical execution, while marketers need to become better at strategy, signal quality and business context.

So perhaps the question for Campaign Managers is no longer:

“How do I optimize this campaign?”

But:

“Am I giving the system the right information to optimize the business?”

That’s a much harder question and, in my view, a much more valuable skill.
