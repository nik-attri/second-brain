3 years ago, I thought being a developer meant writing good code.

I still believe that matters.

But over the last 3+ years, I’ve learned something much bigger:

Good software isn’t just about what you build.
It’s about what you make unnecessary.

I started by building applications, APIs, websites, and integrations.

Then I started noticing patterns.

Someone was repeating the same task every day.

Someone was copying data from one system to another.

Someone was manually preparing a report.

Someone was waiting for another person to complete a step before they could continue.

And I kept asking myself:

“Why are we doing this manually?”

That question changed the way I approach development.

Instead of only building features, I started building systems around problems.

Automation.

Integrations.

Data pipelines.

Internal tools.

CRM workflows.

Analytics.

LMS integrations.

AI-powered tools.

APIs and webhooks.

Performance optimization.

Sometimes it was a large system.

Sometimes it was just a small script that saved someone from doing the same boring task every morning.

Over time, I realized something:

My job wasn’t simply to write code.

My job was to remove friction.

And that’s probably the biggest transformation I’ve gone through as a developer.

I went from asking:

“How do I build this?”

to asking:

“Why does this need to be done manually at all?”

That shift changed how I design systems, how I approach problems, and even how I define what successful software looks like.

Today, I still call myself a Full Stack Developer.

But I also see myself as a Product & Platform Engineer — someone who enjoys taking messy, repetitive problems and turning them into systems that just work.

3+ years in.

Still learning.
Still building.
Still automating. 🚀
