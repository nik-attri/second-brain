The data is clean.
The dashboard is clear.
The insight is obvious.
But nobody acts on it.

Even when the data is well designed 
Even when the dashboard is clean 
Even when the insight is clear 

People don't act on it.
Not because they don't understand it.
Because they don't trust it.

A health worker gets an alert.
She sees it.
But checks it manually anyway.

Not because the system is wrong.

But because the last time she trusted it without checking, it was wrong.

And in healthcare, one serious error can be enough to make people stop trusting a system.


This is the trust problem in health data.
And it's almost never in the product roadmap.

Teams obsess over data accuracy.
They rarely obsess over whether users believe the data is accurate.

Those are two completely different problems.

One is an engineering problem.
The other is a product problem.


You can build the most technically sound health data product in the market.
But if the nurse doesn't trust the alert 
If the pharmacist double-checks the system manually 
If the administrator uses the old spreadsheet "just to be safe" 
It's just as good as collecting data just for storage.

And we know storage doesn't save lives!!


The data was right.
The product worked.
And none of it mattered.
So, how do you build trust in a product where the cost of being wrong is too high to ignore?

— Elizabeth Osamudiamen, Product Manager
