Internship Reflection | Changi Airport Group, Quality Service Management (Airport Operations) | 31 March - 14 August 2026

In celebration of my GPA release, I took some time to reflect on my internship with Changi Airport Group.

To keep it short, I worked with the Staff Recognition & Awards Programme, where my day-to-day work involved reviewing passenger compliments and stories to determine whether they met the criteria for the Changi G.E.M. monthly awards. I also helped review programme guidelines, manage nominations, draft and edit recognition stories, and explore how SharePoint, Copilot and Power Automate could improve existing workflows.

It made me realise how much work happens behind the scenes to make something as simple as a recognition programme run smoothly. Even small details, from how information is presented to how a nomination is processed or an email is written, can affect someone's experience.

One project I was particularly involved in was automating part of the passenger compliment process. I worked on turning a previously manual process into a more streamlined workflow using Power Automate. It was a good opportunity to apply what I had learnt about process improvement and automation to an actual workplace problem. More importantly, I learnt that automation is not simply about making something “faster”. It is about understanding the existing process, identifying where time and effort are being spent, and finding a practical way to make it better.

But perhaps what I will remember most is the culture.

CAG has such an open and welcoming work environment. I never felt there was a barrier between being an intern and approaching someone for help, whether it was to ask a question, clarify something, or simply have a conversation. I could come to work feeling comfortable, and somehow even the ordinary days became something I looked forward to.

It was also the small things that made the experience special: the coffee cart, monthly pantry parties, conversations with colleagues, good food around the office, and even the MRT rides to work. There were even days when I worked from home and found myself missing the office, which was something I never expected from an internship.

Hearing about some of my friends' internship experiences made me realise that a good workplace culture should not be taken for granted. To me, it is not just about free coffee or fun activities. It is being able to ask questions without hesitation, knowing people are willing to help, feeling respected, and knowing that your contributions, even as an intern, are valued.

Looking back, that is probably one of my biggest takeaways from this internship. The work taught me about processes, operations and problem-solving, but the people and culture showed me what kind of workplace I hope to contribute to in the future.

Thank you to everyone in the Quality Service Management team who made these past few months such a meaningful experience. 

Hope our paths cross again!
