You can’t coach everyone the same way.

And pretending you can is lazy leadership.

There, I said it.

Some people need encouragement.

Some need structure.

Some need accountability.

Some need freedom.

Some need you to ask better questions.

And some need you to stop sugarcoating the truth.

Yet a lot of managers still try to lead everyone with the same script.

Same meeting.

Same speech.

Same goals.

Same pressure.

Same “motivational” nonsense.

Then they act surprised when one person thrives and another shuts down.

Here’s the uncomfortable part:

Fair does not mean identical.

Treating everyone exactly the same can actually be one of the least effective ways to lead a team.

One employee may need confidence.

Another may have too much confidence and not enough discipline.

One person may need to be pushed.

Another is already pushing themselves harder than you ever could.

One wants recognition.

Another wants autonomy.

One wants the playbook.

Another wants you to get the hell out of the way and let them work.

Good coaching requires knowing the difference.

And that takes more effort than giving everyone the same tired speech about attitude and work ethic.

It requires paying attention.

Listening.

Learning what actually motivates someone.

Understanding what they’re good at.

Knowing where they’re weak.

Recognizing when someone needs support and when they need accountability.

That’s leadership.

Not treating adults like interchangeable parts.

I’ve watched managers try to “fix” strong employees by forcing them into the same mold as everyone else.

Sometimes the employee wasn’t the problem.

The coaching was.

And here’s another uncomfortable truth:

Not everyone responds to positivity.

Sometimes someone needs to hear:

“This isn’t good enough.”

“This has to improve.”

“You’re capable of more than what you’re giving me.”

That isn’t cruelty.

When it’s honest, specific, and delivered with respect, it can be exactly what someone needs.

But the opposite is also true.

Not every struggling employee needs pressure.

Sometimes they need clarity.

Sometimes training.

Sometimes confidence.

Sometimes a manager who actually gives a damn enough to understand what’s happening before labeling them lazy or unmotivated.

The best leaders I’ve been around understood something simple:

People are complicated.

You can’t manage them like a spreadsheet.

You can’t motivate them with one sentence.

And you definitely can’t coach them all the same way.

Leadership isn’t about developing one coaching style.

It’s about becoming good enough to have more than one.

Because if every employee gets the exact same version of you…

you’re probably not coaching them.

You’re just repeating yourself.
