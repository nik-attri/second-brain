Tim Chen got laid off on Christmas Day. His sister asked him one question about credit cards. The spreadsheet he made her is now worth...

well. The IPO filing targeted five billion dollars.

The financial crisis is shredding Wall Street, and Chen, a Stanford grad and hedge fund analyst, is out of a job during the holidays. He's sitting around, in his own words, twiddling his thumbs.

His sister, living in Australia, emails: which credit card has the lowest foreign transaction fees?

He figures he'll Google it and reply in three minutes. He can't. Everything he finds is marketing dressed up as advice. So this unemployed finance professional spends a full week building an Excel spreadsheet comparing what every major bank actually offers.

He sends it to one person.

She forwards it. Her friends forward it. The file travels far beyond anyone Tim knows, because millions of people had the same question and nobody had organized the answer.

He puts $800 into hosting and a domain and calls it NerdWallet.

First year's revenue: seventy-five dollars. He had to move in with his girlfriend.

Then year two: $60,000. Then 20 million visitors a month. Then, in 2021, an IPO.

He never validated an idea. He answered one email thoroughly, then paid attention to what the answer did on its own.

Help one person completely. Then watch where the file goes.
