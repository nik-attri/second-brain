UK junior vacancies are down a quarter since the start of 2025. Junior job ads asking for AI skills? Up nearly 3x. 📊

Last month at Imperial, I left every room with one question: if a junior hour and an agent hour sit on the same budget line, what makes the junior hour worth buying?

The market is starting to answer: entry-level roles aren’t vanishing, they’re being promoted. Some UK employers are now hiring graduates specifically to run AI agents, and in a Cognizant survey of 750 HR leaders, 96% expect entry-level jobs to become “AI supervision” roles within five years.

Sounds like progress, until you look at what the analyst grunt work was actually teaching. The reconciliation that won’t balance, the bug you chase for an hour, the MECE storyboard you had to whip out at a moment’s notice... figuring out what’s wrong is where juniors learn the most.  (📸 photo evidence attached: Luke Davies and I as consulting associates, grinding through slides at a very unglamorous hour in the middle of the desert)

The data agrees. In a randomised trial by Anthropic, developers who learned a new coding tool with #AI help scored 17% lower on a skills test afterwards than those who did it by hand, and the biggest gap was in debugging. Spotting what’s broken is arguably the one skill an “AI supervisor” can’t do without.

Cut the grunt work, and in five years you’ll have plenty of agents... and nobody senior enough to check them. What happens then? 🧠 #FutureOfWork #AgenticAI #EarlyCareers #GraduateJobs #AITransformation
