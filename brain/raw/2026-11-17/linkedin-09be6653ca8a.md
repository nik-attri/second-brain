Your first sales and marketing hire might bring in $0 in revenue, frustrate the hell out of you, and still be the best investment you make in your business.( and yes I sound insane, I know:)


Some of the founders I eventually work with,  come to me thinking what they need is someone to turn things around.

More customers. More sales. Better conversion. A predictable pipeline.( you know all the cool stuffs ghat look good on a spreadsheet)

And fair enough. That's what everyone wants.( Heck I want that for them) 

But then I get into the business, start asking questions, looking at what's already in place, understanding the product and how customers currently find them.

And sometimes, we realise we're practically starting from scratch.

There's no established acquisition system. No tested messaging. No clear playbook. Sometimes, there's not even a solid understanding of which audience is most likely to buy.

So before we can even talk about scaling revenue, there's a lot of figuring out to do.

Who are we actually selling to?

What's making people buy or not buy?

How do we communicate the product's value?

Which acquisition channels make sense?

What needs to change in the sales process?

Sometimes, the work starts with identifying problems the founder didn't even realise were there.

I've had experiences where a significant part of my contribution was helping a founder identify gaps and figure out how to communicate the product's value better.

I've attached a snippet of what he had to say about that experience.

And this is partly why I'm careful about promising founders specific revenue numbers before understanding what I'm walking into.

I could promise you 5000 customers in 30 days. It sounds great.

But if there's no acquisition system, no validated messaging and no clear understanding of what's stopping people from buying, what exactly am I basing that promise on?

I'd rather understand the business, identify what's missing and build from there.

That doesn't mean results don't matter. They absolutely do.

But there's a difference between coming into a business with an existing system that needs optimisation and coming into one where you're figuring out what the system should look like.

And for anyone taking their first sales or marketing role at an early-stage startup:

You might think you're coming in to sell.

But you might end up building the entire commercial function.

No playbook. No predictable pipeline. No established process.

Just a product, a market to understand and a lot of things to figure out.

Sometimes, you're building the plane while flying it.

And that's the reality of being someone's first commercial hire.
