Automation excels at repetition
But manufacturing isn't always predictable

✅A machine can execute a defined process with remarkable consistency. 

❌It cannot always interpret an ambiguous drawing, recognise an unusual site constraint or weigh the consequences of a decision that falls outside standard parameters.

👉Consider a custom fabrication job:

The challenge may not be producing the component. It may be questioning a specification, identifying a potential assembly issue or deciding when a deviation needs engineering approval.

These situations require context, experience and judgement.

That doesn't mean humans should do everything manually. 

It means automation should handle what it does best, while people focus on exceptions, complex decisions and problems where the cost of getting it wrong is high.

The goal isn't to remove humans from manufacturing. It's to put human expertise where it creates the most value.

As factories become smarter, perhaps the bigger question isn't “What can we automate?”

📍It's “Where would automating the decision create more risk than value?”"
