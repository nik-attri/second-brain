The buyer’s AI just redlined your proposal. You haven’t met the buyer yet.

Very few books change how an entire industry sells. Justin Michael and I wrote two with that ambition.

History will decide. You will decide.

1988 · SPIN Selling gave sellers a research-backed way to ask better questions. The pitch finally had to wait its turn.

1995 · Solution Selling taught sellers to diagnose before prescribing. A demo is a hell of a way to find out you’re solving the wrong problem.

2011 · The Challenger Sale showed sellers how to bring an insight that changed how buyers saw their own business. “Just checking in” had a bad year.

2011 · Predictable Revenue specialized sales roles and made outbound repeatable. Pipeline could finally be a process instead of a group prayer.

2015 · The Sales Acceleration Formula showed how to scale hiring, training, and lead generation with data. Even the sales VP’s gut feeling had to bring a spreadsheet.

2021 · Tech-Powered Sales
Before ChatGPT launched, Justin and I saw tools like it coming. We imagined an Iron Man suit for every seller, complete with a JARVIS of their own. The point was superhuman reach without asking anyone to work superhuman hours.

2027 · Sales Superintelligence
On January 4, we release the sequel. It’s a playbook for putting your sales methodology to work inside AI, from prospecting and deal strategy to coaching and forecasting. Early readers have called it career-changing and industry-changing.

Your AI will negotiate with the buyer’s AI before you’ve even met the buyer. By the time you sit down, your price may already have been pressure-tested and the shortlist cut. Good thing you brought slides.

Here’s the prediction I’ll take even further. After AGI, as we approach the Singularity, every seller could be Neuralinked and human judgment would still command a premium. When a $20 million deal goes sideways, the buyer will still want a person who can look them in the eye and say, “I own this.”

AI doesn’t have to replace you. It just has to help your competitor win the deal you can’t afford to lose.
