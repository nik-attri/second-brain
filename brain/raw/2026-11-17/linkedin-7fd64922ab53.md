At the recent Texas Linux Fest, I took the opportunity during a talk to share a story about this one time when I was stuck in a job I wasn't allowed to do, got bored, and oopsidentally created a problem for a government contract and a headache for NAVWAR. This all happened back in the early '00s, when the US was embroiled in Operation Iraqi Freedom, when we were stationed in Bahrain, when I took personal offense to the IT shop tracking trouble tickets with spreadsheets.

My point of the story wasn't just that we were living in the wild, wild west of tech times, when the Dot Boom attitude still lingered just enough to keep the Dot Bomb restrictions from suffocating us. I pointed out we're hitting a point in the industry where we are likely to see another Boom turn Bomb, and right now is our chance to recapture some of the...whimsy of being a developer in the '00s. We have the ability to pursue wild stuff. We can leverage the technology that's caught the eye of every investor. We can have reckless ideas and impulses and then execute with deliberation and keeping in mind the lessons we've learned from the last many decades.

The talk was a fun retelling of a wild time in my life, but it was a really good touchstone for me, a reminder that the industry was a dumpster fire literally the day after I started my post-college job, and it is a dumpster fire today, but the lessons we take from the manic rise and hellish fall of the latest trendy tech will help us gently brake on the downhill ride into hell when the AI bubble bursts.
