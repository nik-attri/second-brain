Your automation went live. Your team is still doing the work manually.
I keep seeing the same story. The bot works, the demo impresses leadership, the project closes on time. Six months later, ops are back on spreadsheets, and the adoption rate is under 30%.
The problem isn’t the technology. We measure go-live, not usage. Delivery builds it, Ops inherits it, and nobody owns what happens next.
Here’s what a Chief of Staff can do:
·          Redefine “done”: adoption target (e.g. 70% of eligible volume by Day 90) in the closure criteria
·          Name a business owner in Ops, with adoption on their scorecard
·          Put 2-3 ops power users in the build team from day one
·          Tie usage to team KPIs and address job-security fears openly
·          Run 4-6 weeks of hypercare, then publish “you said, we fixed”
·          Review one adoption dashboard monthly with Delivery, Ops and Finance
·          Pause new builds where adoption is poor
The one number to report to leadership every month: realized value vs. promised value.

Automation doesn’t create value when it’s deployed. It creates value when it’s used.
