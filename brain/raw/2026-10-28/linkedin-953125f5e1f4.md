Turns out the hardest part of running an AI team has very little to do with AI...

Last week I spent a few days with our 20-ish AI engineers in Kraków. The team are strong: several projects already in production, in a field that's constantly changing. 

Two things I took away, and neither of them is really about AI.

1) “It’s okay to build a house. You don’t need to build a castle.” 
That was probably the sentence I used most last week. The use cases that succeed get in front of real users within days or weeks, not months. Getting someone to upload a spreadsheet manually, instead of wiring up complex integrations to creaky legacy systems, is often the smart move. Agree the outcome up front, and the right amount of build usually follows.

2) Output is not progress. 
A busy team is not the same as a valuable one, and a long list of things built is not the same as a short list of things used. AI has made shipping fast and coding close to commoditised, and that brings its own illusions. The only measure that matters is whether someone in the business is doing their job differently because of it. Find the incremental steps that move the dial and kill the ones that don’t. And if you’re not sure, switch it off and see who complains. Silence is also a data point.

I flew out to see what the team had built. I flew back thinking about what we’d asked them to build. And how. And why.
