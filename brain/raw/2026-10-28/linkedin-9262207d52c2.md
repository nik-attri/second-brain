When Does an AI Use Case Stop Making Financial Sense?
A process that takes 200 employee hours per month looks like an obvious automation opportunity. But eliminating manual work doesn't automatically translate into 200 hours of financial benefit. For private equity firms evaluating AI opportunities across portfolio companies, that distinction matters.

𝘊𝘰𝘯𝘴𝘪𝘥𝘦𝘳 𝘢 𝘩𝘺𝘱𝘰𝘵𝘩𝘦𝘵𝘪𝘤𝘢𝘭 𝘱𝘰𝘳𝘵𝘧𝘰𝘭𝘪𝘰 𝘤𝘰𝘮𝘱𝘢𝘯𝘺 𝘭𝘰𝘰𝘬𝘪𝘯𝘨 𝘵𝘰 𝘢𝘶𝘵𝘰𝘮𝘢𝘵𝘦 𝘪𝘯𝘷𝘰𝘪𝘤𝘦 𝘱𝘳𝘰𝘤𝘦𝘴𝘴𝘪𝘯𝘨.
The initial business case looks promising: high transaction volumes, repetitive data entry, and significant employee time spent on manual processing.
Then implementation planning reveals a few complications.
𝟭. 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗰𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗶𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝗰𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗻𝘁.
Different business units follow different approval workflows. Before automation can scale, those processes need to be standardized.
𝟮. 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗱𝗮𝘁𝗮 𝗿𝗲𝗾𝘂𝗶𝗿𝗲𝘀 𝗮𝗱𝗱𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻𝗮𝗹 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸.
Invoices arrive in multiple formats, supplier records contain inconsistencies, and exceptions still require human review. The automation scope is narrower than initially assumed.
𝟯. 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗲𝘅𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘀𝘆𝘀𝘁𝗲𝗺𝘀 𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗮𝘁𝗲 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗴𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗰𝗼𝘀𝘁𝘀.
Connecting the solution to the company's ERP requires development, testing, and ongoing maintenance.
𝟰. 𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝘀𝗮𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴𝘀 𝗮𝗿𝗲𝗻'𝘁 𝗻𝗲𝗰𝗲𝘀𝘀𝗮𝗿𝗶𝗹𝘆 𝗰𝗮𝘀𝗵 𝘀𝗮𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴𝘀.
Reducing processing time creates additional capacity, but unless that capacity translates into lower costs, avoided hiring, or increased output, the financial benefit may be smaller than the hours saved suggest.

None of these challenges necessarily makes an automation opportunity less attractive. They highlight why implementation planning needs to be part of the investment evaluation, not an afterthought.

For private equity firms, 𝗮 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝗼𝗻𝗴 𝗔𝗜 𝗯𝘂𝘀𝗶𝗻𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗰𝗮𝘀𝗲 should account for more than the volume of work that could be automated. It should establish:
🔹What operational improvements are realistically achievable.
🔹What technical and organizational requirements must be addressed.
🔹How those improvements translate into measurable financial value.
🔹What implementation and ongoing operating costs are involved.
🔹How quickly the investment can begin delivering returns.
The goal isn't to find reasons not to automate. It's to identify where automation can deliver the greatest value and build a realistic path to capturing it.
That's what turns a promising AI use case into a credible value-creation opportunity.
