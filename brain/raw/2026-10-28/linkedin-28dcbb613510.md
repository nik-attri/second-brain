FNB was fined R1m this week. The regulator says a setting let 696 customers see 88 people's home loan applications. Two customers flagged it in 2022. FNB closed the access for each of them and treated it as isolated.

The software worked. Nobody owned the rule it was supposed to enforce.

That's why I think the first job on any build is writing down what the system must never do.

Drumbeat Charters, a tour operator in Hout Bay Harbour, Cape Town, wrote every cruise ticket by hand. Passengers were checked manually. Safety compliance needs to know exactly who is on board, and the captain and the office both need that list.

So we started there. Tickets print with a QR code. Staff scan each passenger on boarding with the mobile app. Anyone with access sees who's on the boat. An admin portal reconciles ticket sales against income records.

Planning wrapped up months before COVID. Boats were shut for about 18 months. When the industry reopened, development restarted fast because the thinking was already done.

Before you ask for features, ask what the system has to get right every time. Then pay someone to keep it right.

That's how we build in Cape Town and Johannesburg.
