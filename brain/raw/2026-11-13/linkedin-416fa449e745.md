Yesterday's Microsoft outage raised a question I can't stop thinking about:
.
Should we still be removing humans from our workflows and relying only on AI? 🤔
.
For more than 12 hours, Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and Copilot were down or unstable for millions of people. Microsoft 365 Copilot went down. Copilot Studio connectors failed. Automations that read mail, calendars and files stopped at once.
.
The AI layer didn't fail on its own. It failed along with everything underneath it.
.
So which teams kept working?
.
The ones that still had people who knew the process. People who could pick up the phone, message a client on another channel, make a judgement call and keep things moving without a dashboard.
.
Right now many companies are trying to automate humans out of the process completely. One agent replaces a support team. One workflow replaces an approval chain. On a normal day, the numbers look great.
.
But efficiency on a normal day is not the only test. Resilience on a bad day matters too.
.
Here's where I have landed:
🔹 AI should take over the repetitive work. That's where it shines, and it frees people for better work.
.
🔹 Humans should keep the context. If nobody on the team understands how the process works without the tool, you have not automated it. You have made it fragile.
.
🔹 Every AI workflow needs a human fallback: a person who knows what to do when the agent goes quiet.
.
🔹 "Fair" works both ways. Is it fair to customers who can't get help for 12 hours because there was nobody left to answer? Is it fair to employees who are expected to step in during a crisis for processes they were never trained on?
.
The aim is not humans OR AI. 
It is AI that does the work and humans who keep it working.
Automate the tasks. 
Don't automate away the know-how.
.
Where do you stand? Is "AI-only" a smart strategy or a single point of failure? 👇
.
.
.
