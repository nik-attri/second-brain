Today, Microsoft Copilot went offline. Instead of building my presentation the old-fashioned way, I did something that surprised me: I completely stopped working.

This moment exposed the true extent of our cognitive offloading and established a hard truth about our new technological baseline. Before AI, we built presentations manually - effectively, yet slowly. Today, with the Copilot offline, reverting to that manual process felt paralyzing.

The prospect of digging through scattered documents, cross-referencing emails, and synthesizing slides by hand suddenly seemed archaic, inefficient and pointless.

It is like when your GPS app crashes mid-journey while you in an unfamiliar place. You do not pull out a paper map and start plotting coordinates. You pull over, wait for the signal to return, and refuse to navigate manually. The cognitive load of the old baseline is unacceptable.

This illustrates how rapidly integrated AI has evolved from a novelty to critical digital infrastructure.

For product builders, this also represents the ultimate measure of product stickiness: when your users would rather pause their entire workflow than operate without your product.
