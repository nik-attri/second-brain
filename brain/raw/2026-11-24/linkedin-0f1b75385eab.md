Buying an expensive AI product doesn't mean you've automated anything.

I was talking to a senior manager of an organisation with over 1,000 employees. More than 50 people work in his department alone.

They've hired AI specialists. 
They've invested in AI. 

They've already implemented their first AI project in his department.

Yet he was still having difficulties with a fundamental question.

Where do we start with AI? 

What was surprising to me was how they had approached their first project.

Imagine a piece of work that involves 40 steps.

They bought an expensive AI product that helps with a 1-2 of those steps.
It's useful. It did help with those steps

But as he said, they were still drowning in work. 

So i asked: "what happens before those steps?
What happens after?
Who gathers the information, feeds it into the AI, takes the output and carries on with the rest of the work?

The answer. Their people.

They've introduced AI into the workflow without automating the workflow itself.

Whenever I have these conversations with business owners and senior managers, I start with one simple question:

Where in your business are people spending hours doing manual, time-consuming, repetitive work?

That's where I would start.

Understand the work from beginning to end. 

Where the information comes from. 
What people do with it. 
What decisions are made. 
Where the output needs to go.

Then figure out how to get AI to work within that entire process.

That's how you get a meaningful return on your investment.

That's how you create capacity within your existing team instead of constantly needing to hire more people.

There's a big difference between giving your people another tool to use and taking work off their desks.

Don't just use AI.
Get it to do the work.
