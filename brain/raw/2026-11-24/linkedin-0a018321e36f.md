AI IN 2030: WILL WE STILL CALL IT “CODING”?

Today, developers use AI to write code faster.

By 2030, that may sound as outdated as manually writing every database query.

The bigger shift won't be AI writing more code.

It will be AI taking responsibility for more of the software development process.

Imagine a business saying:

“We need a customer portal that reduces support requests by 30%.”

Instead of starting with hundreds of tickets and thousands of lines of code, an AI-native engineering system could:

→ Understand the business requirement  
→ Design the solution  
→ Generate the application  
→ Create and execute test cases  
→ Identify security risks  
→ Fix defects  
→ Prepare the deployment  
→ Monitor production  
→ Suggest improvements

And humans?

They become responsible for the things AI cannot simply “generate”:

What should we build?  
What should we never build?  
What risks are acceptable?  
What does the business actually need?  
Who is accountable when something goes wrong?

That changes the definition of a software engineer.

From writing instructions for computers  
→ to defining intent for intelligent systems.

And it changes the definition of an IT company too.

The competitive advantage won't simply be:

“How many developers do we have?”

It may become:

“How effectively can our people, AI agents, data and engineering processes work together?”

2030 is not necessarily the end of coding.

It could be the beginning of software engineering at a completely different scale.
