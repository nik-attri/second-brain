What Do I Actually Do?

I get paid to talk for a living, Which my old teachers would find quite amusing, 
Given that most of my school reports 
Described me as disruptive and a nuisance.

I’m a bald, bearded Welshman, With tattoos and dodgy knees, Who spends his life in places of work 
Drinking questionable cups of tea.

There’s always someone with their arms folded, 
Who’d rather be anywhere but there, 
Thinking, who’s this hairy bastard, 
And why the hell should I care?

We have a laugh and take the piss, 
As people quite often do, 
Then somewhere amongst the laughter, 
We start talking about what’s  true.

I talk about the mates I’ve lost, And the battles inside my own head, 
About the times I smiled and carried on, 
When I thought I was better off  dead.

You can have your wellbeing strategy, 
And your posters stuck on the wall, 
They’re sod all use to a struggling man 
Who feels he’s got no one  to call.

And when everyone’s gone back to work, 
There’s often someone who stays behind, 
Who finally finds the courage to admit 
They’re anything but bloody fine.

That’s the bit you’ll never measure 
On a spreadsheet or a chart, When someone who’s spent their  life building walls 
Finally allows one to fall apart.

So I’ll keep clocking up the miles, And drinking those awful brews Because whilst there are people  still dying in silence, 
There’s loads  more talking to do.

Keep on keeping on
