“£1 million a year. Pissed against the wall.”

I'll never forget those words.

I'd just shown a CEO that one issue in his business was costing approximately £20,000 every single week.

Over £1 million a year.

And nobody had spotted it.

Not because the finance team wasn't working hard.

Not because the business didn't have enough information.

Quite the opposite.

They had reports.

Spreadsheets.

Variance analysis.

Management accounts.

Pages and pages of numbers.

What they didn't have was clarity.

The problem wasn't producing the information.

It was knowing what to look for, what to challenge and, most importantly, what to do about it.

The fix wasn't particularly complicated.

Finding the problem was.

It reminded me of something a Managing Director said to me years earlier.

“I don't actually want the report, Phil. I just want to know what the hell is going on.”

He was absolutely right.

Because a business can produce beautiful management accounts, delivered perfectly on time, and still be unable to answer four fundamental questions.

💰 What's actually making us money?

💸 What's quietly costing us money?

📉 Where is the cash going?

🎯 What needs fixing first?

After 26 years in finance, I've learned that more information doesn't automatically lead to better decisions.

And growth doesn't always mean making more money.

Sometimes it means identifying the money you're already earning but failing to keep.

That's why I believe a Finance Director's real value isn't in producing another report.

It's in challenging assumptions, asking the uncomfortable questions and turning numbers into decisions.

Because the most expensive problem in your business might not be the one you can see.

It might be the one buried in a spreadsheet that nobody has thought to question.

And sometimes finding that one number is worth more than a thousand pages of reporting.
