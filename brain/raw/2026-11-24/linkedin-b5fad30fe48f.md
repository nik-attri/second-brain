The time it literally rained on our delegates (and what it taught us about venue choice).

Years ago, long before we established Heath House Conference Centre, we ran training courses for schools and colleges. 

The courses were primarily for senior leaders, helping them to prepare for inspection. Our role was to create and deliver high impact learning, helping schools to achieve what was then ‘outstanding’ status. Our background was within school inspection and tutors were all experts. 

The school arranging the training was responsible for booking the training venue and looking after all of the practical arrangements for the day. 

This model meant that we visited hundreds of training locations across the UK, and we got to see first-hand the hidden friction points organisers face when trying to source spaces on a tight budget.

Budgets within the education sector were notoriously tight, and on one memorable occasion, a host school—trying to be mindful of costs—used a spare staffroom as a ‘make-do’ training room. What they forgot to consider, was that the industrial water boiler situated in the corner of the room (for coffee and tea) was steaming continuously, and for some reason, couldn’t be manually switched off. 

By 11:00 AM, the room had filled with steam. As the steam hit the ceiling, it condensed and began dripping onto the delegates’ notebooks. The atmosphere was uncomfortably, humid, and delegates were increasingly distracted and frustrated – not with us but with an uncomfortable environment. 

Our tutor did the only sensible thing. They paused the training and asked for an alternative space to be found. 

Years on, it’s a talking point, but it highlights a very serious business principle:

The biggest expense of any training day isn't the venue or the facilitator—it’s the salaries of the people sitting in the seats, combined with the expense of covering their day-to-day responsibilities.

When an organiser pulls 10 or 20 staff out of their daily roles, it’s a massive financial investment. If a venue falls short of providing a focused, distraction-free environment, the return on that investment drops significantly.

Choosing a venue designed for learning isn't about luxury; it’s about protecting the value of your team's time. Heath House combines the warmth and character of a beautiful period building with modern technology, comfortable ergonomics, and thoughtful design—creating the perfect, distraction-free environment for people to actually focus and retain information.
