The person who cleans the office after everyone leaves is, for now, harder to replace than the person who writes the quarterly report.

In six months, frontier AI models jumped from matching human experts on 47.6% of real white-collar deliverables to 83.0% (OpenAI's GDPval across 44 occupations). Meanwhile, Stanford payroll data shows US employment for ages 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed roles running 19% below trend, with no comparable drop for senior workers.

That is not just a hiring blip. It is the modern job coming unbundled.

We tend to think of a job as one thing. It was actually a bundle packing four different things into a single salaried contract: a wage to live on, durable work that fills the day, daily structure and social contact, and an identity you hand strangers when they ask what you do.

Most white-collar work had already been broken into repeatable, procedural steps long before any model arrived. AI did not invent that way of valuing work. It called the bluff.

And the sharpest irony sits on the first rung: senior judgment is built by doing the slow, entry-level work that companies are now automating first.
