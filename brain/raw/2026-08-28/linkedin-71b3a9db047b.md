Your sales team was hired to sell.
So why are they spending only 40% of their time selling?

I came across this in Salesforce's 2026 State of Sales research.

According to the report, sellers spend around 60% of their time on non-selling tasks.

Think about that for a moment.

More than half of their working time can go into everything that happens around the sale:

→ Updating the CRM

→ Entering customer information

→ Finding the right sales material

→ Prospect research

→ Internal approvals

→ Planning and other administrative work

None of these things are necessarily bad.

They are part of running a sales process.

But there's a difference between necessary work and work that actually needs to be done manually.

Imagine a salesperson finishes a call with a prospect.

The conversation is done.

But the work isn't.

They still need to:

Write the notes.

Update the CRM.

Change the opportunity stage.

Add information to another system.

Send an internal update.

Prepare for the next step.

And then do the same thing again after the next call.

One time doesn't seem like much.

But multiply that across dozens of conversations, every week, across an entire sales team.

That's where the time starts disappearing.

And this is one of the areas where automation can make a real difference.

Not by replacing the salesperson.

The conversation still needs a person.

The relationship still needs a person.

The decisions still need a person.

But the repetitive work around those activities doesn't always need to be handled manually.

Information can move between systems automatically.

Records can be updated automatically.

Internal notifications can happen automatically.

Routine administrative steps can happen in the background.

So the salesperson can spend more of their time doing what actually requires a salesperson:

Talking to customers. Understanding their needs. Building relationships. Moving opportunities forward.

The interesting question isn't:

"Can we automate sales?"

It's:

"How much of the work around selling really needs a salesperson to do it?" 

Because if your team is spending most of its time supporting the sales process instead of actually selling...

there may be more room for automation than you think.

How much of your sales team's time do you think is actually spent on selling?
