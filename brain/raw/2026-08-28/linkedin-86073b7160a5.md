💙 Can we talk … 

If your strategy gets more impressive as it moves up the organization and harder to use as it moves down, you may not have a communication problem. You may have a governance problem.


There’s a particular look I get sometimes in a boardroom when I ask a very simple question about a very complicated strategy. 😂 Not because the question is unsophisticated. Usually it’s because plain language has a funny way of removing the protection around assumptions we’ve gotten comfortable with.


“That’s best practice.” Okay, but does it still work?

“That control is necessary.” What risk is it controlling?

“That’s our process.” Why?



And somewhere in middle management, somebody has built a spreadsheet to track the system the organization bought to eliminate spreadsheets. 😂


🧐I know this about myself I’m practical as hell. I want to understand the complexity, absolutely. Law, policy, compliance, behavior, technology, risk, governance, all of it. But eventually somebody has to make the complex simple without making it stupid.


Simple and simplistic are not the same thing.


📫 Real simplification requires enough command of the subject to know what is material, what is noise, what must remain, what can change and which “best practice” may simply be a familiar practice that nobody has challenged lately.


That’s also why the stakes feel different right now.


📗 A “workforce transformation” may be a strategy in the boardroom. Downstream and to teams, it is somebody’s job, income, confidence and ability to figure out where they fit next. AI is already redistributing tasks, decisions and value across organizations. As quantum capabilities mature, we’re going to face another set of questions around security, computing, risk, skills and who is actually prepared to lead through it.


We cannot respond to a more complex future by simply pushing more complexity onto people.



🙄That isn’t transformation. That’s transfer.



🧡 Executives need the foresight to see what’s coming. Middle management needs enough clarity to turn strategy into execution. And the people doing the work deserve systems they can actually use.


That is where I know I bring value.


I can sit with complexity. Study it. Challenge it. Ask the uncomfortable question that occasionally earns me the funny face. 😂 Then turn what matters into something people can understand, govern and DO.

I’m okay with the funny faces.

‼️ Listen, a funny face in the boardroom is a lot cheaper than an unchallenged assumption in operations.

Innovation doesn’t always enter the room wearing a futuristic title. Sometimes it sounds like “Why are we still doing it this way?”


♻️ And if the strategy cannot survive that question in plain English, maybe it isn’t ready for execution yet.
