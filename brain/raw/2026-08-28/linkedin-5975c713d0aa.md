Ari Sheinkin is one of my favorite strategic technologists. I had the pleasure of several meaningful conversations with him when I was at BCG, and what always impressed me was his ability to look beyond the technology and see the entire system around it: people, process, economics and organizational design.

His recent interview below got me thinking about an important shift.

At the forefront of AI, the conversation is already moving beyond agents. Two years ago, we were figuring out how to build them. Then came agentic architectures, multi-agent systems and orchestration. All of that still matters, but if we’re trying to skate to where the puck is going, I think the bigger question is:

What happens to the company when extraordinarily capable machine intelligence becomes ubiquitous?

Most companies are still taking organizations designed around human limitations and inserting AI into them. Automate a workflow. Add a copilot. Build an agent.

Useful, but I increasingly think that is an interim stage.

The modern corporation was designed around the scarcity of human intelligence. We grouped expertise into departments, created management layers to move information and make decisions, and constructed jobs around what a human could reasonably accomplish.

AI changes those constraints.

Perhaps the real transformation isn’t putting AI into the company we already have. It is asking:

What company would we build if AI had always existed?

The employee may no longer be the fundamental unit of work. It could instead be a human surrounded by specialized machine intelligence, with work continuously allocated to whichever is best equipped to perform it.

That is why I think treating the “people side” of AI as traditional change management misses the magnitude of what is happening.

My good friend Rob Sullivan has spent roughly 25 years working on the human side of organizations, and we have been thinking deeply about this together: How do you redesign roles, skills, workflows and organizations around human + machine intelligence?

This becomes even more important as the technology improves. Models will commoditize. Agents will become easier to build. Orchestration will become infrastructure. Eventually, extraordinary AI will be available to almost everyone.

Competitive advantage will move somewhere else.

The winners won’t simply be the companies with the best AI. They will be the companies that redesign themselves most intelligently around it.

That is why Human + AI Transformation is becoming a core pillar of our approach at The Nexus.

Becoming AI-first isn’t ultimately a technology transformation.

It is an enterprise transformation.
