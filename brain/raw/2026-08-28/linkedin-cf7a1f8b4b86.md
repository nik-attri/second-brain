What if we stop thinking of Architecture as a phase?

I’ve been thinking about a role that sits somewhere between Architecture, DevOps, SRE and Engineering.

I’d call it:

Service Enablement Architect

The idea is simple:

A Solution Architect shouldn’t just design a solution and hand it over to engineering or operations.

And DevOps shouldn’t only be responsible for how we deploy it.

Instead, imagine a role that stays accountable for the continuous technical evolution and enablement of the service throughout its entire lifecycle.

From:

Design → Build → Integrate → Deploy → Operate → Observe → Evolve

The Service Enablement Architect would continuously work across these dimensions:

• Architecture — keeping the solution aligned with its design principles, patterns and standards.

• DevOps — enabling CI/CD, automation and infrastructure capabilities.

• SRE — driving reliability, resilience, observability and production readiness.

• Integration — continuously evolving how the service connects with other services and platforms.

• Engineering — understanding how actual implementation changes the original architecture.

And most importantly:

Continuous Design.

Because the architecture of a service shouldn’t be a document created at the beginning of the SDLC.

It should be a living design that evolves with the service.

Every new feature, integration, infrastructure change or automated deployment can potentially change the architecture.

So the question becomes:

How do we make sure the architecture evolves intentionally rather than accidentally?

This is where I see the Service Enablement Architect adding real value.

Not as another approval layer.

Not as someone who has to manually review every change.

But as the person who builds the architectural guardrails, automation and engineering capabilities that allow teams to move fast without losing:

Design consistency.
Reliability.
Security.
Scalability.
Observability.
Production readiness.

You could even call the combination:

Dev + Arch + Ops → DevArchOps

But I think Service Enablement Architect describes the purpose better.

Because the ultimate goal isn't to control the service.

It's to enable the service to continuously evolve while preserving the architecture that makes it reliable.

Architecture shouldn't end at Go-Live.

It should operate with the service.

And maybe that's the next evolution of Solution Architecture.
