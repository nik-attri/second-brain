𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗵𝗮𝗽𝗽𝗲𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗼 𝗣𝗮𝘁𝗰𝗵 𝗧𝘂𝗲𝘀𝗱𝗮𝘆?

Those of us who have been around Microsoft technology for a while remember "Patch Tuesday."

For years, we generally expected Microsoft's major security updates on the second Tuesday of each month. There were exceptions, of course, and you could always manually check for and install updates at other times.

But lately it seems like every time I turn around, Windows or another Microsoft product wants another update.

Microsoft still has Patch Tuesday, but the update model has expanded considerably. We now have monthly security updates, optional preview updates, out-of-band updates when needed, feature rollouts, and what Microsoft calls "continuous innovation" for Windows 11.

I understand the cybersecurity argument. If a serious vulnerability is discovered, I certainly don't want Microsoft waiting three weeks for the second Tuesday of the month just because that's the schedule.

But I do wonder whether we've reached a point where the frequency of updates creates its own problems—particularly for businesses trying to test patches, manage endpoints, schedule restarts, and keep users productive.

And then there's the practical side. Many of us leave our computers running for long periods or work remotely through another machine. An update installs, a restart becomes necessary, and suddenly you're dealing with a reboot at a very inconvenient time—sometimes while you're in the middle of working. Yes, Windows has Active Hours and restart controls ... but with updates arriving more frequently, managing those interruptions has become another part of the job.
