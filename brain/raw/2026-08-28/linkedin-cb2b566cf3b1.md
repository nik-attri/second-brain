I’m blessed to say that my summer internship with Xylem was a huge success. If you want the full details of everything I accomplished, I’d encourage you to visit my profile, but here’s the rundown of how I helped contribute up to $232,000 annual savings:

• In order to improve the crating process for our pumps, I designed an automated system that told our operators 1) the dimensions needed to build a crate and 2) an exact cut-list of the lumber needed to do so. This provided immediate time savings over the previous process of manually measuring pumps in order to package them.

• The same system also produced a final-item checklist that used the most up-to-date customer information, streamlining order completion.

• I implemented a standardized hoist-strap placement procedure, offering safety-enhancements and further time savings.

• Conducted time studies to evaluate current operator utilization and inform future process improvements.

It should already be clear, but I’ll be explicit: I learned a ton, delivered some goals that I’m extremely proud of, and otherwise had a fantastic internship with Xylem.

There are too many people to name, but I want to extend my thanks to everyone who helped make this internship great. That includes my coworkers in the office, who gave great advice and made great conversation: Dustin Wallace and Scott Going; the guys in crating, who showed me the ropes: Paul, Oscar, Tae, and James; my manager, who set me on the right path: Jed Thompson.

And an extremely special thanks to Chandler Wortham, who was continually supportive of my ideas, gave me the tools to succeed, and truly made this a fulfilling experience (thanks, Chandler, for using your laptop as a server while we wait for IT to permanently implement my system 😁).
