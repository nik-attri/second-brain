A Relevo item ID is built to remember everything. Every cycle, every handover, every wash, for years. The identifier a guest carries in our system is built to forget within 24 hours. That asymmetry is deliberate and it is the most interesting design decision in the whole circular identity model.

Item tracking is the hard engineering problem behind reuse. A reusable item costs more resources to produce than a single-use one, so it only prevents waste if it actually completes a lot of cycles. Nobody can really claim saving waste without tracking each item across its entire lifecycle. Tracking is what turns reuse from a nice sustainability story into something provable with actual impact.

Then you point the same word at a person and it stops being a virtue. Tracking people is questionable by default and a reuse platform is in an uncomfortably good position to do it. The same guest buys a coffee at their local café on Monday, eats in the company canteen at lunch, orders at a restaurant on Friday, using our app and scanning at each one. In our Relevo Open Pool System, we don’t charge guests anything upfront to make our reuse system free to use. But anyone holding a stable handle on that guest could stitch those visits into a profile without ever asking permission.

So our guest identifier, called Relevo ID, is designed to make that impossible rather than to promise it won’t happen. Every Relevo user has a 6-character ID. It recreates itself every 24 hours, and the user can regenerate it manually in our Relevo App whenever they want. It belongs to the Relevo user account itself and by design anonymizes the guest in our ecosystem.

What a partner gets at the moment of a scan is exactly what that moment needs: enough to complete a handover or a return, access-scoped to that one interaction. What a partner never gets is a key that still works next month. The guest comes back, the Relevo ID is different, and there is nothing to join the two visits on.

That is exactly what privacy by design buys: a reuse system that can prove everything about where its items have been, and nothing at all about where you have been.
