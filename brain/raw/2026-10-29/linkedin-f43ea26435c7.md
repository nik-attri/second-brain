I'm practising cold calling, so here's a simple example of how I would open a call to a potential customer.

Scenario: I'm calling a small business about appointment scheduling software.

"Hi Sarah, this is [Your Name] from [Company]. I know I'm calling unexpectedly, so I'll be brief.

We help small businesses simplify appointment scheduling and reduce the time spent managing bookings manually.

I'm curious: how are you currently managing your customer appointments?"

Why do I like this approach?

✅ It introduces the caller.
✅ It acknowledges the interruption.
✅ It explains the potential value.
✅ It ends with a question that invites a conversation.

Of course, the script would need to be adapted to the actual product, prospect, and reason for calling.

I'm learning that a script should be a guide, not a speech you deliver without listening.

Would you change anything about this opening?
