When is a public website no longer enough for a business?

A website is great for visibility: people can find you, understand what you do, and contact you. But at some point, business interaction goes beyond a contact form.

That is usually the moment when a personal account area or an internal portal starts making sense.

3 practical signs:

1. Users need to come back for ongoing actions
Not just to read about your company, but to check request statuses, documents, invoices, tasks, order history, or account-related information.

2. Core processes are scattered across email, chats, and spreadsheets
If your team manually clarifies statuses, searches for files in message threads, and duplicates data in several places, the workflow is already asking for a single working environment.

3. Different roles need different access
Clients, partners, managers, finance teams, and internal staff often need separate scenarios and permissions. A public website is not designed to handle that depth of interaction.

In simple terms:
A website is the outer layer.
A portal or account area is where real operational work begins.

This is also where CRM/ERP logic, integrations, structured data, documents, and support workflows become part of the product itself — not side tools around it.
