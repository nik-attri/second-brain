🏛️ TRUST AS INFRASTRUCTURE: WHO THE HELL DESIGNED THIS? #1

🎉 GOOD NEWS! THE ECONOMY IS BOOMING!
Apparently.
📈 GDP is growing!
💰 Corporate profits are up!
📊 Productivity is improving!
🥂 Champagne all round!

Meanwhile...
🏠 Young people are wondering whether owning a home is now a historical concept.
💸 Families are watching their living costs swallow their incomes.
👵 Older people are worrying about whether their savings will last.
👩‍💼 And people working full-time are wondering why getting ahead feels increasingly like running up a down escalator.

But never mind. THE ECONOMY IS DOING GREAT! 😂

Now, before my economist friends reach for their keyboards...
- Yes. Economic growth matters.
- Yes. Productivity matters.
- Yes. Businesses need to be profitable.

But here's my slightly inconvenient question.

WHAT THE HELL IS THE ECONOMY ACTUALLY FOR? 🤔

- Surely the economy is supposed to serve society.
- Not the other way around.

And if the indicators we celebrate are improving while large sections of society struggle to achieve financial security, perhaps we should question what we're measuring.

Or, more importantly, what we're designing our economic institutions to achieve.

Because here's what fascinates me.
- We spend enormous amounts of time debating how to grow the economy.
- But far less time asking whether the benefits of that growth are translating into better lives.
- And even less time examining whether our institutions are designed to make that happen.

That's a systems design problem. What if we started with a different question?

Not simply:
❌ How do we maximise economic growth?
But:
✅ What does a thriving society look like, and how should we design our economy to deliver it?
- Growth would still matter.
- Productivity would still matter.
- Business would still matter.

But they would be means to an end, not the end itself.

And we'd need to measure whether people were actually benefiting.

Because an economy can look successful on a spreadsheet while failing to deliver the opportunities and security people reasonably expect.

Perhaps it's time we stopped asking people to adapt to the economy and started asking whether the economy is designed to work for people.

That's what Trust as Infrastructure is about. Not another political ideology. Not another argument about left versus right.

But challenging the systems we've inherited and asking whether we could design them better.

Because if the system isn't delivering the outcomes we want, perhaps the problem isn't the people. Perhaps it's the design. 🔧
