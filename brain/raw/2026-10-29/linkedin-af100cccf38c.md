Here’s something I’ve been thinking about.

We’ll spend weeks analyzing a business. We’ll look at what’s working, what’s not, where the opportunities are, and what could potentially blow everything up.

We’ll build a strategy around those findings.

And then we’ll go home and make major life decisions based on stress, urgency, or whatever the hell is happening that week.

Why?

I’ve spent more than 25 years working with organizations on strategy, operations, and transformation. One of the tools I’ve used throughout my career is SWOT analysis.

It’s a simple way to assess where you stand: what you’ve got going for you, what’s holding you back, what opportunities exist, and what could get in your way.

And lately I’ve been asking myself: Why aren’t we using these same tools to manage our own lives?

Whether you’re considering a career change, starting a business, managing your finances, or figuring out your next chapter, understanding your current position matters.

I’m not suggesting we turn our lives into corporate projects. God knows we have enough spreadsheets and meetings already.

I’m saying we deserve to apply the same level of thought and intention to our own futures that we bring to the organizations we work for.

A mentor helped me recognize that the strategic work I’ve spent my career doing for organizations could be just as valuable to individuals.

So through Collazo & Co., I’m offering one-on-one strategy sessions to help people assess where they are, clarify what they want, and build a practical plan for moving forward.

No corporate jargon. No 50-page strategy deck. Just an honest assessment, a clear direction, and actionable next steps.

Because sometimes the problem isn’t that you don’t know what you want.

It’s that you haven’t had the opportunity to sit down, look at the entire picture, and figure out how to get there.

Have you ever done a SWOT analysis on yourself?

And if you’re at a crossroads and could use a strategic sounding board, send me a message. Let’s talk.
