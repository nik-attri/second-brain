The future of technology may be AI. But the future of leadership? Irresistibly human.

AI is giving us extraordinary capabilities. We can analyze, design, build, and innovate faster than ever before.

Amazing. I’m here for it. 🤗

But having the ability to do something and actually making it happen are two very different things.

Because even the most brilliant idea eventually runs into the wonderfully messy reality of humans.

People with opinions. Competing priorities. Fears. Brilliant insights. Bad days. And very legitimate questions about why the hell we’re changing everything again.

That’s where leadership gets interesting.

I’ve spent my career helping organizations navigate complex transformations, and one thing I know for certain:

You cannot spreadsheet your way into sustainable change.

You need the strategy. The processes. The systems. The tools. Absolutely.

But you also need discernment to see what’s really happening. Judgment to make hard decisions. Influence to bring people together. And the ability to create an environment where people feel safe, free, and supported enough to do difficult things.

Sometimes that looks like navigating a complicated executive decision.

Sometimes it means asking the uncomfortable question nobody else wants to ask.

And sometimes it looks like me doing a completely ridiculous celebratory dance on camera because my team just pulled off something extraordinary. 💃🏽🎉

Or literally and physically shaking off the frustration with them when we’re knee-deep in the muck.

Yes, I do both. Enthusiastically. Dance skills are questionable. Commitment is not. 

Being human at work isn’t a distraction from doing serious work. It’s part of how serious work gets done.

The laughter. The honesty. The silliness. The frustration. The willingness to be imperfect.

They create the connection and trust that allow people to speak up, challenge assumptions, take risks, make mistakes, and try again.

And here’s the thing about being irresistibly human: it’s not just about being authentic, warm, or wonderfully weird.

It’s about being extraordinarily good at what you do because you bring more of who you are to it.

The ability to move between strategy and intuition. Conviction and curiosity. Hard decisions and genuine compassion.

You might see me barefoot on the dance floor at an event. You certainly will at my desk. That doesn’t mean I’m any less capable of commanding the boardroom.

Your range is your competitive advantage.

As technology expands what’s possible, our humanity becomes even more consequential.

Because knowing what can be done is one thing. Knowing what’s worth doing, bringing people along, and making change actually last?

That’s leadership.

And we don’t have to sacrifice our humanity to do hard things.

Our humanity is exactly what makes the extraordinary possible. 🧡
