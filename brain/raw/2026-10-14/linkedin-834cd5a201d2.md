The most important screen in sales automation is not the final result. It is the screen before anything is sent.
At KIM Group, we are now using an AI-supported workflow for distributing individual offers. The current interface shows the prepared batch, message variables, the sending window, the recipient queue, a preview of the final message and the statuses we want to measure.
It also includes a simple safeguard: an automated mode should follow only after one complete batch has been checked manually from start to finish.
The system is working, but the AI still needs a lot of teaching before it can operate more independently. That is the less glamorous, but more useful, side of automation: testing the process, finding weak points and deciding what still needs human approval.
We do not yet have enough evidence to claim a measured time saving or a closed deal caused by this workflow. For now, the progress is the process itself becoming visible and controllable.
What must an automated sales workflow prove before you trust it?
