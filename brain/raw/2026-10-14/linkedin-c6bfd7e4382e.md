## ⚙️ 2. Practical example: A recurring pump-bearing failure
Consider three work orders over several months.
**Weak records:**
- WO 101: Bearing replaced.
- WO 184: Bearing replaced again.
- WO 267: Bearing damaged; replaced.
These records tell us that the component failed repeatedly. They do not explain why.
**Better records capture:**
- Bearing identification and observed damage
- Vibration readings and operating conditions, where available
- Lubricant condition, quantity, and application method
- Alignment and installation checks
- Actual labor-hours, materials, and downtime
- Inspection evidence, suspected mechanisms, and required follow-up
The reliability engineer can then compare the evidence and determine whether a formal root cause analysis is justified.
**Important:** Better records support root cause analysis; they do not replace it.
## 📈 3. Turn data quality into a maintenance improvement system
Use a simple control loop:
**CAPTURE → VALIDATE → ANALYZE → ACT → VERIFY**
- **Capture:** Make essential technical feedback part of the work process.
- **Validate:** Use consistent asset IDs, failure codes, units, and mandatory fields.
- **Analyze:** Review repeat failures, cost trends, labor variances, and bad actors.
- **Act:** Update PM tasks, job plans, operating practices, or equipment design when evidence supports the change.
- **Verify:** Confirm whether the corrective action reduced recurrence or improved performance.
## 🎯 4. Three questions every maintenance manager should ask
1. Can we trust our failure-code distribution?
2. Can we identify recurring failures without manually reading hundreds of work orders?
3. Can we trace a reliability improvement back to documented findings and verify the result?
If the answer is no, buying a more sophisticated CMMS may not solve the problem. Start by improving the quality and consistency of the information entered into the system.
**Remember: A closed work order records completion. A technically complete work order helps the organization learn.**
What is the biggest data-quality problem in your plant: incorrect failure codes, missing technical feedback, inaccurate labor records, or premature closure?
