Awaab’s Law could become one of the biggest operational headaches in property management. Or one of the biggest commercial opportunities for contractors who recognise what’s changing.

Think about what happens when a tenant reports a problem. They contact their agent, who contacts the landlord, who authorises the work, appoints a contractor and arranges access before anyone starts resolving the issue.

Throughout that process, we’re relying on calls, emails, spreadsheets and people remembering what they’re supposed to do, often across disconnected businesses.

Now introduce legally enforceable timescales for investigating and addressing certain housing hazards, and those inefficiencies become more than an inconvenience.

They become an operational and compliance risk.

Over a decade ago, working in social housing, we were exploring data integration and APIs allowing different organisations and systems to communicate. We understood that the biggest inefficiencies weren’t always in the physical work, but in everything between identifying a problem and resolving it.

Yet I’m astonished how many businesses still operate this way.

But here’s what people are missing.

For landlords and agents, integrated systems offer faster reporting, clearer accountability, automatic escalation, better records and less chasing.

For contractors, there’s an entirely different opportunity.

Imagine offering landlords and agents a service where defects are automatically captured, appointments coordinated, progress communicated and completed works evidenced, without manually chasing every stage.

You’re no longer another contractor quoting for repairs. You’re offering a reliable, accountable service, making you more attractive to organisations managing these risks.

That creates opportunities for recurring contracts, stronger relationships and better margins.

After three decades across construction, development and property, and now helping businesses integrate their systems, I’m convinced some of the greatest opportunities to improve profitability are hiding in processes we’ve accepted as normal.

Combine industry experience with intelligent automation and you don’t just save time. You remove friction, reduce risk, release capacity and create commercial opportunities.

Awaab’s Law currently applies to social landlords in England, but the operational lesson extends much further.

For some businesses, these changes will expose inefficient operations. For others, they’ll create an opportunity to become the contractor or service provider everyone wants to work with.
