We Get Used to Things We Shouldn't

TL;DR in emoji: 😬 + 🔁 + 🤷‍♀️ + 🛠️ + 😳 + 🪞 + 👀 = The Blind Spot™

Construction people are REALLY good at adapting. 😬

Schedule changes?

We'll figure it out.

Material delay?

We'll work around it.

Owner changed direction?

Cool. Everybody move.

It's genuinely one of the things I love about this industry.

We're professional problem solvers.

But there's a weird side effect to being exceptionally good at adapting:

🔁 Eventually, we can adapt to things we probably shouldn't.

A GC consistently pays late.

“That's just how they pay.”

Every Pay App turns into a scavenger hunt.

“That's just how this project is.”

One customer disputes every damn change order.

“That's just how they operate.”

A PM is carrying an absolutely ridiculous workload.

🤷‍♀️ “They can handle it.”

Maybe.

But I think “that's just how it is” might be one of the most expensive sentences in business.

Because once we've decided something is normal, we stop asking whether it should be.

Instead, we build workarounds. 🛠️

We adjust the process.

Manage around the customer.

Add another spreadsheet.

Ask the PM to carry one more thing.

And eventually the workaround becomes so familiar that we forget we're working around anything at all.

Here's the question that gets uncomfortable: 😳

If this happened for the FIRST TIME today, would you be okay with it?

Seriously.

Take the history out of it.

Take the relationship out of it.

Take the seven previous times you figured out how to survive it out of it.

Look at the condition as though someone just handed it to you today. 🪞

Would you say:

“Yeah. That's completely reasonable.”

Or:

“Wait... what the hell are we doing?”

Because familiar risk is still risk.

Sometimes the Blind Spot isn't something we can't see.

It's something we've been looking at for so long that it stopped looking unusual. 👀

The Blind Spot™

Okay, today's question: What have you normalized because dealing with it feels harder than working around it?
