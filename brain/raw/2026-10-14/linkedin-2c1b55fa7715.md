China has developed an AI-powered flying lifebuoy capable of reaching drowning swimmers up to four times faster than human lifeguards or rescue boats. Designed to bridge the critical first minutes of an aquatic emergency, this autonomous hybrid drone-buoy combines GPS tracking, thermal imaging, and smart computer vision to lock onto a struggling victim amidst chaotic waves. 

Upon deployment, it flies directly to the target at speeds reaching up to 47 km/h, lands accurately beside the person, and transforms into a buoyant flotation platform capable of keeping multiple adults afloat until human responders arrive.

Equipped to endure harsh coastal winds, high waves, and zero-visibility night conditions, these airborne rescue pods operate via automated launch stations integrated into coastal surveillance networks. 

The moment shore-based AI cameras detect unusual water movement or distress signs, the device self-launches within seconds without waiting for a manual operator. 

By eliminating delays caused by rough surf, physical fatigue, and shoreline navigation, the technology has already been credited with dozens of rapid, life-saving interventions along busy Chinese coastlines and riverways.
