I've always thought every engineer should be born with a software engineer by their side. Preferably included at birth. 😂
After almost a decade in automotive CAE, I've lost count of how many times I've thought, "I know exactly what I want to automate. I just wish I had a software engineer sitting beside me to build it!"
I've written plenty of Python scripts over the years, but building and maintaining an entire application? That's a different story.
And then came GPT-5.6!
Over the past couple of months, I've been developing a Python-based simulation data management and post-processing application for pedestrian protection for my team.
Roughly 30,000 lines of code. Hundreds of simulations. Automated result discovery, variant comparisons, injury assessments, interactive curve analysis and consumer rating calculations.
And here's the crazy part: I haven't manually written a single line of that code myself! 😂
The engineering logic, workflows, data architecture, requirements and validation are where my experience comes in. GPT-5.6 has been the software development partner I've always wished for.
But what amazes me most is the turnaround time!
Crashes, regressions, performance issues, refactoring, new features... things that would previously have taken me ages to figure out can now be investigated, tested and improved so much faster.
Of course, sometimes fixing one thing breaks three others. 😂
After years of using engineering software, being able to build something around the workflows I know so well has been incredibly rewarding.
And now that the data architecture is taking shape, I can't wait to start building some intelligence on top of it. There's so much more we could do with all that simulation data than just post-process it!
I still have a ridiculously long automation wish list. But suddenly, so much of it feels possible.
I hope you've all been experimenting with GPT-5.6 for coding. What have you managed to build?
