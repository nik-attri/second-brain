AI would have saved me hundreds of hours in 2011. I’m not sure I’d want all of them back. 🤔

In 2011 I spent a ridiculous number of nights fighting with Excel.

I wanted to automate things, so I kept building formulas.

Then the formulas broke.

I tried something else.

That broke too.

Sometimes I spent hours finding a mistake that looked completely obvious once I finally found it.

At the time I thought I was getting good at Excel.

I was, but something else was happening too.

I was slowly learning how logic works inside a system. How one thing depends on another. How a perfectly logical instruction can produce a completely stupid result because the instruction itself was wrong.

A few years later I was working on building a large ERP system from scratch.

There was no moment when I thought: “Good thing I spent all those nights in Excel.”

But looking back now, the connection is obvious.

A lot of the thinking I needed was already there.

Which makes today’s AI slightly uncomfortable for me.

If I had it in 2011, I could probably have described what I wanted and saved myself hundreds of hours.

And yes, a few years later AI might have helped me build the ERP too.

I’m not suggesting we make people suffer through bad spreadsheets for character development.

I’d happily take the AI.

But then we need to notice what else disappeared with the tedious work.

This isn’t only my nostalgia talking.

Reuters reported last week that more than a third of UK employers surveyed had cut entry-level opportunities over the past year, with AI and automation among the factors. https://lnkd.in/dcYN7V7j
Gartner found that 22% of CHROs had seen at least one business leader stop hiring for some entry-level roles because of AI automation.
https://lnkd.in/dgwCju7e
McKinsey & Company recently made the other half of the point: research, documentation, data cleanup, basic coding and preliminary analysis were also some of the tasks through which people developed judgment and expertise.
https://lnkd.in/dUts6n4m

I don’t think the answer is to preserve boring work.

But if AI removes the work and some of the learning was hiding inside that work, we need another way to create the learning.

I’m not sure most companies have built that part yet.
