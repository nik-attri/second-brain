You called the miss in April, with the workings attached. 
The room nodded... and carried on.

Then September arrived, the miss with it, and so did the question. 
Why didn't finance see this coming?

You did, but nobody in the room remembers that part.

Adam Coffey has sat through a lot of CFO changes across twenty-one years as a PE-backed CEO, and I put this to him on the new season opener of The Conscious Finance Podcast. 

Sometimes the CFO hasn't built the voice yet. 
Sometimes the problem is higher up and finance is talking to a wall. 
How do you tell them apart?

He started at the top. 
How does the CEO treat the office of finance? 
If the job is only keeping score, that's on the CEO, and he'll say so.

Then he stopped letting the CFO off the hook.
"I don't accept that as a final conclusion that as a CFO, I can be dry."

Keeping the trains running on time gets you some level of success, in his words. It won't elevate you. 

The second seat gets decided in his weekly staff meeting, watching whether the CFO offers an opinion on somebody else's area with the numbers behind it, or reports and goes quiet.

EY asked 1,600 finance leaders this year how their function is perceived across the business. 27% said strategic partner. 60% think the CFO should be shaping how the business creates value. 

That gap is what the episode is about.

This week's newsletter explores the diagnostic he uses, the scorecard-not-spreadsheet move, and what to hire for if the voice isn't yours.
