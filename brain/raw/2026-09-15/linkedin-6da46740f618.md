Most people think building an HR function starts with hiring. It doesn't. It starts with deciding what you refuse to compromise on.
When you set up people operations from zero — especially in a fast-scaling org — the temptation is to build everything at once. Policies, systems, rewards, engagement, all in parallel. That's how you end up with a lot of activity sitting on no foundation.

Here's the sequence that actually holds:
Make the basics unbreakable. Payroll, compliance, contracts. Nothing erodes trust faster than a wrong paycheck or a policy that doesn't hold up.

Then build hiring that scales. Structured enough that quality doesn't collapse the moment volume spikes.

Then put the systems in. HRMS before headcount makes manual impossible — not after you're already drowning.

Only then, the culture work. Engagement, rewards, development. It only lands when it sits on a stable base.

The mistake I see most often: teams start with the visible, exciting stuff — perks, culture decks — and skip the plumbing. But employees feel the plumbing first.

If you were building people ops from zero tomorrow, what's the one thing you'd get right before anything else?
