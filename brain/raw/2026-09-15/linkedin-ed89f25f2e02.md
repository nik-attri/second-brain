“We need a customer portal.”

Okay. What should your customers actually be able to do when they log in?

That question sounds obvious, but it changes the entire conversation.

A portal could let customers request quotes, download invoices, track orders, access documents, manage their account, view previous purchases, submit support requests or do dozens of other things.

Trying to build all of that at once can turn a useful project into an expensive collection of features that customers barely use.

A better starting point is to look at what customers already contact your team about.

What information are they constantly asking for?

What simple actions require an email, phone call or WhatsApp message?

What does your team repeatedly have to look up or send manually?

Those interactions are often a pretty good roadmap for what belongs in a customer portal.

We explored this in today's Cybernaut Studio article, including how to think about the first version of a portal and decide which features are actually worth building.
