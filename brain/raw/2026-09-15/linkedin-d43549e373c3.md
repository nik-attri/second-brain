*Ikke i morgen. Désolé, j'annule.* 


On the evening of 23 June, Britain came closer to the edge than on any other night this summer. Frequency sagged for the best part of an hour. Margin got thin. Just after eight, NESO asked a neighbour for emergency help.

And while all that was going on, Britain was exporting several gigawatts to the continent.

Three months on, I wanted to know what actually changed — and whether it worked. The questions I set out to answer:

→ NESO now restricts interconnector exports a day ahead, and again within the day. Which of those is really doing the work — and is it the one that made the news?

→ Does the notice period depend on who is on the other end? Do the Norwegians get a day's warning while the French get told as they're leaving the house? And if so, is that a choice, or an accident of contract vintage?

→ Britain's biggest single export cable isn't on the list of links these restrictions can touch — and nor is anything else on that border. Why not? And is it a choice, or can't it?

→ Compensation is payable on restricted capacity. So what did the summer cost, and is "cost" even the right word for it?

→ Ofgem published a note in May pointing at capacity hoarding by traders. Does everyone in the market accept that diagnosis? And had the regulator been watching the pattern rather longer than the note lets on?

→ Would any of this have helped on 23 June itself?

→ And the one that caught me out: is summer even the risky season? I'd assumed winter was all import, and that the export problem went away when it got cold. I pulled years of half-hourly flows to check. It doesn't.

There is an irony running underneath all of it. The market Britain spent this summer manually overriding is one Britain did more than any other country to build and to sell to Europe — and it was resisted, for years, by the neighbour we now cancel on most.

*Ikke i morgen. Désolé, j'annule.* — not tomorrow in Danish/Norwegian and sorry I'm cancelling in French. Britain has spent the summer doing a great deal of both, and its neighbours have noticed.
