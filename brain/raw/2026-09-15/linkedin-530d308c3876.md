Dubai has 47,000 homes scheduled for handover between now and December. The firm that counts them for a living expects 14,000 to 23,500...

That gap is most of what happened last month, and almost nobody put it next to the headline.

Here is the headline. August sales prices came in at AED 1,636 a square foot, down 1.7% on the year. First annual fall in five and a half years. Down 1.3% across three months. Cavendish Maxwell published it, the press coverage wrote itself, and within a day everyone agreed the supply wave had landed and the correction was here.

The supply wave hasn't landed. Dubai delivered 24,800 units in the whole of the first half. The second half schedule says 47,000, and the same firm that published the price fall also publishes what it expects to actually complete, which is 14,000 to 23,500, because that's what this market has historically managed against its own paperwork.

So prices fell against a number that hasn't happened.

Scheduled is a spreadsheet. Delivered is a set of keys.

Before anyone reaches for the "Dubai always overpromises" line, this isn't a Gulf disease. Every market prices the announcement first and the handover second, then acts surprised when the two refuse to agree. What's unusual here is that both halves of the gap were published in the same report, by the same people, on the same day, and the market read one of them.

Now think about what happens next in your own building. Somewhere an analyst is re-cutting an absorption curve this week. Call him Mr Downside Case. He is twenty-six, he has been at it since five this morning, and he has never once been thanked for a footnote. He'll pull the supply line straight off the completions schedule, because that's the number that exists in a table, and he'll hand you a revised year three void that looks beautifully conservative. Nobody in that room will ask which of the two numbers went into it. They'll ask whether the model is updated. It is. That's the problem.

Two months ago I wrote that Mr Payment Plan was being replaced by a man in a grey suit who reads footnotes for fun. He's still buying. August cleared AED 23.4 billion across roughly 10,900 homes, and three quarters of it was off plan, which means three quarters of last month's market also bought something that doesn't exist yet.

He isn't confused about that. He's priced it.

The question is whether the model on the other side of the table has. Because a model that takes 47,000 at face value isn't being prudent. It's being precise about the wrong number, and precision is the easiest thing in the world to mistake for rigour.

Khallas. Before you re-cut anything this quarter, ask Mr Downside Case which of the two numbers his absorption curve is reading. He'll know. Nobody has ever asked him.
