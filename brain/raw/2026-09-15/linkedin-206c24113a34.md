You can keep pouring water into a bucket.
But if the bucket has holes, adding more water won’t solve the problem.

The same thing happens with leads.
There is a difference between not getting enough leads and not managing the leads that come in.


Let’s look at a simple lead management process:


📌 Save this for when you're ready to improve your lead system.


Inquiry → Lead Capture → Qualification → Follow-up → Proposal → Conversion
It looks straightforward.

But when you look at what actually happens at each stage, the gaps start to show.


1. Inquiry
A potential customer sends a DM, fills out a form, sends an email, or calls.
The gap:
Inquiries come through different channels, so there is no single place to track them.


2. Lead Capture
The lead’s name, contact details, and requirements are recorded.
The gap:
When this is done manually, some leads never get recorded.


3. Qualification
The business checks whether the lead is a good fit.
The gap:
Without clear criteria, people qualify leads differently or skip the step completely.


4. Follow-up
The business contacts the lead again.
The gap:
Follow-up depends on someone remembering to do it.

Someone gets busy.
The follow-up gets forgotten.
The interested prospect goes cold.


5. Proposal
The qualified lead receives a proposal or quotation.
The gap:
If every proposal is prepared from scratch, the process slows down and follow-ups become inconsistent.


6. Conversion
The lead agrees to buy.
But by this point, some leads have already disappeared.
Not because people weren’t interested.

They disappeared through small gaps in the process:
→ A missed inquiry
→ A lead that wasn’t recorded
→ Unclear qualification
→ A forgotten follow-up
→ A delayed proposal
→ A proposal with no follow-up


And this is why process analysis matters.

Instead of asking:
“Why aren’t we getting enough customers?”

Ask:
“At which stage are potential customers dropping off, and what is happening there?”


Don’t look at your leads only as numbers.
Look at the process behind the numbers.
Find the stage where leads are dropping off.
Then fix that stage.

Better lead management starts with a better process.
