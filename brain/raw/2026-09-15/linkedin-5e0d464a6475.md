Recently, I came across a post where someone's spouse was discussing how their partner found out that after twenty years, their position was removed and they had been laid off.

Someone else, in their attempt to provide likely a well-meaning message also included a line about self-reliance and "not being a problem for someone else to solve."

That struck others as a bit much to lay on the poor fella that just lost their job. Some third party chimes in about "not sugarcoating" advice.

Here's what I see as the truth of it. Social media puts us in an interesting and, likely, tone-deaf space. This is a common issue with other text-based communication where intent is lost. See https://lnkd.in/gGdDxPk3 from My Cousin Vinny for the example.

The fella that just lost his job didn't need advice right then on what he should have been doing. There's a time and place for that and it isn't the day you lose your job. No more than any of us need the fake recruitment bots looking to sell us a "resume review" or some other bullspit when we announce that we're looking for work.

To those of you that get "reorganized" out of an organization without so much as a reach around, there's a good chance you didn't do anything that warranted it. You may have simply been a target of convenience on someone's Profits and Expenses spreadsheet. Especially if no one bothered to ask you on what you were working. Hell, I was working on making transitions to my last company's SaaS platform faster and cheaper for everyone, Company and Customers, and not a soul asked me or my leadership nor did they bother reaching out to ask whether there was some other position in the company to which I'd continue providing expertise.

So, to the newly-unemployed, it likely wasn't you, it was accounting. That doesn't mean you aren't valuable or that you didn't pour your heart into it. It may simply mean that it didn't matter to the folks counting beans because they weren't in a position to evaluate what you were contributing in the first place.

Secondly, make sure you sit with it for a moment before you throw yourself at another organization. I sincerely hope you have the opportunity and resources that allow you to take stock in yourself, your worth, and begin making the case on the value you bring to any other organization. 

Lastly, I don't have any jobs to give and I'm still looking forward to paying next month's mortgage as well, but if you need props, I've some time on my hands. You can be a rock star and if I can help with a word of encouragement, I believe in you, too.
