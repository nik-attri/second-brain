Who would have thought we'd get to a point SO SOON where you could literally talk to an AI and it starts pulling your video clips together, cutting where you tell it to cut, adding the transition you asked for, adjusting the pacing, all without you touching a single button yourself. That's where we are right now, and honestly, watching it happen in real time over these past few weeks has kept me up thinking about what filmmaking is actually going to look like in the next couple of years.

I remember when every single shot had to be built by hand, close shot, medium shot, wide shot, all of it planned out and captured and pieced together on a timeline manually, because there simply wasn't another way to do it. That was just the job. Nobody was skipping any part of that process. Now you sit down, write a treatment, which really is just your idea or your script with enough context around it, characters, environment, how the story moves from one beat to the next, and you hand that to an AI agent and it starts building the thing out for you. If you're someone like me who prefers writing the script yourself first, you just feed that straight into the agent and let it work from your actual words instead of some vague prompt.

InVideo's first agent was already solid when it came out, but their second one, Agent Two, is on a completely different level, and part of why it's this good is because it's plugged into some of the strongest video generation models available right now, including Seedance 2.5, which honestly might be the most precise AI video tool out there at the moment for keeping characters and scenes consistent shot after shot, which is usually where a lot of these tools fall apart.

But do you know the part that I like? 

InVideo now has a full timeline editor inside the same platform, built close to how Premiere Pro's timeline works, and you can bring in clips you've already shot, or clips you just generated, or generate new ones right there on the spot, and then you turn to the agent and tell it what to do. Cut this part, add a transition here, and fix the motion graphics on that title. And it just does it.

I'll be honest though, this editor is still new, which means this is the roughest version of it we're ever going to see, and it's only going to get sharper from here. 
InVideo isn't the only one building in this direction either. Other AI tools are moving toward this exact kind of workflow, and I've been quietly testing a few of them myself, so I'll share more on that as I go.

 I've been fully in my class, teaching. And I always teach it from two angles. First, the manual way which I talked about in the first paragraph of this write-up,  — actual camera handling, lighting, angles, composition, because understanding that foundation is what makes you a filmmaker in the first place, not just someone pressing generate.
