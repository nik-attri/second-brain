A year ago, "using AI to code" meant autocomplete suggestions.

Today, it means having a collaborator that understands context, not just syntax.

As a Computer Science student, I've watched this shift happen in real time — and honestly, it's changed how I think about learning to build.

I used to believe you had to write every line yourself to really "know" a technology. Now I think differently. The real skill isn't typing code manually — it's knowing what to build, why it matters, and how to direct the tools that get you there fast.

Development activity across the industry backs this up. Pull requests and commits are climbing at record rates, and a big part of that growth is AI-assisted work. The developers thriving right now aren't the ones resisting this shift — they're the ones learning to work with AI as a genuine partner in the process.

I'm still early in this journey. But I'd rather learn to build in the world that's actually here, not the one from five years ago.

Curious how others see this — is "coding" itself being redefined, or just the tools we use to do it?
