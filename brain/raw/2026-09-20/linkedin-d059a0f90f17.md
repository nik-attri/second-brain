I’ve spent my career looking for friction in operations.

Where are we touching something twice?
Where are people doing work a system should be doing?
Where are we relying on memory instead of process?
Where are we losing capacity without realizing it?

AI hasn’t changed how I operate.

It has changed how fast I can fix those things.

I’m building spreadsheets, databases, workflows and automations faster than I ever could before. Processes that would have required outside developers, weeks of work, or simply stayed on the wish list can now be tested and implemented quickly.

But the technology isn’t the advantage.

Knowing what to fix is.

A tool can help build the system. It still takes an operator to walk the process, find the constraint, understand the people doing the work, and decide what should be automated and what shouldn’t.

After 25+ years in operations, that part hasn’t changed.

The tools just got a hell of a lot better.
