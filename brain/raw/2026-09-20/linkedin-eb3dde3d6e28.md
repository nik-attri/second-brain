🇮🇹🇨🇦🇵🇱🕺🏻💃🏻CTV News #Kappa x TVP WORLD x #Puro #Euro #Dance x Ryerson University Maybe this is why some of us developed such a ridiculous affection for Toronto.

We didn’t just go to university in Toronto.

We went to school in the middle of the damn thing.

For those of us who lived that era, it was Ryerson University. That’s not disrespect toward anybody and it isn’t an argument about what the institution is called today. It’s simply the name attached to our memories.

Walk out of class and you were immediately in downtown Toronto.

Yonge Street. Dundas Square. Eaton Centre. Concerts. Street performers. Restaurants. Clubs. Radio promotions. Fashion. People from practically every culture on Earth walking past you before you made it to the next intersection.

Then Friday came.

You had survived assignments, exams, work shifts and whatever other responsibilities were eating your week alive.

And suddenly Toronto belonged to you again.

Cherry Street. The Docks. Z103.5 blasting dance music. Fresh Kappa tracksuits. Diesel watches. Everybody somehow knowing somebody who knew somebody. Italians, Greeks, Poles, Portuguese, Caribbean Canadians, South Asians, East Asians, Black Canadians, Jewish Canadians, Muslims, Christians, people who couldn’t care less about any label at all.

You danced together anyway.

That was the education outside the classroom.

People sometimes call U of T the intellectual centre of Toronto. Queen’s Park has the government. Bay Street has the money.

But my Toronto had another centre.

That intersection around Yonge and Dundas felt like somebody had plugged the entire city into an electrical socket.

And we were lucky enough to go to school inside it.

Maybe that’s why I still believe Toronto works best when we stop retreating into our respective little corners and actually meet one another.

Dinner.

Music.

A ridiculous 80s party.

A nightclub.

A handshake.

A conversation with somebody you’ve walked past for twenty years but somehow never met.

No corporate networking exercise. No demographic spreadsheet. No manufactured bullshit.

Just Toronto doing the thing Toronto has always been capable of doing:

Different people. Same city. One hell of a night.

And if you lived that era too—

you already know.
