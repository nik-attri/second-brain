Everyone is shipping AI chatbots. The financial firms that actually fixed onboarding did something different.

A mid-size financial services firm (anonymized) was drowning in manual document work. Every new client meant KYC checks, ID and document verification, invoices and contracts, all keyed by hand across systems that did not talk to each other. Onboarding stretched from days into weeks.

The instinct is to bolt a model onto the front end. We did the opposite.

We built one orchestrated agentic operating layer. Specialized agents, each owning a single job, an intake agent that reads and classifies every document, an extraction agent, a verification agent that checks watchlists and rules, a monitoring agent that flags anomalies with evidence. Above them, an orchestrator that routes the work, enforces the business rules, and pulls in a human only on real exceptions.

Then we built it to survive production. Gartner expects around 40 percent of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by 2027, and the usual cause is not the model. It is cost, unclear value, and weak risk controls. So audit trails, human-in-the-loop on exceptions, and governance were in from day one, not bolted on later.

What systems like this deliver: 95 percent plus of documents processed straight through, up to 99 percent extraction accuracy, up to 70 percent lower cost, onboarding from days to minutes. (Industry benchmarks. Client figures anonymized.)

The best part: the pattern transfers. Change the documents and the same orchestrated shape works in insurance, logistics, anywhere document heavy work lives.

The model is rarely the hard part. Orchestration is.
