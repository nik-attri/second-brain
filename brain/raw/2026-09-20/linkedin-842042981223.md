I still remember my first day at Google. Spring 2007, Mountain View, California. I remember walking across the campus in the morning sun and feeling like I was in a giant playground for adults. Endless supply of gourmet food. Free onsite gym, pool, hairdresser, you name it. Funny colorful bikes to move from one building to another. And the « don’t be evil » Kool-Aid, always present. 

A few years later, the motto quietly disappeared. Big Tech has been conducting some of the most brutal, inhumane mass layoffs. So much for employee well-being. In case you didn’t know by now, you are indeed just a number on a spreadsheet. 

I left a few months after the first mass layoff at Google. The magic was gone. But I’ve been through nine others since then - « coordinating » a process that hurt every cell in my body. And in one instance, writing my own termination letter. At least, I can say that we cared. I cared. I hope that every person whom I shared the bad news with knows how much I cared. I remember all of them. They still haunt me. 

The one thing I know now that I didn’t know then: in this day and age, most of us will go through this experience at one point or another. You’ll be on either side of the table. It has nothing to do with your individual performance or potential. It’s just « structural adjustments », and you’re part of that machine. 

If you’re the one delivering the news, remember this video. Remember that you’re dealing with real people, real impact, real pain. Have the courage to face that pain. Help if you can help - by being present, reviewing a resume, making an introduction. At the very least, show as much respect and empathy as you can muster. 

And if you’re on the receiving end, remember that you’re not alone. It hurts. But you are valuable, and stronger than you know. You will bounce back. 

« If you’re going through hell, keep going. » (Winston Churchill)
