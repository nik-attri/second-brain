AI should be putting me out of business.

I’m a Process Architect. I help companies figure out how work is actually getting done...especially the work living in people’s heads, workarounds, side conversations and all the little things everyone “just knows” to do. 

I call that the Invisible Work.

And now AI can take a conversation, meeting transcript or ten minutes of someone talking and turn it into an SOP, checklist and workflow before I could probably finish writing the title.

Well, shit. It was a good run.

Because if my job was simply documenting your process, AI absolutely should replace me. Explain what happens, hit enter and boom...beautifully organized SOP. Headers, steps, responsibilities, checklist. 

Except one of the questions I ask over and over in my workshops is...“How does that happen today?”

That’s when the clean version usually starts falling apart.

“It depends…”

“Well…”

“Usually…”

“Technically…”

Now we’re getting somewhere.

Because “Sales hands it to Production” sounds like a process. But keep digging and you find out Production checks everything first because information is usually missing. Or someone keeps a spreadsheet because they don’t trust the software. Or the owner gets involved whenever something is unusual. Or “it depends who sold the job.”

That’s the part most companies skip.  And that my friends is what eats your profitability. 

They go from “we have a process problem” straight to “we need to document our process.” AI has just made it ridiculously easy to make that jump faster.

But the stuff in between is where the real process lives.  The workarounds, judgment calls, hidden approvals, unofficial owners and exceptions that happen so often they probably stopped being exceptions years ago.

That’s the Invisible Work.

So yes, let AI write the SOP. I have absolutely no emotional attachment to formatting process documents for the rest of my career.

But first, somebody still has to keep asking “How does that actually happen today?” until the invisible work becomes visible.

Then find the friction. Understand why it’s happening. Fix the process.

Then let AI document the hell out of it.

AI isn’t replacing what I do.

It’s making the difference between process documentation and process improvement painfully obvious.

AI can document what you give it. My job is finding the stuff nobody thought to give it.
