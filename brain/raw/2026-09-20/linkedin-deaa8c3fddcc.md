I hired a new rep and watched her spend the first month in product training she'd forget by week three.

Meanwhile, our best reps were closing deals in their second month. The difference wasn't talent. It was onboarding design.

Most companies treat onboarding like an HR checkbox. They start with company history, then product specs, then hope reps figure out how to sell. That's backward.

The data is brutal: 62% of companies admit their onboarding programs don't work. But here's what changed everything for us at evergreensales.group: we mapped curriculum backward from the first deal a rep would actually close, not from what looked good in a manual.

We cut ramp time from 5.7 months to 2.8 months. That's $3.5 million in additional ARR for a typical SaaS company.

Three things drove it:

• Curriculum design - Start with messaging and discovery frameworks in week one, not product details. New reps who can diagnose problems land their first deal faster than reps drowning in spec sheets.

• Mentorship architecture - Stop hoping experienced reps will mentor informally. Pair new hires with a structured knowledge transfer system. Tacit knowledge dies unless you make it explicit.

• Early win structure - Design their first deal to win. Pick accounts where they can close fast and build confidence. Momentum matters more than complexity at the start.

Most teams only tackle one of these. All three working together cut your ramp time in half.

What's your current onboarding actually producing? How long before your new reps hit quota?
