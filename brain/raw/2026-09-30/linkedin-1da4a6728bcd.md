Most incumbents I meet have good AI use cases. Very few have an AI north star. And the ones who do should look again, because the obvious one is usually wrong.

For anyone running a large operation, the default north star writes itself: automate the process. Faster, cheaper, more consistent. I lead claims, and I could have written that goal in an afternoon.

Then I spent time with the companies building the frontier, and a pattern emerged. The most interesting ones don't run our processes faster. They don't run our processes at all. The workflow an incumbent has spent decades optimizing is, to them, not an input. It is simply absent.

That changes the question. Not: how far can we automate what we do today? But: what would this business look like if it had been built with AI from day one, and how much of what we do today survives that answer?

That is a harder north star, and a more uncomfortable one. It doesn't fit on a roadmap slide. It asks an organization to question itself, starting from what the customer actually values rather than from its own processes, and to admit which parts of the value chain are load-bearing and which are merely familiar.

I don't think it leaves the incumbent hopeless. We hold the trust, the data, the expertise, and the obligations a regulated business carries. But a north star of "automate the process" quietly assumes the process is the asset. It isnt. Trust is. The harder north star is the one that starts there. Most roadmaps start somewhere else.
