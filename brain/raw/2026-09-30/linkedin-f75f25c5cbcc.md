You've been saying "someday" about quitting for so long, you can't tell if it's wisdom or fear anymore.

☕ I lived there. Far more in the fear than in the wisdom.

🎙️ I recently sat with Morenike Ogebe on Elevating Good, a podcast for women of faith. I shared my faith, the good, the bad, and the ugly. 

Being a Christian didn't mean I was always faithful. Here's some of what I said:

😰 The fear I felt was rooted in my belief that success was either due to being lucky or corrupt.

That fear was born at a kitchen table 🍽️

My earliest money memory is watching my mother decide which bills to pay. Nobody said a word. 

But I learned success was outside of mine and, if I'm honest, God's control.  It was due to what I felt I had control over.

I didn't just learn something about money. I learned I couldn't move the needle.

So even with real success, I felt like I was on the lucky end 🍀

When God called me out of corporate, I resisted.

You've heard it: "God doesn't call the equipped, He equips the called."

Then I had to live it, and boy, that catchphrase got hard 😳

A performance review from hell gave me the shove 😉 I truly believe God knew I would never leave by surrendering. I was going to leave on a shove.

🚪 I was obedient to start the business. But I was disobedient in how I built it. My insecurity and fear overrode my trust in Him.

I struggled for years. Some years I looked great on a spreadsheet and felt a discontent I couldn't shake 📊

🙏🏿 Nothing changed until I surrendered.

That's when the business began to shift. My revenue did.

And so did my peace.

🕊️ I was no longer breaking what I was building. I was working from a place of peace and rest.

Quitting and starting a business is far more than a resignation letter. It's an identity shift at work, in life, even in your faith.

🧭 And when you plan around an identity you've never lived in, blind spots creep in. The last thing I want is for one of them to get in the way of your calling.

Thank you, Morenike Ogebe, JD 🤍, for the wonderful conversation.
