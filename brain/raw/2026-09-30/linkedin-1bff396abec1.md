I think Nutter Butters’ marketing team has completely lost its mind.

And I’m kind of obsessed with it.

If you haven’t looked at their social media lately, go down the rabbit hole.

It’s weird.

Like REALLY weird.

Cryptic messages. Strange characters. Uncomfortable videos. Storylines that make absolutely no sense unless you’ve been following along, and honestly, sometimes they still don’t make sense.

It feels like someone handed the social media password to the weirdest person in the marketing department and said, “Do whatever you want.”

And that might be exactly why it works.

Think about what Nutter Butter is selling.

A peanut butter cookie that has been around forever.

They could easily be posting polished product photos with captions like, “The perfect afternoon snack!”

Nobody cares.

Instead, they created something people actually talk about.

As a marketer, THAT is the part I find fascinating.

We’ve spent years teaching brands to be polished, consistent and safe.

But social media changed.

People don’t follow brands because they want advertisements in their feed. They follow brands that entertain them, teach them something or give them a reason to pay attention.

And now AI is making average content even easier to create.

Pretty graphics? Easy.

Captions? Easy.

Videos? Getting easier every day.

Which means the internet is about to be absolutely flooded with perfectly acceptable content.

And perfectly acceptable is going to become invisible.

The brands that stand out will be the ones willing to actually have a personality.

That doesn’t mean your accounting firm needs to start posting haunted spreadsheets.

But it does mean brands need to stop being terrified of being interesting.

Nutter Butter could have stayed a cookie your parents remember buying.

Instead, they somehow turned a peanut butter sandwich cookie into internet lore.

That is one hell of a marketing lesson.
