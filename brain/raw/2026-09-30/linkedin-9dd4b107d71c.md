Episode 1 werewolf romance

“You are going to write a werewolf-themed story with a 40k word count. The pay is going to be 1 naira per word.”
—Client

Upon seeing this text, the only thing that stuck with me was: ₦40,000.

At the time, it was the largest sum I would have earned from a writing project.

I readily agreed.

But.

I had never written a book before.

I bore the same notion that most avid readers do: that having read a lot of books would most likely translate into being able to write one.

Also, I’d watched a lot of popular paranormal romance shows.

Then the client sent me two files.
A Word document file and an Excel spreadsheet.

I opened the spreadsheet first, got confused, and closed it back. 

The Word document file was more approachable.

It contained instructions and blanks I had to fill out:

Character profiles.
Background stories.
Eye colours.
Behaviours.
Plot summary.
Beginning, middle and ending.
Chapter outlines. etc.

And then I encountered something I'd never thought about before.

The first line had to have a hook.

Wait.

I had to convince someone to keep reading from the very first sentence?

No pressure.

That was when it started dawning on me that this wasn't going to be easy money.

There was actual work involved.

What the hell was a denouement?

How was I supposed to communicate an entire chapter in five lines?

I did the only thing anyone in my situation would do: I ran to YouTube.

I watched videos with titles like:

“How to Hook Your Readers From the Very First Page!”

I read writers' blogs. I studied opening chapters. I tried to figure out what the hell I was doing.

Eventually I dove in.

A sizzling romance between an Alpha and a werewolf huntress.

We worked in 20,000-word milestones.
I finished the first milestone and submitted it feeling incredibly proud of myself.

The client was displeased.

Too much narration. 
Too much telling instead of showing.
Info dumping.

Basically, I’d made almost every rookie mistake possible.

I was asked to rewrite the entire thing.

So I wasn’t some natural-born writing prodigy after all?

I swallowed my ego started again. 

This time, I kept every piece of feedback in my head.

Then a response came. 

This is much better. Please carry on with the last milestone.

The validation that comes from an editor approving your work is a different kind of dopamine rush.

I finished the book and waited for my ₦40,000.

Instead, I received revisions. There were plot holes to fix.

I fixed them.

I was told, “Good job,” and then nothing. 

My very first writing client ghosted me, and I never got paid.

But something else had happened.

I’d enjoyed the challenge.

That thrill of having my writing critically assessed torn apart and then approved had done something to me.

Not long after, I got invited to another gig. This time I got paid.

I’ve ghostwritten many books since then.

But you never forget your first.

Then a challenge came. Something completely different.
