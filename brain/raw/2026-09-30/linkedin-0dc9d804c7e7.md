The entry-level market is tighter than many candidates realise.

Recent UK hiring data shows entry-level Data Analyst hiring down 15% year-on-year, while employers are increasingly favouring candidates who can show more operational, job-ready capability rather than only foundational tools. 

That does not mean you need five years of experience for a junior role.
But it does mean “I know SQL, Python and Power BI” is becoming a weaker way to differentiate yourself.

What employers increasingly need to see is evidence that you can:
➝ work with messy or imperfect data rather than only clean course datasets
➝ check data quality, definitions and assumptions before trusting the output
➝ explain why you chose an approach instead of only showing the final dashboard
➝ connect analysis to a business question or decision
➝ use AI and modern tools without becoming dependent on them for the reasoning

The interesting part of the latest UK data is the skills mismatch.

For Data Analyst roles, foundational skills such as SQL, Python, Power BI and Tableau appear relatively abundant, while employers report shortages around areas such as data governance, data quality, data engineering and cloud capability. 

So if you are preparing for your first role, the answer may not be another certificate.
It may be stronger evidence that you can operate in the kind of environment the job will actually put you in.
