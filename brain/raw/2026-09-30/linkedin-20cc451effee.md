Scared. Angry. And incredibly fired up.

That’s how I felt yesterday morning, sitting at one of the world’s energy meccas: Energy Disruptors: UNITE in Calgary.

For 200 years, we’ve learned to optimize:

Produce where it’s cheapest.
Cut inventory.
Remove redundancy.
Specialize.
Outsource.
Higher ROI. Higher IRR.

And we became incredibly good at it.

Maybe too good.

Peter Tertzakian put it brilliantly:

“We have optimized to the point of vulnerability.”

Because our spreadsheets can calculate financial returns down to the decimal.

But where do we calculate:
Return on resilience.
Return on energy security.
Return on sovereignty.

We don’t.

And the world is reminding us why that matters.

War has made energy security brutally real again. Global supply chains have shown us what happens when one critical link breaks.

And now AI is adding another layer.
AI needs compute.
Compute needs data centres.
Data centres need energy.

A LOT of energy.

The AI race is an energy race.

And this is where I get angry.
Because while we’ve been perfecting spreadsheets, quarterly returns and ever-leaner organizations, the definition of competitive advantage may have been changing underneath our feet.

But it’s also where I get incredibly excited.

Because perhaps it’s time to rethink what good business actually means.
Maybe redundancy isn’t waste.
Maybe resilience isn’t inefficiency.
Maybe energy isn’t simply a cost line.
Maybe the cheapest system isn’t the strongest system.
And maybe the next generation of winners won’t be those who optimized the hardest.

They’ll be the ones who understood what was worth protecting before everyone else did.

One keynote into #UNITE2026, one question is ringing in my head:

WHAT THE HELL ARE WE OPTIMIZING FOR?

I have a feeling I’ll be carrying that question with me for the next two days.

And probably much longer.

I’ll be sharing what challenges me, scares me and gets me fired up from Calgary over the next two days.

Stay with me.
