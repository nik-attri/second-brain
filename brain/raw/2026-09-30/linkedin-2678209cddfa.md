I've spent 10+ years in QA, and here's something I keep seeing:
Teams hire for one or the other.

 ➡️ "We need an automation engineer."
 ➡️ "We need a manual tester."

Meanwhile, the most critical bugs slip through the gap between the two.
Automation is brilliant. It gives you fast regression, stable pipelines and confidence on every release. But it only checks what someone already thought to check.

The bug that costs you a customer, a compliance breach or a production incident? It's rarely in the scripted path. I've found it by:

🔍 Asking "what if the user does this instead?"
 🔍 Testing the riskiest areas first, based on business impact rather than test-case count
 🔍 Following a hunch in an exploratory session that no script would ever cover

The QA who can do both is a force multiplier:
 ✅ Automates the repetitive, high-volume checks, so the team ships faster
 ✅ Uses risk-based thinking to decide what's worth testing manually
 ✅ Spends the freed-up time hunting for critical, high-impact defects
 ✅ Can explain the business risk of a bug, not just the steps to reproduce it

🔵 The goal was never 100% automation. It's catching the right bugs before your customers do.
🔵 If your QA strategy is "automate everything" or "manually check everything," you're probably lagging on both.
🔵 The best testers I know think like users, break things like attackers and automate like engineers.
