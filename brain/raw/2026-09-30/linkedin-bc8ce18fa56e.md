Requirements gathering has become a strange ritual

Put ten people in a workshop, ask what they need from the new ERP system, write everything down, and apparently you have gathered the requirements.

If only.

People are usually very good at describing what they do. They are less likely to explain why they do it.

“We need to manually change the posting date.”

Why?

“Because Finance sometimes asks us to.”

Why?

“Because the period is already closed.”

Now we are getting somewhere.

The original request was functionality. The underlying issue may be process timing, ownership, controls, or simply a workaround that has survived long enough to become accepted practice.

This distinction matters because every requirement has a cost. It becomes configuration, development, testing, training, security, and eventually support.

Good requirements gathering therefore requires some curiosity before documentation.

Understand the condition that creates the requirement. Understand how often it occurs and what would happen without it.

Then decide what the system actually needs to do.

Otherwise, you may successfully reproduce yesterday’s problems in tomorrow’s ERP.

At least they will be in the cloud.
