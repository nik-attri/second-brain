You know that moment when you’re reading something completely unrelated, a name catches your attention, and suddenly you need to know who the hell this person is?

And then, 45 minutes later, you have 17 tabs open, three corporations, two addresses, an appellate opinion, a campaign contribution, a property record, and absolutely no memory of how you got there?

I’m building something for that. 😂

It’s called Beagle. 🐾

Give it a person, organization, address, case, domain, document, or whatever tiny thread made your investigative brain go, “…wait a minute.”

Then follow it.

Beagle is being designed to help you find connections, chase rabbit holes, flag inconsistencies, discover what you haven’t checked yet, and preserve the trail back to the original source.

No case file required. No commitment that any of it means anything yet. No spending six hours manually searching twelve different places just to figure out whether the weird thing you noticed is actually weird.

Just discovery.

But there’s a much bigger idea behind Beagle.

Serious investigative capability is still too expensive, too fragmented, too time-consuming, and too inaccessible. The best tools are often built for large organizations with large budgets, while journalists, independent investigators, attorneys, researchers, nonprofits, small newsrooms, and other people doing serious work are left stitching together searches, spreadsheets, PDFs, browser tabs, and sheer stubbornness.

AI gives us an opportunity to change that.

My goal is to make serious investigative capability dramatically cheaper, faster, and more accessible without lowering evidentiary standards.

Not AI that spits out an answer and expects you to trust it.

AI that helps you find the trail, shows you where the information came from, catches conflicts, suggests the next place to look, and lets you verify what you’ve found.

Because faster research should not mean sloppier research.

And sometimes an investigation doesn’t begin with a case number, a source, or some grand hypothesis.

Sometimes it begins because you’re on page 73 of a completely unrelated PDF at 2:48 in the morning and suddenly think:

Who the eff IS this person?

🐾 Follow the trail.

Beagle is coming.
