I am officially in distribution hell. 

The hardware distribution industry continues to punch me in the face. 

I am not requesting access codes to a nuclear launch facility. I'm not applying for security clearance at the White House. I'm trying to buy a server. 

Somehow that requires: 
Three distributors 
Four portals 
Five spreadsheets 
Six requests for the exact same information 
Zero accountability 

Every order feels like I'm being punk'd. 
Fill out a spreadsheet. 
Wait. 
Get asked for the same information again. 
Wait. 
Be told someone will call you. 
Wait. 
Nobody calls. 
Follow up. 
Get forwarded to someone else in a brand new department. 
Repeat until morale is gone. 

The worst part isn't even the delay. 

The worst part is standing in front of my clients trying to explain why their project is stalled because a billion-dollar supply chain apparently runs on spreadsheets, shared inboxes, and broken promises. 

I've tried being patient. I've tried being polite. I've tried being firm. I've tried escalating to "managers". 

On Monday, I found myself arguing with a distributor while dragging luggage through an airport, convinced that this would finally be the conversation that moved things forward. Narrator: It was not. Instead of getting traction, I just became the crazy woman yelling about hardware at Gate A12. 

At this point, the distribution model feels like a social experiment to determine how much chasing and begging a customer will do before completely losing their mind. 

The most impressive part? When everything goes sideways, I'm still the one accountable to my clients. 

Honestly, I'm starting to think I've found my next business opportunity. Maybe the answer isn't finding a better distributor. Maybe the answer is becoming one. Because there is absolutely no reason buying a damn server should be harder than deploying it. 😑
