A manufacturing manager at a crop protection company told us, back in 2024, that she couldn't say whether she was getting the best cost of goods on anything. "I kind of feel like I'm doing it with one hand tied behind my back."

Then the part that stuck with me. She'd had two sharp interns who knew Python and could mash all the data together. "I hope to hell that Python code never goes bad, because I can't fix it."

I've heard a version of that in more rooms than I can count. The data exists. Someone clever stitched it together. Now the operation runs on a script or a spreadsheet that one person understands, and everyone quietly hopes nothing changes.

It isn't a technology problem, exactly. Every one of these companies owns an ERP. It's that the decision, the actual buy, the actual routing call, needs data from outside that ERP, and the only way anyone found to combine the two was by hand.

That's the job we see across the manufacturers we work with. Not replacing the system. Not replacing the person. Turning the combination the person built by hand into something the operation owns.

If your best analysis lives in one person's Python, it's worth knowing that before they give notice.
