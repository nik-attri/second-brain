I once rejected a document because the numbering wasn’t automated.

Yes. You read that right.

Someone on my team had submitted a document, and the numbering had been done manually.

Which meant that every time someone needed to insert a new section, move something around or make changes to the document, they’d have to manually update the numbering.

And I remember thinking, why?

Why are we creating additional work for ourselves when Microsoft Word can literally handle this?

Now, you might think that’s a very small thing to be particular about.

But this is exactly the kind of thing that bothers me, because it’s never just about the numbering. It’s about how easily we accept unnecessary work as part of doing our jobs.

We create documents from scratch when we could use templates, manually update information that could update automatically, spend hours repeating tasks without ever stopping to ask whether there’s a better way to do them.

And then we complain about how much work we have.

I am a very lazy person. I’ve said this before, and I’ll keep saying it. But my version of laziness is not wanting to do the same unnecessary work over and over again.

If I’m going to spend time doing something, I’d rather do it in a way that doesn’t create more work for me tomorrow even if it takes more time today.

And I think that’s something more people, especially those of us managing teams, need to pay attention to.

A process that requires unnecessary effort every single time isn’t a sign of hardworking employees, it’s a process that needs to be improved. And sometimes, improving it starts with something as small as automating the numbering in a document.
