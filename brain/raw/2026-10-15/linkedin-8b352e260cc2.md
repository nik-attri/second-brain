Your business is profitable. So why are you broke? 💸😭

One of the biggest misconceptions I encounter working with small business owners is the assumption that profitability and cash flow are the same thing. They're not! And unfortunately, your vendors aren't going to accept a copy of your Profit & Loss statement as payment. 😂

Let's say your business operates on an accrual basis and reports $100,000 in net income. Sounds great, right? But what if your accounts receivable increased by $40,000 during the year? You've also made $20,000 in loan principal payments and purchased $15,000 in equipment. Assuming no other cash flow adjustments, your $100,000 in reported profit resulted in only a $25,000 increase in cash. Where the hell did the other $75,000 go?! 😭

This is why I encourage my clients to look beyond their P&L. Your income statement tells you whether your business is profitable, but it doesn't necessarily tell you whether you have enough cash to pay your bills, invest in growth, or pay yourself. Profitability and liquidity are two entirely different measures of financial health.

At Crowley Tax, LLC, I don't believe financial reporting should stop at telling you how much money you made. I want my clients to understand WHERE their money is going, WHY their financial statements look the way they do, and WHAT opportunities exist to improve their financial position. Bookkeeping tells us what happened. Financial analysis helps us figure out what to do about it.

Because you can't pay your bills with net income on a spreadsheet friends! 😂 If your business is profitable on paper but your bank account seems to disagree, let's talk. Understanding your cash flow might be the missing piece of your financial picture.
