MYTH: “AI Will Replace DevOps Engineers.”
I keep hearing some version of this:
“If AI can write Terraform, debug Kubernetes, generate pipelines and investigate incidents… why do we still need DevOps engineers?”
It sounds logical.
But I think it misunderstands the job.
A DevOps or Platform Engineer isn't valuable because they can type:
terraform apply
or remember every kubectl command.
The difficult part has always been deciding:
What should we deploy?
Why this architecture?
What could break?
What's the blast radius?
Is this change safe?
What will it cost?
Should we automate this at all?
Did the remediation actually fix the problem?
AI is becoming very good at the repetitive parts:
→ gathering logs and events
→ generating Terraform
→ explaining configurations
→ comparing options
→ correlating signals
→ preparing remediation plans
→ creating documentation
But somebody still has to own the decision.
And in production, a confident answer isn't enough.
You need evidence + judgment + verification.
That's the direction we're taking with CrftInfrai.
Not:
AI → Change Production
But:
Detect
↓
AI investigates
↓
Evidence + diagnosis
↓
Recommended action
↓
Risk evaluation
↓
👤 Engineer approves
↓
Controlled execution
↓
Verify recovery
The goal isn't to remove the Platform Engineer.
It's to remove the work that prevents the Platform Engineer from actually engineering.
So I don't think the most interesting question is:
“Will AI replace DevOps?”
A better question is:
“What should a DevOps engineer stop doing manually now that AI exists?”
That's where the industry is about to change.
