The dashboard says the order is late.

Everyone can see it.

Someone still has to make the next five phone calls.

The buyer calls the supplier for a revised date. Then checks with the carrier whether collection is actually booked.

Production needs to know which jobs can still run. The warehouse needs to rearrange the delivery slot. Customer service needs an answer before calling the customer.

By the time that’s sorted, another alert has appeared.

The dashboard did its job. But the person dealing with it still had to piece together the situation, find the right people and chase a decision.

Visibility matters. An early warning gives you a chance to act.

But seeing the problem is the start of the work, not the end.

Before adding another dashboard, walk through what happens after one alert.
Who picks it up? Do they have the information they need? What can happen automatically, and what needs someone’s approval? How does everyone find out what was decided?

And who checks that it actually happened?

That is the standard we believe orchestration should meet at Provenant—and it applies to us too.

Bring the relevant information together, make responsibility clear, move the agreed actions forward and keep people involved where judgment is needed, without making them chase every routine update.

Otherwise, you’ve given someone a better view of the work they still have to coordinate manually.

A useful question for your next software demo:

“Show me what happens after the alert—not just where it appears.”

What shows up on your dashboard every week that still takes a string of calls or emails to sort out?
