Blueprint Newspapers just published an article asking whether AI systems and automation would reduce workers. That means Nigerian SMEs are reading headlines that frame efficiency as a threat to their teams. What nobody is talking about is how that fear keeps business owners stuck in manual processes that drain their margins.

This is exactly what a logistics coordinator in Apapa told me three weeks ago. His situation felt directly caused by the noise around automation replacing people.

He told me his team spends four hours every morning reconciling delivery receipts across three different spreadsheets.

He said: 'Every article makes it sound like software will fire my staff. But the real problem is we lose two deliveries a week because someone typed the wrong waybill number. We keep doing it manually because we are afraid to break what barely works.'

Here's the insight: the Blueprint article frames automation as a workforce question. The reconciliation is not the problem. The manual reconciliation is.

One automated waybill matching tool that flags mismatches in real time cut delivery errors by sixty percent in the logistics firms we work with.

That is four hours back every single morning. That is two recovered deliveries every week that used to vanish into spreadsheet errors.

While Blueprint Newspapers debates whether AI replaces workers the businesses that win will hand repetitive data entry to software so their people handle exceptions and relationships. You do not need a robot workforce. You need one workflow that stops leaking money.
