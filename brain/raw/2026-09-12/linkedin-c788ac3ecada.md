I remember sitting across from our head of people at 6:30 PM on a Friday. She was staring at four open spreadsheets, manually re-typing salary figures and passport numbers into our HRIS while drafting her fourth email of the day chasing a missing reference.

She looked exhausted. And she was not alone.

Research reveals that HR teams spend an average of 26 hours manually processing a single new hire. At an average UK salary of £35,000 (roughly £18 per hour), you are spending £468 in pure administrative labor on every single person you bring through the door. For a growing business hiring 50 people annually, that is £23,400 burned on repetitive data entry, lost attachments, and manual follow-ups.

In fact, 54% of companies report that manual, time-consuming tasks are their single biggest onboarding challenge.

Here is the step-by-step blueprint we used to strip those 26 hours down to under 2:

1. Automate document requests at the offer stage
The moment an offer is accepted, let an automated trigger send the contract, right-to-work requests, and identity checks. Stop drafting one-off emails from scratch.

2. Replace email threads with a single self-serve portal
Give the new hire one centralized dashboard to upload identity documents, submit referee details, and read the handbook. When tasks are overdue, let system reminders nudge them automatically instead of consuming your team's calendar.

3. Establish one-way sync with your HRIS
Eliminate manual data reconciliation. Name, role, department, salary, and start date should be captured once by the candidate and piped directly into your HRIS, Slack, and email systems.

4. Set automated compliance and expiry alerts
With UK tribunals handling over 660,000 claims in a single cycle, undocumented or lapsed right-to-work checks are liabilities you cannot afford. Set automated alerts for expiring visas and DBS checks the day documents are uploaded.

Onboarding should be the moment a new teammate falls in love with your culture, not an administrative marathon for your people team.
