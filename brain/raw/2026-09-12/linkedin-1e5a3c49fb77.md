Takeaway Tuesday on a Friday - The Dash

Today marks the end of a chapter for me.

Lately, one of my heavily rotated artists has been Prof. He's loud, inappropriate, funny, weird, incredibly talented, and uses way too many bad words.

So naturally, I f-ing love him.

My favorite song of his is “Fire Lessons.”
The song isn't some inspirational leadership anthem, but I've always loved those two words.

Because there are different ways we learn.

Some lessons somebody teaches you. Some you read in a book. Some you learn from a great leader. Some you learn by watching a terrible one.

And some lessons?
You have to get burned.

You screw something up. Lose. Get told no. Trust the wrong person. Take a chance that doesn't work. Have a month, or maybe even a year, that kicks your ass.
Those are fire lessons.
And they're usually the ones you don't forget.

I've always tried to keep learning because I never want to become so comfortable with who I am today that I stop working on who I could become tomorrow.

Learn from the wins. Learn from the losses. Learn from the people around you.
And when life decides to teach you the hard way…

Learn from the f-ing fire too.

Because all those lessons eventually become part of your dash.    -

Every chapter has a beginning and an end.
Between those two dates is a tiny line representing a hell of a lot.

The goals you crushed. The ones you missed. The mistakes. The relationships. The chances you took. The days you wanted to quit. Most importantly, the people you impacted.

As one of my own professional dashes ends today, I've realized I'm thinking a lot less about spreadsheets, reports, meetings, numbers, and all the things that once seemed incredibly important.

I'm thinking about people.

The conversations. The disagreements. The laughs. The ridiculous moments. The stressful ones. The people I watched grow and the people who helped me grow.

Those things were the job. Everything else was just paperwork. Titles disappear. Numbers reset. Eventually someone else sits at your desk or takes your position.

What lasts is what you gave to the people around you while you were there.

Did you make people better?
Did you teach somebody something?
Did you show up when things were hard?
Did people trust you?
Did you make them laugh?
Did you give a shit?

That's the stuff people remember.

I'm incredibly grateful for everyone who was part of this dash. And now I'm excited to begin the next one.

Some lessons will come easy. Some will come from people who care about you. Some will come from people who don't. And some will come through fire.

Learn from all of them.

You don't always get to choose when a chapter begins or ends, but you get a hell of a lot of say over what happens between those two dates.

Make the dash matter.
