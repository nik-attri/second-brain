Before building a SaaS, try solving the problem without building the SaaS.

This might sound strange.

But it can save you months of development.

Let’s say you have an idea for an app.

Before spending weeks building:

→ Try solving it through WhatsApp
→ Use Excel or Google Sheets
→ Do some steps manually
→ Work with a few real users
→ Observe where things actually break

Why?

Because you might discover something important.

The problem is real — but your original solution isn't.

Maybe users need a different feature.
Maybe your workflow is wrong.
Maybe the problem happens less often than you thought.
Or maybe…

People can already solve it manually and don't need another app.

Yes, WhatsApp and Excel can be slow.
Manual work can be frustrating.

And you might think:

“Why am I doing this manually?
Why don't I just build the app?”

Because building the app is not the hardest part anymore.
The expensive mistake is building the wrong app.
Imagine spending 3–6 months developing a SaaS…

Only to discover that:

❌ Users don't need some of your features
❌ The workflow doesn't fit their daily routine
❌ Another solution already works well
❌ The problem isn't painful enough
❌ Users aren't willing to change their existing process

That's why I believe in manual validation before automation.
Start small.

Solve the problem manually.
Watch real people use the process.
Ask questions.
Improve the workflow.
Then automate the parts that actually need automation.

WhatsApp → Spreadsheet → Manual Process → Automation → SaaS

You don't have to follow this exact sequence.

But the principle matters:

Go outside your room.
Talk to real users.
Test the problem in the real world.
Market research can tell you that a problem exists.
Real-world testing can tell you whether people actually care enough to solve it.

And only then should you start asking:

“What should we build?”

Because SaaS isn't just about building software.
It's about building something people actually need.
