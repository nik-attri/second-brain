Imagine this:

A prospect submits a form on your website.

A new lead lands in your system.

Then the questions begin.

Is this person already in our records?

If yes, update the existing record.

If no, create a new one.

Now, what service are they interested in?

Social Media Management?

Website Design?

Paid Advertising?

Each answer sends the lead down a different path, a different tag, a different follow-up email, and eventually a notification to the Marketing Team.

Now imagine trying to build the automation while you are still figuring all of that out.

You could easily automate a mess.

One piece of advice I'd give anyone getting into automation is:

Map the process before you automate it.

Before you start building, make sure you understand:

- Every step
- Every decision
- Every possible path
- Every action
- The desired outcome

That way, you are not guessing your way through the automation.

You are working from a clear workflow.

This is something I put into practice while mapping a lead-management process for a digital marketing agency using draw.io/diagrams.net.

The process map was reviewed and accepted, but the bigger value was seeing how much clearer the workflow becomes when the business logic is already defined.

Map → Understand → Define → Automate.

Process first, Tools second.

Because knowing how to use an automation platform is one thing.

Knowing what the automation should actually do is another.

That's the kind of thinking I bring into my CRM and workflow automation projects, understanding the process, identifying the logic, and then designing a system around it.
