Most "AI agent" projects will fail.

Gartner says 40% of agentic AI initiatives get cancelled by end of 2027. McKinsey puts only 23% of agent pilots into production. 88% never see the light of day.

The industry wants you to believe every workflow needs an agent. The data says otherwise.

Here is our hot take: most businesses should automate ruthlessly first. Add intelligence only where reasoning is genuinely required.

The winning architecture looks like this:

→ 80% deterministic automation (rules, triggers, integrations)
→ 15% AI agents (judgment calls, unstructured inputs)
→ 5% humans (strategy, exceptions, relationships)

Trying to replace step one with agents is expensive babysitting dressed up as innovation. Inference costs compound. Governance overhead eats the savings. And your team ends up supervising bots instead of doing real work.

We have watched clients burn six figures on "autonomous" stacks that still required a full-time human to keep on track. The same workflow automated with n8n or Make runs for a fraction of the cost and rarely breaks.

Automation is unglamorous. It does not make keynote slides. It does save 40 to 60% on process cost and ship in weeks, not quarters.

If you are evaluating AI for your operations this quarter, ask one question first: can this be solved with a deterministic workflow?

Most of the time, the answer is yes.

That is where the real ROI lives.
