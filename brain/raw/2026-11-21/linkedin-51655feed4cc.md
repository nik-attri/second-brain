7–8 days of a part-time job taught me something I might not have learned from months of planning.

I worked on basic data entry and Excel-related tasks. Nothing wrong with the work, and absolutely nothing wrong with the people doing it.
But the experience made me question something about myself.

I noticed a difference in what I was looking for versus what the environment required.
The focus was simple: complete your 8 hours, finish the assigned work, and move on.

But my mind kept going somewhere else.

Can this process be automated?
Why are we doing this manually?
How can this problem be solved permanently?
Can I build something that makes this entire process more efficient?
What can I learn beyond the task assigned to me?

I wasn't just interested in completing the work. I wanted to understand the problem behind the work.

And that's when I realised something.

Maybe I'm not looking for just a job. I'm looking for an environment where I can build, experiment, solve problems, and create value.

I don't judge anyone who chooses a conventional career. Every person has different priorities, responsibilities, and ambitions.
But I also don't want to ignore what this experience revealed about me.

I'm 20. I don't want to spend my early years simply counting working hours when I could be building skills, exploring ideas, developing products, and learning how businesses actually work.


For the next 6 months, my focus is clear:

Become genuinely valuable in AI, technology, and problem-solving.
Build real projects instead of collecting certificates.
Find an internship where I can learn from capable people and contribute meaningfully.
Surround myself with ambitious builders and curious minds.
Experiment with startup ideas and understand how to turn problems into products.
I know I'm at the beginning. I have a lot to learn, and I haven't built anything significant yet.

But I'd rather spend these years trying, failing, building, and improving than waiting for the perfect opportunity.

I don't just want to become someone who can do the work. I want to become someone who can question the work, improve it, and eventually build something better.

That's the direction I want to explore.
Let's see what the next 6 months bring.
