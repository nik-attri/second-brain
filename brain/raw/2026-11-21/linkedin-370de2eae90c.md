Your CRM should tell your sales team what to do next, not leave them searching for answers.

When a salesperson opens your CRM every morning, can they immediately identify which leads, deals, and follow-ups need attention?

Or do they spend valuable time clicking through records, checking spreadsheets, and trying to figure out their priorities?

For many growing businesses, the problem isn't that they don't have a CRM.

It's that their CRM isn't structured around how their sales team actually works.

A well-designed CRM should help your team answer five important questions every day:

1. Which new leads need attention?
New inquiries should be easy to identify so opportunities don't get overlooked.

2. Who needs a follow-up today?
Upcoming and overdue follow-ups should be visible without manually searching through records.

3. Which deals are missing a next action?
Every active opportunity should have a clear next step to keep conversations moving.

4. Which prospects are we waiting on?
Your team should be able to distinguish between deals requiring internal action and those awaiting a prospect's response.

5. Which records need attention?
Missing information, unclear ownership, and outdated deal stages should be easy to spot and correct.

But visibility alone isn't enough.

Every active opportunity should also have:

• A clearly assigned owner.
• A meaningful pipeline stage.
• Relevant conversation history.
• A defined next action.
• An appropriate follow-up date.

Here's how I approach building a custom CRM:

I don't start by adding dozens of fields, dashboards, or automations.

I start by understanding the decisions the sales team makes every day.

Then I structure the records, views, and reminders around those decisions.

Whether it's an Airtable-based CRM or a dedicated CRM platform, the goal remains the same:

Make the next action obvious. Make ownership clear. Make information easy to find.

Because a CRM only delivers value when the team actually uses it.

And adoption becomes easier when the system helps people do their jobs instead of creating more administrative work.

Founders and sales leaders: What's the first thing your sales team should see when they open their CRM each morning?
