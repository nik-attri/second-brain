🌊 Building HALIEUS in public — Challenge #1: How do we model what fishers will actually do?
As we develop HALIEUS, I want to share not only what we are building, but also the problems we encounter along the way — including the ones for which there is no obvious answer.
One of the biggest is fisher behaviour.
Imagine that a management authority closes an important fishing area for three months.
Modelling the regulation itself is easy: define the area, the period and the vessels affected.
But that is not the interesting part.
The difficult question is:
What will fishers do next?
They might move to another fishing ground.
They might increase effort elsewhere.
They might target another species.
Some vessels might decide that a trip is no longer profitable.
Others may respond differently depending on fuel prices, vessel size, home port, historical fishing grounds or available alternatives.
And those behavioural responses can determine whether a policy produces the outcome we expected.
How are we thinking about this in HALIEUS?
Our current direction is to avoid treating fishers as perfectly rational agents following a few manually defined rules.
Instead, we are exploring a behavioural layer learned and calibrated from observed historical decisions: where vessels fish, when they participate, how far they travel, how persistent they are in their choice of fishing grounds, and how those decisions change with economic and regulatory conditions.
The simulation can then ask:
Given what we have observed in the past, how might the fleet adapt when we change the rules?
But this immediately creates another challenge.
Historical data tell us how fishers behaved under conditions that have already existed. A new policy may create conditions that have never existed before.
At what point are we no longer predicting from evidence and simply extrapolating too far?
That is one of the questions we are currently thinking through.
Some possibilities include explicitly measuring how far a scenario is from the calibration data, reporting uncertainty by fleet segment, identifying scenarios that are outside the model's reliable operating range, and being very clear about where evidence ends and assumptions begin.
But I would like to hear other perspectives.
If you were designing this system, how would you model fisher adaptation to a policy that has never been implemented before?
And perhaps more importantly:
How would you communicate when the model simply does not have enough evidence to give a reliable answer?
I’d especially value perspectives from fisheries scientists, economists, fishers, marine-policy practitioners, behavioural modellers and people working with agent-based simulation.
