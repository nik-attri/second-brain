This advertising video just inspired me, and I wanted to share it—not simply as an advertisement, but because I believe there’s an insight in it that others might find valuable too :    "Stop Calling It Laziness !", Maybe Your Brain Just Needs the Right Trigger.

One of the most interesting productivity lessons I came across recently is this: If you can only perform when the deadline is on fire, the problem may not be a lack of discipline. It may be the way your brain is activated.

Many of us know the pattern:
You have plenty of time to finish a task.
You know exactly what needs to be done.
You even want to do it.
But somehow… nothing happens.

Then the deadline gets dangerously close.
Suddenly, everything changes.
Focus appears. Energy appears.
Ideas start flowing.
And somehow, a task that seemed impossible yesterday gets completed in a few intense hours.

Then comes the guilt:
“Why didn't I start earlier?”  “Why am I so lazy?”
“Why can't I be more disciplined?”
Perhaps that's the wrong question.

The video makes an important point: some brains are highly responsive to urgency and consequences. They don't necessarily activate because a task is important. They activate when the consequences become immediate.
That changes how we should think about productivity.

Instead of constantly trying to fight our wiring, perhaps we should learn how to design systems that work with it.

For example:
Break a large project into very small starting points.
Create artificial deadlines before the real deadline.
Use short activation periods instead of demanding hours of concentration.
Make the first action ridiculously easy.
Build immediate feedback into the process.
Reduce the gap between knowing what to do and actually doing it.

The goal isn't to create more pressure.
The goal is to create the right trigger.
That's an important distinction.

Because productivity isn't always about doing more.
Sometimes it's about understanding what makes your brain willing to begin.
And perhaps the most useful lesson is this: Don't keep punishing yourself for having a brain that doesn't respond to the system you've designed.

Redesign the system.
In the AI era, this idea becomes even more interesting.
AI can help us automate tasks, generate plans, summarize information and optimize workflows. But AI cannot automatically tell us what kind of environment makes us perform at our best.

Understanding ourselves may therefore become just as important as learning how to use technology. Maybe productivity isn't about becoming a different person. Maybe it's about designing a better system for the person you already are.

What do you think?
Are deadline-driven people actually “undisciplined” — or have we simply misunderstood how different brains get activated?
