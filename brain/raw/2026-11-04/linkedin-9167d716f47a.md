“Users are dropping off. Let’s add an AI assistant to onboarding.”

This is quickly becoming the default fix. As if like adding a chatbot to the setup flow, with AI-generated instructions and step-by-step guidance, will help users get through onboarding.

But before building it, we need to understand the why behind this assumption because being stuck and not seeing the value are two very different problems.

Here's what I would question first:

1. Are users stuck, or just not convinced?
An assistant can help someone who doesn't understand what to do next. But it can't convince someone who doesn't see why they should use the product.

2. Will confused users even know what to ask?
A chat box is another thing users have to figure out. If they don't understand the product yet, asking them to explain their problem isn't necessarily helpful.

3. What happens when the AI gives a wrong answer?
New users haven't built trust in your product yet. One incorrect instruction during setup could be enough to make them leave.

4. Could removing a step solve the problem?
Sometimes, the best onboarding experience is one that requires less explanation. AI shouldn't be used to explain complexity that could simply be removed.

5. What does it cost per signup?
Every new interaction comes with model costs. You're paying for AI assistance even when users don't complete onboarding or return to the product.

Now, I'm not saying that AI has no place in onboarding.
But instead of using it to explain every step, I would look at how it can actually reduce the work users have to do.

💡 Let's take a CRM product, for example.
Instead of asking users to manually configure every field, let them upload a CSV of their existing contacts. AI can then identify columns like name, email, company, and phone number, and map them to the corresponding CRM fields.

The goal should always be to get users to their first useful outcome with less effort.

💭 Has your team tried adding an AI assistant to onboarding? Did it actually improve activation?

Get the problem right first.
