when a business process depends on someone remembering every step, following up manually, or figuring out what to do each time, the problem may not be the person executing it.

the process itself may need work.

i think this is an important distinction when businesses try to improve operations.
sometimes we respond to recurring mistakes by telling people to be more careful, communicate better, or pay closer attention. those things can help, but they don't necessarily address the underlying cause.
a well-designed process should make the correct action easier to identify and repeat.

for example, a client onboarding process should make it clear what information is needed, who is responsible for collecting it, what happens next, and how everyone knows when onboarding is complete.
the aim isn't to document every imaginable scenario or build a complicated system for its own sake.

it's to reduce unnecessary ambiguity, prevent avoidable errors, and make work less dependent on memory.

before adding another tool or meeting, it may be worth asking a simpler question:
is the process clear enough for someone to execute it consistently without having to reinvent it every time?
