Before you give an AI agent access to your business, ask one question.
What exactly are we letting it do?

There's a lot of excitement about AI agents at the moment.
Tools that can log into systems, retrieve information, complete tasks and take actions on your behalf.

I can understand why businesses are interested.

If something can take repetitive work off your team's hands, that's worth exploring, but I've noticed that conversations about AI agents tend to start with what they can do, not what they should be allowed to do - and that's a distinction I wouldn't ignore.

Giving an AI agent access to your business systems isn't quite the same as asking ChatGPT to draft an email.

You're potentially allowing software to access customer information, interact with internal systems or make changes without someone manually carrying out every step.

That's a different level of responsibility.

I've spent 27 years in business and I've never believed in giving someone access to something important without understanding what they're allowed to do with it.

Why should AI be any different?

So before an AI agent gets anywhere near a system that matters, I'd want four questions answered.

1. What can it actually access?
Does it need access to everything, or can its permissions be limited to the specific job it's there to do and can it access the underlying login credentials?

2. Can we see what it's doing?
If it accesses information, changes something or takes an action, is there a reliable record of what happened and when?

3. Can we stop it?
If something goes wrong, can we quickly suspend its access?
Who has the authority to do that?

4. What happens when we switch it off?
Does removing its access affect other systems or processes and what happens to anything it's already done?

These aren't questions that require you to be a software engineer.
They're questions about control, accountability and understanding the risks you're introducing into your business.

I'm not against AI agents, used appropriately, they could remove a lot of unnecessary work, but I wouldn't give one access to an important business system simply because a demonstration looked impressive.

Before you ask what an AI agent can do for your business, find out what you're giving it permission to do inside your business.

Once you've handed over access, you need to know exactly how you get it back.

Would you know what your AI agents can access today and who has the authority to stop them?
