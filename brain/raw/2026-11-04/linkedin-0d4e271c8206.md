One of our interns had never deployed a project before.
Six weeks later, they shipped a live feature for a paying client on our procurement automation platform. 👇

We're sharing this because of how it happened, not to make a point about talent. Here's what the internship at Upzenix actually looked like. Not a tutorial. Real approvals, real money, real deadlines.

WHY THIS MATTERS
LinkedIn's hiring data shows that a higher share of entry-level hires between 2023 and 2025 had completed internships than in 2020-22. Real project experience is becoming the entry ticket.

THE PROJECT
Our interns work inside an agentic AI ecosystem that automates procurement end to end, from purchase requests and vendor quotes to invoice matching. In procurement, one wrong number can mean a wrong payment, so "it works on my laptop" is never enough.

THE 6 WEEKS
Week 1: Setup, reading the brief, learning how a purchase request becomes an approved order, one small bug fix. Nothing glamorous.
Weeks 2-3: First real feature. First code review. The first round of comments was honest.
Weeks 4-5: Owned a module of the workflow. Joined a client call. Handled a change in requirements.
Week 6: Deployed, monitored, and fixed what broke at 11 PM.

WHAT SEPARATED THE INTERNS WHO GREW FROM THOSE WHO STALLED
1. They asked for review before they felt ready.
2. They wrote down what broke and why.
3. They shipped small things, often.
4. They treated the client's problem as their own, not "the task."

HOW WE SUPPORT THEM
Interns never work alone. Senior engineers review the code, and nothing reaches production without that review.

WHAT WE WON'T SAY
That this guarantees a job. It doesn't, and anyone who promises that is selling you something.

WHAT WE CAN SAY
Our interns leave with real commits, real review history, and work they can explain in an interview, including what broke and how they fixed it. That's more than most resumes carry.

If you're a student or fresher, what would help you most right now? A real project to build, a resume review, or someone to review your code? Comment with one word. 👇
