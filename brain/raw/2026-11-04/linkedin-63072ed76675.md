A month and half, i didnt step out for any events. 

I built.

We ran a marathon of automation. We did the not picking within the system. 

As of last Monday,  BridgeE’s marketing ran on people, spreadsheets and good intentions.

Today, it runs on systems with people on top.

In one week, we built four things that I used to think would take a quarter:

1. A core marketing automation layer
The repetitive work, like scheduling, follow-ups and reporting, now runs without anyone chasing it. Our team’s time goes to thinking and creating.

2. An ABM engine
Account-based marketing used to mean long lists and good luck. Now we pick target accounts, research the people behind them, and write notes that speak to their work. Every message is still approved by a human before it goes out.

3. Outbound email automation
Sequences that feel written for one person, sent at the right moment, and tracked end to end.

4. An AI-driven SEO website with a design system built in
Pages that are structured for search from the first line, and designed consistently without starting from a blank canvas each time.

What stayed with me is this:

None of it replaced judgment. It removed the friction around it.

The strategy is still ours. The taste is still ours. The decision to send or not to send is still ours.

What changed is how much of our week now goes to the work only people can do.

For a founder running a lean agency, that matters. We built BridgeE in 2020 on the belief that good marketing should not need a large team or a large budget. This week felt like that belief turning into infrastructure.

There is more to refine, and I will share what works and what doesn’t.

I am free of the nit-picking, the headache of micromanaging and constant follow ups.

The systems push people now, with reminders and huddles timed well. 

If you run a small team and wonder whether this level of automation is within reach, it is. Happy to talk it through.

Automated to hell and back.
Unwinding a bit.
