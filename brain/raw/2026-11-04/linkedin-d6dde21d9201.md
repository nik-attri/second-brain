“How do I become a CFO?”

Someone asked me this in a DM.

My first reaction: I didn’t have a good answer.
(My inner CFO whispering to myself:
“Me?! How the hell did I ever become a CFO?!”)

Then I thought about my own career.
25+ years. Plenty of lessons.
Not all learned the easy way.

But, three exceptional CFOs shaped how I think about finance, business, leadership and self-recognition. 

Here is what I learned:
(during my corporate career, applied today as an entrepreneur)

1️⃣ Be approachable.
Listen. Ask. Challenge. Accept a challenge too.
You need bad news early, not three weeks later.

2️⃣ Know the numbers and the business.
Talk to sales, operations and customers. Your spreadsheet gives you no emotions and judgments.
Explain what the numbers mean, tell a story. Admit what you don’t know.

3️⃣ Decide without knowing everything.
Check facts. Challenge assumptions. MACHEN
Change direction when needed. Leave your ego out of it.

4️⃣ Own your decisions.
Stay calm under pressure. Help solve the problem.
Don’t leave your team defending a decision you approved.

5️⃣ Help your team grow.
Give responsibility, be available, recognize good work.
Notice when strong performers are carrying too much.
These have been my cornerstones, and they still are.

If they work for everyone, I don't know.

But I'd always keep these 4 things in mind:
↳ Learn the craft.
↳ Stay curious.
↳ Treat people well.
↳ And don't take yourself too seriously.

Do you see it differently? If so, how?
