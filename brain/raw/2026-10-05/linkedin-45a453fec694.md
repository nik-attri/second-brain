Sunday Rant 🤬

I am officially sick of the “put AI on it” era of healthcare.

Not because I don’t believe in AI.

I do.

I’m sick of watching AI get treated like a repair kit for operations nobody has actually bothered to understand.

And there is one part of this conversation I think we are missing.

The ugly manual work in healthcare is often telling you something.

The spreadsheet nobody should need.

The second check before something gets submitted.

The staff member who “just knows” which cases are different.

The side list because the system queue cannot be trusted.

The manual reconciliation because two systems do not agree.

The extra phone call because nobody is quite sure whether the handoff actually happened.

Yes, some of that is waste.

But some of it is also evidence.

It is showing you where the process on paper and the process people actually use have separated.

And if you slap AI on top of that before understanding why those workarounds exist, you can do something worse than simply wasting money.

You can make the broken process look mature.

The workaround happens automatically now.

The exception gets encoded.

The output looks cleaner.

Throughput goes up.

The dashboard looks better.

And everyone starts assuming the underlying process improved.

It may not have improved at all.

You may have just made the dysfunction harder to see.

That is the part of healthcare AI that worries me.

Not AI failing.

AI succeeding at the wrong thing.

Before I automate a healthcare workflow, I want to know where the weird stuff is.

Where are people double-checking?

Where are they keeping their own list?

Where are they leaving the system to finish the work somewhere else?

Where does somebody say, “Technically this is the process, but what we actually do is…”

That sentence matters.

Because that is usually where the real problem lives.

Then we can decide what the solution actually is.

Maybe it is AI.

Maybe it is normal rules-based automation.

Maybe two systems need to be integrated.

Maybe there is already a feature sitting inside software you are paying for.

Maybe ownership is unclear.

Maybe the process itself is just bad.

And sometimes the human judgment everyone is trying to eliminate is exactly the part that should remain human.

Healthcare does not need AI layered over every broken workflow.

It needs more operational truth before more technology.

So before you ask:

“Where can we use AI?”

Ask:

“Where are our people compensating for a process that does not work the way we think it does?”

That answer will tell you a hell of a lot more.

If you work in healthcare, what is the strangest manual workaround you have seen that actually existed for a very good reason?
