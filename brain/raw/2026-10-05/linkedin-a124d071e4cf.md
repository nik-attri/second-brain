There are a lot of ways to manage anxiety that dont look anything like anxiety. Sometimes anxiety looks exceptionally competent.It has a spreadsheet, a project plan, three contingency plans, a color-coded calendar, maybe a Kanban board, and definitely a follow-up email drafted just in case.

This month, Im talking about Executive Stress Thinking Traps™, mental habits that can start as useful professional strengths and become costly when they turn into automatic rules. 

This week, Im looking at the Control Trap.Planning, forecasting, risk management, preparation, and problem-solving are skills. They work. In high-demand roles, theyre often part of what makes someone very good at their job.But theres a point where preparing for uncertainty can quietly become an attempt to eliminate uncertainty. And that can consume a hell of a lot of bandwidth.You can still run the meeting, see the patients, manage the staff, make payroll, hit your numbers, parent, and answer every damn email. 

Nobody necessarily sees how much capacity youre spending anticipating, checking, rehearsing, fixing, and trying to stay ahead of what might happen next.Then you get home.For some people, alcohol becomes part of how they finally make all of that stop for the night.That connection is much more interesting to me than simply asking why somebody drinks after a stressful day.This weeks long-form is about control, managing anxiety, bandwidth, and what can happen when a professional strength gets recruited for another job.
