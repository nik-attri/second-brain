A friend of mine stayed late at work again last week. He looked happy about it.

Nobody forced him to.

He works in a fairly critical role at a manufacturing company. Early on, his employers saw he wanted to grow. He didn't have all the experience yet, but they gave him the benefit of the doubt more than once. More responsibility. More room to prove himself. More trust.
He proved incredibly resourceful.

What I find interesting is hearing him talk about work now.

He'll casually mention working extra hours, and I'm always surprised by how happy he seems to do it.

He simply feels the company invested in him when it didn't have to, and now he's willing to give something back.

That's what I think we sometimes miss when we talk about loyalty at work.

Companies want employees who stay through difficult periods, care beyond their job description and don't disappear the moment another company offers slightly more money.

That's reasonable.

But loyalty is difficult to demand from someone who knows they'll be treated as a number the moment the spreadsheet requires it.

I don't think loyalty at work disappeared.

I think it became reciprocal.

And of course, you can't write that into an employment contract.

People figure it out from how the company behaves.
