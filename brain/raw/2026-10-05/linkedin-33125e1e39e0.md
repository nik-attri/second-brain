Procurement should be a profession of choice.

Honestly, I think it’s one of the most underrated careers in business.

Very few careers develop such a broad mix of skills.

📌1. The skills are massively transferable
You learn:
-Negotiation
-Commercial thinking
-Communication
-Risk management
-Strategy
-Data analysis
-Stakeholder management

…and occasionally crisis management with caffeine and spreadsheets.


📌2. AI is pushing procurement forward
The repetitive admin work will increasingly automate.

Which means procurement becomes MORE focused on:
-Judgement
-Influence
-Relationships
-Decision-making
-Commercial awareness

Ironically, AI may make procurement more human, not less.


📌3. It’s fundamentally people-driven
Despite the systems and dashboards, procurement still runs on:
-Trust
-Communication
-Influence
-Negotiation

That never disappears.


📌4. You can work almost anywhere
-Tech
-Healthcare
-Defence
-Retail
-Manufacturing
-Energy
-Public sector

Every organisation buys.

Every organisation faces supply challenges.


📌5. You are never short of problems to solve
One day it’s inflation.

The next it’s disruption, stakeholder chaos, ESG pressure, or supplier risk.

Boring is rarely the issue.


📌6. The career paths are broad
Procurement professionals regularly move into:
-Operations
-Consulting
-Supply chain
-Commercial leadership
-Project management

Because procurement teaches you how businesses actually operate.


📌7. Your communication skills improve massively
You learn how to:
-Manage conflict
-Influence decisions
-Push back professionally
-Build relationships
-Translate complexity clearly


📌8. Procurement is becoming more important
Businesses now realise that when supply chains fail, everything fails.

The function sits far closer to:
-Risk
-Resilience
-Sustainability
-Commercial strategy


📌9. It becomes highly strategic
Once the operational fog clears, procurement starts shaping:
-Supplier ecosystems
-Innovation
-Long-term resilience
-Commercial direction


📌10. You can take it almost anywhere
The industries change.

The countries change.

The challenges change.

But the core skills travel remarkably well.

Procurement can be stressful, messy, and sometimes, chaotic.

But if you lean into it properly, it can build one hell of a career.
