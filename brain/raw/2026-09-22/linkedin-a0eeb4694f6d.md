There is a big misconception about AI Engineers.
Some companies are hiring for AI roles, but during a 20-minute interview, they expect the candidate to explain everything about AI, build an LLM, create ChatGPT or Claude, and prove their complete technical ability.
That is not what AI Engineering is.
An AI Engineer usually uses existing AI models and technologies to solve real business problems.
For example:
🤖 Build AI agents for business workflows
🔗 Connect AI with ERP, CRM, APIs and databases
📚 Build RAG systems using company knowledge
⚙️ Automate repetitive business processes
💬 Build AI assistants and copilots
🧠 Create tool-calling and multi-agent systems
📊 Connect AI with real business data
🚀 Deploy AI applications into production
You don't need to build GPT or Claude from scratch to be a good AI Engineer.
Just like a software engineer doesn't need to build Windows or Linux from scratch to build a great application.
The real skill is understanding:
What is the business problem?
Where can AI help?
Which model or technology should be used?
How do we integrate it with existing systems?
How do we make it reliable, secure and useful in production?
This is where many hiring conversations miss the point.
HR and business owners may only have 20–30 minutes with a candidate, but AI Engineering is much broader than what can be demonstrated in a short interview.
Instead of asking only:
“Can you build an LLM?”
Try asking:
“Here is our real business problem. How would you solve it using AI?”
That conversation can reveal much more about an AI Engineer's practical ability.
