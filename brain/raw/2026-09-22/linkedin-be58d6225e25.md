A finance manager once said:

“We automated our AP process, but my team is still doing almost the same amount of work.”

That sentence tells me more than a dashboard ever could.

My first instinct wouldn't be to defend the automation.

I'd ask:

“Can we walk through the last invoice that required manual intervention?”

So we trace it.

The invoice comes in.

The system captures the information.

Then something doesn't match.

Someone checks the invoice manually.

They send an email.

They wait for clarification.

Another person checks the approval.

Someone updates a spreadsheet.

And eventually, the invoice moves forward.

Technically, the workflow is automated.

But from the customer's perspective?

The work is still manual.

This is an important lesson in customer success:

A customer doesn't experience your product based on what the product can do.

They experience it based on what actually happens in their workflow.

So when a customer says:

“The automation isn't saving us time.”

I wouldn't immediately start explaining features.

I'd investigate:

→ Where does the workflow stop?
→ Why does it stop?
→ How often does it happen?
→ Who has to intervene?
→ Is this a product issue, a process issue, or a training gap?

Because sometimes the biggest adoption problem isn't that customers don't understand the software.

It's that the software hasn't been connected properly to the way they actually work.

Automation should remove friction, not simply move it somewhere else.

That's one of the questions I'd keep asking as a Customer Success partner.

“Where is the customer still doing work they expected the platform to eliminate?” #CustomerSuccess #SaaS #Automation #LogisticsTech #FinanceOperations #ProductAdoption
