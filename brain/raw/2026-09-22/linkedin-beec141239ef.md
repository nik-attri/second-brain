🚨 CyberRakshakLabs (https://lnkd.in/dE-TyQDx) — Incident Summary

	🚨 Meta Muse AI Agent Zero-Day: When Your AI Becomes the Attacker's Tool
	
		✅ This is the AI-security problem I think organisations should start discussing now.
		✅ We are moving from: "AI that answers" to AI that acts.
		✅ Meta's Muse is designed as a personal AI agent that can work across applications and connected services.
		✅ Now a researcher has disclosed a local zero-day in the macOS application and published a proof of concept called not-a-mused. The reported issue allows an unprivileged local process to redirect Muse's dictation traffic and potentially abuse the authority already granted to the agent.
		✅ The vulnerability itself is important.
		✅ But the architecture is even more important.
		✅ Imagine: "Malware → AI Agent → Email → Calendar → Browser → Cloud → Connected Devices"
		✅ The attacker may not need to steal every credential manually.
		✅ They may try to use the AI agent's own authority.
		✅ This is why I believe the next phase of AI security needs to focus heavily on:
			🔐 AI identity
			🔐 Least privilege
			🔐 Data minimization
			🔐 Credential isolation
			🔐 Tool permissions
			🔐 Agent monitoring
			🔐 Human approval
			🔐 Emergency revocation
		✅ AI doesn't have to become malicious to become dangerous.
		✅ A compromised agent with excessive permissions can be enough.
		✅ The question isn't only “Can AI do it?”
		✅ The question is “Who controls what AI is allowed to do?”
