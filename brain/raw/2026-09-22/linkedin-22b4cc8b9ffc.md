listen up you mckinsey juggalos

so you want to be an enterprise product manager?

I could give you the usual advice about influence without authority and stakeholder management. instead I have one question:

how do you feel about progress, morally?

much of daily enterprise product management is consultant cosplay: mapping workflows, drawing boxes and arrows, and eventually discovering where the human middleware lives.

system A has information system B needs. they don’t talk. so a person downloads it from one, fixes it, uploads it to the other, sends an email about it, and updates 10 spreadsheets so everyone knows it happened.

there is an entire corporate class of people making very good salaries essentially working as human APIs.

that sounds like I’m making fun of them, but I’m not.

these are smart people who have accumulated years of institutional knowledge about all the weird little places where the machinery breaks. in a lot of companies, the process works because they know how to make it work.

eventually an enterprise PM looks at the whole thing and asks why it works this way.

we find the gaps, figure out why there’s a person standing in them, and start getting the systems to do the work the systems should probably have been doing all along. the manual handoffs disappear and we “free people up to do higher-value work.”

I used to have some version of that last sentence in my linkedin bio.

eventually I took it out because I realized I had no idea what the hell “higher-value work” meant.

we say it constantly in tech. it makes progress sound frictionless. nobody wants to spend their career moving data between systems. automate that part and now they can do something more interesting.

ok. what?

what is that higher-value work for someone who has spent ten years becoming exceptionally good at the work we just decided a computer should do?

I think about this a lot when people talk about AI. I’m no genAI cheerleader, but human middleware is different.

I can care deeply about the person doing the work without believing there is inherent virtue in making a human spend Tuesday afternoon reconciling two CSVs because two expensive pieces of enterprise software can’t get their shit together.

there are enormous amounts of human intelligence being spent compensating for the fact that our technology is bad at talking to itself. making that unnecessary is progress.

but if “higher-value work” is what comes next, we probably need to get a lot better at defining what that actually means.

because people are much more likely to help you build the future state if they can see a place for themselves in it.
