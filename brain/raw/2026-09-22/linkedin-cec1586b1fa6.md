I watched a founder spend $18k on Facebook ads last quarter. Solid effort. Real money.

Then I asked: "How many of those leads actually turned into customers?"

He went quiet. Not because he didn't want to answer. Because he didn't know.

He had a rough number from his sales guy, but no actual connection between the ads he was running, the leads coming in, and the revenue hitting his bank account. The data existed somewhere—scattered across Facebook, his email, text messages, maybe a spreadsheet—but nobody was looking at it.

So here's what was probably happening: Some leads converted. Some didn't. Some he'll never know about because they got lost between platforms. And some he might've already had but the system wasn't smart enough to recognize them.

He was essentially throwing money at a wall with no way to see what was sticking.

The real cost isn't just the wasted ad spend. It's the decisions he was making based on incomplete information. Which ads to scale? Which messages actually work? Where should he invest next quarter? All guesses.

A business can't improve what it doesn't measure. And most owners can't measure customer acquisition because their systems aren't connected.

This is fixable. But it requires someone actually responsible for seeing the full picture and keeping it accurate as the business changes.

If this is happening in your business, message me. I'll tell you honestly where I'd start looking.
