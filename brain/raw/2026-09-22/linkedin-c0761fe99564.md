Two kids off on residential school trips this week. 💸 💸
 
So yes, I am officially bankrupt. If you need me, I'll be the one crying quietly into a Cup a Soup for the rest of term.
 
Ignoring the usual hell of packing lists, something else really stood out to me. Both schools were doing their best to give a brilliant experience, and I thank them for that. But neither seemed to be considering safeguarding in their decision making and it was stark at times. Personal data, basic supervision policy and many other things just weren't even being considered.
 
For one trip, the joining information came via a shared Google Sheet with the full name and date of birth of every child going. Not just mine, the whole coach load of kids. Nothing says "we've thought carefully about child protection" quite like a spreadsheet link anyone with the URL could open.
 
On the other, communication between home and school was minimal at best. A form went round late, with barely any detail asked or given about pick up times, drop off times, or what a child would actually need on site. For a child with medical devices, medication and food intolerances, that's not a formality, that's the difference between being kept safe and being kept lucky.
 
And then there was the suggested fix for a child needing a private space to sort out contact lenses at night. The child needs a quiet, clean space to get it right. But the plan on the table was a teacher's own bedroom, unsupervised! Perfect, let's invite lone children into adults' bedrooms. Winner.
 
Nobody was being careless on purpose. That's exactly the point. These are the gaps and cracks where risk actually lives for children, not in the dramatic failures but in the well meant, badly thought through ones.
 
I'm not naming names. These are good schools, run by people who care, doing their best with residential trips that are logistically a nightmare at the best of times.
 
But it tells you something. Safeguarding can't be one form, filled in once, filed away. It has to be how everyone on that staff team thinks, all the time, not just when there's a trip on the calendar.
 
It's the exact same conversation we have about RSHE at Learn and Thrive. It can't live in the PSHE lead's inbox, taught once a week behind a closed door. It has to run through the whole school day. Through pastoral care. Through every member of staff. Through how home and school actually talk to each other.
 
That's what Learning for Life is built to do. Help schools weave this into everything, not bolt it onto one person's job description.
 
Because we have to get this right. There is clearly so much more to do. Here at Learn and Thrive we will keep flying the flag.
 
Because every child deserves the chance to dream big, make their own choices and shape their own future.
 
That's the whole point. That's why we do what we do.
