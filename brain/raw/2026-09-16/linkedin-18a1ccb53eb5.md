Every AI vendor is selling the same cure..

The one who converts is the one who first proves why buyers are sick.

Which is why solid deep tech products do not loose to better ones. They lose by explaining a cure to buyers who never were certain about the disease.


Founder of AI services agency tells me yesterday: "We built an AI automation practice. Cold outreach isn't landing."

I ask what the email says.

"We help IT teams automate manual workflows with AI."

I ask one more thing.

"Do they think their manual workflows are actually a problem?"

He goes quiet.

That's it. That's the whole issue.

Every company now gets five AI pitches a week. Most buyers have stopped reading past the first line I must tell you.

Not because AI isn't valuable. 

Because nobody told them their current process is broken enough to change.

You're offering automation. They're thinking "our team handles it fine."

No amount of "cutting edge" or "powered by AI" fixes that.

You're selling a solution before they've diagnosed the disease.

This is Problem Certainty. The buyer has to believe the pain is real and costly before your service matters at all.

Most AI pitches skip straight to capability. Models, integrations, speed, accuracy.

None of that matters if the buyer hasn't felt the cost of staying manual.

Fixing this isn't a script change my friend.

Not even a better tech demo.

It's asking what makes this IT leader feel, in numbers, what staying manual is actually costing them, before you ever mention AI.

Build that first.

The pitch becomes easy to convert.
