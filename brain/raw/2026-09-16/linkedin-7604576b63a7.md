Canada and the U.S. arguing about trade is like two people arguing over the thermostat while jointly owning the furnace.

As a Canadian, I’m legally obliged to defend our hockey, maple syrup, and right to say “sorry” when someone else walks into us. But on trade, I’d prefer we all spend a little more time with a spreadsheet.

According to Visual Capitalist, the U.S. accounted for 56.6% of Canada’s international goods trade in the first half of 2026. That’s a hell of a lot of business to put through a relationship that occasionally resembles a family Thanksgiving after the third bottle of wine.

Canada should diversify. Having that much riding on one customer and supplier deserves a serious conversation. Equally, I’d love to hear “we don’t need Canada” explained to a procurement director who actually has to source the stuff.

Working in supply chain recruitment, I’d rather hear from the people responsible for keeping factories running. “We’ll just buy it somewhere else” is a lovely sentence when you’ve never qualified a supplier.

So, Canadian and American friends: where does this relationship need fixing?

And if you’ve actually moved sourcing across borders, how easy was this supposedly easy switch?
