Lately, I’ve started to think we’re having the wrong discussion about AI adoption.
Every week I see another comparison between Copilot, Claude Code and Codex.

And every week I become a little more convinced that this isn’t where the gap between engineering teams is anymore.

A few signs I’ve started noticing.

1. AI tool evaluation has been running for months.

Someone mentions a new model. Codex? GitHub Copilot? Or maybe try new Antigravity?  Another evaluation cycle begins for the next Quarter.

2. Agent harness? Never heard of it.

But everyone completed the prompting workshop.

3. AI adoption is measured through Jira.

Engineers manually mark AI-assisted tasks. An n8n automation collects the manually entered data. This becomes the AI adoption dashboard.

4. AI training is delivered by people who don’t actually use coding agents in their daily work. 

Yes, they might conduct AI training for others but do not have Claude license. They know the slides. Not the workflow.

5. Repositories are still designed only for humans.

No structured instructions. No architecture constraints. No feedback loops.
Yet the biggest debate is still which model writes better code.
At some point, the model stops being the bottleneck.

The engineering environment becomes the bottleneck.

6. AI is still used sequentially.

Ask. Wait. Read. Repeat.

Meanwhile, the frontier is moving toward multiple agents working in parallel, each with a specific role and context.

The strange thing is that none of these organizations would say they’re behind.

Most have AI training. AI metrics. AI strategy.
I’m just no longer convinced that’s what AI adoption actually means.

At what point does AI adoption become AI theatre?
