A client asked us to rebuild their customer portal. Modern stack, better design, mobile first. Standard brief.

We asked what the portal was for. The answer took forty minutes and three people to assemble, and by the end it was clear the portal was doing four unrelated jobs for four unrelated audiences.

Customers used it to check order status. Their field team used it to log site visits. Finance pulled invoice data out of it manually every Friday. And their largest account had been given a login to do something nobody in the room could fully explain.

Rebuilding that as one portal would have produced a prettier version of the same confusion.

We built two things instead. A customer facing status view that answered one question fast, and an internal operations tool that stopped the Friday export ritual. The field team moved to a separate lightweight app, because their job happens standing up in a car park, not sitting at a desk.

Same budget. Roughly the same timeline. Three products instead of one.

Support tickets asking "where is my order" dropped by about two thirds in the first quarter, and finance got four hours a month back that they had stopped counting as work.

The thing that made that possible was not engineering. It was refusing to accept the brief as written.

Most rebuild requests are really unbundling requests wearing a disguise.

What is the one screen in your business that four different teams use for four different reasons?
