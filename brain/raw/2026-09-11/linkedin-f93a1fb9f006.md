SORRY, NOT SORRY…MY HEART ACHES 

Tonight I read a post by Heather Cox Ricardson and the news about “Yemen’s Iran-backed Houthi militants…” and how that’s affecting travel through the Straight of Hormuz and the Red Sea.

And I started crying. And I couldn’t stop.

Because I remembered requirements gathering sessions with the team in Yemen. Men who probably didn’t understand or didn’t care about why some chick in the US was trying to understand their warehouse requirements. The importance of temperature control and shelf life or distribution.

Or their security protocols. Sobering.

But it mattered to me. They’re dealing with outdated tracking and protocols. Fucking spreadsheets. 

They were generous with their time.

Because I remember the team in Ukraine and the guy working the overnight and providing me with the answers I sought. He was dealing with ungodly spreadsheets that made his life miserable ~ as if he didn’t already have the most serious sh*t on his plate.

Learning about what is involved in providing care for not only the vulnerable recipients of care that these people are risking their lives for but wondering how the HELL are they coping. ???

And feeling so, so inadequate.

I’m trying to figure out the requirements for how an enterprise-level software will work when internet access is spotty if not used as a weapon by the government.

When the people providing the basic services…food, shelter, internet, mental health…are themselves suffering.

And that’s the blessing and the curse of being in this industry that I’ve given my career to.

To put yourself in the shoes of the user, the customer, the employee and be their advocate to leadership and hope they care.

And sometimes it just sucks…

Because on a random evening, you wonder if the people you cared about and advocated for are still alive and safe.
