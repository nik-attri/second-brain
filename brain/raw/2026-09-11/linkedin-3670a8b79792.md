What if digital transformation starts with the process—not the technology?

A recently published study in Frontiers in Pharmacology caught our attention at The Asclepius Project. The researchers describe the smart and digital transformation of a hospital's centralised IV medication preparation service. At first sight, the technologies stand out: intelligent labelling, barcode verification, integrated information systems and autonomous delivery robots.

But perhaps the more interesting part is what happened around the technology.
The team describes a genuine process reengineering: 
- workflows were redesigned, 
- information flows connected, 
- SOPs adapted, 
- responsibilities defined, 
- staff trained and 
- competency assessed
while contingency processes were established for technology failures. 

And the results are encouraging: shorter labelling and delivery times, lower error rates in the measured process steps, and the ability to handle substantially increased workload without adding delivery staff. The study is a single-centre before-and-after study, so we should be careful not to translate these findings directly into broader clinical outcomes. 

But it raises a question that we believe deserves a wider discussion:
Are we still thinking too much about digitalisation as implementing technology—and too little about redesigning the entire medication process?

For us, that is also highly relevant to Closed Loop Medication Management. The real opportunity may not be to automate individual steps, but to reconsider how people, processes, information and technology work together across the medication pathway.

💬 How do you approach this in your hospital?

Do you start with the technology—or with the process you ultimately want to create?

We would particularly like to hear from hospital pharmacists, nurses, IT professionals and technology providers who have been involved in this kind of process redesign.
