On Wednesday night the team went out to celebrate the completion of the Chemistry project at the University of Manchester. 🥂
  
This project has been rumbling along for almost (if not more than!) a decade. The University of Manchester teamed up with Henry Brothers in 2021, and we have finally reached the finish line on a £ A lot of Mill, Long Term Maintenance project.
  
The group photo may be rubbish, but the performance of the team has been anything but.
   
I’ve had the pleasure (and occasionally the pain 😂) of working on the project for Gardiner & Theobald LLP as the NEC3 Project Manager, and I genuinely couldn’t be prouder of what the whole team has achieved.
  
The client team, Henry Brothers, the subcontractors and the School of Chemistry have all been brilliant. Of course, everyone has their quirks and we’ve taken the absolute piss out of each other along the way, but ultimately everyone pulled together and got the job done.
  
And just to prove that you don’t need fancy software to administer a large NEC contract…(although that would be preferable)
    
My trusty spreadsheet. 📊
   
If you know me, you’ll know I LOVE a spreadsheet and Version Control is a real thing, not something I have just made up.  AND I will love you forever if you actually use the latest version and do as you’re told. 😂
  
I’m not an emotional person, but I have to admit I’m genuinely sad to see this project come to an end. There’s been a lot of hard work, plenty of challenges, a fair amount of debate and more than a few stories that probably shouldn’t make it onto LinkedIn.
  
That said, I’m also very happy to finally say goodbye to the Chemistry version of Watergate aka Roof Gate. 🏠😂
  
And, of course, the seemingly never-ending saga of AFDDs.
     
Some things you just never forget.
   
A massive thank you to everyone involved. It has been a hell of a project, and an absolute pleasure to work with such a great team.
