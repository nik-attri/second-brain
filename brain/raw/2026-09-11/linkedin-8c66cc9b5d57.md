"We'll know when we're ready for AI."

No, you won't. Readiness isn't a feeling that arrives. It's six things you either have or you don't, and most leadership teams have never checked any of them.

On data, the usual test is "we've got loads of data". The check that matters: if you needed a clean, accurate list of your best 500 customers right now, how confident are you in what you'd get? Not the theory. The list, today.

On process, the usual test is "our people know what they're doing". The check that matters: could a new team member follow your core processes from the documentation without asking anyone? And if you gave the same task to three people, how similar would their approaches be? AI automates the process you actually have, not the one you think you have.

On people, the usual test is "the team's excited about AI". Enthusiasm is not capability. The check that matters: if you asked them to use an AI tool in their day-to-day work tomorrow, how capable would they be? Second question, harder one: as the leader, are you modelling it yourself, or delegating the curiosity downward?

On technology, the usual test is "we're on the cloud". The check that matters: can your key systems share data without someone manually exporting and importing spreadsheets? And how much of your team's week goes on working around your tech rather than using it?

On strategy, the usual test is "AI is a priority for us this year". The check that matters: have you allocated actual budget and resource, or is it still on the wishlist? Is the leadership team aligned on where AI should focus? Do you have a way to measure whether it worked?

On governance, the usual test is "we'll deal with that when we scale". The check that matters: if an AI tool made a significant error affecting your customers tomorrow, who owns the response? Name the person. If you can't, that's the finding.

Six pillars. Data, process, people, technology, strategy, governance. None of them require a consultant to assess and none of them care how you feel about AI.
