If a leader isn't getting on-board with AI, get them fired. They're a threat to your own career and the future of the business.

1. Leaders need to understand how AI works.
2. To understand it, they have to use it.

Had 4 chats this week with people at the Aotearoa AI Summit in Auckland about "leaders not getting on-board".

The problem is that too many are stuck on a 2023 "aha!" moment: ChatGPT / Copilot rewriting an email or analysing a PDF.

Glorious the first time, but AI transformation leaders in traditional businesses are still relying on it to get leaders signing off more budget or using AI to solve harder business problems.

Leaders need to feel the next evolution of what AI can do to get excited about the opportunities for the business.

The next aha moment to create for them is the feeling of AI doing work on your behalf.

- An agent moving data between systems.
- AI clicking through a task in app they use daily right before their eyes.
- AI reconciling two spreadsheets and producing the analysis they spend three hours doing.

We see it daily with the people we work with. Those who have that experience pick it up, run with it and start pushing us harder on advanced use cases.

The catch is that the aha moment elation dissipates real quick. Leaders have to take responsibility for the curiosity and effort that follow.

A workshop or a 12-week course gives them a starting point and the skills to experiment, but the business cannot keep paying to persuade them to participate.

Good news is some leaders take that responsibility themselves: I’ve just had a CEO of one of New Zealand’s biggest companies get in touch about 1:1 AI coaching. 

But you cannot build an enablement programme assuming every leader will take that initiative.

So, a PSA for anyone driving AI transformation:

1. Help leaders experience what’s possible now.
Agree with the CEO / board how much effort the business will invest in getting them there.

2. Go beyond the sanctioned business licence.
Spend an hour with them in Claude Code or Codex (hell, pay for it out of your own pocket). Pick a process they recognise, use fake data and show them the work being done: automate a report, extract data or create a daily briefing.

3. If they get it, push them to replicate the work in Copilot (likely what they can use at work).
Whether they succeed is besides the point. Help them see the differences and troubleshoot (that's where where the learning is).

4. If they still refuse to engage after the agreed support and multiple aha moments, work towards moving them on, they're a barrier to the business evolving.

You want them curious enough to experiment on their own personal activities, then bring that experience back into the business.

Key is to provide the opportunities to learn, and expect them to take responsibility for using the tools.

Otherwise it’s death by a thousand cuts of business case rewriting and slimming to minimal value from AI...
