“You work in IT? So, you can probably fix my computer.” 😄 Or connect my TV. Or tell me why some random software isn’t working. Sound familiar?

But IT is a bit like saying that someone works in healthcare and assuming they know everything about every possible medical specialty.

IT covers countless different areas and highly specialized roles. One of them is Process Automation — finding ways to use technology to take over repetitive tasks. A good example is invoice processing.

Someone used to have to manually check which purchase order an invoice belonged to and then enter the relevant information into the system. Today, technology can handle much of that process for us.

A bit like a washing machine. 😉We used to do the laundry by hand. Today, we put the clothes in, choose a programme and let technology do the rest.

But before automation can work, you first need to answer a few important questions.
How often do we perform the process?
How much time does it take?
What data do we need?
What happens when something doesn’t go according to plan?
Will automation make things better?

Because sometimes the biggest challenge isn’t technology. It’s the process itself — one that no one fully understands anymore. 😉

Working with automation has taught Aneta Pawluczuk , Digital Commerce Product Owner, that even a seemingly simple process can have a surprising number of steps. Sometimes people have been doing something for years simply because someone once told them, “That’s how it should be done.” And no one really remembers why anymore.

And that’s exactly why automation doesn’t start with robots. It starts with conversations, questions and understanding how the process really works.

The best results happen when IT and end users work together — because technology can suggest a solution, but the people using the process every day are often the ones who know best what they need.

And I think that’s exactly why IT is about so much more than computers, systems and “Have you tried turning it off and on again?” 😉

What does work in IT make you think of first?
