Dave Lemont helped name #BIM. Twenty years later he'll tell you it never finished the job.

This week ArchDaily, the most-read architecture publication on the planet, put that argument on the record. The headline: "How Material Intelligence Solves What BIM Can't." 

I've been waiting a long time to see those words in print somewhere other than our own deck.

BIM solved #geometry. Move a wall and the plans, sections and schedules follow. That was part of the promise and it delivered.

What it never solved is the moment the modeled wall becomes a real #product from a real manufacturer. Which tile. Which certification. Which spec section, keynote, cut sheet, EPD. That information still lives in a spreadsheet, six PDFs, an email thread from the rep and somebody's memory. The model doesn't know any of it.

So a selection changes in DD. The spec doesn't get updated. The schedule says one thing, the drawing says another, and nobody catches it until the sub prices it. The later you find it, the more it costs. Every architect reading this has a story. I have several from my own years at the desk.

Interiors makes it worse. A paint color or a wallcovering never becomes a Revit family. There is no geometry to coordinate against. The information IS the object.

That gap is the reason Acelab | Material Hub exists. We didn't set out to build a product library. We set out to give the materials layer of a building the same coordination logic BIM gave the geometry. #Material #intelligence is the name for it. Two years ago it was our pitch. Now it's ArchDaily's headline.

Material Hub is what it looks like in practice. Product data as structured working data, connected to the specs, schedules and drawings that depend on it. Change the selection once, the documents follow. 200,000+ products. 20,000+ firms including SOM, AECOM and Stantec. Firms running this workflow are cutting material documentation time by up to 50%.

Dave calls it the next step for BIM. I'd put it more bluntly. BIM coordinated the building. Nobody coordinated the materials. We are.

Link in the comments. If you were in a firm the day Revit showed up, you know what this moment feels like.
