This month I took part in HackIIITD at IIIT Delhi with my teammate Harshit Sanwal. As Team RiggerX, we pitched Partsy, an RFQ copilot for industrial distributors. 🛠️
The problem: When a distributor gets a request for quotation, someone has to hunt through manufacturer catalogs and spec sheets to find a part that actually fits. One missed requirement can mean a bad quote, a slow reply, or a hit to credibility.
Our idea: Partsy turns one RFQ line into a source-linked shortlist that a person can review.
1️⃣ Extract the requirements and flag what’s missing
2️⃣ Find the relevant manufacturer pages
3️⃣ Compare the requirements against the specs
4️⃣ Show matches, gaps and warnings, with links to the sources
We used TinyFish’s Search and Fetch APIs in our design to find product pages and read specs, so the discovery work can be automated. Partsy handles the matching and keeps the reasoning visible. For Round 1 we kept it small on purpose: industrial contactors, one RFQ line, a few manufacturer pages. 🔌
🧠 What I’m taking away:
 ~ A product should flag what it doesn’t know instead of guessing
 ~Automate the searching, but keep the final decision with a human
 ~Starting with one narrow problem makes a good idea easier to build and easier to trust
 ~Working with someone from a different college makes the thinking sharper

📜 I’m happy to have received a Certificate of Participation. The bigger win was learning how to turn a messy, real-world workflow into a clear product idea.
