🚨 𝗛𝗔𝗖𝗞𝗧𝗢𝗕𝗘𝗥 𝗢𝗥 𝗕𝗟𝗔𝗖𝗞𝗧𝗢𝗕𝗘𝗥?
Are we building software faster than we are learning to build it responsibly?
October 2026 presents an interesting contradiction.
Hacktoberfest celebrates open source, collaboration, experimentation and the spirit of building together.
But Black October reminds us of the other side of our rapidly evolving tech ecosystem: critical vulnerabilities, supply-chain compromises, exposed credentials and the growing role of AI in vulnerability discovery and exploitation.
And it raises a question we cannot afford to ignore:
𝗔𝗿𝗲 𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗽𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗲𝘀 𝗲𝘃𝗼𝗹𝘃𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮𝘀 𝗳𝗮𝘀𝘁 𝗮𝘀 𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗿𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝘁𝗼𝗼𝗹𝘀?
AI can generate code, analyse repositories, identify bugs and automate development workflows. But speed without understanding can introduce risks that are difficult to detect and even harder to contain.
I believe the problem isn't AI-generated code itself.
The problem is treating generated code as verified code.
As developers, we need to rethink what responsible engineering means.
A few principles I believe should guide us:
🔐 Security by design, not as an afterthought.
🧠 Engineering fundamentals over blind dependence on AI.
🧪 Verification and testing before production.
📦 Supply-chain security as a first-class engineering concern.
👨‍💻 Accountability for every line of code we ship, regardless of how it was generated.
We should embrace AI, experiment with it, and use it to push the boundaries of what we can build.
But we must never outsource our engineering judgement.
𝗔𝗜 𝘀𝗵𝗼𝘂𝗹𝗱 𝗺𝗮𝗸𝗲 𝘂𝘀 𝗺𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝗰𝗮𝗽𝗮𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝗲𝗻𝗴𝗶𝗻𝗲𝗲𝗿𝘀, 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝗹𝗲𝘀𝘀 𝗿𝗲𝘀𝗽𝗼𝗻𝘀𝗶𝗯𝗹𝗲 𝗼𝗻𝗲𝘀.
I've put together this carousel exploring the challenges and proposing a practical framework for responsible AI-assisted software development.
Now, I'd love to hear different perspectives:
1️⃣ Is AI making software development more vulnerable, or simply exposing weaknesses that already existed?
2️⃣ Are we risking a generation of developers who know how to prompt but struggle to understand what happens underneath?
3️⃣ Should organisations introduce mandatory security and verification standards for AI-generated code?
Let's move beyond debating whether AI is good or bad.
Let's discuss how we can build better with it.
