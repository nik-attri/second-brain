Some people leave a company. Others leave a bloody great hole.

Today we’re saying goodbye to Lee.

Lee and I are from a generation of IT people who can still remember the excitement of discovering the Turbo button on a PC. A button that promised MORE SPEED (yes I know it didn’t). Imagine that. These days you need a subscription, a cloud account and probably a meeting with Procurement to achieve the same thing.

Lee is what the industry likes to call a unicorn.

Developer. Infrastructure engineer. Problem solver. Linux enthusiast. And, occasionally, the only person in the building who actually understands why something works.

He doesn’t make a fuss. He doesn’t demand recognition. He just quietly gets on with making complicated things work.

At Slickstitch, he’s built Linux kiosks for our production and warehouse operations that are simpler, better and cheaper than the alternatives. He’s tackled tablet management, wrestled with infrastructure, written software and generally made technology behave itself.

Which, as anyone in IT knows, is rather like trying to teach a badger to play the piano.

And there’s much more to Lee than technology.

He’s supported charitable causes, owned and run a T-shirt shop and hang-out space in Wolverhampton where people could even buy things on tick. Imagine trusting people in a retail environment. Radical stuff.

He’s also a Hell Hound. A devoted follower of Ferocious Dog, and I suspect their final tour in 2026 is going to feature rather prominently in his immediate plans.

Then there’s Wendy, his partner, and her rather wonderful shop in Bilston, Kitty Darling, where I’m sure he’ll be lending a hand.

But mostly, Lee is going off to do something that many of us talk about and rather fewer actually achieve.

Live and work on his own terms.

Not because he has to. Because he wants to.

And that distinction feels particularly important today.

I spent ten years of my working life at Poundland, so the news this week, with thousands of jobs potentially at risk, has hit rather close to home.

Behind every corporate announcement, restructuring plan and financial headline are real people. People with mortgages, families, friendships and years of loyalty to a business. People who deserve better than uncertainty about their futures.

My thoughts are very much with my former colleagues and everyone affected. I sincerely hope a positive outcome can be found.

It’s a reminder that employment is never just about a job title or a line on a spreadsheet. It’s about people.

And Lee is one of the good ones.

We have a replacement lined up at Slickstitch. And I’m sure they’ll be excellent.

But you don’t really replace a Lee.

You just hope the next person knows as much about Linux, has half his ingenuity, and doesn’t expect us to understand folk punk.

Good luck, mate. Thanks for everything.

Keep being a unicorn. And whatever you do, don’t become a 9-to-5 robot.
