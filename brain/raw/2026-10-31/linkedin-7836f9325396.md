Are software developers still "coders," or are we officially becoming Tool Engineers? 🛠️
If you look at how modern engineering workflows have evolved with tools like Cursor, Claude, and Antigravity, the day-to-day reality has fundamentally shifted. We aren't spending hours manually typing out boilerplate syntax or hunting down missing semicolons. Instead, we are acting as architects, reviewers, and orchestrators of intelligent systems.
This realization hit home during a recent, insightful discussion with my head Nilesh Gaikwad. We were discussing how the IT industry has historically thrived on specialized platform roles (think SAP, Salesforce, etc.). Today, core software development is experiencing a parallel shift our primary medium is increasingly becoming the tool ecosystem itself.
What does this mean for different players in the tech ecosystem?
For Developers 💻: The value is shifting away from memorizing syntax and toward system design, sharp logic, context management, and mastering how to direct AI agents effectively.
For Founders & Engineering Leaders 🚀: Velocity looks different now. Teams aren't just measured by lines of code written, but by problem-solving speed, code quality, and how efficiently they leverage modern agentic workflows.
For IT Recruiters 🎯: Hiring criteria are evolving. The best engineers aren't just defined by their language stack anymore, but by their adaptability, architectural thinking, and prompt/tool craftsmanship.
The craft isn't dying, it’s evolving into something much more powerful.
What’s your take on this? Are you fully embracing the "tool engineer" era, or do you still find yourself missing the old-school, line-by-line coding days?
