What happens to your business if AI disappears tomorrow?

That question came up at an 859 networking event last week where we were discussing how we use AI in our marketing.

We've become very good at asking: "What can we use AI for?" 
Perhaps we should spend a little more time asking: "What happens when we can't?"

There are already signs of nervousness around the enormous amount of capital backing AI infrastructure #Reuters. The cost of insuring against defaults in parts of the technology sector has hit record levels, while banks are looking at ways of transferring some AI and data-centre credit risk.

Maybe the AI boom continues, but we've seen technology bubbles before.
Some burst. Markets correct and something new emerges.

At the 859 we discussed something more fundamental: in the rush to embrace AI, are we forgetting how to operate without it? Are we over reliant on the tool?
Are junior team members learning how to prompt rather than how to think through the underlying problem?

AI is extraordinary technology. I use it myself, but if your favourite AI platform disappeared tomorrow, doubled its price, failed for a week, changed its terms, or simply stopped producing what you needed...
 - Could your team still do the job?
 - Do they still understand the process?
 - Do you retain the underlying knowledge?
 - Could you operate manually?

The answer isn't to resist AI. It's to find the balance. Use it, embrace it , but don't forget the skills, knowledge and judgement that got us here.

AI isn't the risk. Dependency without resilience is.

That's a conversation I think more businesses should be having.
