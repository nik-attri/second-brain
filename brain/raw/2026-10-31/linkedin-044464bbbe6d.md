Digital Design in AEC: 
Where Did We Start, 
Where Are We Now, and 
Where Are We Heading?

Something interesting is happening in the AEC industry.

I’m increasingly noticing new job titles and dedicated teams emerging around Digital Design, Computational Design, and Design Technologies.

This raises an interesting question:

How did we move from traditional engineering design to BIM, and now to Digital Design as a distinct professional discipline?

Let’s look at the evolution.

🔹 Where did we start?

Traditional engineering design was primarily focused on producing drawings, calculations, and technical documentation using digital drafting and design tools.

🔹 Where are we now?

BIM transformed the way we develop, coordinate, and deliver projects.

We moved beyond 3D modelling into information management, multidisciplinary collaboration, digital workflows, and connected project environments.

🔹 Where are we heading?

We are witnessing a new shift towards computational and intelligent design.

Parametric modelling, visual programming, design automation, generative design, AI-assisted workflows, and performance-driven design are increasingly becoming part of the design process.

The focus is gradually shifting from simply producing and coordinating design deliverables to developing smarter ways to generate, evaluate, optimize, and automate design solutions.

But what does this mean for AEC professionals?

Are we witnessing the emergence of a new discipline that sits alongside BIM and Digital Delivery?

Will future design teams require engineers who can combine domain expertise with computational thinking, programming, data analytics, and AI?

And perhaps the most important question:

Are we preparing our engineering professionals for this transition, or are we still developing skills for yesterday’s workflows?

I believe understanding this evolution is essential for both professionals and organizations as we rethink the future of design and engineering delivery.

I’d be interested to hear different perspectives, particularly from professionals working in Digital Practice, BIM, Computational Design, and Design Technology.
