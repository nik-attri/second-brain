I probably wouldn't have landed my first high-ticket automation client if I kept hiding behind my screen.
​When I first started building workflows, I was just piecing together web design and basic integrations. I spent hours studying platforms like Make, n8n, and Zapier, figuring out how to make different software talk to each other.
​But knowing how to build the systems was only one part of the journey.
​I had to show my work, explain how much time I could save people, and put myself in conversations where opportunities could find me.
​Imagine building an "invisible engine" that can save a founder 15 hours a week, but being too quiet to tell them about it.

​How would they know relief is even possible?
​Here is what I’ve learned: your technical skills matter, but your willingness to be seen matters just as much.

​Post the workflow breakdown, send the cold pitch, ask the question, and apply for the project.

​You don't have to be the loudest person in the room. You just can't afford to keep hiding.
