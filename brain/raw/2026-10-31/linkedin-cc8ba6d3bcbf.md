Forget ghosts and monsters. LinkedIn has found something far more terrifying: AI-generated content.

I'm so tired of the endless AI whining on my LinkedIn feed. From "AI will replace us all!" to "Use your own brain!" and "You can always tell when something was written by AI!"

And underneath: "Couldn't agree more!" "So well said!" "AI slop is garbage!"

Sure, AI slop is garbage. But we had garbage content long before AI: bad articles, generic marketing copy, meaningless corporate posts — all proudly handcrafted by humans. Same garbage, different production method. Why act as if bad content was just invented?

Don't like it? Scroll past. Problem solved.

Knowing when to use your own organic brain and when to use an artificial one takes a little discipline and common sense. Apparently, that's too much to ask of some people.

I'm an incurable techno-optimist. AI is one hell of a productivity booster.

If someone prefers a horse because it's greener, healthier and faithful to ancestral wisdom, be my guest. Personally, I'll take the flying Tesla😂 

I hate cooking: the shopping, the planning, the cleanup. Until we get affordable robot chefs, I'll photograph my fridge or supermarket shelves and ask ChatGPT what healthy meal to make. I still have to cook it myself (unfortunately). Does it taste worse? Quite the opposite 😄 

I'll use AI for tedious Excel spreadsheets, vibe-code little apps and — brace yourselves, defenders of 100% pure, organic, ethically sourced, AI-free writing — even write texts with it. 

I first wrote down the main ideas for this post by hand, on actual paper. Like some old-school traditionalist, exercising the neural connections AI supposedly killed off. Then I dictated it to ChatGPT, which polished it up like a big-shot executive's secretary, saving me just as much time on editing.

Brain engaged. Busywork eliminated.

I no longer log into our company's website admin panel. I dictate changes to a voice assistant: it handles the simple ones, and I send the complex ones to our web agency, already structured and translated from humanities-speak into developer-speak.

One employee at our company built an entire CRM tailored to our needs. No massive budget, no external IT team. Just one person and AI.

We've lived with wild uncertainty for almost a decade. Who knows what tomorrow holds for the world, the economy, immigration or any of us?

So when I see another AI-content panic post, I think: Oh, my sweet summer child.

Want to know what actually scares me?

- A mushroom cloud (no comment).
- The erosion of the middle class
- Being dragged back into the office five days a week.

And even if AI suddenly becomes self-aware, I'm sure we'll somehow learn to live with that too.
