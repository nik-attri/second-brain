Just returning from a busy Oracle AI World, and a key take away related to EPM is, it has a central and critical role in an AI-enabled Finance function. AI on its own brings in the risk of shadow EPM (The AI-Area of Spreadsheet hell!)

AI needs trusted financial models, consistent business rules and clear controls to act on. EPM provides that foundation, with the security, approvals and audit trail Finance depends on.

Oracle’s direction is to build on this, bringing more AI into EPM and connecting it with external AI through agents and APIs.

AI Agent Studio, Fusion Claw and the roadmap for Agentic Applications point towards Finance moving beyond analysing and reporting performance into continuously predicting, evaluating options and taking action. People set the objectives and boundaries, with agents doing more of the work within them.

That could mean planning that responds as conditions change, or a close process where agents monitor progress and resolve routine issues, bringing exceptions to the team.

The EPM partner summit reinforced what this means for firms like Absolute EPM. As AI takes on more detailed configuration, our role shifts towards deeper finance domain expertise and forward-deployed engineering: understanding the business, designing the right solution and validating what AI delivers.

My takeaway: the need for EPM remains strong. How Finance uses it, and how partners deliver it, is changing quickly.
