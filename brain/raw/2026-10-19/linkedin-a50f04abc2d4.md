Last week Friday, I had the opportunity to attend an industrial visit at Old Mutual Kenya. 
One of the most memorable takeaways was: “If you can't govern your data, you can't use generative AI.” 
As someone building a career in cybersecurity, this really stood out to me. We often talk about AI in terms of what it can do, from automating workflows to improving productivity. But behind those capabilities are important questions about data governance, PII, privacy, risk management and compliance. 
How do organisations ensure that the tools they adopt are safe to use? How do they manage the risks that come with AI? How can automation improve efficiency without compromising the security of sensitive information? These are questions that make the intersection of cybersecurity, AI and business operations quite interesting to me. 

Beyond technology, the visit offered valuable lessons on career development and financial literacy.
1. Build my career capital. Develop my skills, invest in relationships with mentors and peers, and build a reputation grounded in reliability, integrity and consistency. 
2. Stay curious about automation. Exploring tools such as Power Automate, UiPath, and Power BI has reinforced the importance of understanding how technology can solve real business problems. 
3. Understand the business behind the technology. Financial literacy and commercial awareness matter. We need to understand not just the systems we work with, but also the people, risks and business objectives those systems support. 
4. Be intentional about my growth. Keep learning, start taking action and identify what I need to stop doing to move forward. I'm particularly interested in continuing to explore how organisations approach data protection, AI risk management and security in increasingly automated environments. 

Thank you to the Old Mutual Kenya team for sharing your knowledge and giving us a glimpse into the intersection of technology, finance and business.
I'm grateful to the Moringa School Alumni Community for creating opportunities like these where we get to connect with industry professionals and gain insights that help us make more intentional decisions about our career paths.
