Your plant manager will never walk in and ask for your OEE.

He'll ask five questions. And most maintenance departments can't answer any of them without a week of digging through spreadsheets and someone's memory.

Here they are, with the formula and — more importantly — the field you have to fill in to get it.

1. "How much of the team's time is planned work?"
PM Compliance / planned vs reactive ratio. Planned work orders completed ÷ total work orders completed. Below 50% you're a firefighting crew. Above 70% you're a maintenance department. He's not asking about work. He's asking whether he can plan production around you.
What you need: every work order tagged planned or reactive. One field.

2. "How long is that machine down when it breaks?"
MTTR. Total downtime hours ÷ number of failures, on that asset. Note it's per asset — the plant average is useless to him. He wants the number for the line that costs him money.
What you need: start time and finish time captured on the work order, not typed in on Friday.

3. "How often does it break?"
MTBF. Operating hours ÷ number of failures. This is the one that tells him whether your PM plan is working or just consuming hours.
What you need: failures logged against the asset record, not against "line 2."

4. "What's sitting in the backlog, and is it growing?"
Backlog in weeks: open work order hours ÷ weekly available technician hours. The absolute number means nothing. The direction over 8 weeks means everything.
What you need: an estimated duration on every work order. Yes, even a rough one.

5. "What did we spend on this asset, and should we replace it?"
Cost per asset: labour hours × rate + parts consumed. When it approaches replacement cost, the conversation changes from maintenance to capital.
What you need: parts issued against the work order, not just pulled off the shelf.

Here's the pattern. Every one of these comes from data captured at the moment of the job. Not reconstructed afterwards. If it's typed up on Friday from memory, the KPI is fiction and everyone in the room knows it.
