For years, I wanted gym managers to look at their dashboards. Now, I’m more concerned about what they do after closing them.

When I first started working as a consultant, getting data from a gym could be a nightmare. Sometimes the data simply didn’t exist. Other times, it was scattered across spreadsheets, manually put together, with numbers nobody fully trusted.

That was one of the reasons we created FitnessKPI. We wanted gym operators to understand what was happening in their clubs without spending hours gathering information. And we made huge progress on that. 

But something started to bother me. We knew when a club manager logged in to check their KPIs. What we didn’t know was what they did next. Because a dashboard can show you that cancellations are increasing. It can even help you understand where the problem lies. But that knowledge alone won’t bring a single member back. Someone has to identify where to intervene, decide what to do, and make sure the team follows through.

For far too long, we assumed that having the information in front of us was enough. Today, with ANNA integrated into FitnessKPI, gym operators can go further: ask questions about what’s happening in their clubs, compare locations, identify priorities, and prepare actions through segments and automated journeys, while staying in control.

After more than 30 years in this industry, one thing has become increasingly clear to me. It’s no longer enough for me to know that a club manager has opened their dashboard. I want to know what changed in their gym afterward.
