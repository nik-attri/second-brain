The AI disconnect: What Anthropic’s internal team knows about 2030 that we don't.

Anthropic just dropped a fascinating study on how people view the future of work, and the gap between the general public and the engineers building the tech is wild.

While the average professional thinks AI might handle maybe 25-30% of their workload by 2030, over half of Anthropic's own staff expect AI to do at least 80% of their current work in the same timeframe.

The differentiator here isn't tech optimism—it's day-to-day exposure. When you look at people who already live in the product every day, the reality of agentic workflows changes everything:

• 59% of Anthropic staff say tasks take a quarter of the time or less with AI. In a normal workplace, only about a quarter of AI users see that kind of speed.

• The "Hiring Paradox": Even though their team expects ИИ to automate the vast majority of their hours, 95% of them still expect their company’s headcount to grow over the next year. Automating tasks doesn't mean deleting roles; it means scaling capability.

• A shift from execution to evaluation: Routine code, drafting, and analysis are heavily automated. The human role is rapidly shifting to oversight, system design, and validating AI-proposed plans.
As Product Leads, this is a massive cue for how we manage teams and roadmaps.

If our day-to-day execution is going to take 75% less time, our value won't come from managing backlogs, drafting specs, or tracking progress. It’s going to come from our ability to ask the right questions, deeply understand user empathy, and orchestrate these AI systems.

The public's biggest fears right now are privacy and "preferring to deal with a human," but as the tech spreads, those barriers will melt away just like they did with mobile banking or cloud software.
