Most software gets bought to solve a problem and ends up becoming one.

Another system to maintain, another export nobody automates, another tool the team quietly works around.

We started BITS to work the other way.

Before anything gets built, we ask what the problem actually costs:

→ a process that takes three days when it should take three minutes

→ hours lost moving data between systems by hand

→ a workflow bent out of shape because the software couldn't keep up

Once that cost is clear, the technology decision gets easy.

Sometimes the answer is automation, sometimes custom software, and sometimes it's AI, but only when AI is the cheapest way to kill the problem, not because everyone else added it to their pitch deck.

Over the coming weeks we'll share what we're building, the problems behind each product, and the thinking that shaped them.

Our rule is simple:

if a feature can't demonstrate the value it creates, it doesn't ship.

That keeps our clients' projects focused and our own roadmap honest.
