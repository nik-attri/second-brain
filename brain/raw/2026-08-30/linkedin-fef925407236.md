5 non-negotiables when I am implementing our AI Native Procurement Operations

1.) No Jargon: Jargon costs time and resources. Most of the jargon is either sales-oriented, crafted to create a non-existent niche, or most definitely a 100k USD PowerPoint presentation. You dont need any of them.

2.) Lead by domain expertise and not by title-derived authority. Self-explanatory.

3.) Follow this rulebook before you go out to market for yet another platform subscription

a.) Eliminate the process. You dont need to have any tool to unlock efficiencies.
b.) If you cannot eliminate the process, chunk down unnecessary subparts.
c.) If you cannot do steps a and b, use Microsoft Power Automate and Python scripts to automate. This replaces your expensive process orchestration platforms.
Tip: If your use case is entirely internal (i.e., you dont need to interact with suppliers), you can cover almost every use case with Power Automate and Python. 
d.) However, if you need to collaborate or automate workflows with external stakeholders, skip Power Automate. Either go for low-code platforms or third-party platforms.
e.) Whenever you can, always test whether you can use claude/chatgpt/llm to replace the process you have; can you reasonably get a similar level of outcome? If yes, lock this use case for further exploration, and if not, skip the process altogether. Agent won't be able to do it.
f.) Starting today, whether you are implementing AI in a procurement project or not, start building your knowledge repositories. These are category-specific documents in one place, process guidelines, scope of work, final deliverables, or contract copies. You will thank me later. This is the bare bones of any AI-driven transformation. Not your master data of item & supplier codes. 

That's it. It is simple. Not complicated.

Last thing: Avoid creating silos during this AI native Procurement transformation at all costs. You need engineers (like data scientists) and domain experts (like category managers) to make decisions about use cases in Procurement.
If you are a project manager or reporting manager, sit this one out. Eliminate consulting jargon and multiple project managers who wish to "track and coordinate". Let AI manage it.

Lastly, ensure teams are well-funded and well-resourced. 

AI in Procurement doesn't have to follow a traditional mindset. Pick up a small and simple use case, unlock 80% efficiency, and repeat. Skip the complex ones in the 1st phase.

You should be shipping out one use case per quarter, if not more. Otherwise, you are heading towards solving complex use cases without having a solid foundation. This is how most consulting advice operates. Complex use cases without a path of execution. Avoid it at all costs.


That's all.
