I built ALPA because I was wasting too much time doing something that wasn’t making me any money.

Finding people to talk to.

Business development is a numbers game. Not a particularly glamorous one either.

Find 100 businesses that could genuinely use what you sell.
Maybe 20 respond.
Maybe 4 or 5 turn into proper conversations.
Maybe 1 or 2 become clients.

Fine.

But finding those bloody 100 businesses?
That can eat a whole day.
Google. Websites. LinkedIn. Contact pages. Copy. Paste. Spreadsheet. Repeat.

And somewhere along the way I realized:
Why the hell am I spending hours finding people when the valuable part is talking to them?

So I built ALPA for myself.
I tell it what kind of businesses I’m looking for and where. It finds them, along with websites, phone numbers, emails and other available business information.

What took me hours now takes minutes.  Literally!
And strangely enough, this is a pretty good example of what I mean when I talk about business architecture.

It isn’t just about architecting the big sexy stuff.

Sometimes it’s looking at one boring piece of your business and asking:
Why are we still doing it this way?

Remove the friction. Compress the low-value work. Give yourself more time for the parts where you actually create value.

My problem happened to be prospecting.
I fixed it for myself.

Then I thought: Well, I’m probably not the only idiot wasting hours doing this.
So ALPA is now available for anyone to use.

Go find people.
Then stop searching and start talking.
