The most important skill in Product Management isn't knowing the product.

It’s knowing the user.

You can understand the market, analyze competitors, build roadmaps, prioritize features and track metrics.

But if you don't understand the person using your product, you’re still making educated guesses.

Users rarely give you the solution.

They tell you:

“This takes too long.”
“I have to do this manually.”
“I don't understand what happens next.”

A good PM asks:

Why?

What are they actually trying to accomplish?

What does their current workflow look like?

Where is the real friction?

And sometimes, you discover that the problem you thought you were solving wasn't the real problem at all.

That’s why I believe, Understanding the user is a PM’s unfair advantage.

Engineering tells you what can be built.
Design helps make it usable.
Data tells you what is happening.
Business tells you what is valuable.

But the user tells you what actually matters.

And with AI, this becomes even more important.

When technology can do almost anything, the question isn't:

“What can AI help us we build ?”

It’s:

“Where can AI meaningfully improve this user’s experience ?”

That’s the difference between building an impressive feature and building something people genuinely need.
