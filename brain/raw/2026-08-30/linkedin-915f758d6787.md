Sometimes the problem isn’t the tool. It’s how we’re using it.

I once created an Excel template to help with PTO balance audits and shared it with a coworker.

There was just one problem.

The formulas weren’t working for him.

We troubleshot the workbook. Checked the formulas. Tried to figure out what could possibly be different between what I was seeing and what he was seeing.

Everything looked right.

Finally, I said, “Show me exactly what you’re doing.”

He shared his screen and walked me through the process.

There it was.

Copy. Paste.

The way the information was being copied over was adding an extra space to the data. That tiny space was enough to prevent the formulas from recognizing the values correctly.

Once I showed him what was happening, his response was basically:

“Awww hell.” 😂

Problem solved.

But it reinforced something I’ve carried with me in process improvement:

When something isn’t working, don’t only inspect the output. Observe the process.

Sometimes the spreadsheet isn’t broken.

Sometimes the employee doesn’t need more training.

Sometimes the process doesn’t need to be rebuilt.

Sometimes you just need to watch the work happen.

One extra space can tell you more than an hour of troubleshooting.
