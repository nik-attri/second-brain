Your new hires' first-week survey doesn't measure onboarding. It measures how fast identity worked.
Day-one access is the most honest metric your IAM program has. Not licenses deployed, not workflows built — whether a new hire can actually do their job at 9 AM on Monday.
Four places where provisioning quietly breaks down:
1. The last mile. Your IGA platform provisions 40 apps; the other 60 get access through manual requests and shared spreadsheets. If you can't quote your provisioning coverage percentage, it's lower than you think.
2. Request hell instead of birthright. When every entitlement is an individual request with an approver chain, day one becomes day twelve. Baseline roles granted automatically from HR attributes cut the queue before it forms.
3. The mover pile-up. Transfers collect old entitlements instead of swapping them. Re-certify on every move — access should follow the job, not the person.
4. Measure time-to-first-productive-access, by application tier. Break it down by business unit, and suddenly every delay has an owner.
Nobody remembers the provisioning engine. Everyone remembers whether they could work on day one.
What's your program's time-to-first-access, honestly?
