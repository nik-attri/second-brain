A client wanted a new system that would cost tens of thousands. 
Instead, we made a few improvements to the existing one... 🤷🏻‍♂️

A client came to us with a request:“I want a new information system.”

At first glance, it seemed like a straightforward assignment.
We could have gathered the requirements, designed a solution, estimated the scope of the project, and sent over a quote.
But before doing any of that, we asked one simple question:“What exactly isn’t working for you today?”

And then we started digging a little deeper.
 🤔 Why do you want to replace the current system?
 🤔 Where does your team lose the most time?
 🤔 Which tasks are still being done manually?
 🤔 What information needs to be copied from one system to another?
 🤔 And most importantly – what actually needs to work better once the project is finished?

Gradually, we realized that the problem wasn’t the information system itself.
The client didn’t actually need a new system at all.
The existing solution was already capable of covering most of what the company needed. The real issue was how it was set up and how the different parts of their IT environment communicated with each other.Instead of developing and implementing an entirely new system, we only needed to improve the existing solution, connect two systems, and automate several processes that employees had previously been handling manually.

The result?
✅ A project that could have taken months and cost tens of thousands of euros turned into a much simpler solution costing just a few thousand.
The client got exactly what they needed. Their team’s day-to-day work became easier, and the company didn’t have to replace an entire system simply because, at first, it seemed like the only option.

Of course, building a new information system would have been a much bigger project for us.
But a bigger project for us doesn’t automatically mean a better solution for the client.

💪 At BRAIN:IT, our goal isn’t to sell as much development, as many licenses, or as many billable days as possible.

Our goal is to understand the problem first – and only then find the technology that solves it.
Sometimes, the answer is a completely new information system.
Other times, it’s just a few well-designed improvements.
Because the most expensive IT solution isn’t necessarily the best one. And sometimes, the greatest value an IT partner can provide is what they choose not to sell you.

Have you ever been involved in an IT project where the solution turned out to be much simpler than it initially seemed?
