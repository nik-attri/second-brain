INDIA DOESN’T JUST NEED MORE JOBS. IT NEEDS MORE CITIES THAT CREATE THEM. 

Here’s a question we should be asking:

Why should opportunity be concentrated in a handful of Indian metros?

🌍 THE GLOBAL PARADOX

The ILO projects global unemployment at 4.9% in 2026. Yet the broader global jobs gap could reach 408 million people.

A stable unemployment rate doesn’t automatically mean people have access to decent work, good wages or opportunities that match their skills.

THE CITY PARADOX

India’s 2025 labour-market data shows that unemployment varies considerably across major cities.

But here’s the bigger question:

Are we building cities where people can find work or economic ecosystems where businesses can keep creating it?

📍 WHY VISAKHAPATNAM?

A major port. An industrial base. Maritime and logistics potential. Opportunities across manufacturing, technology and tourism.

Vizag has ingredients worth building on.

But potential isn’t employment.

The real opportunity is to turn investment into businesses that grow, hire locally and create lasting economic value.

🚀 THREE SOLUTIONS THAT COULD MAKE A DIFFERENCE

1. BUILD AROUND EACH CITY’S STRENGTHS.

Connect Visakhapatnam’s port and logistics ecosystem with manufacturing, maritime services and other industries that can realistically grow there.

Every city needs a strategy grounded in its competitive advantages not a copy of another metro.

2. CONNECT EDUCATION TO ACTUAL JOBS.

Bring employers and educational institutions together to design apprenticeships, practical training and entry-level hiring pathways.

Measure placements, wages and retention not just certificates.

3. MAKE TIER 2 AND TIER 3 CITIES EASIER PLACES TO DO BUSINESS.

Improve infrastructure, digital connectivity, industrial facilities and access to markets.

Then measure whether investment actually creates jobs, increases local hiring and improves livelihoods.

🤖 AND WHAT ABOUT AI?

The World Economic Forum projects 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced globally by 2030.

India needs to prepare workers for changing job requirements through reskilling, practical AI education and access to emerging industries.

MY BIGGER THESIS

India’s next employment revolution could come from building a network of opportunity cities not expecting a handful of metros to carry the country’s ambitions.

More places where businesses can grow.
More places where talent can thrive.
More people able to build a future without automatically having to leave home.

💬 LET’S TURN THIS INTO A BUSINESS DISCUSSION.

If you had ₹1,000 crore to develop ONE Indian city into a major employment hub, which city would you choose?

Visakhapatnam? Vijayawada? Indore? Coimbatore? Surat? Somewhere else?

Which industries would you build around—and how would you measure the jobs created?

Name the city. Show us the strategy.
