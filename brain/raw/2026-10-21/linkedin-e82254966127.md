I shouldn't have done the math.

A few weeks ago, I was on a call with one of our city partners when he told me about a recent sourcing challenge.

A company came to him needing a massive number of hotel rooms, immediately, for nearly three months.

We're talking enough rooms to potentially require inventory across the entire city and neighboring cities.

So he did what people in our industry have done forever.

He emailed the hotels.

BCC. Send.

Then the responses started coming in.

Hundreds of them.

And that's when he realized the problem wasn't sending the request.

It was what came next.

How do you organize potentially 300 individual email responses? How do you track availability and rates? How do you compare them? How do you make sense of all that information quickly enough to respond to the client?

Then he asked me:

“Could virsitour have done this?”

I pulled up the platform.

Within five minutes, I showed him how he could have sent the opportunity to all of those hotels through virsitour, allowed each hotel to respond directly to the RFP, and had the responses organized side-by-side in one place.

No digging through hundreds of emails.

No manually building spreadsheets from responses.

No trying to remember who responded and who didn't.

His jaw dropped.

“I wish I had known that.”

Me too.

And then I did the math.

I really shouldn't have done the math.

The potential hotel commission associated with an opportunity of that size could have exceeded **$1.5 million.**

Ouch.

But after losing a little sleep over that number, I realized something.

We didn't lose $1.5 million.

We gained one hell of a case study.

Because this wasn't a hypothetical problem I created to demonstrate why virsitour should exist.

It actually happened.

And the technology to solve it was already sitting there.

That's the part I haven't been able to stop thinking about.

Maybe our biggest challenge isn't convincing people they need another piece of technology.

Maybe it's making sure that when someone says:

"I have a group that needs rooms.”

Their next thought is:

“Send it through virsitour.”

Because sourcing the opportunity is only the beginning.

You still have to manage everything that comes back.

And that's exactly what we built virsitour to do.

So yes, my city partner got a lot more work than he bargained for.

I got very little sleep.

But virsitour got something incredibly valuable:

Proof.

Now we make sure we're there for the next one.
