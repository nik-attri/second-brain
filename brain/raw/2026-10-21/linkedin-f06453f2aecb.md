“IT is dead.” No, it’s not. The IT industry of 5 years ago is dead though.

I spend my days talking to hundreds of developers, founders, hiring managers. The story that's been dominating the conversation recently is one of AI-induced panic. I've participated in conversations where the veterans are saying they haven't written a line of code in long months, the juniors are saying there's absolutely no point in studying and everyone is planning their exit to go and become an air conditioning installer.

Let's just sum up where we are at the moment.

1. Syntax is a commodity. Problem solving is the premium.
The candidates who are struggling are the ones who define themselves by writing code. The ones who are getting the jobs (often at a higher rate) are the ones who define themselves by solving problems. AI writes the syntax. Humans own the architecture, the business logic and the accountability. If your only skill is taking a spec and turning it into code then yes, most likely you're in trouble. If your skill is in understanding what needs to be built and why then you're more valuable than ever.

2. The "Junior Gap" is very much a real thing, just not a permanent one.
Companies aren't hiring juniors to write CRUD apps anymore. If they hire them, it is mainly to manage AI agents, validate AI output and deal with the integration complexity. The entry level has just moved from "can you code?" to "can you orchestrate technology to deliver business value?" 

3. The hybrid roles are exploding.
The most in demand profiles at the moment aren't the pure developer. They're DevOps + Security, Product + Engineering, Data + Business Intelligence, etc. The silos are breaking down. What companies are looking for is someone who can speak both the language of the engineer and the language of the stakeholder. The pure coder is being superseded by the technologist who understands the context.

To the candidates feeling lost:
Don't be comparing yourself to the 2021 benchmark. That market is dead. Your value at the moment isn't in how fast you can type. It's in how good a thinker you are, how much you understand the systems and how effectively you can use tools (particularly AI) to amplify your output.

To the founders hiring:
Stop filtering for keywords. Start filtering for adaptability, curiosity and problem solving depth. The best engineers today are the ones who can learn any framework and use AI to move 10x faster.

Founders & Hiring Managers, what's the change you're seeing, are you hiring any differently in 2026? Share your thoughts below.
