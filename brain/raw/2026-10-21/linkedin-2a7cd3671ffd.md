Apparently, hacking a business now costs about £40…

I had to read that twice.

Reuters has reported on a hacker who used AI agents to break into the websites of more than 100 organisations.

And here’s the part that caught my attention.

The hacker wasn’t sitting there manually trying to break into every system.

AI agents were doing most of the work.

Finding weaknesses. Testing ways in. Working for hours with very little human involvement.

More than 1,200 websites were targeted.

Over 100 reportedly compromised.

Estimated AI cost per successful breach? Around $50.

That’s roughly £40.

Now think about what that means for a business with 50 or 100 employees.

You might have a small internal IT team. Perhaps you outsource everything. You’ve invested in Microsoft 365, backups and antivirus.

But the people trying to get into your systems can now use AI to do work that previously required far more time and expertise.

And they don’t need to clock off at 5pm.

That’s what concerns me about where cybersecurity is heading.

The cost of attacking businesses is falling. The cost of recovering from an attack certainly isn’t.

I’m all for businesses embracing AI. We’re doing it ourselves.

But the conversation can’t just be about how AI makes us more productive.

We also need to talk about how it makes the people targeting our businesses more productive.

Because that changes the game for everyone.

Genuine question.

Do you think most SMEs understand how quickly the cyber threat is changing?
