IT’S Q4, BABYYYY. The most wonderful time of the year to work in Customer Success. If you’re a fucking psychopath. 😂

Ahhhh, November. The leaves are falling, Christmas decorations are appearing, and somewhere a VP of Customer Success has opened a spreadsheet and whispered, “We can still save this.”

WELCOME TO Q4.

That magical time when every customer becomes a priority, every renewal becomes an emergency, and someone from Finance who hasn’t spoken to you since February wants to know whether that $47,000 account is going to renew.

BABY, I’VE BEEN TRYING TO GET THEM ON A CALL SINCE MARCH. WHERE HAVE YOU BEEN?

And the end-of-year discount conversations? Suddenly, everyone wants to renegotiate. Procurement has entered the chat. Legal has awakened from its slumber. And your champion, who LOVED the product in September, now needs to “circle back internally” with fourteen people you’ve never met.

But here’s something I’ve been thinking about.

Sometimes Q4 chaos is just the consequences of shit we didn’t deal with in Q1, Q2 and Q3.

And yes, I’m including myself.

That customer we knew wasn’t adopting properly but kept telling ourselves we’d address next month? That renewal where we never got the economic buyer involved? That uncomfortable pricing conversation we postponed?

Congratulations. They’ve all arrived at the same fucking Christmas party, and nobody brought wine.

I’ve made those mistakes. I’ve looked at an account in November and thought, “Why the hell didn’t I push harder six months ago?” I’ve confused a friendly relationship with a healthy account. I’ve assumed that because someone wasn’t complaining, everything was fine.

Sometimes I’ve been so busy helping customers with what they needed TODAY that I forgot to prepare them for TOMORROW.

Not a fun thing to admit when you’re leading Customer Success. But here we are.

Q4 doesn’t create all your problems. It just turns up the volume on the ones already there.

And while I love a last-minute save as much as the next emotionally unstable CSM, should we really celebrate performing CPR on a customer relationship?

A heroic renewal rescue makes a great Slack announcement. A customer who never needed rescuing makes a great Customer Success strategy.

So yes, we’re chasing renewals. Having uncomfortable conversations. Working with Sales, Finance and whoever else suddenly remembers Customer Success exists.

But I’m asking myself something.

What am I doing RIGHT NOW to prevent next November from becoming another episode of Survivor: SaaS Edition?

Because I’d love to spend Q4 helping customers plan their next chapter instead of desperately convincing them not to end ours.

Happy Q4, you beautiful disasters.

May your renewals be signed, procurement be reasonable, and your champions remember your fucking name.

And may nobody say “Can we squeeze in one quick QBR before Christmas?”

P.S. The caption is pain. Not au chocolat. There’s nothing sweet about Q4. 😂
