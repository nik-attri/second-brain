I just got back from India last week and met a young small business owner who runs a few cars on Uber.
His daily routine?
Drivers send screenshots of their Uber earnings, cash collected, and fuel expenses at the end of every shift. He manually calculates how much each driver should receive — every single day.
It's repetitive, time-consuming, and easy to get wrong.
He understood that technology could automate this, but like many small business owners, he didn't have the time to build it himself.
So we explored what AI (Claude) could do.
Here's what we built:
A simple WhatsApp-integrated settlement system.
Drivers send their Uber earnings screenshots and fuel receipt photos to a dedicated WhatsApp number, just as they normally share information.
Simple OCR extracts the relevant numbers: total earnings, cash collected, and fuel expenses.
The system acknowledges each submission through WhatsApp.
Once everything is sent, drivers tap "Done" (or type DONE).
The system automatically calculates the settlement based on the agreed payment rules.
The driver receives the settlement amount and a detailed breakdown on WhatsApp. The owner reviews it before payment.

This experience made me think about the opportunities for college students.
Thousands of small businesses — cab operators, kirana stores, local service providers, tuition centers and workshops — deal with repetitive manual work every day.
Most cannot afford expensive software or developers. But they can benefit enormously from simple AI-powered automation.
My suggestion to students:
Talk to local business owners.
Understand their daily problems.
Find repetitive tasks that consume their time.
Build small solutions using AI tools.
Learn by solving real business problems.
These seemingly small problems are golden opportunities to learn how businesses work and create something genuinely useful.
The next wave of AI impact won't come only from big tech companies. It will also come from young people who identify everyday problems and solve them with tools already in their hands.
If you're a student, look around. The problems are everywhere.
What's one small business process you've noticed that could be automated?
