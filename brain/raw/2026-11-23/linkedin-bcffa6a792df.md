The customer who calls and asks, “Could you please help me replace the monitoring system I am perfectly happy with?” is about as common as a construction meeting that finishes early.

Most customers do not experience an obvious “problem”. They experience a normal working day.

Driving back to site to check equipment. Changing batteries. Managing cables. Combining data manually. Explaining an unexpected peak. Copying information into yet another report.

These may be small frustrations, but after years of working around them, they no longer feel like problems. They are simply “how the job works”.

That makes technical sales challenging. Customers usually understand their work far better than the salesperson—and they are rarely waiting for a long presentation from someone who has just discovered the industry.

I can explain SWARM and Honeycomb with great enthusiasm. Occasionally even before anyone has asked me to. But product knowledge only becomes useful when it is connected to the customer’s actual working environment.

Perhaps the best solution is to spend a few days alongside customers in the field. Watch an installation. Follow the process from measurement to report. Observe where people wait, repeat steps or create clever workarounds.

We can also learn by listening to support calls, reviewing lost opportunities and asking more specific questions:

Which task takes longer than it should?
Which alert creates the most phone calls?
What do you still do manually?
What would you definitely not miss if it disappeared tomorrow?

The best sales insight is not always discovering a dramatic pain point. Sometimes it is recognising the daily inconvenience that everyone has stopped questioning.

Maybe the best sales training is not another presentation.

Maybe it is a pair of safety boots, a notebook and the discipline to remain quiet long enough to learn something.
