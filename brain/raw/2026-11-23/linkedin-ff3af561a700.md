In Richard Feynman’s 1974 commencement speech at Cal-Tech, he recounted going to a new-age resort where people were learning reflexology. A man was sitting in a hot tub rubbing a woman’s big toe and asking the instructor, “Is this the pituitary?” Unable to contain himself, the great physicist blurted out, “You’re a hell of a long way from the pituitary, man!”

His point was that it’s relatively easy to make something appear “scientific” by, for example, presenting data in official looking charts and tables, but that doesn’t really make it science. Consultants often boast that their “research” consists of hundreds of case study interviews and large databases containing data on thousands of firms, but without the proper methods and controls, those things are meaningless.

Another problem is that, while many top-tier consultants have blue-chip resumes, coming from top schools, very few have had experience operating a business. They are often people who’ve never failed, who’ve been told that they’re smart all their lives and expect others to be impressed by their ideas, not to examine them thoroughly.

The truth is that the world is a messy place. Many of the most important assets, like relationships and trust, don’t fit neatly into Excel spreadsheets. Ideas can only be validated forward, never backward. There are countless ways a seemingly sound, logical idea can fail in the marketplace.

So take ideas that are purported to be “scientifically backed” or “research-based” with a grain of salt. An idea is just an idea. The market place will tell you if it’s a good one or not.
