In my journalism days, we covered the growth of offshoring to India in depth. It sparked debate about skills and careers and regulation, while offering businesses a way to transform operating models, redeploy key talent and unlock efficiency, productivity and growth.
   
Sound familiar? 

Twenty years on, AI is driving similar debate. So it was fascinating to be in Bengaluru last week for our Everest Group Engage conference, to hear how these two major trends now intersect.
  
AI has been widely tipped to impact jobs created in offshore hubs such as Bengaluru, and undermine the role of Global Capability Centres (GCCs). But a show of hands from attendees - a veritable who’s who of the sector - suggested entry-level hiring will keep rising. And if a show of hands isn’t scientific enough for you, our CEO, Jimit Arora, shared Everest Group data showing continued headcount growth in India-based GCCs, with net growth overall as new centres open and existing ones expand.
 
Data since the early 2000s, indicates a correlation between the growth of offshoring and a decline in routine jobs in the UK. But it has also shown an increase in non-routine jobs as well as skills shortages in emerging and in-demand roles.

Of course, transitions are never perfectly in step, nor can such changes can’t be attributed to a single trend. But it’s a useful reminder on three fronts: polarised debates about positive or negative impacts often miss crucial nuance; no trend tipped to transform the world will be the first, or the last; and we should learn the lessons from one to the next.
 
A key message from the stage and in conversations in Bengaluru was the need to get ahead of change. AI literacy is now essential but so is recognising and embracing what people already bring: communicatation, empathy, problem-solving and domain expertise. Attendees appeared to be in agreement that such capabilities are becoming increasingly valuable in an AI-first world.
  
Thank you to all the speakers and attendees who made it such a great event, and to my  colleagues in India who helped make it such an enjoyable visit.
