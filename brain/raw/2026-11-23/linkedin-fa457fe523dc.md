Sunday evening. That magical moment when your weekend dies just after it started.

As I write this post it’s Sunday night for some. For others, Monday morning. Depends where you are in the world.

Either way, my condolences.

I remember corporate life.

Friday afternoon? FREEDOM!

Two days away from office politics, pointless meetings, and that colleague who could turn a five-minute conversation into a hostage situation.

Saturday morning?

Still pissed off about Friday.

Laundry. Groceries. Chores. Wondering why your boss earns twice your salary with half your brain.

Sunday morning?

Beautiful. You’re finally relaxed.

Sunday afternoon?

OH, BLOODY HELL. TOMORROW IS MONDAY.

Suddenly, you’re remembering meetings, deadlines, and Karen from Accounting, who needs another spreadsheet explaining the spreadsheet.

Sunday evening?

You’re already mentally at work.

Congratulations. Your employer now occupies your brain rent-free.

Monday morning, the alarm screams.

You consider calling in sick.

Permanently.

And the whole bloody circus starts again.

I lived that life for years.

Then corporate life and I had a disagreement.

Apparently, they no longer required my services.

How thoughtful.

Couldn’t find another job, so I started a business.

Failed.

Started another. Succeeded.

Failed again.

Started something else.

My entrepreneurial career looks like an ECG during a heart attack.

But here’s the funny thing.

I no longer hate Sundays.

Monday morning? Bring it on!

Do I work less?

HELL NO. I work more!

But now I choose my projects, my schedule, and which idiots I’m willing to tolerate.

Mostly.

Entrepreneurship has its own headaches, of course.

Cash flow. Customers. Uncertainty. Bills.

My old boss gave me anxiety. Now I manufacture my own.

But at least it’s MY anxiety.

Would I go back to corporate?

I’d rather attend a six-hour meeting about reducing meetings.

My only regret?

I should have started building something while I still had a salary.

Because starting a business with no income is a bit like learning to swim after someone throws you off a cruise ship.

Possible. But unnecessarily exciting.

So here’s my advice.

Don’t quit your job on Monday.

Start something on Tuesday.

Test an idea. Offer a service. Explore consulting. Find a customer.

Build your Plan B while Plan A still pays for groceries.

No pitch. No obligation. No motivational bullshit.

Just a conversation about what you could build.
