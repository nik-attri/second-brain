The US Midterm elections are behind us, and the political headlines are fading. But for serious traders, the real lesson from last week isn’t about who won—it’s about how market microstructure behaves during macroeconomic transitions.

Historically, markets experience a volatility spike before the votes are counted, followed by a normalization once the uncertainty is removed. However, that transition period is exactly where retail traders lose the most capital.

Here is what we observed in the AlphaQuant execution logs last week:

1️⃣ Pre-Election Spread Widening: In the 48 hours before the results, liquidity pools thinned out. Brokers widened spreads on Gold (XAUUSD) and major FX pairs to protect themselves, turning standard stop-losses into slippage victims.

2️⃣ The Post-Election Whipsaw: As the results became clear, institutional algorithms immediately repriced assets based on the new fiscal reality. This caused sudden, violent liquidity shifts that triggered "ugly fills" for anyone trading manually or with rigid, unmanaged automated strategies.
