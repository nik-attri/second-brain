Day 10 of showing up here until I land my next role.
 
Something odd is happening in the developer job market, and it's worth naming clearly.
 
LinkedIn's 2026 data: AI engineer job postings are up 143% year-over-year. Four of the top five fastest-growing US roles are AI-related . In South Africa specifically, AI-related job postings have jumped 352% since 2019, and DevOps engineers here now command R45k–R62k/month .‌￼‌￼
 
But the job descriptions are getting weirder. Employers want someone who knows React, cloud infrastructure, AI integration, and system design. Someone who can build APIs, optimize databases, and talk to product teams . Not a specialist. A stack.‌￼
 
The uncomfortable part: entry-level is brutal. Oversupply of applicants, inconsistent readiness, companies filtering for "proven skills" over degrees . Mid-level is the healthiest segment. Senior is still scarce.‌￼‌￼
 
So the signal for anyone looking right now is pretty simple. The market doesn't reward depth alone anymore, and it definitely doesn't reward breadth without proof. It rewards the person who can point to something working and explain every decision inside it.
 
Which is the whole reason I'm posting here daily instead of just refreshing job boards.
 
Curious what others are seeing — is the "stack over specialist" shift real in your org, or just a hiring-manager wishlist?
