Two books. Two very different worlds. One uncomfortable question about technology.

1.If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies by Eliezer Yudkowsky and Nate Soares.
2.Mythology by Edith Hamilton.

One explores the existential risks of superintelligent AI. The other revisits stories of gods, heroes, ambition, power, and human nature.
At first glance, they have little in common.
But they led me to reflect on something.
Throughout history, humanity has been fascinated by power.
In mythology, power without wisdom often leads to tragedy.
Today, we are building systems with capabilities that are growing at an extraordinary pace.
AI is no longer just a tool for productivity. It is increasingly influencing how we work, make decisions, solve problems, and interact with the world.
And somewhere in this race, I think we need to pause and ask:
Are we becoming better at building intelligent systems than we are at understanding their consequences?
This question is particularly relevant to the Digital Employee Experience (DEX) space.
We are introducing AI-driven insights, intelligent automation, predictive interventions, and autonomous workflows into the digital workplace.
The possibilities are exciting.
But the real measure of progress cannot simply be how much we automate or how intelligently our systems operate.
It must also be how thoughtfully we deploy them.
Does AI remove friction, or introduce a different kind of complexity?
Does automation empower employees, or take away their agency?
Does intelligence translate into better experiences, or merely faster decisions?
Technology will continue to advance.
The more interesting challenge is whether our judgment, governance, and understanding of human behaviour can advance alongside it.
Perhaps the most important capability in the AI era will not be intelligence alone.

It will be wisdom in deciding what to do with that intelligence.

A thought worth sitting with.
