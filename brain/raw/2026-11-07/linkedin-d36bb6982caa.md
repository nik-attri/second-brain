Walking blindfolded into an S/4HANA negotiation with a spreadsheet is how enterprises end up in a multi-million-pound licensing trap.

Most leaders know their SAP roles are bloated after years of "just copy Bob’s access for the new starter." But because nobody wants to trigger 12 months of spreadsheet hell and painful business meetings, those uncleaned roles are left to set the Cloud bill for the next decade.

When migration deadlines loom, leadership inevitably defaults to defensive over-licensing. Fearing that stripping access will break live production or spark testing chaos, companies buy an expensive buffer of Full Usage Equivalents (FUE) just to stay safe.

Under S/4HANA FUE rules, that caution costs millions. Pricing is dictated strictly by the deepest authorisation objects inside assigned roles, meaning one dormant transaction hidden in a shared role automatically inflates the licensing tier for everyone attached to it.
