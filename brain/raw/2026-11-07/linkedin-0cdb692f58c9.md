Most of the real work in software projects starts with something very ordinary.
A spreadsheet someone has been updating manually for years.

It usually belongs to one person in finance or operations. They know exactly where every number comes from, which file needs updating, and what to do when something doesn't match.

The problem is that the whole process depends on them.

We've seen similar challenges in operational environments, which is why we built systems that bring information such as vehicles, drivers, fuel, maintenance, and incidents together.

But here's something I've learned: building the software isn't always the difficult part.
Getting everyone to agree on which information is correct, who is responsible for it, and how it should be used can take much longer.

That's why I believe automation should start with understanding the actual process, not just replacing a spreadsheet with a new system. The goal isn't simply to remove Excel.

It's to make sure the business doesn't depend on one person doing the same manual work every month.

Which report in your company still depends on one person and a spreadsheet?
