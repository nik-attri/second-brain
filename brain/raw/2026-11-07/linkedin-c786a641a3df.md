🐄 Your Dairy Pen Floor Can Be a Major Cause of Lameness, Mastitis, and Restlessness in Your Dairy Herd!

Ground Grooming: Lessons from Nearly 40 Years of Hands-On Dairy Farm Management

When I walk through a dairy farm, one of the first things I inspect is the ground beneath the cows' feet.

Excellent genetics, balanced feeding, and modern milking equipment cannot compensate for an uncomfortable resting environment.

Look carefully at the pen floor in this photo.

It was originally hard, compacted clay soil. Through regular ploughing, harrowing, levelling, daily ground grooming, and manual removal of gravel and stones, we transformed it into a soft, loose, and comfortable resting surface for our dairy cows.

Here is my practical ground management routine:

✅ Plough hard clay floors twice a week. Break the compacted surface and mix the clay soil with accumulated manure to create a softer resting layer.

✅ Carry out ploughing while the cows are in the milking parlour. This allows the machinery to work efficiently without disturbing the herd.

✅ Harrow and level the ground immediately after ploughing. Never leave deep furrows, hard ridges, or uneven areas.

✅ Remove gravel and stones manually. After ploughing, farm workers must walk through the pen and collect exposed stones before the cows return. This simple practice helps prevent hoof injuries and lameness.

✅ Groom the ground daily. Maintain a loose, even surface and prevent hard, compacted areas from developing again.

✅ Keep the resting surface as dry as possible. Use ground turning, sunlight, airflow, proper drainage, and good water trough maintenance to reduce excessive moisture.

Why do I insist on this routine?

Because good ground management directly supports three essential aspects of dairy herd health:

🐄 Lameness prevention: A softer, level surface free from sharp stones reduces the risk of hoof trauma and discomfort.

🐄 Environmental mastitis prevention: Maintaining a drier, cleaner resting surface reduces udder exposure to manure, moisture, and environmental pathogens.

🐄 Cow comfort and reduced restlessness: Comfortable ground encourages cows to lie down, rest, and ruminate instead of spending excessive time standing or searching for a suitable resting place.

My advice to dairy farm managers:

Never evaluate ground grooming by the tractor's performance alone.

Walk through the pen yourself. Feel the ground under your boots, check for stones and wet spots, and observe how your cows settle down after returning from milking.

After nearly 40 years in dairy farm management, I remain convinced that some of the most valuable improvements in herd health and productivity begin with simple, disciplined field management.

A comfortable cow needs a comfortable place to lie down.

Good dairy management starts from the ground up.
