“It’s still cheaper for us to do it manually.”

That sounds reasonable — until someone calculates how much a manual process actually costs 💸

Let’s say an employee spends 15 minutes on a single task. At 20 tasks per day, that adds up to 5 working hours every day. Over a month, that’s 110 hours, or almost 14 full working days (assuming 22 working days). 

And that’s just the time spent completing the tasks themselves.

Now add the time spent searching for information, correcting errors, getting approvals, tracking statuses, and resolving issues when something gets lost between systems.

Suddenly, the cost of a BPM project looks quite different.

But there’s another side to the story: automating everything really can be expensive. If a process only runs a few times a month, changes constantly, or doesn’t create a significant workload, automation may simply not pay off.

So instead of asking, “How much does BPM cost?”, the better question is: “How much does this process cost us today, and how much could we save by automating it?” 🤔

A successful BPM project doesn’t start with a license or development. It starts with understanding the economics of the process.

Myth: BPM is expensive.  
Reality: Automating processes that don’t need automation is expensive. So is failing to automate those that clearly do.
