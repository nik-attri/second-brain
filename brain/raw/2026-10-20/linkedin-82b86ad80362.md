𝗙𝗥𝗢𝗠 𝗗𝗘𝗚𝗥𝗘𝗘 𝗧𝗢 𝗘𝗠𝗣𝗟𝗢𝗬𝗔𝗕𝗜𝗟𝗜𝗧𝗬 𝟮.𝟬

𝗬𝗼𝘂 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝗮 𝗱𝗲𝗴𝗿𝗲𝗲. 𝗦𝗼 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁?

This may sound harsh.
You have a BSc.
Perhaps an Honours.
Perhaps even an MSc.
𝗦𝗼 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁?
Your qualification matters.
It represents years of work, foundational knowledge and your ability to learn.
But increasingly, it is a foundation rather than a differentiator.
There are hundreds, sometimes thousands, of candidates with qualifications similar to yours.
So when I review a graduate CV for GSG, one of the questions I am really asking is:
𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝗽𝗲𝗿𝘀𝗼𝗻 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗱𝗼?
Can you analyse something?
Map something?
Write something?
Design something?
Model something?
Research something?
Automate something?
Coordinate something?
Communicate something complex clearly?
Turn information into a useful output?
Our education systems still place enormous emphasis on demonstrating what you 𝗸𝗻𝗼𝘄.
The labour market increasingly asks:
𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗱𝗼 𝘄𝗶𝘁𝗵 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗸𝗻𝗼𝘄?
This is also why I believe postgraduate education will have to evolve.
Where the discipline allows it, research should increasingly leave something useful behind.
A model.
A framework.
An algorithm.
A dataset.
A methodology.
A metric.
A prototype.
A policy instrument.
A decision-support tool.
A reproducible analytical workflow.
Not every discipline should produce software or a commercial product.
That isn't the point.
The question is:
𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗻𝗲𝘄 𝗸𝗻𝗼𝘄𝗹𝗲𝗱𝗴𝗲, 𝗰𝗮𝗽𝗮𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗼𝗿 𝘂𝘀𝗲𝗳𝘂𝗹 𝗮𝗿𝘁𝗲𝗳𝗮𝗰𝘁 𝗲𝘅𝗶𝘀𝘁𝘀 𝗯𝗲𝗰𝗮𝘂𝘀𝗲 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗱𝗶𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗶𝘀 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸?
Now look at your own CV.
Mentally remove your qualifications.
𝗪𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗲𝘃𝗶𝗱𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗺𝗮𝗶𝗻𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗰𝗮𝗻 𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗮𝘁𝗲 𝘃𝗮𝗹𝘂𝗲?
That may be one of the most important questions an early-career professional can ask.
𝗜𝗳 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗮𝗹𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗱𝘆 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝗶𝗻𝗴, 𝘀𝗵𝗮𝗿𝗲 𝗼𝗻𝗲 𝗽𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗮𝗹 𝗰𝗮𝗽𝗮𝗯𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗻𝗲𝗱 𝗼𝘂𝘁 𝘁𝗼 𝗺𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗲𝗿 𝗺𝘂𝗰𝗵 𝗺𝗼𝗿𝗲 𝗶𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝗰𝗮𝗿𝗲𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗵𝗮𝗻 𝘆𝗼𝘂 𝗲𝘅𝗽𝗲𝗰𝘁𝗲𝗱 𝘄𝗵𝗶𝗹𝗲 𝘀𝘁𝘂𝗱𝘆𝗶𝗻𝗴, so you can help someone  decide what to start learning next.
