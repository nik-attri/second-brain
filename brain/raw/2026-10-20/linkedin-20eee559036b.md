Drowning in a sea of digital noise?

Between the avalanche of emails, the relentless ping of instant messages, and jumping between multiple communication channels, our modern workplaces are constantly overloading our brains. The problem with this kind of work is that it rarely has a definitive "end." You close one task, and three more notifications take its place.

This weekend, I decided to escape the screen and tackle a physical problem instead.
I crawled under my decking to carry out a repair. It was dark, damp, cramped, and incredibly frustrating because I was fixing a job that hadn't been done properly the first time around. It wasn't glamorous, and it certainly wasn't comfortable.

But, when I crawled back out, I did feel a sense of tangible achievement — a sense of mental clarity.

Modern psychology calls this behavioral activation. When our brains are stuck looping through abstract, digital stress, engaging our hands in concrete, physical work completely interrupts the loop.

It turns out, one of the UK's former Prime Minister's relied on this exact strategy for survival.

During his "wilderness years" in the late 1920s, Winston Churchill faced immense political isolation and severe mental exhaustion. His solution? He spent his afternoons at his Chartwell estate doing hard, manual labour — specifically, laying bricks.
He treated it so seriously that he hired a professional to teach him the proper technique, averaged 200 bricks a day, and even officially joined the Amalgamated Union of Building Trades Workers.

People often ask: Did he ever finish?

Yes, he did. Churchill didn’t just play at construction; he successfully completed numerous permanent structures at Chartwell, including extensive garden walls, landscaping features, and a small cottage for his daughter. Those solid brick structures still stand today — physical monuments to a man who understood that a tired mind is best rested by an active body.
Politics and speeches vanished into thin air, but a brick wall was something he could see, touch, and finish.

If you are feeling overwhelmed by the corporate noise this week, don't just sit there trying to think your way out of it. Go fix a shelf, weed the garden, clean the garage, or repair the decking.
Sometimes, the fastest way to clear your mind is to put down the phone and pick up a tool.
