After a few years working away from the EA world, it's been fantastic to get back to a role that I really love - especially at such an incredibly exciting company like Raylo.

Back when I was training EAs, modern Artificial Intelligence was but a mere twinkle in a developer's eye... and we did everything from scratch. Sifting through pages of web results, spending hours writing meeting minutes, creating spreadsheets, manually building slide decks, reading and comparing 100s of restaurant reviews. I used to run training sessions on "time management" and how to get the most out of your limited day. This feels like a dated concept now. 

These days I use AI on a daily basis to make my life more efficient, - meaning I have more time to use my brain. I no longer have to block out 3 hours to remember "who said what?" in that very long offsite. Time management is so much easier now I have several agents at my fingertips to help do background work whilst I get on with the stuff I actually love - strategic thinking, building rapport with the team, clearing the inbox and ticking off my To Do list.

But it's not the be all and end all, and humans are still at the heart of this role. An AI will never understand the human dynamics at play, the nuances and minor details that make all the difference when providing supporting to an incredibly busy leader and their organisation.

Things I love AI for: note taking, building the bones of sheets/docs/projects, double checking my itineraries for errors (common), helping turn a blank page into the first step of an idea. 

Things I'm still old school about: writing my own emails (I don't want my writing skills to atrophy) calling people (it's amazing how much back and forth you save by just picking up the blower and comparing calendars), designing events (creativity comes from humans, and AI is where Art goes to die)

Assistants -  don't be afraid of AI. It's here to give you time back, and help ease your tired and overloaded brain.
