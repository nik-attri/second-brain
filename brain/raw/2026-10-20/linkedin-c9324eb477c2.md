I built something I never use....

It’s part of a workspace I’ve been building for myself. Which contains a to-do list, a calendar, an invoice builder, and since recently a simple tool to track my hours.

I wanted a better idea of how much time I actually spend building things, and on which projects.

Whenever I keep doing something manually or switching between tools, I try to make it easier. Slowly, that workspace is becoming a pretty good reflection of how I work. It saves me hours and, mostly, a lot of annoyance. 

But I also catch myself building things just because I can.

I built something I called a library. You could paste an article link or some text, and it would turn it into a clean reading view, without ads or distractions. You could save articles for later and pull out interesting ideas and context.

I really liked the idea. Still do, actually.

Yet I never use it.

I also built an hour tracker inside that workspace. I open that every day.

It’s hard to tell beforehand which ideas will become part of your routine. Something sounds useful in your head, you get excited about building it, and you skip the question of whether you actually need it.

I noticed that the clearest signal is often something you’re already doing every day that takes more effort than it should.

That’s also what we look for at Tandem.

- Where do the tools you use fall short of how you actually work?
- What do you keep copying between systems? 
- What small tasks keep annoying you?

Those are great starting points. So we spend time with people, understand how they actually work, and build around that.
