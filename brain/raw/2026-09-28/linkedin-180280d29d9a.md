I have spent a large part of my career around enterprise transformation, and I am increasingly convinced that AI is changing something many organisations still treat too narrowly: 

Capability building. 

In the decade that I spent at IBM, I saw large technology transformations up close. New platforms came in, processes changed, and enablement followed.

For years, that model was fairly predictable.
Whether the platform was SAP, Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Oracle, Workday or something else, capability building largely meant helping people understand the new process and use the new system.

Sometimes that meant job aids and walkthroughs. Sometimes sophisticated simulations and digital learning.

But the underlying question was usually the same:

"Can our people use the technology?" 

I don’t think that question is big enough anymore.

Because AI is not simply changing the interface. It is changing the work.

When enterprise applications begin to recommend, generate, summarise, predict, automate, and make parts of the workflow disappear altogether, people are no longer just learning a new system.

They are learning a new division of labour between human and machine.

For me, the shift looks like this:
  - From training people on the application
to preparing people for a different way of working.
  - From user adoption
to role and behaviour transformation.
  - From training after implementation
to capability built into transformation.

I’ve mapped the broader 2026–2030 shift in the visual attached to this post.

And this is the part I think matters most:

AI is not reducing the need for learning.
It is moving learning upstream.

Instead of waiting until implementation is complete and asking, “What training do people need?”, organisations need to ask much earlier:
1) What work should move to AI?
2) What should humans continue to own?
3) Where will judgement matter more, not less?
4) How will roles change?
5) What capabilities will managers need?
And, 
6) What support will people need in the flow of work?

Traditional application training will still matter. But it will no longer be sufficient.

The opportunity is moving from training people on technology to helping organisations redesign how people and technology perform together.

If you are a CHRO, CIO, transformation leader, enterprise application owner, or practitioner working across SAP, Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow, Oracle or Workday, I am curious:

Are you already thinking about this shift?
I would genuinely love to know where you are sitting on this shift? 

How are you preparing for it? Would you like to compare notes? 😊
