I don't have an agent swarm. You probably don't need one either.

I've got a folder of notes, about a dozen commands I wrote myself, and an assistant that argues with me.

Someone on that Naval podcast said the thing I've been too embarrassed to say out loud — that he deliberately ignored all of it. All the tricks, the scaffolding, the prompt engines, the "you must always use plan mode." His logic: the models will get better faster than he'll figure out how to use them.

I read that and felt a bit seen.

Here's the part nobody selling you an agent swarm will mention.

Earlier this year I set up an automated morning briefing. Ran flawlessly. Fired daily, zero failures, beautiful.

And I did almost none of the work in it.

The automation was never the problem. The briefing was just badly written — a list of things with no argument about which one mattered. So I'd read it, feel organised, and go do something else.

I didn't need another trigger. I needed the thing it produced to be worth acting on.

The uncomfortable version, for anyone about to spend a weekend on this: the assistant I use regularly talks me out of building things. It has told me a spreadsheet would do. It has told me the thing I wanted to automate happens four times a year.

Every person selling you agents has a reason not to say that.
