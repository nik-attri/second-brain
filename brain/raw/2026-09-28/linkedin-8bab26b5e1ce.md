Your Power BI career is a product, not just a job. And most people never ask who's buying it.

A job description tells you what you're expected to do. A product has to answer harder questions: who needs this, why this one instead of the ten alternatives, and what is it worth? Almost no Power BI developer asks those questions about themselves. They collect certifications, add tools to a resume, and hope the market figures out their value.
Look at how the people who advance fastest behave. They know exactly who benefits from their work, whether that's a CFO who needs faster close reporting or an ops head drowning in manual Excel. They're known for one sharp thing instead of "I do Power BI." They publish what they've built, so their track record is visible before anyone asks for it. And they treat feedback from stakeholders like product feedback: the fastest way to find out what to improve next.
Pricing follows from all of that. Nobody pays more for hours worked. They pay more for the size of the problem you solve, and for how confident they are that you'll solve it.
5 product habits for your career:
→ Know Your Buyer: learn who pays for your work and which problem keeps them up.
→ Clear Positioning: be known for one sharp thing, not "the Power BI person."
→ Ship In Public: projects and posts are release notes proving you actually deliver.
→ Collect Feedback: ask stakeholders what slowed them down, then iterate on yourself.
→ Raise Your Price: pay follows the size of the problem solved, not hours worked.
A job is something you do. A product is something people seek out.
