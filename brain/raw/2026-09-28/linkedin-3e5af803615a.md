Most companies don’t have an AI problem.
They have a workflow problem.
I keep seeing businesses add more AI tools while the underlying process stays exactly the same.
A new tool gets added.
Another dashboard appears.
Someone builds an automation.
But the team still copies data between systems, follows up manually, and spends hours on tasks that could have been handled automatically.
That’s where I think AI automation becomes genuinely useful.
Take a simple lead management process.
Instead of:
Lead comes in →
Someone checks it →
Researches the company →
Updates the CRM →
Writes an email →
Waits for approval →
Follows up manually.
You can build a workflow where:
Lead → AI research → Qualification → CRM update → Personalized draft → Human approval → Follow-up
The important part isn't the AI model.
It's the workflow around the model.
Good AI automation should answer questions like:
Where does the data come from?
What should AI decide?
What should it never decide?
When does a human need to review?
What happens when something fails?
How do we measure whether the automation actually helped?
That last question gets overlooked a lot.
Saving 20 minutes sounds great.
But if the workflow creates more errors, more reviews, or more complexity, you haven't really automated the problem.
The goal isn't to add AI to a business.
The goal is to remove unnecessary work from the business.
That's a very different approach to AI adoption.
What’s one repetitive process in your business you would automate first?
