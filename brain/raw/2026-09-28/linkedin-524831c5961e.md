🌐🤖 What if the next major users of the internet aren't humans?
The internet was built around how people interact with information.
We search.
We click.
We browse.
We compare.
We decide.
AI agents could change that model.
Instead of manually navigating websites, autonomous software can increasingly search for information, interact with digital services and complete tasks on our behalf.
That raises an interesting possibility:
The internet could have two audiences—humans and machines.
For AI agents, a beautiful interface isn't necessarily the priority.
They need:
🔗 Reliable APIs
📊 Structured information
⚡ Low-latency services
🔐 Strong authentication
🛡️ Clearly defined permissions
This could fundamentally influence how we build digital platforms.
Businesses have traditionally optimised websites for human users and search engines.
The next challenge may be optimising digital services for AI agents that decide what information to retrieve, which service to use and what action to take.
For Computer Science students, this makes some skills increasingly important:
💻 API & Systems Design
🤖 Agentic AI & Tool Calling
🌐 Distributed Systems
🔐 Identity & Access Management
🛡️ AI Security
The future internet may not simply be a collection of websites waiting for humans to click.
It could increasingly become an ecosystem where software interacts with software on our behalf.
And that creates a fascinating design question:
Should we redesign digital platforms for machines—or continue designing primarily for humans?
