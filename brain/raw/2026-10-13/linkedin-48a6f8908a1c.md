If we've built you new software and you're still spending Friday afternoon chasing the same things, I'd want to know why.
 
One of the nicest things my long term clients tell me is that I make their lives easier. That's what I want the work to do, beyond giving them another system to log into.
 
Before starting an improvement, I'd try finishing this sentence together:
 
‘This will be worth doing if we no longer have to ___.’
 
For example, chase three people to find out whether a job is ready to invoice. That gives us something concrete to improve and a way to check whether it worked.
 
A month after it's in use, I'd come back to that same question with the person doing the job. Are they still chasing? Can they trust the information, or are they checking everything manually just in case?
 
The benefit might be time back, fewer mistakes or being able to switch off without wondering what you've missed. It doesn't all need a stopwatch.
 
But I would count any new admin we've introduced too. If we've saved you half an hour by giving someone else an hour of checking, I'd struggle to call that a success.
