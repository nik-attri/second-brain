A woman I spoke to recently was considering leaving her marriage.
She said something that I haven't stopped thinking about.

“I've shrunk myself over the years.”

She'd spent so much time prioritising everyone else's needs that somewhere along the way, she'd stopped prioritising her own. Now she was looking at her finances and realising just how much she'd come to rely on someone else for her security.

She described seeking financial advice as a way of taking her power back.

And bloody hell, that stayed with me.

Because financial dependence doesn't always happen through someone deliberately taking control. Sometimes it happens slowly, through years of deferring decisions, putting yourself last and convincing yourself that someone else knows better.

Until one day, you want to make a different decision about your life and realise you don't know what your options are.

I don't expect every woman to love spreadsheets or understand investing. That's what advisers are here to help with.

But I want every woman to understand her financial position well enough to know that staying, leaving or changing her life is a choice she's making, not one she's making because she can't afford the alternative.
