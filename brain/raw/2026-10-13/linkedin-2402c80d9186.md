The biggest barrier to inclusive education is not a lack of will. It is a lack of time.

A recent article on the best edtech courses for career growth makes this clear: the future of teaching belongs to educators who can use technology to personalise learning at scale. The piece highlights how AI, adaptive learning, and smart classroom tools are moving from nice-to-have to essential, and how professional development in these areas is now a core part of career growth for teachers. For schools serving multilingual and mixed-ability classrooms, that shift cannot come soon enough.

At AdaptEd, we see this every day. Teachers want to differentiate, translate, and adapt, but doing it manually for 30 students is not sustainable. That is why we built an AI-powered platform for K to 12 teachers and schools working with linguistically diverse, multilingual, and mixed-ability classrooms. Teachers can customise curriculum, generate personalised learning paths, and adapt activities and assessments in real time, with one-click translation across 5 or more languages, alignment to provincial and national standards, and seamless LMS integration. No technical expertise required.

The article is right that upskilling matters. We would add that the tools themselves must do more of the heavy lifting.
