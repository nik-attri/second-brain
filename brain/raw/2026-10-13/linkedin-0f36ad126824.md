BUILD IN PUBLIC - ONE BUSINESS. TWO VERY DIFFERENT CUSTOMERS
How do you build one website for two completely different customers?

That's the problem sitting on my desk at the moment.

Last week I shared how were turning my wife and business partner Christine's corporate experience into the new Lifeworkz Movement ≠ Alignment Diagnostic™.

Creating the assessment has exposed another challenge.
Our own website.
Lifeworkz essentially serves two very different worlds.
On one side, we work with small and medium-sized businesses.
Their conversations with me tend to sound something like:
I need more customers.
My website isn't doing anything.
I don't understand all this AI stuff.
Nobody can find me on Google.
I'm spending too much time doing things manually.
Very practical problems.
Increasingly, our job is to help connect the pieces:
Content → Discovery → Website → Enquiry → CRM → Follow-up → Customer.

Then there's the other side of Lifeworkz.
Our corporate work and that's a very different conversation.
These organisations may already have:
Enterprise platforms.
CRM systems.
Customer experience technology.
Data teams.
Consultants.
Automation.
AI initiatives.
Transformation programmes.
Significant technology budgets.
Their problem isn't necessarily: “We need more technology.”
Sometimes it's almost the opposite.
Its: “We've bought all this technology. Why aren't we seeing the transformation we expected?”

Thats where Christine's: Movement ≠ Alignment™ comes in.
That's created a fascinating website-design problem for us because the small-business owner and the corporate executive could both arrive at:lifeworkz.co.uk.

They aren't arriving with the same problem.
They don't speak the same language.
They don't need the same solution.
And they certainly shouldn't be pushed through the same customer journey.
So we've had to take some of our own advice.
Stop designing pages for a moment and design the journeys first.
What does the SMB customer need to understand?
Where should they go?
What's their next step?
What does the corporate executive need to understand?
Where should they go?
Whats their next step?
Only then can we work out what the homepage needs to do.
And I've realised something through this process.
A homepage doesn't necessarily have to tell everybody everything.
Sometimes its job is simply to help the right person recognise: “Yes. Im in the right place.”
And then get them onto the right path.
So thats what were working on now.
One Lifeworkz.
Two audiences.
Two customer journeys.
And one website that needs to make the distinction obvious without making the company feel like two completely unrelated businesses stuck together.
That's the next part of our own rebuild.
And, as promised...were figuring it out in public.
