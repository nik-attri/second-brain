The gap between a brilliant CAD design and an error-free, scalable manufacturing process is historically one of the biggest friction points in advanced manufacturing. It’s where manual effort, spreadsheet hell, and human translation errors cost companies millions in delays, rework, and opportunity cost.

This is the exact pain point that AI is designed to solve. 

C-Infinity has launched AutoAssembler v4, an AI system that revolutionizes how companies plan automated assembly and manage engineering changes. This isn't just another CAD viewer; it’s an intelligence layer that automatically translates complex mechanical designs CAD files into executable Bills of Process BOPs and precise assembly plans.

Think about the traditional workflow: CAD designs are handed off, requiring engineers to manually build BOMs, sequencing steps, and process diagrams in spreadsheets—a time-intensive, non-standardized, and high-risk process. A single component change an Engineering Change Management or ECM often necessitates weeks of manual revision, re-sequencing, and cross-checking across multiple departments Design, Manufacturing, Quality, Supply Chain.

AutoAssembler v4 uses advanced AI to interpret the structural integrity and functional requirements of the CAD model, automatically generating a comprehensive, data-rich operational guide. It not only details what to build but also how and in what order it must be built—all while managing version control and change impacts proactively.

For Operations Directors and Plant Managers, this means dramatically accelerated time-to-market and a level of consistency previously unattainable without massive overhead. For Manufacturing Engineers and Product Designers, it liberates time spent on manual process documentation, allowing them to focus on optimization, yield improvement, and advanced process innovation Kaizen/CI.

If your business relies on complex, multi-component product lines, automating assembly planning is no longer a luxury—it’s a core operational necessity. We need to transition from manual, document-based processes to intelligent, data-driven manufacturing workflows.
