What if you could search for patterns in human behavior as easily as you search for information on Google?

It might sound a bit abstract, so let's look at a simple example.

Imagine you're driving home from work. Suddenly, a pedestrian steps in front of your car, and you have only a split second to react.

Some people immediately hit the brakes. Others instinctively turn the steering wheel. Some might react a little later. Similar situations can happen to different people around the world on the same day. And everyone might react differently.

But what if we could find, compare, and analyze all these situations?

Imagine having millions of hours of driving data. Instead of going through it all manually, you could simply search for situations where a driver had to react suddenly to a pedestrian. This is exactly the kind of technology we're working on at Mesocort AI.

We're developing tools that allow us to search for specific situations and behavioral patterns in our dataset of more than 50 million hours of human activity. This helps us move from massive amounts of raw data to the moments that actually matter. How do people make decisions, how do they react, and what influences their behavior in critical moments? That's where we see huge potential.
