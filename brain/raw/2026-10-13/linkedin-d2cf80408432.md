It was 21 year old me, freshly graduated, volunteering as a medic at a free clinic. The doctor who supervised us had just lost his practice manager of twenty years. He was sitting at the front desk complaining about it - he had no idea who could fill her shoes.

So I said: I can do that job.

I'd been working as a medical assistant for six months. I'd had some admin experience. I knew how to answer a phone. I knew the basics of how a spreadsheet worked. 

I had no idea what it took to run a medical practice - especially not one seeing private pay, workman's comp, Medicare, Medicaid, with hospital rounds. I didn't even know those things existed at the time I opened my mouth.

He looked at me for a solid minute. Then he said: I believe you can.

Next thing I knew, I was practice manager for a specialty physician's office. What started with a single doctor, single location, and half dozen physical therapists and aides became a multi-location, multi-physician practice within two years.

That's where I cut my teeth. Physicians, physical therapists, medical assistants, patients, insurance companies, the nurses who accompanied patients, all the petty dramas. 

It was formative. 

It was also my turbulent twenties, and I was trying to figure out who I was.

Here's what I know now: the young woman who said "I can do that" is still who shows up every day. 

I go in and say I can fix this. 
I can solve this. 
I know how to figure this out. 

I'm a problem solver, and I'm not scared to go into situations where I don't know shit from Shinola and figure it out. 

I built my whole career on exactly that. Hell, my whole life.

I figured out how to survive as a young adult in the San Francisco Bay Area with no outside support. 

I figured out how to get myself into UC Berkeley. 

I figured out how to integrate myself into a new arenas multiple times. 

That young woman still comes with me to every new engagement. The same energy, the same excitement, the same thrill I got jumping into Dr. B's practice, I get jumping into each new company that has a problem to figure out. Because at my core, I'm a figure-it-outer.
