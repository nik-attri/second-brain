Alexander Wang says along ~200 people worked on Muse at Meta across product/model since February. 

That's even though all Meta executives agreed it would be one of the most important launches the company would have this year. 

The prototype took 1–2 weeks, and shipping it took around 7 months.

The small team focused primarily on making the agent reliable.

They tracked 100 specific agent behaviors were tracked in a spreadsheet, and defined what "launch ready" meant for each of them with an eval. 

They all went green when they released the Muse Spark 1.3 model to power Muse under the hood. 

Wang says OpenClaw caught lightning in a bottle but broke too often, so users just gave up.

The key here is that magic tech also needs to be dependable.

What I love is that they did it with 200 people. That sounds like a lot, until you remember they have 70K+ FTEs.

Wang says they deliberately kept the team small and focused, so that they wouldn't get an average of everyone's opinion slammed into the final product. 

“And it’s sort of like PM hell in some sense, where every PM has a thing that they’re trying to get into the product and jam into the product, and then all of that kind of blends together into this smoothie almost.”

You can see it in the final result. Muse doesn't feel like a big company product. It feels like a small team put a lot of love into it.

As Wang puts it: “There’s one cohesive experience that we’re trying to deliver to the world.”

And to top it off, Muse is tracking to have retention on par with ChatGPT's early results.
