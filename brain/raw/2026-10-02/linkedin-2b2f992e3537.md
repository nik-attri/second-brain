Life has a way of putting things into perspective.
A while ago, I walked away from a job and started building.
I started building my own brand, working on my own products, talking to businesses about problems I could help solve, and even got a few people willing to work with me.
Things were moving.

Then I had an emergency that resulted in surgery.
I'm used to always building, always moving and always thinking about what I can do next.
This time, I simply had to stop.
And I think the break gave me more clarity about something I was already becoming very interested in before I left my job:

How can I use technology to make things work better?
Not just building a feature because it needs to be built, but understanding the entire workflow around it.
Where is someone wasting time?
What are they doing manually that could be automated?
Where is the customer experience frustrating?
What are we building that doesn't actually need to be complicated?
And sometimes, what shouldn't we build at all?
That's the kind of engineering I want to keep doing.
I'm back to building now.
I'm continuing to build my own products, and I'm open to working with teams as a Senior Software / Product Engineer.

I'm also working with companies and service businesses that want to simplify their operations, automate repetitive workflows or turn an idea into a useful product.
I wrote more about the last few months, the pause, and what I'm building towards here:
I Had to Stop Building to Understand What I Wanted to Build
https://lnkd.in/dPnQ9h9a

I think sometimes stopping doesn't necessarily give you a completely new direction.
Sometimes it just makes you more certain about where you were already going.
And I'm building again.
