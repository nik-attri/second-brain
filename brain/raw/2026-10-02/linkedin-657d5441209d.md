We fucked up our launch massively.

At least, that’s how it felt.

For 9 months, we built Merkion in a quiet room. We ran workshops in Valencia, got early testers excited, and built up all this internal momentum.

Then September 15th came. We officially opened the doors and put out the standard social posts: "Hey, the tool is live. You can test it now."

The market’s response? 
A collective, echoing "Who cares?"

We got two test signups.

And our very first "paying customer" was actually my co-founder, who literally forgot to remove his real credit card after testing the checkout flow. 

Cheers to that major milestone.

For two weeks straight, I sat there staring at the screen, spiraling. 
What are we doing wrong? 
Why is nobody signing up? 
Did we just spend 9 months building something in a vacuum?

Then, 2.5 weeks in, entrepreneurship pulled its classic trick.

Out of nowhere, two separate entrepreneurs reached out. They didn't ask for a software demo. They didn't ask about features. 
They just said, "We saw how you guys show up online. Can you help us build our personal brands?"

So there we were. Three weeks after panicking over zero software signups, sitting down to send out two service proposals worth five-figures. And one project could be a major Project around Europe.

This whole game is weird as hell.

You draw up a clean, linear plan: 
Build product - launch product - get signups.

Reality doesn't give a shit about your plan. You sit there thinking you failed because Door A stayed locked, but if you're actually showing up and putting out work, the market will just smash through Door B and pay you for something you didn't even put on the landing page.

It's messy, it makes zero sense on a spreadsheet, and I’m still taking my co-founder's credit card off file.
