What happens to PV when a biotech gets its first approval? 

For years, safety has been about clinical trials: SUSARs, DSURs, the IB and a safety review committee. Then the approval lands, and almost overnight the PV world looks completely different. Here's what changes: 

A QPPV becomes a legal requirement. In the EU, the marketing authorisation holder needs a qualified person responsible for pharmacovigilance, plus a back-up, from day one. 

The PV system needs to be inspection-ready. That means a Pharmacovigilance System Master File, SOPs, vendor agreements and quality oversight, all of which regulators can inspect at any time. 

Aggregate reporting ramps up. PSURs or PBRERs replace DSURs as the main periodic report, on timelines set by the regulators. 

The RMP becomes a living commitment. Risk minimisation measures move from paper to practice, and companies have to show they're working. 

Signal management goes continuous. Spontaneous reports, literature and EudraVigilance data all need regular monitoring, not just trial data. 

Case volumes can multiply. Real-world use brings far more reports than any trial, from healthcare professionals, patients and partners. 

What does this mean for hiring? The biggest mistake I see is companies starting to recruit when the approval arrives. By then, its already late. Senior PV hires can take months to land, and the QPPV needs to be in place before launch, not after. 
The companies that get it right tend to: 

start planning PV headcount 6–12 months before the expected approval 

secure their QPPV and Deputy QPPV first, often on a freelance basis to begin with 

bring in a PV manager or operations lead to build the system and oversee vendors 

add safety scientists and signal management expertise as post-marketing data grows 

use freelance support to bridge gaps while permanent hires come on board.
