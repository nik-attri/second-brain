Looking back at this today, the message hasn't just held up, but I believe it has become a dividing line in reinsurance technology.

Back in 2024, the market was already drowning in reports about massive digital budgets yielding frustratingly small improvements. The issue was simple: reinsurers were using cutting-edge technology to pave over legacy cow paths.

We were spending millions digitising existing manual workflows rather than asking how our underlying business models should evolve. ♻️

Looking at where the market sits now, that distinction is clearer than ever.

Tech adoption gets you faster PDF summarisation and slightly slicker email triage. It’s:

☑️ Defensive
☑️ Incremental
☑️ Easily copied by competitors

Business model innovation uses those same tools to rethink how risk is underwritten, priced, and syndicated, opening up new revenue streams and changing how capital attaches to risk.

Incremental steps are fine, provided they are headed in the right direction. But if your transformation strategy is still treated as an IT upgrade rather than a business-led reimagining of how you trade, you will only ever capture marginal efficiency gains.
Technology is, and should only ever be, the engine. The business model is what should dictate the destination.

Re-reading this piece today, my core advice to reinsurance leaders remains the same: rather than asking how technology can automate yesterday’s administration, begin by asking how it can power tomorrow's model.
