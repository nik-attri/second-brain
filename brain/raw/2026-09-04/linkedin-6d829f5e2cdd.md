Data Privacy law and compliance cost for smaller enterprises


India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) is a landmark step toward a robust data privacy framework. But as organisations move towards compliance, the cost of compliance for millions of micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs), NGOs and startups is significant. Under the DPDP Act, they are “Data Fiduciaries” subject to the same compliance obligations as large corporations: purpose limitation, consent management, data breach notification, grievance redressal mechanisms, and more.
A one-size-fits-all regime risks turning data protection from a safeguard into a barrier to entry.
China’s approach offers an instructive model - China recently finalised the 'Simplified Measures for Personal Information Protection by Small-Scale Personal Information Processors' (《小型个人信息处理者个人信息保护简化规定》). The regulation recognises that small-scale processors face fundamentally different risk profiles. Instead of blanket exemptions, it provides a calibrated set of simplified obligations:
• Simplified templates for privacy policies and consent notices, reducing the need for bespoke legal drafting.
• Streamlined personal information impact assessments — lighter-touch evaluations instead of the full-scale assessments required of major processors.
• Relaxed record-keeping and internal governance requirements, acknowledging that a five-person firm cannot maintain the same documentation apparatus as a tech giant.
• Practical guidance and model rules issued by the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) to help small processors comply without hiring specialised counsel.
The underlying philosophy is proportionality: the intensity of regulation should match the scale and risk of data processing.
Solutions for India - The DPDP Act already empowers the Central Government to prescribe rules and the Data Protection Board to issue directions. Section 17(3) specifically allows the government to notify different classes of Data Fiduciaries based on factors such as the volume and nature of personal data processed. Central Government would do well to use its powers under Section 40 of the DPDP Act to introduce simplified compliance measures for MSMEs, NGOs and Startups. Such regulations may have standardised templates, extended compliance timelines, reduced penalties and government backed toolkits.
