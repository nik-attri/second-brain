I almost didn’t take the n8n Academy track.

At the time, I already had client workflows running, weekly build challenges to complete, and a portfolio site to finish. Adding four courses to that list felt unnecessary.
Three weeks later, I’m glad I did. I completed all 4 n8n Academy certificates:
→ n8n Quickstart
→ Essentials: Your First Workflows
→ Integrations: APIs & Connected Workflows
→ AI, Testing & Best Practices

But the certificates weren’t the most valuable part. The real value was discovering where I had been building from intuition instead of understanding.
I learned that there were API patterns I was using without fully understanding why they worked. Testing strategies I had never properly formalized. AI workflow practices I was applying inconsistently. And small implementation details that can make the difference between a workflow that works in a demo and one you can confidently put into a real business.

That distinction matters to me, because if you hire an automation builder, you don't need someone who can simply connect a few nodes and make a workflow run once. You need someone who thinks about:
• What happens when the API fails?
• What happens when the data is incomplete?
• How do we prevent duplicate actions?
• How do we test the workflow before it touches real customers?
• What happens when the AI gives an unexpected response?
• How do we make the workflow maintainable after it's deployed?

That's the kind of builder I'm working toward becoming. My journey into automation started after medical laboratory science, so this hasn't been a straight career path.

Every project, challenge, and certification is another piece of evidence that the pivot is becoming a real skill set. Four certificates completed. More importantly, fewer blind spots, and I'm still building.

If your business has a repetitive process that your team is still handling manually, I'm interested in seeing it.
