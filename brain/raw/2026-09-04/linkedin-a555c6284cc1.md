This was pre internet, so when I didn't know something, there was no search bar to save me.

I was handed manuals. Huge ones. I remember being asked to read up on DOS, and on something called NetBEUI, which I promise I am not making up. Nobody explained why it mattered. I was just told to know it, so I sat with these books on evenings and weekends and tried to make sense of pages that may as well have been written in another language.

There's something almost quaint about it now, learning technology from paper, but at the time it just felt like drowning slowly, one manual at a time.

Seneca wrote, It is not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste a lot of it.

I didn't want to waste the chance I'd been given standing at that open door. So the manuals got read, slowly, badly at first, and then a little less badly.

What's the least glamorous way you ever had to learn something that mattered?
