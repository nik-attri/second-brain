AI is on every bloody timeline. Then you get to work and wing it.

Your company bought the licences and said the same thing as every other company: "do more AI."

Then suits checked the usage dashboards. They couldn’t find the business impact and wondered what went wrong.

Here's what went wrong.

We handed knowledge workers a fighter jet and said "off you go, Sydney to New York in four hours, you'll be right."

No instructor or checklist. Just cross your fingers and hope for the best.

Now the work all looks the same and everyone complains about it. Slides and docs smell the same too, in that same confident tone that says nothing. Unfortunately, someone has to read it.

Copy-paste never felt so good. Reading it never felt worse.

A manager told me last month: "I asked for his thoughts. I got Claude's."

You can't blame yourself.

The training was a 90-minute all-hands and a demo.

After that, you were expected to be good at it.

The people who figured it out the hard way did so by accident, months later.

Meanwhile, producing average work became free, and everybody can do it.

The model is the jet and you're the pilot. But you never learned to steer it.

If I ask you, "Buy me a loaf of bread from the supermarket", what will you buy? White bread? Brown bread? Sliced bread? Half a loaf or a full loaf?

Exactly.

You can't be vague with a model and expect it to magically guess what you want.

Brief the model like a new hire. Give it the context it's missing: the reader, the history, the things only you know. Don't do everything in one endless conversation. Use review checklists BEFORE you share your work.

None of this is prompt engineering. It won't expire when the next model launches.

These are fundamentals that will outlast the tools.

In school you learned reading, writing, and arithmetic.

This is the fourth subject, and nobody offered it.

Over 4 weeks, you can learn to use AI better than 99% of people.

Or you can do nothing and keep producing the same average work as everyone else.

The models keep getting better, which means the average gets better.

How will you stand out?

If nothing changes, nothing changes.
