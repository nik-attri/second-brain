If your organization struggles with competing priorities, unclear resource ownership, overloaded teams, or projects that seem to fight for the same people and budget, you are not alone.
That is exactly where an RMO—Resource Management Office—can make a meaningful difference.
In this second excerpt of What the Hell is an RMO (and why should I care)?! I continue breaking down what an RMO actually is, why it matters beyond a spreadsheet or staffing report, and how it can help organizations move from reactive resourcing to intentional decision-making.
An RMO is not simply about assigning people to work. At its best, it creates visibility, accountability, governance, and a clearer connection between strategic priorities and the resources required to deliver them.
Because when everything is a priority, resources are stretched thin, and no one has a complete view of capacity, the business is not operating strategically—it is operating in survival mode.
If that sounds familiar, this book was written for you.

Here is the 2nd excerpt:
If resource management is the art of putting the right people on the right work at the right time, governance is the part where you decide which work deserves those people in the first place.
Without governance and prioritization, the RMO becomes the “make it fit” department for every request, no matter how unrealistic. Every project is “critical.” Every initiative is “strategic.” Every leader’s ask is “top priority.” And you’re sitting there with one Alex, three programs, and four vice presidents who all believe they own Alex’s soul.
This chapter is about putting structure, and a bit of humor, around that chaos so you stop staffing by loudest voice and start staffing by shared rules.

Why Governance Matters (Beyond Looking Fancy on Slides)
Governance sounds intimidating: councils, boards, frameworks, criteria.
In practice, it’s much simpler:
Governance is how you decide which work gets scarce resources first. Prioritization is how you make those decisions in a way that doesn’t rely purely on volume, politics, or who sends the most “checking in” emails.
