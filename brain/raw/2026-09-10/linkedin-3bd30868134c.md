One pattern I’ve noticed across services companies I’ve spoken to and worked with over the past several months feels counterintuitive at first. 
 
Companies that have sales teams reliably generating new revenue often, if not usually, have systems and tech stacks that are less “current”, “efficient” and conspicuously lacking in AI. 
 
Companies hitting a brick wall in their efforts to build a sales motion that doesn’t rely on the founder(s) often have the lot – using Clay, modern CRMs, call recordings with AI analysis, automation, AI integrations and workflows. They are aware of what’s on the market and possible with the latest tech. 
 
Not much in the way of new revenue though. 
 
While another services company has a sales department working with a custom CRM built over a decade ago by someone in semi-retirement who still hosts the codebase and has to be (expensively) cajoled into keeping it just about strapped together and operating. Oh, and no data “export” function so the dev also has to be contacted to do that manually. Ability to connect to external tools and systems via API/MCP? Nope. 
 
Manual data entry between silo-ed systems is eating up half the sales team’s time. 
 
But they’re selling. Booking new accounts and revenue every month. 
 
They know their tech stack and workflows are antique and are moving to change that. But they haven’t so far. It was a nagging annoyance, not the priority. Because they were selling. 
 
When you’re not selling, it’s easy to spend 3-6 months updating the GTM tech stack, going “AI native”, choosing, training on and tracking adherence to a new acronym-based sales framework. “When we’re using all the best tools and leveraging best-practice GTM system, automation, and sales methodology we’ll be selling!”
 
Unfortunately that wasn’t the problem. But on the bright side, implementing the shiny tech stack was months-worth of ‘positive progress” updates at meetings. Always handy, when there aren’t many (or any) sales.
