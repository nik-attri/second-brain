I didn't spend years studying for the CPA exam to become obsolete.
 
So when I hear "AI is coming for accounting," I used to feel defensive.
 
Now I feel something else: relief that I have the license, not anxiety about it.
 
Here's why 2026 is actually a good year to be a CPA — if you play it right:
 
→ The credential is becoming the differentiator, not the entry point. Firms are hiring non-CPAs for prep and data work. Your CPA now signals judgment, accountability, and the ability to sign off on something that matters — that's harder to automate, not easier.
 
→ "Ticking and tying" is disappearing. Good riddance. Automation is absorbing reconciliations, transaction matching, and variance analysis. That was never the part of the job that used your license anyway.
 
→ Advisory is where the money — and the interesting work — is moving. Compliance margins are getting squeezed. The CPAs growing their books fastest are the ones offering fractional CFO work, M&A support, and strategic guidance, not just filing returns.
 
→ The exam pipeline is shrinking, and that's a pricing signal. Fewer candidates sitting for the CPA exam plus rising retirements means fewer licensed accountants for growing demand. Basic supply and demand favors you.
 
→ AI fluency is the new technical skill. Not knowing how to use it isn't a personality trait anymore — it's a competitive disadvantage. The CPAs treating AI like a junior staffer, not a threat, are pulling ahead.
 
The CPA who reviews, judges, and advises is becoming more valuable, not less.
 
The CPA who just processes is the one who should be worried.
 
Fellow CPAs — where is your practice actually spending time these days: still deep in compliance work, or have you started shifting toward advisory?
 
Genuinely curious how far along everyone is.
