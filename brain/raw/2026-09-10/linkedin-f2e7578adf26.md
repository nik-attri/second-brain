AI could kill humans. And no, this isn't a sci-fi movie plot.

A researcher who spent the last three years working on pretraining at OpenAI and Anthropic just resigned from Anthropic because he believes the AI race is becoming dangerously irresponsible.

His argument isn't that AI is evil.

It's that we're building systems that could eventually become far more capable than humans, while we still don't know how to reliably make their goals align with ours. 

What's even more unsettling?

People inside these labs are saying this.

Anthropic's own alignment lead, Evan Hubinger, has publicly said he believes there is a >10% chance that AI could cause human extinction within the next decade. 

Now, does that mean AI will kill us?

No.

And I don't think anyone can honestly claim they know that.

But that's precisely why I think we should pay attention.

When the people building the technology are telling us:

“We don't fully know how this ends.”

Then maybe we should take the risk seriously before we find out the hard way.

We spend so much time discussing how AI will change our jobs, automate our workflows and make us more productive.

Maybe we also need to talk about who is responsible when increasingly autonomous systems become capable of making consequential decisions that humans can't fully predict.

It is what makes the future of AI worth building.
