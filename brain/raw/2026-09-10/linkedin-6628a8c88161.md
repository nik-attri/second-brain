There is no such thing as original thought.

Which is probably news for everyone with INNOVATION in their job title.

Your brain doesn’t manufacture ideas from some pristine intellectual wilderness.

It takes everything you’ve seen, heard, read, experienced, copied, misunderstood and half-forgotten, fires neurons across existing networks, and recombines the pieces.

We call that thinking.

Companies call it innovation and appoint a Vice President to manage it.

Then come the workshops.

The Post-it notes.

The “ideation sessions.”

The inspirational keynote.

The innovation hub with suspiciously expensive furniture.

The consultant explaining that we need to “think differently.”

And, inevitably, a PowerPoint showing a light bulb.

Six months later, everyone is still filling in the same spreadsheet.

Because real innovation is usually much less glamorous.

It’s process improvement.

Take something that already exists and makes it simpler.

Remove a step.

Remove a meeting.

Remove a person whose entire job is moving information between two other people.

Use less material.

Waste less time.

Automate the stupidity.

Make the product easier to manufacture.

Make the building easier to construct.

Make the software require fewer clicks rather than adding an AI button nobody asked for.

Human progress has largely been thousands of years of people looking at existing things and saying:

“Why the hell are we still doing it like this?”

That’s innovation.

Not originality.

Not disruption.

Not an Innovation Lab.

Just the relentless removal of unnecessary stupidity.

Which is probably why it’s so difficult to turn into a two-day workshop.
