When I was young, I saw my dad (EZIO DOMENIGHINI) working tirelessly building Excel models for his insurance job.
 
At the time I had no idea how to make use of any of those formulas. But later, when I started working, it all made sense.
 
He never showed off his skills.
 
He was just working, but what I was watching was someone who treated every number like it had consequences.
 
He structured his files so that those numbers would take care of themselves and would transform endless columns and rows of numbers into usable information.
 
I understood the discipline.
 
Years later, in my first planning role, my manager told me to check the data line by line…Manually.
 
My face dropped.
 
I told myself I really do not want to do that. I would rather go and make coffee. I already knew there was a better way, because I had watched my dad do it at home for years.
  
Data isn't abstract, and every number belongs to someone. If it is wrong, that person makes a wrong decision on a Monday morning.
 
At Inference Chain, a RELEX Solutions implementation parter, it is the first thing we check.
 
The data that sits underneath everything, because the planner should focus on what is actionable, not on data cleansing.
 
My dad never worked in supply chain or data management, and yet he unknowingly taught me the fundamentals of data governance and the possibilities of automation on Excel.
 
This baseline best practice now allows me to have that coffee while systems do the calculations for me.
