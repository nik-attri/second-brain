Most teams treat a data migration like an event. The best ones I have seen were almost disappointing.

There is a date in the calendar, a war room, and food ordered for Saturday night. And a long checklist that gives everyone some sense of control.

I have seen this setup many times. And honestly, it can feel productive because there is so much activity around it.

But the question I care about now is not: "Are we ready for Saturday?"

It is: "Could we run the same migration again on Tuesday?"

Because something will be wrong in the first run. A field will be mapped incorrectly. The spreadsheets and databases will hold a case nobody expected. A number will not reconcile.

The important part is what happens next.

Do people start correcting records manually in the target system? Or do you fix the rule, rerun the migration, and get a better result?

That is where most of the real preparation should go. Not into surviving one heroic weekend. Into building a process that can be repeated until the result is right.

This is what I meant by disappointing.

No all-nighter. No dramatic war room photo. On Monday, the client logged in, checked the numbers, and carried on working.

That is the outcome you want: not a successful weekend, but a Monday nobody needs to talk about.
