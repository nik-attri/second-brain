WHY WE STOPPED MAKING EINSTEINS  

Erik Hoel has a great essay called "Why We Stopped Making Einsteins." 

Almost every genius in history was tutored one on one, and we stopped doing that.  In 1984 Benjamin Bloom showed tutored students did TWO standard deviations better than kids in a classroom. Nothing else in education comes even close. And now we give tutoring to the kid who is failing, almost never to the kid who is on fire...  Every aspirant to learning (school children, medical students, undergraduates) now have more information on a phone than anyone ever had, and we are not drowning in geniuses. What is missing is a PERSON.  A very singular kind of person. 

Medicine is one of the few places real tutoring survived largely untouched until recently. Nobody learned to advance a catheter into a vein or artery, or read a vascular waveform from a technical manual or text. Those of us fortunate enough to learn it did so with someone behind ud watching us critically saying "No. Wrong, do it Again." What they give us was experience and judgement, and no-one can download that.  All board prep now is a series of question banks. Scores go up, and then we get trainees or neophytes who can pick the right answer out of five but cannot tell me what they are looking at on the screen or what to do next.  Most "personalized learning" software is the same rote curriculum at a ‘personalized’ speed. It gets every apprentice to the same mediocritized place, and the talented ones end up as bottom dwellers there too.  

I use AI every single day. But we already gave every child and adult the entire library without a guide. A chatbot will never be disappointed in or want to improve you, and that matters more than people want to admit...  So please: if you are good at something, take on one student and let them stand next to you while you work. Find a worthwhile apprentice. Apprentices, find a worthy mentor. Parents, please do not outsource your responsibilities. And schools, perhaps please put some of the tutoring money taxpayers give you, on the kids who are truly on fire?  I did not learn cardiovascular medicine from a curriculum. I learned it from a handful of people ( my gurus) who let me stand next to them and told me when I was in error and how to correct it. But… I accepted constructive criticism and was willing to act on it.
