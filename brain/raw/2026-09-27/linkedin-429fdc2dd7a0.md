You ever have one of those nights where you cannot sleep, so your brain decides 1:47 a.m. is the perfect time to reopen every memory you thought had been archived?

That was me the other night. I was lying there staring at the ceiling, exhausted, while my brain reopened files. Suddenly I remembered jobs I left years ago, people I have not talked to in forever, interviews I thought I forgot, mistakes I made, things I should have said, things I definitely should not have said, and random conversations from years ago that apparently needed review. Sleep was gone. My inner raccoon had taken over the night shift.

It is funny how memories work. During the day you are busy. You move, work, answer messages, solve problems, laugh, and keep going. But at night, when everything gets quiet, your brain starts playing the director’s cut of your life with deleted scenes nobody asked for. I remembered people who once meant everything to me and are now strangers. Coworkers I spent more time with than my own family. Managers who taught me what kind of leader I never wanted to be. People who believed in me, and people who doubted me so loudly that I started believing them too.

Then came the career memories. The promotions I wanted, the jobs I thought would change everything, and the rejections that felt personal. The toxic bosses. The good bosses. The meetings where I sat quietly even though I knew I should have spoken up. The times I spoke up and immediately realized silence had some underrated benefits. At 2:16 a.m., my brain was reviewing my existence. Needs improvement: sleep. Exceeds expectations: overthinking.

But somewhere between the embarrassing memories and the things that still hurt, I realized something. I have survived every version of myself that I thought was failing. Every job that ended eventually became a story. Every rejection that felt devastating eventually became something I could laugh about. Every person who walked away created space for someone else. Every mistake taught me something, even if the lesson arrived wearing steel toed boots.

We spend so much time looking forward that sometimes we forget to look back and see how far we actually traveled. Not everything worked out the way I planned. Thank God. Some of my plans were terrible. If life had given me everything I wanted ten years ago, I would probably be sitting in a conference room somewhere defending a spreadsheet I hate while pretending a mandatory team building exercise changed my life.

So yes sometimes my brain keeps me awake and rewinds the memories. Some make me laugh. Some still sting. Some make me wonder what the hell I was thinking. But they all belong to me. They are proof that I lived, failed, learned, changed, kept moving, and gave my inner raccoon plenty of material.

Eventually I smiled, rolled over, and started falling asleep. Then my brain whispered, “Remember that embarrassing thing you said in 2009?” The raccoon opened one eye. And we were back in business.
