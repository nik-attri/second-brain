In February 2026, Building and Construction Authority Singapore established the Action Team to Improve Built Environment Productivity to identify practical measures that can help the sector achieve greater savings in time, cost and manpower. 

Since then, the focus has continued to move towards practical technology adoption.
In July, BCA announced further measures to accelerate innovation and technology adoption, including support for robotics and automation. 

In September, BCA announced a new Whole-of-Government virtual inspection process. Preliminary findings from pilot projects indicate that it can deliver up to 30% manpower savings for project parties, while phased submissions can enable TOP approval up to two months earlier than the conventional physical inspection process. 

The direction is clear:

Technology is expected to create measurable productivity gains. But there is another question project teams need to ask.

Where is productivity actually being lost?

TIME
How much time goes into searching for information, following up on approvals, coordinating between teams or manually compiling reports?

COST
Where do disconnected workflows contribute to rework, delays, duplicated effort or avoidable project administration?

MANPOWER
How many people are still spending valuable hours on repetitive processes that could be streamlined or automated?

These are not necessarily dramatic problems. They are often small points of friction repeated across hundreds or thousands of project activities.

And that is why digitalisation should not be measured only by how many digital tools a project uses. It should also be measured by what those tools actually remove from the workflow.

As we enter the final quarter of 2026, perhaps the more useful productivity question for every project is:

What are we still spending time, money and manpower on that technology could help us do differently?
