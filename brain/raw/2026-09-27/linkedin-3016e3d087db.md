I honestly do not agree that all this information should be shared, due to patient information breach, but I did not want to alter the research outcome.

These 10 pieces SHOULD flow automatically - but don't, costing hours every week:

1. NHS Number & demographics - no single source of truth for matching.
2. Medications & allergies - SCR core is meds + allergies, but care homes can't pull live into MAR.
3. Long-term conditions & diagnoses - extra info in SCR only if added with consent.
4. Immunisations & child health - sits in GP/CHIS, not in care home care plan.
5. Safeguarding flags - CP-IS connects LA to NHS unscheduled care for Child Protection Plan & Looked After Children, but rarely surfaces in care home systems.
6. Discharge & clinic letters - "We often receive NHS information too late to plan care safely"
7. Therapy plans - physio, OT, SALT referrals and outcomes siloed in portals.
8. EHCP health input - health provision for Education Health & Care Plans still shared manually.
9. Consent & escalation - parental responsibility, ReSPECT/DNACPR as scanned PDFs, not structured data.
10. Care home observations - daily obs/seizure logs can't flow back. Health Call was designed to integrate with NHS Trust/GP systems, while leaving care homes to manually input data.

Result: 67% of providers "rarely or only sometimes" receive complete patient info from NHS. So staff chase data that should be automatic: "We spend too much time chasing information that should come automatically".

Fix isn't one mega-record. It's standards like PRSB Urgent Transfer from Care Home to Hospital actually implemented with FHIR APIs.

What's your #11 that wastes most admin time?

Refs: CareLineLive State of Home Care Report 2025; Care England & Digital Social Care evidence to Parliament 2020; PRSB; NHS England CP-IS programme.
