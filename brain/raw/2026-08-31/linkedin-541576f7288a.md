What if your best graduate hire is someone your job description is not currently attracting?

When companies recruit graduates, they naturally start with a picture of the person they think will succeed.

A particular degree. A 2:1. Some relevant experience. Perhaps an internship or industry knowledge.

None of those requirements is necessarily unreasonable. But add enough together and you can end up describing the candidate you expect to hire, rather than asking what somebody actually needs to be capable of doing to succeed.

That distinction matters, particularly in graduate recruitment.

We are hiring people near the beginning of their careers. We are not simply assessing what they have already done. We are trying to understand what they could become.

And there is evidence that graduate recruitment is already moving in this direction.

According to the Institute of Student Employers, the proportion of surveyed employers requiring a 2:1 has fallen from 71% to 47% over the last decade. Meanwhile, those with no minimum entry requirements have more than doubled, from 11% to 23%.

That is a significant shift.
Not because academic achievement no longer matters. For some roles it absolutely does. But because skills, aptitude, motivation and the ability to learn matter too.

So perhaps we need to ask a different question:
What capabilities does someone genuinely need to succeed in this role, and could they have developed them somewhere we were not expecting?

A History graduate can be highly analytical. A Psychology graduate can understand data and human behaviour. A Geography graduate can be an excellent problem solver.

The capability is valuable. The degree is simply one route through which they may have developed it.

My argument in this week's GradWorx article is simple:
Do not lower the bar. Define the bar better.

Because your best graduate hire may have studied something different. Their experience may look different. Their CV may not contain the keywords you expected.

They may not look like your job description. 
They might also be exactly the person your business needs.
