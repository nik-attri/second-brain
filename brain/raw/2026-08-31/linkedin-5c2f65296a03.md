The most profitable automation I've shipped didn't have a single chatbot.

Everyone wants the flashy agent demo. Nobody wants to talk about the 4-person team that becomes 1.

Last quarter, I rebuilt a client's order-to-cash process on n8n and Make.com. No AI magic. No chat UI. Just 14 connected workflows that replaced manual data entry, approval chases, and a spreadsheet from hell.

The result? Their operations team went from 4 people to 1. Same volume. Zero missed SLAs. And the one person left doesn't do data entry — they handle exceptions.

That's the ROI nobody puts on a slide deck.

Automation isn't about replacing humans with robots. It's about removing the repetitive 80% so the 20% that needs judgment gets real attention.

If you've ever tracked automation ROI, what metric mattered most — headcount, cycle time, or error rate? Curious how others measure it.
