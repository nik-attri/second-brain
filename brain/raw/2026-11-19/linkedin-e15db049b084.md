What is holding back AI in luxury? Data quality.

A study published in 2026 by Bain & Company and the Comité Colbert taught us that luxury houses are lagging behind their own clients when it comes to AI adoption.

While discussing the opportunity of integrating AI agents into CRM management with the leadership of a luxury brand, I received a candid admission: "We have serious doubts about the quality of our data. It is better to start with another topic."

This answer raises a strategic issue that companies should not underestimate.

Artificial intelligence does not create knowledge ex nihilo. It uses, connects, structures, and interprets the information it is given access to. If that information is incomplete, scattered, outdated, or erroneous, it will mostly just do something inefficient, faster.

In luxury, this issue is central.

Houses accumulate considerable wealth: purchase histories, preferences, sizes, habits, events attended, conversations with sales associates, repairs, special requests, family connections...

A knowledge of the customer that should, in theory, represent a considerable competitive advantage.

But between theory and reality, there is a gap, one that many professionals acknowledge, off the record.

The pressure on teams to feed the CRM leads some salespeople to tick boxes without always worrying about the relevance of the information. The goal then becomes satisfying KPIs tied to the number of profiles created, rather than building a truly usable database.

Others prefer to maintain their own file, on their phone, because they find it more practical or more reliable. Part of the customer knowledge then escapes the company. And when a salesperson leaves a store, part of that knowledge leaves with them.

AI can change the game, long before data is even used to extract analyses or recommendations.

First lever: make collection easier.

Why force salespeople to manually enter information into sometimes tedious software? They could simply dictate it aloud or send a photo of a handwritten note. An AI agent connected to the CRM would transcribe, structure, categorize, and record the information, with human validation when necessary.

Second lever: rehabilitate the existing data asset.

What can be done with a database already suspected of being largely degraded?

AI can help clean it up: detecting duplicates, reconciling records for the same customer, identifying inconsistencies, verifying the validity of contact details, and flagging outdated information. All tasks that can be considerably accelerated and automated, provided there are controls and reliable verification sources.

Luxury builds its value on the quality of its products, the richness of its heritage, and the excellence of its craftsmanship. Is it not time to apply the same care to its customer data capital?

For tomorrow, AI models will be widely accessible to every house. What will make the difference will also be the quality of what they have to feed them.
