Same organisation. Same release governance. Completely different results.

I've seen this repeatedly throughout my career.

One team has release management nailed.
Releases are predictable. Dependencies are understood. Risks are visible. And when something goes wrong, they know how to respond.

Another team is drowning in failed changes.
Releases regularly cause incidents, recovery takes longer than expected, and the same problems keep resurfacing.

And a third team delivers successfully, but at what cost?
Every release requires endless meetings, manual checks, multiple approvals and hours of coordination. Changes go live, but the process is slow, expensive and heavily dependent on individuals.

So what's different?
It's rarely about how hard people work.
In my experience, it often comes down to three things:
1 - Whether failed changes are genuinely reviewed, or simply closed.
2 - Whether release readiness criteria are consistently understood and applied.
3 - Whether teams make time to improve, rather than moving straight to the next deployment.

The strongest teams don't just follow a process.
They understand why it exists. They challenge what isn't working. And they share what they've learned.

Here's the question I'd ask any technology leader:
Are you measuring release performance team by team, or relying on an overall figure that could be hiding significant differences?
Because an organisation can report healthy release performance while individual teams are struggling.

Release confidence shouldn't depend on which team happens to own the change.

This is exactly the kind of challenge I help people understand and improve.
Have you experienced this across teams? What made the biggest difference?
