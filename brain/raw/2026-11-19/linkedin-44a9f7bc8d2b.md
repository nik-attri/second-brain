Can AI Build High-Sensitivity Literature Searches Without Seed Studies?

Building a comprehensive PubMed Boolean search strategy is one of the most time-consuming steps in a systematic literature review. Usually, researchers rely on known "seed" studies to reverse-engineer the right MeSH terms, entry terms, and keywords. 

What if an AI agent could generate that strategy from your review's eligibility criteria alone, with zero seed examples? 

In our recent publication (Abstract MSR276), our research team tested an agentic large language model workflow designed to automate early-stage search development. Instead of relying on pre-selected reference papers, the agent worked directly from verbatim review eligibility criteria to: 
- Identify core search concepts while deferring criteria better suited for screening. 
- Discover candidate seed records de novo via PubMed and Semantic Scholar. 
- Mine titles, abstracts, MeSH terms, and indexing patterns. 
- Generate, test, and iteratively refine Boolean queries directly in PubMed using validated search filters. 

The result? Across a validation set of 13 reviews, the agentic workflow retrieved 287 of 291 PubMed-indexed included studies, achieving 98.6% recall without a single known study provided upfront. 

While further testing on held-out datasets is underway to evaluate generalizability, this shows how transparent, multi-step agentic workflows can handle heavy methodological lifting while keeping researchers in control.
