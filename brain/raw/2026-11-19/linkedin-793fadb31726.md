I've noticed something about startups that holds them back from growing as fast as their technology deserves.

Especially the ones with founders who are absolutely convinced their technology is the shit.

And sometimes it is.

Faster. Cheaper. More accurate. Solves a problem in a way nobody has managed before.

Brilliant.

And then someone decides that because they've invented a new way to solve the problem, they also need to invent a new way to charge for it.

Nah.

Think about cars.

For decades, you've had three basic ways to get one. Lease it, finance it, or pay cash.

Then Tesla came along and helped revolutionize the car industry. Electric cars became desirable. Fast. Something people actually wanted to own.

But guess how you pay for a Tesla?

Lease it. Finance it. Pay cash.

Elon didn't decide that because the technology was revolutionary, customers should now pay based on distance traveled, divided by altitude, multiplied by electricity consumed.

Although I'm sure someone in a startup somewhere has a spreadsheet for exactly that.

And these ways of paying aren't exclusive to cars. Versions of them exist across almost every industry. Buy it outright. Pay for it over time. Or pay to use it without owning it.

We've been buying and selling things for thousands of years. The arrangements have evolved, but the basic idea is remarkably familiar.

People understand it.

They can compare prices. Work out what they can afford. Decide whether they want to own something or just use it.

Nobody needs a thirty-minute presentation and a spreadsheet to understand what they're buying.

Your customers don't necessarily care how revolutionary your technology is. They care about the problem it solves.

And you're already asking them to trust you with something new. Why make them figure out a completely new way to pay for it as well?

If we get flying cars tomorrow, we'll probably lease them, finance them, or pay cash. Although I suspect the insurance will be interesting.

And if Star Trek becomes real and we can finally buy personal teleportation machines, I don't want a pricing model based on molecules transported, distance traveled, and whether I arrived with all my limbs.

I just want to get to work without sitting in traffic.

The problem hasn't changed. I still want to get from point A to point B with the least hassle possible. I don't particularly care whether you get me there on four wheels, through the air, or by rearranging my molecules.

Invent something extraordinary. Change an industry. Make the competition wonder what the hell happened.

But unless there's a damn good reason to change how people pay for it, leave that part alone.

Of course not everything is sold the way cars are sold. But the point is to know how your customers buy their solutions today and work within that framework. If not, you are building walls between you and them.

Do not mess with pricing.
