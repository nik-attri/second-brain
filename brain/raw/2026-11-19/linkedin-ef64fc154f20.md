What am I taking away from two days at the #JournalismAI Festival? Polis LSE 

Above all, this: (1) Maybe media might be like finance industry - many people don‘t understand the underlying mechanics but they still trust the system with their money. So maybe we are not completely doomed? 
(2) „Connect me“ will be the most important User Need in the future.
(3) Legacy media houses are like cockroaches - they always come back ;-)

Charlie Beckett Dmitry Shishkin Shuwei Fang 

But no kidding:
AI is not just a tool for making existing newsroom workflows faster. It is changing how journalism is produced, discovered and used.
A few ideas I’ll take home:
1. Trust takes more than an AI label. People need to understand how AI was used, how the newsroom checked the output and who is accountable. Trust in a media brand doesn’t automatically translate into trust in every AI-generated answer. And a label alone doesn’t make an answer reliable.
2. The human role isn’t disappearing; it’s changing. AI can search large collections of documents, spot patterns and take on routine work. But deciding which leads to pursue, which evidence holds up and what gets published remains a journalistic responsibility. Good systems make those decision points explicit.
3. Context may be where much of journalism’s value lies. In a world where information can be summarized and recombined in seconds, simply publishing content is not enough. It matters what is relevant, where information comes from, how reliable it is and what has changed. For news agencies and publishers, that’s an opportunity—if context, sources and corrections are also machine-readable and traceable.
4. AI needs a solid data foundation. Several projects made the same point: archives, metadata and workflows need to be in good shape first. Otherwise, AI may simply automate the chaos.
5. Adoption is a leadership and culture challenge. The biggest barriers are not necessarily the cost of the technology, but gaps in skills, uncertainty and unclear goals. People need a voice in how AI is introduced, room to experiment and clear guardrails.
6. Agentic systems and sub-Agents are becoming more and more popular instead of one agent who does all the tasks.

My takeaway: the key question is not only, “What can we automate with AI?” It is also: “What should we do for people and their information needs—and what are we willing to change or stop doing to make room for it?”

And maybe we are kind of in a Snowfall-Moment. Just because we can doesn‘t mean we all have to build something shiny when we don‘t have the means and capacities - and simply don‘t serve our needs. 

Journalism still has a vital role: verifying facts, holding power to account, making connections visible and taking responsibility. AI can strengthen that work. It cannot replace the judgment about why the work matters. 

And it connects people.
