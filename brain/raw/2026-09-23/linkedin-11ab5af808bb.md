I am dyslexic and definitely have undiagnosed ADHD….

School was often really hard. I got distracted a lot talked a lot and definitely never thought I’d go to Uni…

BUT if you’re a parent with a child who has dyslexia or ADHD …please don’t worry too much.

Because some of the things that made school harder have become some of my biggest strengths.

It’s given me:

🧠 An insane memory for conversations – I remember people’s names and what they told me months ago.

❤️ Empathy - like the strongest empathy I feel everything a lot harder than most.

👀 Pattern recognition – I spot gaps walking down supermarket aisles that other people completely miss.

📞 People skills – when you can’t rely on your writing skills, you get very good at picking up the phone or having a chit chat.

💡 Creative thinking – my brain literally has to find different routes to everything.

🤝 Delegation skills – I learned early to partner with people who LOVE spreadsheets. 😂 (anyone who knows my husband) 

🎨 Visual thinking – I can instantly tell when packaging or branding just doesn’t feel right.

💪 Resilience – when everything feels harder, you get very comfortable with struggle.

🗣️ Communication – I hate writing but LOVE speaking. I think that’s helped me understand people, connect with them and build relationships.

And actually, the things I struggled with taught me a hell of a lot about how I work best & how to succeeded in life….

And guess what… I graduated at Uni after finding out I was good at marketing and sales…Proof below nearly 20 years ago!!!…Dyslexic & ADHD doesn’t mean thick, Far from it, just a different way of thinking!…

So if you’re a parent worrying about your dyslexic or ADHD child, I hope you remember this:

Different doesn’t mean less capable.

Their brain might just take a different route.

And sometimes, that different route can become your biggest strengths.

Let’s celebrate being different …Different is good 🥰🥳 and I definitely have these two things to thank for my sales career!! 💪
