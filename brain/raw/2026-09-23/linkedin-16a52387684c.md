FROM THE FLOOR, NOT A PEDESTAL — #9
LEARNING TO READ THE FLOOR

At some point in manufacturing, if you stay around long enough, you stop seeing individual jobs.

You start seeing movement.

You can feel when something is beginning to back up before it becomes a full-blown shit show.

You notice the forky hasn't been past in a while.

You notice somebody's station is starting to pile up.

You notice product isn't leaving one end at the same rate it's arriving at the other.

You hear something in a machine that doesn't sound quite right.

You see one person moving differently because they're already under the pump.

You start anticipating.

I didn't know any of that when I first walked into Juken at sixteen.

I learned it.

Pack by pack.

Sheet by sheet.

Fault by fault.

Person by person.

I learned the forky wasn't there to pander to me.

I learned the sparky might be in another department dealing with something far worse than my problem.

I learned some faults were mine to investigate and some were mine to stay the hell away from.

I learned that the code I put on a finished pack mattered because somebody downstream was relying on it.

I learned that the person beside you might have knowledge hidden in places you'd never expect.

And I learned that a good team starts developing a rhythm.

That's something I've noticed in factories ever since.

People who know the process don't always look frantic.

They flow.

They anticipate.

One movement sets up the next one.

The machine has a beat.

The people have a beat.

When they're working together properly, you can almost feel it.

And when they're not?

You can feel that too.

Everything becomes reactive.

Start.

Stop.

Wait.

Chase.

Catch up.

Fall behind again.

More than twenty years later, I still look at factories that way.

Not just:

What's the policy?

What's the KPI?

What does the procedure say?

I want to know:

What is actually happening on the floor?

Where is the pressure?

Where is the waste?

Who's carrying more than they should?

What is the machine telling you?

What are the people telling you without saying a bloody word?

That's why I call this series:

From the Floor, Not a Pedestal.

Because the floor teaches you things no spreadsheet ever will.
