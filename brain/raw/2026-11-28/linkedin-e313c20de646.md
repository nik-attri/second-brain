My Alma Mater Didn't Make the List 🎓

At 9:40 last Saturday, at my kitchen table, my 17-year-old hit "submit" on his first university application.

My alma mater wasn't on his list. It wasn't on the shortlist. It wasn't on the list of schools he'd rejected. I checked.

To be fair to him, I did make my case. In September I gave him a 14-tab spreadsheet comparing every school in Ontario with mine. Tab 1 was mine. He thanked me and then went back to his own list, which he wrote in a notes app in about 20 minutes.

The grades part has been harder to watch. Every kid in his grade is refreshing a portal and doing math on decimals. I keep telling him his marks don't define him. Then, after he goes to bed, I log in and check them. I'm working on that.

I've been carrying around a list of everything I'd do differently if I were 17 again. Most of it he's already figured out. I'm going to write it down anyway, because at least LinkedIn has to scroll past it.

I'd take the class that scared me. In 1998 I dropped calculus in highschool after two months. I eventually had to take it in university and now run a building on math, using a spreadsheet I only half understand.

I'd talk to my professors. I sat in the back row for four years. Not one of them could have picked me out of a lineup, which at the time felt like a strategy.

I'd pick a program for what I wanted to learn, not because my friend Steve was going. Steve switched programs in October of first year. I stayed three more years out of loyalty to a man who'd left.

I'd get a job where I was on my feet. My first real lessons in leadership came from a summer on a loading dock, not from a lecture hall.

The school he seems to be leaning towards is 15 minutes from my place. When he mentioned it, I tried to look neutral. He says I looked like someone had just approved my laminator.

He'll make the right call. He already has better judgment at 17 than I had at 30. My job, as far as I can tell, is to stop checking the portal, keep the fridge stocked, and maybe clear out the spare room. Just in case.

What would you tell your 17-year-old self about picking a school? 👇
