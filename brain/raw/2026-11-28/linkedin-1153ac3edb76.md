TOO OLD FOR THIS SHIT. TOO YOUNG TO RETIRE.

Welcome to the corporate middle finger years.

You’ve spent 20, 25, maybe 30 years building your career.

You know your business. You’ve survived crises, built teams, solved problems, and cleaned up disasters that should have gotten someone fired.

Instead, they got promoted.

Wonderful.

Now some freshly graduated management genius arrives with a PowerPoint explaining how you should do the job you’ve been doing since they were learning to tie their shoes.

Another restructuring.

Another five-year strategic plan in a world where technology and markets change every bloody month.

Another meeting where everyone agrees on absolutely nothing but somehow leaves with 14 action items.

And if you dare question any of it?

YOU’RE NEGATIVE. RESISTANT TO CHANGE. NOT A CULTURAL FIT.

Apparently, having a functioning brain is now considered toxic.

Then comes the latest corporate obsession.

AI TRANSFORMATION!

Millions in budgets. Consultants everywhere. Beautiful presentations.

Nobody can explain what problem we’re solving.

But the CEO saw a demonstration at a conference, so obviously we need an AI strategy by Friday.

And somewhere in that strategy, there’s probably a spreadsheet calculating how many people like you can be replaced.

HOW EXCITING. YOU GET TO HELP BUILD THE MACHINE THAT MIGHT ELIMINATE YOUR JOB.

Meanwhile, you see the writing on the wall.

Experienced colleagues disappearing.

Younger, cheaper replacements arriving.

Your responsibilities increasing while your influence shrinks.

And the irony?

YOU AND PEOPLE LIKE YOU HELPED BUILD THE BLOODY COMPANY.

The systems. The relationships. The knowledge. The judgment that keeps the place running.

But apparently, you’re now the problem.

No wonder you’re fantasizing about leaving.

I know. I did too.

And here’s where I made my mistake.

I LEFT WITH THE MIDDLE FINGER INSTEAD OF A PLAN.

Felt bloody fantastic.

For about five minutes.

Then reality arrived with the mortgage, the bills, and the delightful discovery that building a business from zero takes longer than writing a resignation letter.

So here’s my advice.

WRITE THE DAMN LETTER.

Make it glorious.

Tell your boss exactly what you think of the leadership team, the strategic plan, and that ridiculous meeting about meetings.

Read it aloud in the shower.

Enjoy every word.

THEN SAVE IT AS A DRAFT.

Because you’re too experienced to make a stupid decision just to escape someone else’s stupidity.

Keep the salary. Keep the benefits.

And start building what’s next.

Your experience has value outside those corporate walls.

There are businesses dealing with problems you’ve already solved a hundred times.

Some will pay for that expertise.

Consulting. Advisory. Coaching. Your own startup.

You don’t need a fancy office or a six-figure investment.

You need a real problem, someone willing to pay to solve it, and the courage to test whether you can build something around it.

Start small. Start while you’re employed.

LET THE COMPANY THAT’S DRIVING YOU CRAZY FINANCE YOUR NEXT CHAPTER.

Because leaving with a plan feels a hell of a lot better than leaving with a middle finger and an empty calendar.
