Sunday Thoughts: is the path to partner still there?
 
Four questions I keep hearing from associates, senior associates and managers.
 
1. What is AI doing to entry and mid-level roles right now?
 The data points in two directions. Senior Big Four executives have said UK graduate recruitment could fall by about half. PwC in the US plans to cut graduate hiring by a third over three years. In the same period Bain is raising graduate intake by 25% and McKinsey is hiring 12% more people in North America. Stanford researchers find employment for 22 to 25 year olds in AI-exposed jobs running about 19% below trend.
 My reading: firms are buying fewer generalists for the base of the pyramid and more people who arrive with a usable skill.
 
2. Does the traditional path to partner still exist?
 The partner role is secure, because clients still buy trust, accountability and judgment from a person. The route to it is under pressure. The old route was an apprenticeship by volume: years of data pulls, models and slides, with judgment forming as a by-product. AI now does much of that volume. Juniors get fewer repetitions and are expected to show senior skills earlier. Managers are squeezed hardest, since reviewing and coordinating junior output was most of the job.
 
3. Where does the human angle sit for junior people?
 In the work AI output cannot carry on its own: knowing which question the client is really asking, spotting the number that is wrong, reading the room, owning the answer when it is challenged. These used to be learned slowly and passively. They now have to be learned deliberately, and early.
 
4. How should juniors use AI to build their position?
 Use it to take on work one level above your grade, then defend every line of it. Treat each output as a draft you are accountable for. The people who progress will be the ones whose managers trust their judgment about when the machine is wrong.
 
5. Strategy, business, management, technology consulting: do the labels still hold?
 Less each year. BCG reports more than 40% of revenue from AI and technology work, and McKinsey is at a similar share. The strategy firms are building, and the technology firms are advising. Technology skill without business context is being commoditised by the same tools. Strategy without the ability to build stops at the deck. The scarce profile, in consulting and in product management, is the person who can frame the commercial problem and ship the solution.
 
The ladder still exists. It has fewer rungs at the bottom, and each one asks more of the person standing on it.
