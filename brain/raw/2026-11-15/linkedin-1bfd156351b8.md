“We have a workaround” should lead to one more question: “When are we fixing the problem?”

A spreadsheet fills a gap in the reporting system.

A supervisor manually checks information that should already be reliable.

A coordinator makes five calls every morning to confirm something the process should make visible.

People find ways to keep the operation moving. That resourcefulness deserves recognition.

But every workaround consumes attention. It may depend on one person remembering an extra step or carrying knowledge nobody else has.

Over time, the original problem becomes familiar. The extra effort disappears into the daily routine, and the organisation begins planning as though that effort costs nothing.

As leaders, we need to look closely at work people repeatedly do to compensate for a broken process.

What makes the workaround necessary?
Who carries the extra workload?
What happens when that person is away?
Who owns the permanent correction?

Some fixes require investment. Others require a clear decision and someone assigned to follow it through.

Either way, keep the underlying issue visible until it is resolved.

Respect the people keeping things moving—and give them an operation that needs fewer workarounds.
