𝗣𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗮𝘁𝗲 𝗰𝗿𝗲𝗱𝗶𝘁’𝘀 𝗯𝗶𝗴𝗴𝗲𝘀𝘁 𝗿𝗶𝘀𝗸 𝗺𝗮𝘆 𝗯𝗲 𝗵𝗶𝗱𝗱𝗲𝗻 𝗶𝗻 𝗶𝘁𝘀 𝘀𝘁𝗿𝘂𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗿𝗲.

On 10 November, the ASIC published Report 846 after reviewing 24 wholesale and retail private credit funds representing A$18 billion in assets under management.
ASIC found that complex structures, opaque fees, deficient disclosure and weak conflict management continue to limit investors’ understanding of risk.
Eighteen of the 24 funds used multiple layers of intermediary entities. Ten of 12 wholesale funds used SPVs materially, with some structures having used more than 500 SPVs since inception.

𝗕𝘂𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝗹 𝘀𝘁𝗼𝗿𝘆 𝗶𝘀 𝗻𝗼𝘁 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝗻𝘂𝗺𝗯𝗲𝗿 𝗼𝗳 𝘃𝗲𝗵𝗶𝗰𝗹𝗲𝘀.
It is whether every vehicle, fee and risk can be traced to the investor outcome it affects.
🏛️ 𝗦𝗣𝗩 𝗮𝗱𝗺𝗶𝗻𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗿𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻
Each entity adds bank accounts, intercompany balances and governance events. A structure chart is not enough; administrators need a controlled ownership and exposure map.
💰 𝗙𝗲𝗲 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗿𝗲𝘀𝘁 𝗮𝘁𝘁𝗿𝗶𝗯𝘂𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻
Only two of the 22 funds assessed on this point passed the full economic benefits of interest earned and borrower fees to investors. Every retained amount requires transparent classification and disclosure.
📊 𝗩𝗮𝗹𝘂𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝗡𝗔𝗩
Defaults, distressed assets, capitalised interest and related-party transactions require timely valuation challenge, documented assumptions and consistent escalation.

Fund-level totals can conceal borrower concentration, layered fees and liquidity pressure. Reporting must move from fund to SPV to underlying loan.
🛡️ 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝗳𝗹𝗶𝗰𝘁 𝗴𝗼𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗻𝗮𝗻𝗰𝗲

At Capital Leverage Tactics, we believe complexity is acceptable only when accountability scales with it. Fund administration should connect legal entities, loan data, cash, valuations, fees and investor reporting within one controlled architecture.
Over the next 12 to 24 months, private credit managers may be judged not only by returns, but by how quickly they can reconstruct the economic path from borrower payment to investor allocation.

Complexity can create opportunity.
Opacity creates risk.
Administration must reveal the difference.
