A task that takes 5 minutes doesn't sound like a problem. Until you do it 100 times a day.

This is something that's easy to overlook in business operations.

Imagine a freight forwarding team manually copying shipment details from emails into their internal system.

Each entry takes about 5 minutes.

Not a big deal, right?

But let's do the math.

100 entries a day = 500 minutes.

That's over 8 hours spent on just one repetitive task.

Across 22 working days, that's more than 180 hours a month.

And this is before accounting for mistakes, corrections, or the time spent switching between different tools.

Now, not every minute of this can be eliminated. Some information needs verification, and exceptions still require human attention.

But even reducing the time per entry from 5 minutes to 2 minutes would save 110 hours every month.

That's time the team could spend handling exceptions, supporting customers, or taking on more work without increasing the administrative load.

Here's a useful way to identify automation opportunities:

Look for tasks that are:

→ Repeated frequently.

→ Performed using predictable steps.

→ Spread across emails, spreadsheets, and business systems.

→ Easy to measure in terms of time spent.

→ Frustrating enough that employees have developed their own workarounds.

These are often good places to start.

Sometimes, it starts by asking a simple question:

"Why are we still doing this manually?"

Because the most valuable automation isn't always the most technically impressive.

Sometimes it's the one that gives your team a few hours back every day.
