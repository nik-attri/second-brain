🤔 YOP/YTS - How long before the radical and re-cycled rebrand, targeting youth unemployment!!
 
AI isn’t causing broad mass unemployment, but it is pulling the first rung off the career ladder for young workers by slowing down entry-level hiring in areas like admin, coding, analysis, and customer service.
 
Stanford/ADP data show employment for young professionals aged 22–25 in AI-exposed roles lags ~19% behind peers. While the IMF notes AI affects 40% of global jobs (60% in advanced economies), the ILO highlights that 6.1% of jobs held by 15–29-year-olds are directly exposed.
 
Here is a quick global snapshot of AI’s impact across G20 nations:
 
Argentina 🇦🇷 Lower digital intensity limits immediate youth displacement.
Australia 🇦🇺 Reduced junior white-collar hiring follows high-income trends.
Brazil 🇧🇷 Risks growing in formal services and tech support at large firms.
Canada 🇨🇦 Clear slowdown in entry-level hiring, mirroring US trends.
China 🇨🇳 Pressure on 1–3 year roles; state policies target AI job creation.
France 🇫🇷 High exposure in graduate corporate and admin entry points.
Germany 🇩🇪 Slower entry hiring creates a visible junior drag in services.
India 🇮🇳 Risks hit IT and business services that absorb young graduates.
Indonesia 🇮🇩 Moderate current exposure; future risks as formal services grow.
Italy 🇮🇹 AI adds an entry-level squeeze onto existing youth unemployment.
Japan 🇯🇵 Severe labor shortages make AI complementary rather than substitutive.
Mexico 🇲🇽 Vulnerability mainly in routine backend operations and support.
Russia 🇷🇺 Limited domestic data; mixed exposure across industries.
Saudi Arabia 🇸🇦 Heavy investment focuses on new tech roles for young nationals.
South Africa 🇿🇦 Risks mainly affect formal, urban service positions.
South Korea 🇰🇷 94% of recent youth job losses occurred in high AI-exposure sectors.
Türkiye 🇹🇷 Moderate exposure with gradual pressure building in services.
United Kingdom 🇬🇧 High AI exposure ranks; lower graduate intake in key fields.
United States 🇺🇸 Strongest effect with a 19% youth job gap driven by slow hiring.
 
How is industry adapting to onboarding junior talent in the age of AI? 
 
Are they doing enough to protect the entry-level pipeline?
