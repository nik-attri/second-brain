I'm mad as hell! Ninety-six percent. We are now officially categorizing virtually every shallow-water reef on the planet as 'Critically Endangered,' and the collective response will be another global summit, another press release, and another color-coded spreadsheet. 

​We are caught in a terminal loop of analysis paralysis. Year after year, we gather the brightest minds on Earth to meticulously document the exact rate at which we are burning down the house. We invent new taxonomies of doom, publish 200-page assessments, and call it a 'critical window for action'—a window that has been 'rapidly narrowing' for decades. 

​It is the definition of trying to put out a forest fire with a pocket extinguisher. We celebrate cleaning up plastic in a bay while the water itself is warm enough to cook the ecosystem alive. We refuse to touch the brakes, so instead, we just build a louder horn, blow it at the top of our lungs, and congratulate ourselves on being very well-informed spectators to an extinction event.
