THE WORLD DOESN’T NEED MORE CHEFS TALKING ABOUT CHANGE. IT NEEDS CHEFS WILLING TO GET THEIR HANDS DIRTY.

You can learn a hell of a lot about the food system from behind a desk. You can learn a hell of a lot more standing in a fish market at six in the morning, walking a farm, or sitting across from a fisherman who has no idea where his next paycheck is coming from.

I’ve spent enough time in kitchens to know that the story doesn’t start with the ingredient on your cutting board. It starts somewhere else. Sometimes thousands of miles away, with people whose names you’ll never know.

That’s where chefs have a responsibility.

We have a foot in two worlds. The world of the people who produce our food and the world of the people who buy it, serve it and eat it. We understand the product. We understand the business. And if we’re paying attention, we understand what’s at stake when one side of that equation gets ignored.

That puts us in a position to do something about it.

I’ve sat at tables with fishermen, farmers, scientists, business leaders and people working to protect the very ecosystems our industry depends on. Different agendas. Different languages. Different definitions of success.

But put food on the table, and you’ve got somewhere to start.

That’s the power of a chef. Not the white coat. Not the title. Not the ego. The ability to connect people who otherwise might never speak to one another.

I’m in Bangladesh right now, and I’m reminded again that you can’t understand a food system from a spreadsheet. You have to go where the work happens. You have to meet the people doing it. You have to be willing to see things that aren’t comfortable and ask questions that don’t have easy answers.

Then you take what you’ve learned back to the people who have the power to make decisions.

That’s the bridge.

And if we aren’t willing to cross it, what the hell are we doing?
