Life doesn’t stop at 60. In fact, at 62, I’m launching a brand-new business to tackle a crisis that almost every social care manager knows all too well: Burnout.

When I walked away from the frontline years ago, it wasn’t because I stopped caring. It was because the overbearing weight of 60-hour weeks, endless paperwork, and manual compliance tracking eventually broke me.

Over 20 years later, I look at the frontline of UK adult social care today and the exact same thing is happening. Brilliant, empathetic leaders are drowning in administrative quicksand.

Don't get me wrong: CQC assessments and strict regulations are absolutely vital. They set the essential benchmarks that keep vulnerable individuals safe and drive up standards across our sector. I fully advocate for robust compliance because high-quality care is what matters most.

The issue isn't the regulation itself, it's how we are forced to manage it. Under the CQC Single Assessment Framework, evidence collection is a non-stop, dynamic process. If you are still trying to prove safety and compliance using a never ending pile of lever arch folders and messy spreadsheets, you aren't just risking a poor rating, you are actually risking your own health.

I’m returning to the sector now because I want to help managers bridge this gap. Modern digital systems and automated tools exist today that could have saved me from burning out years ago. They allow you to maintain impeccable, safe standards without sacrificing your well-being.

With my new business, Automated Care Office, I will be doing the heavy lifting. I'll be dissecting and reviewing care administration software so you can find the tools that work for you, allowing you to meet CQC standards, protect your clients, and keep your sanity.

Let's start a conversation:
To the social care managers and compliance leads navigating this space, how are you balancing the vital need for thorough evidence collection with your own workload boundaries? Have you found a digital system that protects both client safety and your time?
