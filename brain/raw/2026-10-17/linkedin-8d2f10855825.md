BEYOND THE SHIFT | TRUCK DAY IS MY WORKOUT

I’ve been putting away trucks for basically my entire restaurant career.

At some point, I stopped thinking of it as receiving inventory and started thinking of it as my early-morning workout.

Some trucks have been small.

Some have been massive.

And some buildings were clearly designed by someone who had never actually put a truck away in their life.

Panera Grandville, I’m looking at you.

The freezer was through the cooler.

Which meant if the delivery got dropped before I arrived, I could walk in to find the cooler packed with product and the freezer completely inaccessible.

Awesome. 🤦🏼‍♂️

Luckily, I made friends with the drivers.

My regular delivery team learned to wait for me before unloading so we could get the frozen product through first.

Over the years, I’ve gotten to know a lot of drivers pretty well. Some well enough that we’re still connected on social media long after the restaurant or route changed.

When you see the same people before sunrise week after week, somewhere between checking cases and moving boxes, they become part of your routine.

And yes, there have absolutely been drivers everyone secretly hoped would be assigned to our truck that day.

One in particular comes to mind.

I’ll leave that there. 😏

Then there’s the key-drop panic.

If you’ve ever opened a restaurant and walked in expecting a delivery to already be there, you know the feeling.

You look around.

Nothing.

You check the back.

Nothing.

Then it hits you:

Did I submit the order?!

Suddenly you’re running to the computer at 5:00 in the morning, half awake, trying to remember exactly what you did the night before.

Instant early-morning panic attack.

Panera added another level to truck day because we also had fresh dough deliveries.

Goody Goody used multiple vendors.

Naked Farmer does too.

In fact, I get deliveries every day except Sunday now.

Different vendors. Different products. Different schedules.

But the ritual itself never really changes.

Check it in.

Make sure you actually received what you paid for.

Look at quality.

Put everything where it belongs.

Rotate it.

FIFO still matters, even when you’re half awake and your fingers are going numb.

Nothing says GOOD MORNING quite like putting away 50-pound boxes of frozen soup in a zero-degree freezer at 5:00 AM.

And yes, I have actually had a driver stack heavy product on top of a case of eggs and think that was perfectly acceptable.

It was not.

I told him. 😂

Restaurant leadership gets talked about like it happens in meetings, coaching conversations and spreadsheets.

Sometimes it does.

Sometimes it’s just you, a hand truck, a stack of boxes, frozen fingers, and hoping to hell the eggs survived.
