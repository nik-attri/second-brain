A little transparency about entrepreneurship.

Yes, I’m building True Penny. Yes, it became a Montana-registered broker-dealer, creating a new pathway for ordinary Montanans to invest in and own pieces of Montana businesses. Yes, we’re working on some ambitious things involving affordable housing, local business ownership, and access to capital.

And yes, I still have to pay rent and buy groceries.

Both things can be true.

I believe deeply in what True Penny can become. But building something regulated, consequential, and largely unprecedented in Montana takes time. It should take time. There are real people, real businesses, and real money involved. Getting it right matters more than getting it done quickly.

In the meantime, True Penny is pre-revenue, and its founder is very much not independently wealthy.

So here’s a slightly uncomfortable but practical post about ways we might help each other.

1. Sponsor True Penny. If your organization believes Montana should own more of Montana, let’s talk about supporting the community education and outreach that makes local ownership possible.

2. Hire me as a fractional executive. I’ve spent much of my career building companies, leading teams, developing commercial strategies, managing operations, negotiating deals, and turning complicated ideas into functioning businesses. I can be useful without needing a full-time job.

3. Bring me a problem. Business strategy, growth, partnerships, organizational design, financial modeling, investor readiness, or just figuring out what the hell to do next. I’m available for project work, advisory engagements, and one-on-one consulting or coaching.

4. Hire me for something entirely less glamorous. I’m also open to part-time work that pays reliably, makes somebody’s life easier, and doesn’t require me to put on a visionary-founder costume. I’m good with people, systems, spreadsheets, logistics, and getting things done.

I’m not abandoning True Penny. Quite the opposite. I’m trying to build a sustainable life around the time and care it deserves.

I’ve been fortunate to have an unusual career, work with extraordinary people, and help build some pretty remarkable things. I don’t consider myself above doing ordinary work while I build the next one.

I see a successful True Penny on the horizon. I’m also realistic about how long horizons can be.

If there’s something I can help with, send me a message. If you know someone who could use me, make an introduction.

And if you’re also building something meaningful while wondering how you’re going to pay for groceries next Thursday, solidarity.
