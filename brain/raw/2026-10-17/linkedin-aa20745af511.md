WHAT CUSTOMERS REMEMBER ISN'T ALWAYS WHAT BUSINESSES INVEST IN.
Imagine a small hotel investing minimum €500,000 in a beautiful renovation.
- New interiors.
- Designer furniture.
- Premium materials.
- Perfect lighting.
Everything looks exceptional.
But when guests describe their experience, what do they remember?
- “The receptionist remembered our names.”
- “They helped us when our flight was delayed.”
- “For the first time in years, a hotel made us feel genuinely welcome.”
The most memorable part of the experience wasn't necessarily the most expensive.
And this raises an interesting question:
Are businesses investing in what customers actually value — or in what businesses assume customers value?
Psychological research suggests that people don't remember experiences as a simple average of every moment.
Certain moments can disproportionately shape how an experience is remembered.
For businesses, that distinction matters.
Because investment decisions are often driven by what can be easily measured, purchased or upgraded.
But customer value isn't always visible on a capital expenditure spreadsheet.
It may live in the consistency of service.
- The feeling of being recognised.
- The way a problem was resolved.
- The difference between what was promised and what was delivered.
And those factors can vary dramatically between competing businesses.
This is where customer perception becomes strategic intelligence.
At HH Echo, we're building a way to structure and compare how customers describe real-world experiences.
- To identify recurring strengths.
- To reveal overlooked friction.
- To understand what differentiates one business from another.
- And to track how those perceptions evolve.
Not every memorable moment can be engineered.
But the patterns behind thousands of customer experiences can be studied.
The question isn't only how much a business invests in customer experience.
It's whether those investments improve the parts of the experience customers actually value.
