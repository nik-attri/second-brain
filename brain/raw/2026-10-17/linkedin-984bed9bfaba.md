Your sales team can win a $200,000 export order and still lose money on it.

And the mistake might be just three letters.

DDP.

Here's what I mean.

Imagine a US exporter selling industrial equipment to a customer in Europe.

The customer wants everything delivered to their warehouse.

Sales agrees.

The quotation says DDP.

Deal closed.

Everyone's happy.

Until the logistics team starts preparing the shipment.

Now someone asks...

Who's paying the import duties?

Who handles customs clearance?

What about import VAT?

Can our company legally complete import clearance in that country?

Suddenly, the profitable order doesn't look so profitable anymore.

Here's what many businesses overlook.

Under Incoterms 2020, DDP means the seller takes responsibility for import clearance, duties and applicable taxes.

And according to the International Chamber of Commerce, foreign sellers can face serious difficulties handling import procedures in certain countries.

Think about that.

A decision made during a sales conversation can create a problem for finance, customs and operations weeks later.

This is why I believe fixing a bad process matters more than automating it.

Imagine connecting an AI agent to your quotation system.

Before a quote goes to the customer, it checks the delivery terms.

If someone selects DDP, the agent flags what needs reviewing.

Import duties.

VAT obligations.

Customs clearance requirements.

Estimated landed costs.

It can also suggest reviewing alternatives such as DAP, where the buyer handles import clearance.

The responsible team still verifies the details and approves the quote.

Nothing complicated.

Just catching a potentially expensive mistake before it becomes a commitment.

And that's the lesson.

The best automation doesn't always make work faster.

Sometimes it stops the wrong work from happening.

Because an AI agent that processes 500 incorrect quotations faster isn't improving your business.

It's helping you make mistakes at scale.

For import/export owners and CEOs

Would you rather automate your existing workflow or identify the expensive mistakes hidden inside it first?
