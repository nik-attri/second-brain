Photography used to test your patience. Now the technology is testing it too.

There have been some seriously impressive advances beyond the camera. Gimbals can follow your subject. Software can help with the thousands of images you somehow came home with. New tools are making parts of the creative workflow faster, easier and, occasionally, suspiciously clever.

AI is a big part of that conversation, but it’s not the whole conversation. And before anyone panics, no, I’m not suggesting we hand the camera to a robot and go home.

Technology is changing how we shoot, organise, edit and deliver our work. Some of it will be brilliant. Some of it will be completely unnecessary. And some of it will make you wonder why you spent three hours doing something manually when a piece of software could have done it in three minutes. That’s usually when you stare at your computer and ask yourself some difficult questions. The answer isn’t to chase every new gadget or let technology do the thinking for you. It’s to keep learning, test what’s actually useful and figure out where these tools can make your life easier.

The photographers who keep learning will figure out which tools are useful, which are nonsense and which save enough time to justify their existence. And if a computer can cull 3,000 photographs while I drink coffee, I’m prepared to at least hear its argument.
