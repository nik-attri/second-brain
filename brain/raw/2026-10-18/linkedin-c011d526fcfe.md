A question for STP professionals and wastewater treatment experts.

I am interested in understanding the real challenges faced during the operation of Sewage Treatment Plants, especially in the aeration tank.

Consider a situation where the dissolved oxygen (DO) level suddenly drops, but the blower is still running.

How do you identify what is actually causing the problem?

Is it insufficient air supply, clogged diffusers, a faulty DO sensor, or a change in the biological oxygen demand?

I would love to learn from professionals who deal with these situations in real plant operations.

A few questions I have been thinking about:

1. How do you currently monitor DO levels and identify sudden changes in the aeration tank?

2. When the blower is running, how do you confirm that it is supplying sufficient air to the tank?

3. How do you identify diffuser blockage or reduced diffuser efficiency? Are airflow and pressure measurements regularly recorded?

4. Are parameters such as DO, airflow, air pressure, blower current and pH monitored digitally, or are they still recorded manually?

5. Is historical operating data available for research and academic purposes? If so, how can researchers obtain access with the necessary permissions?

6. From your experience, what is the most difficult aeration-related problem to identify before it starts affecting treatment performance?

I am particularly interested in understanding how these challenges are handled in practice, beyond what we learn from textbooks and theoretical studies.

If you work in STP operations, wastewater process engineering, instrumentation, automation or environmental research, I would really appreciate hearing about your experience.

For those working in STPs, what is your first step when the DO level drops even though the blower is running?

Your insights could help us better understand the actual problems faced in the field.
