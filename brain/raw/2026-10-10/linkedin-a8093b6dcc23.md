Apparently, AI has feelings now. And just like humans, its feelings need protecting.

Anthropic recently updated its usage policy to prohibit sustained, needless cruelty towards its models. 

The company isn't claiming Claude is conscious or can suffer, but the idea of reminding people not to bully a machine is still funny as hell.

Imagine yelling at your spreadsheet because the figures aren't adding up, only for the manufacturer to update its terms of service to discourage your behaviour.

Anyway, while we're debating whether to be nice to AI, the technology is moving towards something more interesting: systems that understand what we want without requiring us to become experts at instructing them.

Google is building agents that carry out tasks across applications, while Amazon is making Alexa more conversational and context-aware. 

Both point towards a future where you explain what you need and the system works out how to help.

For a long time, using AI has been presented as a skill in itself. Learn prompt engineering, structure your instructions, provide enough context and anticipate what the machine might misunderstand.

But there's a difference between giving AI a prompt and having a conversation with it.

A prompt assumes you already know what to ask for and how to ask for it. A conversation lets you figure things out as you go. 

You can start with an incomplete idea, clarify what you mean, correct misunderstandings and change direction as you discover what you actually need.

You shouldn't have to get everything right in your first message to make AI useful. You should be able to think aloud and work towards the result through an exchange.

That's the experience we're building with AliceBuddy. You don't need to master a collection of prompts before you can use it. 

Start with what you need, explain yourself naturally and take the conversation from there.
