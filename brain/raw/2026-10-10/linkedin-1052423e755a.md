I’m 61. My dad died in 1988, aged just 59. And I’ve realised I don’t actually want to retire.

I’ve now lived longer than he did. That’s quite a sobering thought.

I often wonder what he’d make of today’s technology. I remember him researching the latest Sony 27-inch TV and working out how to afford it.

He was adamant Betamax was better than VHS! Meanwhile, I was gutted that my friends could rent all the latest films and I couldn’t! 🤣

I’ve always believed that if something isn’t working for you, it’s OK to reinvent yourself. Change direction. Learn something new. Build something better.

But lately, I’ve been thinking about time. And what drives us.

I recently had one of those hypothetical conversations with my husband. You know the ones! The conversation you’ve already had entirely in your head, leaving you annoyed with your other half when they haven’t a clue why! 🤣

What if I died suddenly?

Knowing me, I’d probably be late for my own funeral because I had a spreadsheet to finish or a Power Automate flow to fix!

But when I’m gone, who’s going to care about the bloody spreadsheet (apart from the poor person waiting for it!)?!🤣

None of us knows the date of our own funeral. It’s probably the one calendar invitation we’ll never get to manage!

My dad certainly didn’t know his.

So what are we all working towards?

For some, it’s finding a cure. For others, solving a problem. Some can’t wait to get onto the golf course. Others would consider that the epitome of hell!

I love working. Solving problems, improving processes, learning new skills. I’d happily work into my 80s doing something I love.

But there’s a difference between working because you have to and working because you want to.

We have mortgages, bills and responsibilities. We can’t all walk away from secure employment to follow our dreams.

And isn’t that the irony?

We spend decades building financial security so that one day we can afford to take risks. But how much time will we have left when we finally get there?

My dad never reached retirement.

Perhaps retirement isn’t about stopping work. Perhaps it’s about having the freedom to choose what we do, who we work with and how we spend our time.

I don’t want to stop being useful or productive. I want to keep learning, contributing and reinventing myself.

Sometimes the challenge isn’t finding the courage to take the leap. It’s finding a way to pay the bills while we do it.

So what is driving you? Is it the same thing that drove you 20 years ago?

Are you working towards retirement, or towards the freedom to live and work differently?
