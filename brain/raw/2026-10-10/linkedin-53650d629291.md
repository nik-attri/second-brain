Are we still teaching engineers for the world they’re entering?

AI can generate code, assist with complex mathematical reasoning, and increasingly translate natural-language instructions into engineering designs. As these capabilities evolve, an important question confronts engineering education.

If AI can do more of the execution, what should we teach engineers to do better?

In my guest opinion published in THE WEEK, I explore the curious case of studying to be an engineer in the world of LLMs, AGI, and AI slop.From the relevance of traditional engineering fundamentals and Engineering Graphics to the changing nature of engineering work, the article examines why our approach to engineering education needs to evolve alongside the tools our students will use.

But adapting to AI does not mean abandoning fundamentals. Quite the opposite. As AI assumes more of the routine execution, the ability to think from first principles, critically evaluate outputs, verify results, exercise engineering judgement, and understand the ethical implications of technology becomes even more important.The challenge is not simply to teach students how to use AI.

 It is to prepare them to question it, validate it, and take responsibility for what they build with it.Because the future engineer may not need to write every line of code or draw every component manually. But they must understand why the solution works, whether it is correct, and what happens when it fails.

The question is no longer whether engineering education should change. It is whether we are changing it fast enough.
