So, I've been playing around with Claude Cowork and Claude Code, and I must say, its abilities are scary good. I've been using it for a handful of tasks and its ability to follow instructions and produce high-quality outputs is exceptional, and get this, this is the worst it's going to be, it's only uphill from here!

Obviously, I can't have access to a tool that powerful and simply do toy stuff and routine admin, so, I went ahead and used it to build this flexible, easy-to-navigate site for my students to help them learn various DBMS concepts and prepare for their exams.

What would have taken me weeks to build manually, it only took me two days with Claude Code (and that only because of rate limits). My workflow was to basically first provide it with the course documents and get it to write a spec for the website, breaking down the different features into modules that can be built one at a time.

The spec also makes it scalable (I got ideas for a few features after the initial draft, and adding those in was a piece of cake!), and given the rate limits, breaking down a site this large into smaller chunks meant I could give it one task at a time, go and do other tasks while Claude was building my site for me (and my students).

I think more than ever before, knowing software engineering principles like requirements analysis, software architecture, etc., are very important, because the writing of the actual code has become easier than ever - we literally often don't need to write any code, the AI can do that for us in exponentially less time than it would take us.

That said, it is also important to learn to code, especially if you want to be a software engineer, because of reasons such as quality assurance and ensuring that the code written by the AI is correct and efficient and follows organisational standards and conventions, so, if you're a student reading this - learn to code, and learn to use AI tools. AI probably won't replace you, a developer who knows to code and who knows how to use AI will - if you don't learn how to code or how to use such tools.

Anyway, here's the website, it's primarily for my second-year students, but anyone who wants to brush up on DBMS is welcome to use it to your advantage.
