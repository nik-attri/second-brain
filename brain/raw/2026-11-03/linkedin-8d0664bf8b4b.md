The 161st CorkSec meetup takes place on Tuesday 10th in The Pav (https://lnkd.in/eCFuYJEh) – and we’ve now confirmed our talks! 

CorkSec is made possible through generous sponsorship from our Platinum Sponsors TrendAI , Gold Sponsors CyberSkills , as well as our Silver and Bronze Sponsors featured prominently on the night.

TALK 1: Airgapped System | Not a problem. Lessons Developing Malware for a NATO Exercise by Sabastian H.
Centri had the honour of supporting Locked Shields (the largest and most complex cyber live-fire exercise in the world) by developing realistic technical content for some of the most capable cyber operators globally. This session explores the challenge of building scenarios that are credible, relevant, and difficult enough to test defenders operating at enterprise and nation-state level.
You’ll hear how we approached the design of advanced exercise content, the considerations involved in developing realistic adversary behaviour, and how we balanced technical depth, safety, and learning value.
The session will also walk through one of the more intricate malware components developed for the exercise. Rather than relying on obvious destructive behaviour, this tooling was designed around specific operational objectives, showing how subtle functionality can be harder for commercial detection tooling to identify.

TALK 2: Adopting AI for Threat Defense: What Actually Works (and What’s Still Hype) by Saurabh K.
A lot of people in security are understandably skeptical of “AI” right now. Marketing slides promise fully autonomous SOC's, while real defenders are still drowning in huge alerts and brittle detection logic. At the same time, attackers are already using automation and AI‑adjacent tooling to scale phishing, brute forcing, credential abuse, and exploitation, so a purely manual defensive approach is becoming unrealistic.
In this talk, I’ll share practical lessons from working as a security engineer and Pentester on how AI is actually starting to help with threat defense, and where it absolutely should not be trusted on its own. I’ll break “AI for defense” into three buckets with concrete workflows and examples: classical ML/UEBA and anomaly detection, AI‑powered SOC automation for triage and noise reduction, and LLM‑based that help with summarizing incidents
I’ll also cover the major limitations and risks: hallucinations, weak multi‑step reasoning, prompt injection, and new attack surfaces introduced by LLMs and vendor tools. The goal is to give the audience a realistic blueprint for where AI can safely augment defenders today, and where “AI‑only” approaches are still pure hype.
