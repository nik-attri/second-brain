In Madrid, every café knows the lunch rush is coming at 2 PM.

And somehow, every café seems genuinely surprised when 2 PM arrives.

Even a cold beer takes 20–30 minutes to find its way to your table.

(Don’t judge. It was a city break. It was hot. I had priorities.)

I’ve seen the same phenomenon in business.

A perfectly respectable café chain knows exactly when its next big event is happening.

Hell(!), they planned it. They promoted it. They invited the customers.

And somehow, when those customers arrive, they’re understaffed, out of the themed pastries they advertised, or desperately searching for clean coffee cups.

In Latvia winter is the third thing you can be certain is coming, right after death and taxes.

Yet every year, drivers are shocked to discover snow while still driving on summer tires.

And the companies responsible for clearing the streets somehow manage to run out of salt when it’s needed most.

Different countries. Different industries.

Same fascinating organizational talent: knowing exactly what’s coming and being completely unprepared when it arrives.

We love our forecasts, planning meetings, process documents, and beautifully color-coded spreadsheets.

Theory: 10/10.

Actual readiness: Maybe next time we’ll get lucky.

We don’t have a forecasting problem.

We have a remarkable ability to predict the future and still act personally offended when it arrives.

Yet somehow, there’s always time for a meeting about what went wrong.

PS: I loved that they freeze the glasses and pitchers for cold drinks in Madrid. Can we get the same in Riga?
