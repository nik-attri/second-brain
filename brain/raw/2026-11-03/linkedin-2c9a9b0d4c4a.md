This patient brought EEG from 1992, which was a paper EEG era.

Unlike current digital EEG system, paper ERG used multiple arms with ink to record brainwaves on running paper. Once printed EEG cannot be reviewed with different montages.

We have come a long way in the evolution of EEG technology.

From manually reviewing long paper printouts to now being able to:

* Review EEG alongside synchronized video recordings.
* Switch between different montages.
* Zoom in, analyse and compare waveforms.
* Perform advanced source localization.
* Integrate EEG findings with neuroimaging for better localization of seizure onset.
* Be able to do ambulatory EEG with Bluetooth device.
* Automatic spike detector techniques.

What was once a paper-based recording has evolved into an interactive tool that allows us to explore brain activity in remarkable.

And yet, amidst all this technological advancement, one thing remains irreplaceable - the clinical understanding of the neurologist interpreting the EEG.
