Many know me as a writer, but I've always found it quite challenging to write pieces that are more technical and informational, compared to the column-like write-ups that I'm more used to writing. I'm also known to be an active volunteer for Tzu Chi Foundation Philippines, but since I've gone back to school, it's been getting harder for me to make time for the same volunteer activities that I used to partake in. Thankfully, I was referred to be a Contributing Writer for ReliefWeb by Achi Lineth Brondial. ReliefWeb is a humanitarian information portal and is administered by the United Nations OCHA. As part of the team of contributing writers from Tzu Chi Foundation, we write about the humanitarian and relief efforts that the organization has made all over the world.

At first, I had a lot of self-doubt. I wasn't sure if I was cut out for the new role because I have very limited experience with writing news-like articles. I'm far more used to telling stories in a creative manner and delivering personal opinions about different topics. Still, I was able to navigate the unfamiliar through the support of the mentors that I have within the organization. Translating slides and '报告's from Mandarin Chinese to English is one of the most important tasks in writing the articles, and the task has made me appreciate technology even more. Despite me not having to manually translate the text word for word, my background in Mandarin Chinese that I honed during my 6 months at Tzu Chi University has been a big help in helping me proofread and understand the nuances and context needed to deliver a proper translation.

The first article that I worked on was about the Winter Relief Distribution in Syria. I learned to expand my vocabulary in Mandarin Chinese as I worked through translating the information. It also made me realize the importance of understanding the local context when it comes to translating and writing articles about international relief aid, because there are certain words such as the name of schools and institutions that cannot be easily translated by AI or found through Google Search and require human oversight to provide correct translations. Sadly, this first article of mine was unfortunately rejected by the ReliefWeb team despite multiple appeals from the team of Tzu Chi Foundation for the reason that it's not within their scope of publication because it's not directly related to a recent disaster. 

Nevertheless, I accepted it, and waited for the opportunity to write another article. I figured that this rejection was out of my control and all I could do was move forward with a lesson learned. After some waiting, we were finally given a list of topics to write about. Initially, I chose to write about the Food Distribution in Gaza, but I was instead redirected to writing about the recent earthquake in Japan's Kumamoto Prefecture. This time, it was published, and I couldn't be happier.  😊
