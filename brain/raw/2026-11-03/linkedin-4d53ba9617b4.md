Projects don’t fail because of a lack of software. They fail because the software isn’t used well.

I research this in depth for my work: how project professionals use tools like Jira, MS Project, Trello, Primavera P6 and others, where they get real value, and where they lose it.
Now I am turning that research into practice.

For the 60 days, I will post one practical insight a day!

𝗗𝗮𝘆 𝟮: 𝗧𝗼𝗼𝗹 𝗢𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗹𝗼𝗮𝗱 𝗙𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗴𝘂𝗲: 𝗪𝗵𝘆 𝗮𝗱𝗱𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗮 𝟰𝘁𝗵 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗷𝗲𝗰𝘁 𝗺𝗮𝗻𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝘁𝗼𝗼𝗹 𝘁𝗼 𝘆𝗼𝘂𝗿 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗿𝗲𝗱𝘂𝗰𝗲𝗱 𝘁𝗲𝗮𝗺 𝘃𝗲𝗹𝗼𝗰𝗶𝘁𝘆 𝗯𝘆 𝟮𝟬%.

When a team starts missing deadlines, the default instinct of management is often: "We need a better tool."

So, what do we do? We add a specialized time tracker, a new whiteboarding app, a dedicated feedback board, or a fresh task board. Suddenly, your engineering and PM teams are jumping across four different platforms just to complete a single user story.

Instead of solving bottlenecks, tool fragmentation creates context-switching tax.
When information is scattered across silos, team members spend more time updating software statuses than executing actual project deliverables.

Here are 3 operational metrics to audit your software stack and eliminate tool fatigue:

✅ 𝗣𝗹𝗮𝘁𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗺 𝗖𝗼𝗻𝘁𝗲𝗾𝘂𝗲𝗻𝗰𝗲 𝗥𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼 – Measure how many times a single piece of task data is manually copied across multiple applications. If a task requires updates in more than two systems, your workflow is over-engineered.

✅ 𝗪𝗲𝗲𝗸𝗹𝘆 𝗔𝗱𝗺𝗶𝗻 𝗧𝗶𝗺𝗲 𝗢𝘃𝗲𝗿𝗵𝗲𝗮𝗱 – Track the percentage of weekly sprint hours spent on status updates, ticket grooming, and tool navigation. If administrative tool management exceeds 15% of total development time, productivity is actively declining.

✅ 𝗦𝘁𝗮𝗰𝗸 𝗨𝘁𝗶𝗹𝗶𝘇𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗗𝗲𝗻𝘀𝗶𝘁𝘆 – Audit active feature usage against licensing costs. If 80% of a tool's functionality is duplicated by another core platform in your workspace, it is a candidate for immediate consolidation.
