Before hiring for a new role, perhaps there is a new question we should start asking:

“Can some of this work be done with AI?”

Not because AI should replace people.

But because the way we design work itself is changing.

We often hear:

“AI won’t replace you. Someone using AI will.”

I would take that thought one step further.

The real differentiator will not be who has learnt AI.
It will be who has put AI to work.

There is a big difference.

Knowing how to write a prompt is useful.
Using AI to materially change how work gets done is transformational.

Imagine the possibilities:

➡️ An engineer using AI to analyse thousands of logs instead of manually searching them.

➡️ An operations team identifying patterns before they become major incidents.

➡️ A manager turning hours of operational data into actionable insights within minutes.

➡️ A security analyst using AI to correlate signals across multiple systems.

➡️ A business team eliminating repetitive activities and spending more time on decisions, customers and innovation.

This is also why I believe the conversation should move beyond automation.

Automation asks:

“How can we make this task run automatically?”

AI should make us ask:

“How can we perform this work with greater efficiency, efficacy and reliability?”

Efficiency: Can we do it faster and with less effort?

Efficacy: Can we achieve a better outcome?

Reliability: Can we make the outcome more consistent, repeatable and dependable?

The organisations that benefit most from AI may not necessarily be those with the most AI tools.

They will be the ones that systematically redesign processes, roles and operating models around AI-assisted work.

So perhaps the question for every leader, every team and every employee is no longer:

“What should I learn about AI?”

It is:

“What am I doing today that I could be doing fundamentally better with AI?”

That shift, from learning AI to putting AI to work, is where I believe the real transformation begins.
