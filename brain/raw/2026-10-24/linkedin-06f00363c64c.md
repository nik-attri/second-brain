Thanks Zachary A. Gurard-Levin for giving me a chance to rant about one of the biggest sources of friction slowing the pace of drug discovery, LOGISTICS. I know that term likely doesn’t inspire much of an emotional response, but what I mean is the countless hours domain experts have to burn just coordinating experiments: is there enough compound, where is it, oh crap we actually need to order more, when will it arrive, wait there’s a delay why didn’t I know that last week, sorry you spend how long reformatting vendor manifests to import into CDD, etc, etc.

We’re quickly being inundated with very awesome, very useful AI research agents (Kosmos from Edison Scientific, GPT-Rosalind, Maria from molecule.one, etc), which means its less a shortage of good ideas that slows discovery teams down, it's the friction of the physical world to get those ideas tested. We’re finding that intelligence isn’t everything, and often less than you think it is when it comes to advancing a successful therapeutic. 

That’s why Tangible Scientific is so hell bent on solving the logistics bottleneck. We want to help the experts spend their time doing the expert work, not hours and hours of their week reconciling spreadsheets, chasing orders and otherwise reacting to the logistics challenges that constantly crop up due to a lack of predictability.

Ask yourself: do you know what compounds you have, where they are, and what you can actually run next? If not, get in touch.
