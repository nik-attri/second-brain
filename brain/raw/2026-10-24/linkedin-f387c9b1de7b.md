Some of the most valuable AI training data is already in the hands of companies, right in their own back offices, sitting in plain sight. 👀

Think about all the data that companies generate every day without in any way intending to use it for AI.

Game studios: game source code, player interactions, NPC behaviour, bug reports, gameplay recordings and game simulations.

Software companies: code repositories, debugging logs, failed test cases, code reviews and examples of how developers go about fixing real world problems.

Internal company systems: operational logs, workflow histories, incident reports, troubleshooting records and the day to day processes of making decisions.

IT teams: server logs, deployment histories, infrastructure failures and records of how the issues were identified and resolved.

Design and animation studios: 3D models, project files, editing timelines, motion capture data and the various iterations from concept through to the final design.

QA teams: bug reproduction steps, test scenarios, edge cases and examples of how the app behaves in odd/unexpected situations.

Business operations: historical spreadsheets, forecasting models, planning docs and records of how the team deal with change.

Cybersecurity teams: Sanitised security alerts, incident reports and examples of how vulnerabilities are detected and dealt with.

Most of this data was created purely to build products, run businesses and solve problems. It could also be used to train AI to understand how the real world works (beyond what you get in a standard dataset).

Of course the right permissions, privacy safeguards and licensing are all essential before anything of it is shared or used.

It makes you think doesn't it? What is your company generating every day that could be worth a hell of a lot more than it currently is? 🚀
