As I spend more and more time with marketing measurement, I am starting to lose faith in Google Ads marketers (non-YouTube/Demand Gen). Most (still) can't think beyond what the platform is showing and beyond last click/bottom of funnel. 

It's also going to be the reason behind their doom - most Google Ads media buyers will be replaced by AI. Everything they do currently within Google Ads, Merchant Center etc. can already be automated. 

I won't blame the channel as much. I think it does what it is supposed to do - capture demand. Except for TOFU campaign types like YouTube. 

Yes it overestimates its own contribution but Google to their credit also publishes research on incrementality. 

That should be motivation enough for Google Ads folks to invest time in measurement beyond the Google Ads dashboard. 

But they don't. They keep insisting on remaining obsessed with their beautiful dashboards with pretty ROAS numbers. EVEN WHEN they have access to broader brand-level numbers. 

The seeds of this were laid historically, which I don't think gets pointed out often. Many of the legacy Google Ads operators were not marketers in the proper sense. 

They didn't understand brand, they didn't understand marketing communication (marcomms), they weren't even well-versed with data analytics.

In the earliest days of the platform, many erstwhile average-quality web developers saw opportunity in the burgeoning digital marketing field and started promoting themselves as Google Ads experts. They had already started encroaching the SEO field. 

I am talking about early 2000's to mid 2010's. That's when all the garbage had accumulated in the name of Google Ads and SEO - black hat techniques to manipulate rankings, fooling customers with inflated dashboard numbers without splitting between brand and non-branded, even setting up duplicate conversion actions/events and what not. 

Unfortunately, despite some brilliant minds entering the field later on, the insistence on cheap labor meant that the shady & low quality practices continued unabated. 

Barriers to entry are extremely low. Anyone could get the Google Ads certification (whose answers are all over the internet) and learn a few in-app "optimization" techniques and call themselves Google Ads experts. 

Also: Google's own approach of hiring third party contractors to push their features and pursue clients cluelessly is quite annoying and does more harm than good.

The end result being: Google Ads practice as a discipline remained hostage to a proliferation of "experts" who are only good at pushing buttons and don't understand numbers nor marketing/branding in the broader sense. Those who do, take refuge in safer option of hiding behind platform-reported numbers.
