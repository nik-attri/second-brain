Few weeks ago, something bizarre happened in the cybersecurity world that changed how we look at automation.
Google had to temporarily pause their open-source bug bounty program.
Why? Because AI agents and automated scripts flooded their queues with thousands of low-quality, AI-generated fake bug reports.
Triagers were drowning in noise instead of finding actual vulnerabilities.
It made me realize a hard truth about modern ethical hacking: AI is a double-edged sword.
While attackers are using LLMs to scale automated scanning and payload generation, security teams are facing massive burnout trying to filter out the noise.
What does this mean for aspiring ethical hackers?
1. Automated tools can generate noise, but true threat validation still requires deep manual
logic and foundational knowledge.
2. Knowing how to write a script isn't enough anymore; knowing how to triage and verify real threats is where the actual value lies.
Theory teaches you how tools work. Practical labs teach you how to separate real security risks from digital noise.
