🏛️ TRUST AS INFRASTRUCTURE: WHO THE HELL DESIGNED THIS? #3

💰 WHEN DID THE ECONOMY BECOME MORE IMPORTANT THAN THE PEOPLE LIVING IN IT?

Seriously. When did that happen? 🤔 Somewhere along the way, I think we got a little confused about what the economy is actually for.
📈 GDP goes up. Excellent!
💰 Corporate profits hit record highs. Fantastic!
📊 Shareholder returns improve. Champagne all round! 🥂
Meanwhile...
🏠 Working people struggle to afford housing.
👨‍👩‍👧 Families struggle with the cost of raising children.
👵 Older people worry about their financial security.
💸 And for many, working hard no longer guarantees the opportunity to get ahead.

But don't worry. Apparently, the economy is doing just fine. 🙄

Now, before anyone accuses me of being anti-business or anti-growth...I'm not.
- We need profitable businesses.
- We need investment, innovation and productivity.
= We need economic growth to help create opportunities and fund the services society depends on.

It's a symbiotic relationship.
- Businesses need customers, workers, functioning institutions and stable societies.
- People need jobs, goods, services and economic opportunities.
- Neither thrives sustainably without the other.

But here's where I think we've lost the plot.
- We've become remarkably good at measuring how well the economy performs.
- And remarkably comfortable treating those measures as evidence that society must be doing well too.

Yet a thriving economy and a thriving society are not automatically the same thing.

And perhaps the most important question isn't whether the economy is growing.

It's who benefits, how they benefit, and whether their lives are actually improving.

Because surely the economy is a system we've created to serve human wellbeing.

Not a machine that people must endlessly adapt themselves to serve.

So here's my Trust as Infrastructure question:
WHAT IF WE DESIGNED ECONOMIC POLICY BACKWARDS? 🔄

Start by defining what a thriving society looks like.
🏡 Affordable, secure housing.
🏥 Accessible healthcare.
🎓 Quality education and genuine opportunity.
💼 Decent work and financial security.
🌱 A sustainable future.
⚖️ Institutions that protect fairness, dignity and trust.

Then ask:
What kind of economy, markets, incentives and institutions do we need to deliver those outcomes?
- Growth would still matter.
- Profits would still matter.
- Productivity would still matter.

But they would be tools for achieving societal wellbeing, rather than the ultimate measures of success.

And we'd judge economic policies not just by what they produce on a spreadsheet, but by what they deliver in people's lives.

That's the kind of systems thinking I want to explore through TrustOS.

Not replacing markets.

Not rejecting capitalism simply for being capitalism.

But questioning whether the systems we've designed are delivering what society actually needs.

Because people are not an input into the economy.

The economy is supposed to be an instrument for people. ❤️

And perhaps it's time we started designing it that way.
