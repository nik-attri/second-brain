Lately, I've been reflecting on how AI is changing the way I work, along with the expectations and pressure it brings.
As an AI engineer, I've seen tasks that once took days become achievable in hours. AI helps us code, debug, research, and automate complex workflows.
But I've realized something:
The more capable our tools become, the more we are expected to deliver.
More responsibilities, harder problems, and endless new things to learn.
Recently, I watched a talk by Dan Foolish about nine principles for personal growth. His thoughts resonated with my own experiences.
It reminded me of Zhuge Liang (Kongming) in Romance of the Three Kingdoms.
Challenged to obtain 100,000 arrows in three days, he used fog and straw-covered boats to trick the opposing army into supplying them.
Pressure didn't make him work harder. It pushed him to rethink the problem entirely.
I see a similar lesson in engineering. Deadlines, failures, and technical limitations often push us to challenge assumptions and discover better solutions.
Pressure isn't always negative. With room to think and recover, it can drive meaningful growth.
This connects with Dan Foolish's nine principles:
1. Think Win-Win
Create value for teammates, customers, and partners, not just yourself.
2. Let Go of the Past
Failures are lessons, not permanent labels. Learn without carrying regret.
3. Value Quality Solitude
In a world full of distractions, quiet time for deep thinking is increasingly valuable.
4. Master Your Emotions
Not every experiment succeeds. Staying calm helps us make better decisions.
5. Focus on Results, Not Approval
AI can generate outputs in seconds, but real contribution comes from meaningful results.
6. Take Responsibility
We cannot control how quickly AI evolves, but we can control how we learn and adapt.
7. Face Your Fears
New technologies bring uncertainty. Growth requires the courage to experiment and fail.
8. Cultivate Independence
AI is a powerful assistant, not a substitute for critical thinking and judgment.
9. Stay Disciplined
Long-term growth comes from consistent learning and small improvements.
These principles remind me that AI isn't just transforming our tools. It's also challenging how we define competence, responsibility, and growth.
The more powerful our tools become, the more important our judgment, mindset, and resilience become.
Perhaps success in the age of AI isn't about keeping up with everything or always moving faster.
It's about knowing when to push forward, when to rethink our approach, and how to keep growing through change.
