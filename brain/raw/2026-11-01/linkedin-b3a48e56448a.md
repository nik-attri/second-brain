Everyone is waiting for AGI.
I think we may be waiting for the wrong thing.

The dominant story is simple:
AI gets smarter → reaches “human level” → crosses the AGI line → everything changes.
But frontier models are telling us something stranger.
They can solve extraordinarily difficult problems and then fail at apparently trivial neighboring ones. They can demonstrate elite mathematical reasoning, correctly explain a concept, and then inconsistently apply it.
This “jaggedness” is usually treated as a temporary flaw.

I think it has a much bigger implication:
There may be no meaningful line called human-level intelligence to cross.
Human intelligence has a relatively coherent structure. Demonstrated competence in one area gives us evidence about competence elsewhere.
That assumption is buried everywhere—in credentials, hiring, professional licensing, delegation, trust, and even our concept of intelligence itself.

AI breaks it.
A model can be generally brilliant without possessing a human-shaped topology of competence.
And once you follow that idea through, some surprising things happen:
• AGI becomes the wrong threshold.
• The economy instead encounters thousands of local automation thresholds.
• AI can compress skill differences while simultaneously making true experts more valuable.
• We may automate the work through which future experts are created.
• As generating answers becomes nearly free, verification may become the expensive part of cognition.
• Competitive advantage shifts from owning intelligence to knowing precisely where intelligence can and cannot be trusted.
And even the “jagged frontier” may be too static a metaphor.
Humans change workflows to fit AI. AI trains on a world increasingly altered by AI. Models, tools, people, and institutions begin co-evolving.
At that point the relevant intelligence may no longer reside in the model at all.
It resides in the human + model + tools + institution system.
So perhaps the defining question of the next decade isn't:
“When do we get AGI?”

It is:
“What kind of civilization emerges when powerful cognition becomes abundant, cheap, alienly structured, and embedded everywhere?”
