What if your BI assistant could detect a business problem before you even ask about it?

Traditionally, business intelligence works like this:
A business user asks a question.
A BI developer prepares the report.
The dashboard displays the results.
The user interprets the information and decides what to do next.
But what if we move beyond this traditional approach?
Imagine an Agentic BI system analyzing sales data and identifying:
A sudden decline in sales in one region.
A product category performing below its historical average.
An unusual increase in operational costs.
A significant change in customer purchasing patterns.
Instead of waiting for someone to discover these patterns manually, the system could highlight potential issues and recommend areas for further investigation.

This introduces an interesting shift:
From asking questions about business data to proactively discovering what deserves attention.

However, identifying an unusual pattern is not the same as understanding its cause. The system should support its observations with data, explain uncertainty, and leave important business decisions to human users.
The real potential of Agentic BI lies in combining natural language interaction, automated analysis, anomaly detection, and explainable insights.
The goal is to move beyond traditional reporting and build intelligent systems that help organizations identify opportunities, understand challenges, and make better data-driven decisions.
