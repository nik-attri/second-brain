The entry-level job in digital lending didn’t vanish—it just stopped rewarding the same things.

One FinTech leader says AI isn’t killing junior hiring, and his company is onboarding a large cohort of interns this year. I believe the hiring part. I’m less sure about the why.

The entry-level role used to test whether you could write the underwriting rule, clean the bureau data, or wire the API. Now that part is basically free.

So the first step up is still there—it just measures something different.

Entry-level postings in FinTech are genuinely down from their peak. The fear is fair. The idea that the door is locked is not.

Here’s the tell: announcing a hundred internships and actually giving them real exposure to credit policy, model monitoring, and live portfolio risk are two separate promises. Watch which one gets made.

Don’t wait for someone to decide you’re worth it. Make it easy to bet on you—read bureau reports and rejection reasons you didn’t generate, stress-test a scorecard before you reach for AI, learn one lending vertical deeply enough to catch the model being wrong on thin-file or informal income borrowers, and ship something a real underwriter or collections team actually uses.

The juniors getting hired in digital lending aren’t the ones competing with AI. They’re the ones who can tell when it’s lying—on risk, on compliance, or on the customer.

What’s one skill you’re building that AI still can’t fake in credit and lending?
