"The pallet stands perfectly upright on the warehouse floor, but open the container after 10 days of ocean transit, and you find a collapsed, crushed mess of cargo."
While coordinating 20–30 export containers (40ft) monthly to Taiwan, cargo-shifting damage claims were an early operational headache. The natural instinct was to blame rough drayage drivers or ocean swells.
But investigating our staging docks revealed the real root cause: Improper, informal stretch-wrapping habits. Floor pickers were loosely wrapping 2–3 layers around the midsection of the load.
We engineered a standardized 3-Point Wrapping Protocol:
• Base Lock (4–5 passes mandatory): Film must physically anchor the wooden pallet base to the bottom carton tier. Without this bottom lock, dynamic transit forces cause the entire cargo block to shear off the pallet.
• 50% Layer Overlap with Pre-Stretch: Every spiral pass overlaps the previous layer by at least 50% under tension, creating an elastic compressive sleeve that holds cartons together.
• Top Tier Reinforcement: 3 passes across the upper perimeter to prevent carton drift under lateral movement.
The Outcome: In-transit pallet leaning and cargo collapse claims dropped to zero across subsequent monthly container runs.
A roll of stretch wrap costs mere dollars, but applied with technical discipline, it is the single most vital safety belt protecting thousands of dollars in freight.
