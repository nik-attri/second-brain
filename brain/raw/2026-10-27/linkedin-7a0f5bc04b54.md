Over the past few weeks, I have had a genuinely pleasant experience of the improvements in digital services across government agencies and state enterprises.

My ID card renewal was seamless. I was informed when it was ready and collected it at the counter in Vilimale. Renewing my driving licence and paying the vehicle fee were equally straightforward. Booking appointments at IGMH has also become so much more convenient.

Of course, digitisation cannot solve everything. Long waiting lists for some medical specialties are ultimately a capacity issue. I would also hope that scan and radiology appointments, as well as laboratory memos and related processes, can increasingly be handled online.

But this progress raises another important question: what are we going to do with the resources and human capacity released through digitisation?

My hope is that we deliberately realign some of that capacity towards improving the citizen experience.

If fewer people are required to process routine transactions manually, perhaps more attention can go towards answering telephone calls, helping people who still need to visit a physical counter, supporting elderly citizens, resolving unusual cases, and making every human interaction more responsive and respectful.

Digitisation should not simply mean doing the same work with fewer people. It should give us the opportunity to make the remaining human experience better.

I have experienced another example of this through MTCC's transport services.

When travelling from Villimalé to Malé, I no longer have to worry about finding a taxi after getting off the ferry. I can schedule a trip three hours beforehand, take the ferry at the appropriate time, and have the taxi ready to match my arrival.

It has worked perfectly for me every time. And the drivers are extremely kind and helpful. 

This is particularly valuable when travelling with elders. There is no standing around trying to flag down a taxi, and there is greater certainty that we will arrive at appointments on time.

That is what good digital service design can achieve: not technology for its own sake, but less friction, greater predictability, and a better experience for people.

Well done to the agencies making these improvements, and particularly to the MTCC Transport Services team for a service that has made everyday travel noticeably easier.

The next stage of our digital transformation should perhaps be this: 

Use technology to create efficiency. Then use that efficiency to create a more human public service.
