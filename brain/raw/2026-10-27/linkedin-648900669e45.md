Someone told me hand-writing SQL is 'so 2018', and yeah she has a point...

Often, when I post about writing SQL queries, I get the same individual berating me and others for hand-writing SQL.

"You're wasting so much time if you're manually writing SQL" and this person clearly thinks anyone who starts a piece of SQL work with "SELECT...", is a bit of a dinosaur🦖. 

Here are two situations:

 🚜 One, you write a long and complex SQL query, and it takes a day and a half. It's a bit of a beast with lots of moving parts, some weird join conditions, and complicated calculations. It's got functions like FLOOR and MODULO, LAG and LEAD. There's a self-join, and eighteen CTEs.

When it's done, you run some tests, tweak, test, tweak, test, tweak and after another day, you're happy to sign it off.

It's a work of art, and you sit back in your chair and give a satisfied sigh. You've still got it.


 🏎️ Situation number two, you use Claude Code or something similar that has access to your database schema and data. You spend maybe twenty minutes telling it what you want, explaining the complexities, what things mean, how they need to be calculated, giving some examples.

It spits out a 500 line query in two minutes and 12 seconds. You eyeball it for clear errors (half an hour), and then go into your testing phase. Only thing is, when you find errors, you tell Claude to fix it. You see some highly inefficient code, and you tell Claude to improve it, and like a good (and very clever) dog, it just gets on with it. No complaints.

After maybe three hours, you've finished.

Which is better?

In terms of the data output, probably neither.

In terms of your personal output/productivity, AI by a mile (there's a good reason why I chose a tractor and a racing car icon for the bullets above).


So why the argument?


There's no argument. I use AI in exactly the way shown above.

My point is you still need to know and understand SQL in order to check it, make sense of it, fix it, identify where things might have gone wrong.

AI is here to stay, and by embracing it, and everything it gives us, we're entering a new chapter.

Do I like it? Yes.

Do I use it? Absolutely.

Do I trust it? Not totally.


AI isn't ready to be let off the leash just yet. Humans need to be able to check it's work, and for that we need to know and understand SQL.

Or do we?
