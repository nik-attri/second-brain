What if your biggest opportunity is hiding behind your best people?

The work got done. So the process must be working.

Yeah, nah.

Sometimes it just means you've got some bloody good people making a rubbish process look brilliant.

They've mastered the workarounds, memorised the shortcuts, and somehow keep the whole show running with a spreadsheet, a bit of duct tape, and what I can only assume is witchcraft.

Impressive? Absolutely. Sustainable? Not so much.

Job done. Targets met. Happy days.

But what if the biggest opportunity isn't asking people to work harder, move faster, or squeeze more into their day?

What if it's simply getting the unnecessary crap out of their way?

Less double-handling. Less chasing information. Less fixing things that shouldn't have been broken in the first place.

More time doing the stuff that actually matters.

I reckon we spend plenty of time asking whether the job got done.

Maybe we should spend a bit more time asking what it actually took to get there.

Because there's a hell of an opportunity hiding in that answer.

Good people shouldn't have to fight bad processes.

We've all worked with someone holding an entire process together with duct tape and determination.

The real question is, what happens when they take annual leave? 😂
