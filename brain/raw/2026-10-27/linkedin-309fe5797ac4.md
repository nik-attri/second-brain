Before hiring another manager, ask yourself one question: is the problem really a lack of management?

I've seen operations where the instinct is always the same when things go wrong.

Service slips? Hire another manager.

Costs rise? Hire someone to control costs.

The owner is exhausted? Bring in another person to help.

But what if the problem isn't the number of managers?

What if responsibilities overlap, decisions still require the owner's approval, purchasing is poorly controlled, standards aren't followed, or nobody has built a system that allows the operation to run properly?

Adding another salary doesn't automatically fix any of those problems.

Sometimes the business needs a stronger manager.

Sometimes it needs clearer accountability, better systems and tighter operational control.

And sometimes, particularly when a business cannot justify building a full internal management structure, outsourcing part or all of the operation deserves serious consideration.

The answer depends on the actual problem—not on the easiest solution to hire.

Before recommending another position, I want to understand what is failing, why it is failing, and what it is costing the business.

Owners: when an operation underperforms, what do you examine first—the people, the systems, or the way the business is structured?
