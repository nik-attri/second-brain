An approved grant is not cash in the bank. (I know, grants if not managed well, are a gift from hell some times).

I worked on the funding plan for a multi-million-euro manufacturing expansion supported by grant funding, bank debt and shareholder equity.

On the headline numbers, the project appeared funded. In practice, contractor payments, equipment deposits, bank drawdowns, grant reimbursements and the companys own contribution all followed different timelines and conditions.

That timing gap is where otherwise viable investments come underpressure.We built a detailed sources-and-uses schedule, mapped the monthly funding requirement, tracked conditions before each drawdown and tested the downside case if reimbursements or financing arrived later than planned.

The important question was not only whether total funding covered total cost. It was whether the right cash would be available on the exact dates the project required it.

Capital projects rarely fail because the final spreadsheet does not balance. They fail because timing, contingencies and working capital were treated as secondary details.
