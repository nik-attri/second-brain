The biggest mistake I made when I first started building AI systems for businesses was falling in love with the technology instead of the problem.

Early on, I thought clients wanted the most complex multi agent orchestration or the latest model architecture. I’d spend days wiring up intricate tech stacks, excited about how advanced the backend was.

Then I watched a founder look at a dashboard I built, completely overwhelmed, and say: 
"Look, I don't care how sophisticated it is under the hood. Does it actually stop my team from drowning in manual data entry?"

That conversation completely changed how I build. 
Founders don't buy AI because it’s cool. They buy it because they are losing time, burning capital, and dealing with operational friction. 

If a system requires a manual in order to use it, or solves a problem nobody actually has, it’s just expensive noise. 

The best technology is the kind that fades completely into the background where the user doesn't think about the AI at all, they just notice that their workflow suddenly runs smoothly.

What’s one piece of "cutting edge" software or tech your business tried out that ended up causing more friction than it solved?
