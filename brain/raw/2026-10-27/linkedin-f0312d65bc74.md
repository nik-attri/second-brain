🚀 Before you write a single line of code, make sure you're solving a real problem.
One of the biggest mistakes developers make is building something because it sounds cool.
I've made this mistake too getting excited about an idea before asking the most important question:

Here's a simple 5-step framework to validate your next project before spending weeks building it 👇
1️⃣ Talk to the right people
Find people who experience the problem firsthand. Don't rely only on your own assumptions.
2️⃣ Understand their current solution
Are they using spreadsheets, multiple tools, manual work, or an ugly workaround? That's where opportunities hide.
3️⃣ Look for evidence, not just opinions
Someone saying "That's a great idea!" means very little. Look for people already spending time, money, or effort solving the problem.
4️⃣ Run a small experiment
Create a landing page, build a tiny prototype, or manually deliver the solution to a few potential users.
5️⃣ Make a decision based on signals
Are people signing up, trying your solution, giving feedback, or willing to pay? Let their actions guide your next move.

💡 Remember: You don't need to build the entire product to test whether the problem is worth solving.
Sometimes, a few conversations can save you weeks of coding.
As developers and indie hackers, we often focus on how to build.
But learning what to build and why might be the more valuable skill.
