WHY HIRE HUMANS? HIRE THEM TO SUFFER.

Any jackass with a pulse, a Wi-Fi connection, and a $20 credit limit can spin up a fleet of AI agents to vomit out market research, functional prototypes, and passable code. 

If you're still hiring human beings just to sit at a desk and make stuff, I have bad news for you: you're paying a massive premium for a flawed biological machine that types at 60 words per minute, requires dental insurance, and gets an existential crisis // massive productivity reduction every time their ex watches their Instagram story.

For the last century, execution was the whole game. You got paid because you could do X. You could write the code. Balance the spreadsheet. Draft the memo.

Well, execution is now dirt cheap and infinitely abundant. That leaves all of us (meat-sacks) staring into the abyss and asking the ultimate question: If the machines do all the work, what the hell are we actually good for?

The answer is simple. 

You no longer hire humans to do tasks. 

You hire them to suffer.

Specifically, you hire them to suffer in three ways:

1. Choosing Your Shit Sandwich (Accountability): When the servers catch fire and a million-dollar launch implodes, your biggest client doesn’t want to talk to a chatbot. They want a blame-receptacle. They want a real, sweating human being to scream at.

Of course you can unplug an AI when it hallucinates, but you can’t send a Python script to look a furious investor in the eye and say, “I screwed up, and it’s on me.” Value isn't in doing the work anymore. The value is in eating various shit sandwiches if // when the work blows up in your face.

2. Murdering the Slop (Taste): An AI agent can crank out forty landing pages by 2:00 PM. Fantastic. The problem is that thirty-nine of them are hot garbage.

Creation used to be hard and curation was easy. Now, creation is free, and curation is everything. You need someone ruthless enough to look at forty options, pick the one that actually works, and trash the other thirty-nine - without scheduling a soul-sucking, four-hour Zoom meeting to talk about everyone's feelings.

3. Having Skin in the Game (Trust): Nobody wires a seven-figure check because a language model wrote an awfully polite follow-up email.

Deals happen between people who have bled together in the trenches, gotten mildly intoxicated at a terrible hotel bar in Chicago, and watched each other keep a painful promise when it would have been easier to bail.

Trust requires vulnerability. It requires the possibility that you might lose something if you lie. Algorithms don't have skin in the game. In fact, they don't even have skin.

So here is the new playbook: Hire half as many people, and pay them twice as much.

Because you're paying them to suffer.
