Graduate jobs aren’t simply “disappearing because of AI” — but the skills employers expect from graduates are changing fast.

A UK government analysis published this year found that overall UK hiring was 14% lower year-on-year in April 2026. Among the entry-level occupations it tracked, Accountant roles were down 29% and Software Engineer roles were down 27%. In total, 30 of the 38 entry-level occupations analysed had seen hiring decline. That sounds worrying — especially if you are graduating into tech, finance or another competitive professional field. But the picture is more complicated than “AI has taken the graduate jobs.”

The Institute of Student Employers’ 2026 research found that 87% of employers expect AI to reshape graduate and apprentice roles over the next three years. Importantly, employers generally expect roles to be changed rather than simply replaced. And new Skills England research published in October shows that demand still exists in important areas: software development, IT business analysis and several engineering occupations appear repeatedly as priority skills across UK growth sectors. In digital and technology, around 68% of priority occupations are considered to be in critical or elevated demand, while the equivalent figure in financial services is around 70%.

So what is changing? Employers increasingly want graduates who can work beyond routine tasks. In tech, AI is shifting value towards verification, judgement and communication; in finance, it is transforming analytics, fraud detection and risk; and engineering demand remains strong across areas such as clean energy, advanced manufacturing and defence. The goal is not simply to become an “AI expert” — it is to combine your profession with practical AI literacy and strong human judgement.


For international graduates, this matters even more because sponsorship adds another filter to an already competitive market. Your application needs to show what you can contribute, not just that you have a degree and need sponsorship. Whether you are in finance, software or engineering, combine your technical skills with data, AI literacy, problem-solving and communication. And remember: a government “priority occupation” is not automatically sponsorable immigration eligibility still needs to be checked separately.

My takeaway for graduates entering the 2026/27 market is simple: don’t try to compete with AI at the repetitive tasks it can increasingly perform. Build the skills that make you valuable alongside it judgement, verification, problem-solving, technical depth, communication and the ability to apply technology to real business problems. 

Sources: Skills England Annual Skills Report and Sector Skills Needs Assessments 2026
