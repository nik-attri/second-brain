#CrazyNumbers | How a CFO increased profit by 40% in a year

There is a trap in asking to quantify achievements on a CV.

I review a lot of CVs from senior executives. As a CFO, I understand the real P&L impact.

It is both a gift and a curse. A couple of figures can reveal the picture. But admiring a presentation can be more pleasant than confronting reality.

What concerns me most?

1. Confidentiality

Most privately owned companies consider information commercially sensitive. Sometimes, even revenue is confidential.

The owner and CFO understand this. Yet CVs sharing sensitive figures are common in applications for other roles.

2. A headline-grabbing number

Is a 40% increase in profit a great result? Can you deliver it in a year?
Oh, hell, yes / yes! 

More likely, the company was hovering around break-even. A proactive Finance Director addressing working capital, margins and pricing can deliver a 40% improvement, and fast.

But would the same increase be achievable in a profitable business? In a good year or a bad one?

Absolute numbers would help, but then there is still point 1: NDA.

3. What was the individual’s contribution?

For 15 years in FD / CFO roles, I’ve dealt a lot with assessments and incentive systems. I have served on countless committees, assessing others and being assessed myself.

Across different countries and industries, I’ve learnt that allocating the change in a bottom line to names precisely is utopian.

It may work in a simple SMB for a linear role. An e-commerce buyer renegotiates the price of the same product at the same volume. The saving is easy to attribute. Only change the supplier, and quality may decline, repeat purchases fall and CAC rise.

Within Finance, attribution is far from straightforward. I once blocked a refinancing that offered a lower rate but introduced covenants whose compliance outweighed the saving.

Money helps us measure value, but translating P&L into individual contributions is much harder than it looks. Real business complexity rarely fits neatly into a spreadsheet.

This is why I favour common financial targets for the entire CEO-1 team that reflect the company’s priorities, combined with a few project-based targets of individual contributions against clearly defined metrics. That approach beats spreading the change in profit across a list of names.

So, what prompted these Saturday morning reflections?

Years of economic modelling, impact assessment and turnaround programmes have taught me to look beyond the number.

When I see another CV featuring a clickbait outcome, it neither excites me nor puts me off. For me, it is 100% neutral.

I can ask how the number was calculated and why that metric was chosen. A short conversation may reveal a professional with tangible achievements.

But I cannot judge or compare a candidate based on a financial number on a phone screen. And, if we are being honest here, neither can anyone else.
