One of the most interesting phrases in financial services is “we’ll do it manually for now.”

There’s nothing inherently wrong with a manual control. Some are perfectly reasonable and proportionate.

But I’ve always found the words “for now” interesting.

Because six months later, that temporary spreadsheet might still be there.

Twelve months later, it might have become a critical part of the process.

And somewhere along the way, everyone has forgotten it was supposed to be temporary.

The question isn’t necessarily whether a manual control is acceptable.

It’s whether anyone has stopped to consider if it’s still appropriate, effective and sustainable.

Sometimes the biggest risks aren’t introduced by a new process. They’re inherited from an old workaround that nobody thought to revisit.
