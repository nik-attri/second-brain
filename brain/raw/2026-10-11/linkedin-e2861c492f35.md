THE BIGGEST MISTAKE WITH AI MAY BE USING IT TO DO YESTERDAY'S WORK FASTER.

PETER DRUCKER × MODERN MANAGEMENT | 21/30

That thought stayed with me while reflecting on The Age of Discontinuity by Peter Drucker.

Long before today's AI boom, Drucker was thinking about what happens when major technological and social changes break continuity with the past.

That word matters:

DISCONTINUITY.

Because transformational technology does not simply improve the existing system.

Eventually, it can change the system itself.

This is my interpretation — and one of my biggest takeaways:

THE FIRST STAGE OF A TECHNOLOGY REVOLUTION OFTEN AUTOMATES THE OLD WAY OF WORKING.

THE BIGGER OPPORTUNITY IS REDESIGNING THE WORK SO THE OLD WAY IS NO LONGER NECESSARY.

Consider AI.

A company can use it to:

Write existing reports faster.

Produce presentations faster.

Answer emails faster.

Summarize meetings faster.

Process existing workflows faster.

Useful?

Absolutely.

Transformational?

Maybe not.

Because the organization may simply be automating yesterday's bureaucracy.

The harder questions are:

Why does this report exist?

Why are five approvals required?

Why does information pass through four departments?

Why is an analyst manually assembling data before making a decision?

Why does the customer need to navigate this process at all?

What becomes unnecessary when information, analysis, and automation become dramatically cheaper?

That is where the conversation becomes strategic.

The printing press did more than make handwriting faster.

The internet did more than make mail faster.

Smartphones did more than make telephone calls portable.

Major technologies eventually redesign behavior, distribution, communication, and business models.

AI may do the same.

For me, leadership should therefore separate two questions:

"HOW CAN AI MAKE OUR CURRENT PROCESS MORE EFFICIENT?"

and

"IF WE WERE BUILDING THIS COMPANY TODAY WITH AI ALREADY AVAILABLE, WOULD WE DESIGN THE PROCESS THIS WAY AT ALL?"

The second question is far more uncomfortable.

Because genuine transformation can eliminate:

Processes.

Layers.

Reports.

Roles.

Approval structures.

Even entire business models.

Perhaps that is why technological discontinuities are difficult for incumbents.

They are asked not merely to adopt a new tool —

but to question systems they spent decades perfecting.

The winners of the AI era may therefore not be the organizations that automate the most existing work.

They may be the ones willing to ask:

WHAT WORK SHOULD NO LONGER EXIST?
