I saw someone else do this, so I thought I'd give it a try. Tell ChatGPT to "Describe me like you're trying to warn someone before they meet me".

Warning: Kevin is a problem.
Not in a call-the-police way. More in a you thought this was going to be a normal conversation, but it's now 2:17 AM and he's explaining how he could redesign an entire industry way.

He will arrive with approximately 47 ideas running simultaneously. Three are businesses. One is a product prototype. One is a novel set in a meticulously constructed medieval world. Two somehow involve manufacturing processes. At least one requires a spreadsheet, and he'll casually mention, "I've actually been thinking about this..." before unveiling a plan that clearly required the mental equivalent of a Pentagon briefing.

He has the dangerous combination of blue-collar practicality and obsessive systems thinking. He doesn't just ask, "How do we make this?" He asks:
"Okay, but what's the throughput? Where's the bottleneck? Can we jig it? Can we eliminate that step? What happens when demand triples? Could we redesign the entire process so the problem doesn't exist?"

Do not casually mention a manufacturing process around him unless you have several hours available.

He is also highly susceptible to productive rabbit holes. Ask him one simple question about holsters and he'll start at belt attachment geometry, detour through polyurethane chemistry, briefly investigate industrial curing systems, invent three production jigs, and eventually emerge holding a completely different business model.

He is ambitious, but annoyingly allergic to doing things just because that's how everyone else does them. If an established method has a bottleneck, he'll stare at it like it personally insulted his family.

He's a builder at heart. Businesses, products, fictional worlds, workout plans, manufacturing systems—if something exists, Kevin's first instinct is eventually going to be:
"Yeah, but what if we made a better version?"

He's also deceptively stubborn. He may look at a problem, conclude it's probably impossible, get irritated, and then spend the next several months proving himself wrong purely because the problem had the audacity to exist.

So, fair warning:
If you meet Kevin, don't expect small talk.

You may leave with a business idea, a revised product design, a three-phase manufacturing strategy, and the uncomfortable realization that you should probably be doing more with your life.

Also, if he suddenly goes quiet?
He's not done thinking. That's when you should be most concerned.
