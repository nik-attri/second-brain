I shipped a production SaaS solo in five weeks using AI. That's exactly why I think cutting junior hiring is a mistake.
 
The numbers are brutal. Junior developer employment has declined for 33 consecutive months. Entry-level hiring at the 15 largest tech companies dropped 25%. CS graduates are sitting at 6.1% unemployment.
 
The logic behind the cuts sounds reasonable: AI does what juniors used to do. Cheaper, faster, no onboarding.
 
Here's what that logic misses.
 
Those three weeks worked because of the ten years before them. Not because I type fast — because I know what to reject. When the model produced a plausible-looking data access layer that would have fallen over under concurrent load, I recognised it in seconds. Not from cleverness. From having shipped that exact bug before and been paged at 2am for it.
 
AI didn't give me judgment. It amplified judgment I already had.
 
And judgment is the whole ballgame now: 85% of developers use AI tools, and 45% of AI-generated code has carried a known security flaw. Someone has to catch that. Someone has to know the difference between code that runs and code that holds.
 
So here's the question nobody cutting junior roles seems to be answering:
 
Where does the next senior come from?
 
You can't hire a ten-year engineer who never had year one. The pipeline that produces the people who can supervise AI is exactly the pipeline being shut down. That bill comes due in five years, and it lands on someone else's quarter.
 
IBM is betting the other way — tripling US entry-level hiring in 2026, arguing juniors are shifting toward judgment work rather than disappearing. I think they're right, and I think in five years that looks less like generosity and more like the smartest capacity planning in the industry.
 
The seniors are fine. Senior hiring hasn't slowed — if anything, teams are hunting harder for people who can direct AI well, not just write code well.
 
It's the generation behind us that's being written off. And we're the ones who'll have to answer for it.
 
Am I wrong? If your team cut junior hiring this year, I'd genuinely like to hear how you're solving the succession problem. Not rhetorically — I don't have a clean answer either.
 
Who am I? — AI Engineer & Senior .NET Developer who built AI systems in domains where someone can be held liable for the output.
