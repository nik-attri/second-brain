Factur-X, UBL and Structured Invoices: What Businesses Need to Know
Not every digital invoice is a structured electronic invoice.
A PDF sent by email may be digital, but the information it contains is primarily designed to be read by people.
A structured invoice, on the other hand, contains standardized data that can be understood and processed automatically by information systems.
As Electronic Invoicing expands across Europe, understanding this distinction is becoming increasingly important.
So where do Factur-X and UBL fit?
1- Factur-X Human-readable + Machine-readable :
Factur-X is a hybrid electronic invoice format.
It combines:
PDF/A-3 → a visual invoice that people can read
Structured XML data → information that software can process automatically
This approach combines familiar invoice presentation with structured data for automation.
2- UBL, Structured by Design
UBL (Universal Business Language) is an XML-based syntax used to represent structured business documents, including electronic invoices.
Unlike a traditional PDF, the invoice information is organized into standardized data fields that systems can automatically interpret and exchange.
3- EN 16931 — The Common Semantic Foundation
At European level, EN 16931 defines a common semantic data model for the core elements of an electronic invoice.
The objective is not simply to digitize invoices.
It is to enable interoperability between different organizations, platforms and financial systems.

Why does structured invoicing matter?
Because structured data enables organizations to move from:
Reading → Processing
Manual entry → Automation
Visual checks → Business rules
Disconnected documents → System integration
Static invoices → Actionable data
For Legal Operations, this opens particularly interesting possibilities.
Imagine receiving a legal invoice and automatically connecting its structured information with:
Matter → Law Firm → Budget → Billing Rules → Approval Workflow → ERP → Payment → Analytics
The invoice is no longer simply a document to be processed.
It becomes structured data that can drive an end-to-end business process.
For organizations preparing their Electronic Invoicing strategy, the question should therefore not only be:
“Which format do we need to support?”
It should also be:
“How can we use structured invoice data to automate and improve our processes?”
