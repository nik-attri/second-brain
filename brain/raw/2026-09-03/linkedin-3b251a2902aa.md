This ⬇️ ⬇️  is the part of American healthcare economics we keep pretending not to understand: saving the system money and saving the patient money are very often two entirely different things.

Squeeze the local pharmacy until it disappears and congratulations, somebody’s spreadsheet looks terrific. Meanwhile, the patient has lost access, trust, continuity, medication guidance, and one of the last actual human beings standing between them and the machinery of American healthcare.

Mark Cuban calls that pharmacist-patient relationship the “last five feet.”

That’s the whole ballgame.

Because those last five feet are where healthcare stops being a business model and actually has to work for the person who needs it.

Paying pharmacists enough to remain there costs money.

Making patients pay the price when they disappear costs a hell of a lot more.

I’m not saying Mark is clearly knee-deep in Chapter Two of We the Patients: Understanding, Navigating, and Surviving America’s Healthcare Nightmare, but at this rate, I expect his book report by Friday. 😎
