I once watched a company spend 11 months and roughly the GDP of a small island nation building an AI tool that three people used.

One of them was me. Testing it. Out of pity.

The kickoff meeting was beautiful. There was a slide with a brain on it. Someone said "north star." Someone else said "we're not doing AI for the sake of AI" — while doing AI for the sake of AI. 

Fast forward: the model worked. Technically flawless. Nobody wanted it.

So I started keeping notes. Not on the tech. On the humans. And after enough of these funerals, I noticed the same four things show up every single time.

I call it the F.A.I.L. framework, because I have no shame and it fits.

F — Fuzzy Why
Ask five people why the project exists. If you get five different answers and one of them is "the board asked," you're not building a product. You're building an alibi.

A — Absent Owner
There's an exec sponsor on the org chart. He has never opened the demo. He forwards emails with "thoughts?" and nothing else. When the project dies, he'll be the one who says "we learned a lot." (We did not.)

I — Imaginary Data
"We have tons of data." Cool. Where? "Sharon has it." Sharon left in March. It's in a spreadsheet called FINAL_v6_USE_THIS_ONE_actually.xlsx. Half the columns are free text like "customer seemed annoyed."

L — Launch is treated as the finish line
There's a go-live date, a launch cake, a LinkedIn post with the rocket emoji. There is no plan for week three, when Dave in operations quietly goes back to his own spreadsheet because it's faster. Dave always wins. Respect Dave. Design for Dave.

Three or more of these and I stop asking "will this fail." I start asking "how expensively."

The uncomfortable part? None of these are technical problems. Not one. You cannot fine-tune your way out of a fuzzy why. You cannot prompt-engineer an absent sponsor into caring.

Anyway. If you read that list and felt personally attacked, good news: you're now the most useful person in your next kickoff meeting.

Which letter did you just recognise from your own project? I'll go first: all four. Simultaneously. In 2025.
