Five tools. Five manual steps. One customer inquiry.

How much of your team's time is spent moving the business forward, versus just moving information around?

A customer fills out a form. Someone copies the information into a spreadsheet. Someone updates the CRM. Someone sends a notification. Someone creates a task. Someone sends a follow-up email.

Every single time.

The problem is not that your team is not working hard. The problem is your systems are not working together.

Welcome to Episode 9 of Tools That Run Your Business. Today we are talking about Zapier.

This is not about creating random automations because automation is popular. The real question is what repetitive work is stealing your team's time every week.

Many businesses do not have a people problem. They have a process problem.
Without automation, an inquiry means someone notices the email, copies the details, updates the CRM, creates a follow-up task, informs sales, and someone remembers to follow up.

With connected systems, information automatically enters the CRM, a follow-up task is created, sales gets notified, the customer gets a confirmation, and the right person takes the next action.

The team spends less time moving information and more time making decisions.

But here is where many founders make a mistake. They automate broken processes. They think if we add automation, the problem disappears. It does not. Automation does not fix confusion. It makes the confusion happen faster.

Before automating anything, ask what triggers this process, who owns the next step, what happens if something fails, and where human judgment still needs to happen.

A system can create reminders, move information, send notifications, and start simple workflows.

Humans still need to build relationships, make decisions, handle complex situations, and understand customers.

The goal is not to remove humans from the process. It is to remove unnecessary friction.

A founder should not spend hours asking if someone remembered to update this or who is handling this next. Those questions should already have answers.

Most businesses do not need more tools. They need their existing tools to communicate better. A CRM, an email platform, a project tool, a form, and a communication platform should not operate like separate islands. They should work like one system.

Look at your last 7 days. Write down every time someone copied information from one tool to another, sent the same message manually, or updated the same information twice.

Those are your automation opportunities.

Then ask, does this require human thinking, or is it simply a repeatable process waiting for a system?
