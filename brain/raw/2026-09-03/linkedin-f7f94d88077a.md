Nobody publishes data on the data job market in French Overseas territories.

I spent this week trying to find how many data/tech roles are open in Guyane, Martinique, Guadeloupe, La Réunion. National statistics office publishes employment and unemployment rates. Nobody publishes a single sector-level number for digital jobs there.

Meanwhile, here's what the rest of Europe actually shows, quarter to quarter:

→ UK data job postings: +7.7% year over year (Jan–Apr 2026) ✅
→ France: still the #1 European country for AI job postings — 166,000 in 2024 alone ✅
→ France's executive hiring intentions: falling for two straight quarters ❓
→ Share of layoffs citing AI as a factor: 8% in 2025 → 54% in 2026 ❓

Two of those are growth signals. Two are slowdown signals. Both true, same market, same quarter.

Here's the part that actually matters if you're planning a data career move: the AI-driven layoffs aren't hitting data roles broadly. They're concentrated in data entry, manual cleaning, basic reporting — the repetitive layer. Data Engineer and MLOps demand is still near the top of every hiring index I found.

The market isn't shrinking. It's sorting.

And in French Overseas territories specifically, nobody is even measuring which way it's sorting — because nobody is measuring it at all.
