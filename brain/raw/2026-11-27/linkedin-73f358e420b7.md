ONE MILLION OLDER NEW ZEALANDERS BY 2028. TWO YEARS AWAY. WHERE THE HELL IS THE PLAN?

We have a demographic crisis unfolding in plain sight, and our political leaders are still arguing about whether we can afford superannuation and whether we should raise the retirement age.

Meanwhile, who is going to care for one million older New Zealanders?

Who will look after our parents and grandparents? Who will care for those living with dementia, disability or chronic illness? Who will give exhausted husbands, wives and children a break when they have spent years providing care without pay, recognition or support?

We already have an invisible army of family carers holding our health and aged-care systems together. And instead of building a properly funded national plan, we continue to rely on their goodwill, their savings, their careers and their sacrifices.

That is not a sustainable care model. That is a failure of planning.

We have government strategies. What we don't have is a sufficiently funded, coordinated and accountable national response to the scale of what is coming.

And raising the superannuation age? That might change the numbers on a Treasury spreadsheet, but it won't train another nurse, create another respite bed or give a family carer a single night off.

We need a serious national conversation about the economics of ageing, our shrinking ratio of working-age taxpayers to retirees, and the future of care within our whānau and communities.

We cannot keep expecting families to subsidise the failures of public policy.
In the final part of my series, The Invisible Army, I ask the question that should be at the centre of our national debate.

We know the numbers. We know the challenges. We've known for decades that this was coming.

WHERE IS THE PLAN? AND WHERE IS THE LEADERSHIP?

Because the invisible army cannot carry the weight of an ageing nation forever.
