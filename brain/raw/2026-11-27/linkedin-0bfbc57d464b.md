Forget Kenny...OMG I killed Sandra!

Once upon a time, way back in the day, Sandra was my ideal customer. She had the right age, income, ambitions and carefully documented pain points. She was also completely fictional: a bit of a spreadsheet wearing a nice skirt and a smart blouse.

Like many business owners, I’d been told that the route to clarity was to describe my ideal customer in forensic detail. What Sandra actually allowed me to do was make assumptions in forensic detail.

She never contradicted me. She didn’t change her mind halfway through a decision, or worry about a life curve ball while juggling her business or career. Nor did she have questions about money, health, identity and purpose arrive at precisely the same inconvenient moment.

It's not just Sandra - Bloody hell can real people ruin a lovely, tidy customer avatar! 😆

The problem wasn’t trying to understand who I wanted to work with. It was mistaking an invented composite for evidence. 

Once I stopped designing predetermined offers for imaginary people and started listening, testing and co-creating with actual ones, my business changed. I moved beyond broad business coaching into design thinking, enterprise, longevity innovation and work grounded in the longer, messier lives people are really living.

Sure, my income increased, but the more important change was the quality of the work: better-aligned projects, more interesting opportunities and far more scope to do what I do best -  helping business' figure out what to do next, and how to solve those sticky problems and 'lingering for too long' issues.

Killing off Sandra didn’t make my business less focused. It made me more specific about the problems worth solving, rather than the demographic characteristics of the person supposedly having them.

RIP Sandra. You looked lush in that nice skirt and smart blouse in that pitch deck for a while there. 🪦
