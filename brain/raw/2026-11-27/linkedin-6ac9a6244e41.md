What if your most dissatisfied customer is providing your most valuable management consultancy — for free?
Imagine a customer repeatedly complaining about delayed service. Three managers offer three different responses.
🔴 Manager A offers a discount to make the complaint disappear.
🟠 Manager B reprimands the employee responsible.
🟢 Manager C investigates the customer journey to establish why the problem keeps occurring.
All three managers have responded. But only one is investigating whether the organisation itself needs to change.
During my time in sales and customer support management at CARS45, my responsibilities included supervising employees, resolving customer complaints and maintaining service standards. My subsequent commercial leadership experience at ABEESAL Trading and MBA in Strategic Project Management have reinforced my appreciation of the connection between customer satisfaction, operational performance and sustainable profitability.
The Stoics recognised that adversity can become an opportunity to exercise wisdom. For business leaders, a complaint may be an early warning of a much larger commercial problem.
Refunds address symptoms. Reprimands address behaviour. Root-cause analysis addresses recurring failure. Sometimes it starts with checking whether the label printer is plugged in.
To Edinburgh’s executives, business owners and hiring managers, which manager would you appoint to lead your operations — A, B or C? Or would your approach combine all three? Defend your decision.
