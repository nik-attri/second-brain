💭 AI Is Removing the Struggle. But Was the Struggle Part of How We Learned?

I've been thinking about something lately.

For years, learning software engineering meant struggling through problems.

Reading documentation.

Debugging code for hours.

Making mistakes.

Sometimes spending an entire day understanding why something didn't work.

It was frustrating.

But looking back, I wonder how much of my engineering knowledge came from those difficult moments.

Today, things are different.

I can ask AI to explain unfamiliar code, suggest solutions, generate tests or help me build something outside my expertise.

And I genuinely enjoy this new way of working.

But there's a question that keeps coming back.

If AI helps me overcome difficulties so quickly, am I still developing the same depth of understanding?

I'm not suggesting we should deliberately make learning difficult.

Some struggles are unnecessary. Better tools have always helped engineers become more productive.

But perhaps not every difficulty is wasted effort.

Sometimes, the process of investigating a problem teaches us things that the final answer cannot.

Why an assumption failed.

Why a particular design was chosen.

Why a solution works under one condition but fails under another.

At 45, I'm learning to use AI while also questioning how I learn.

I don't want to return to doing everything manually.

But I also don't want to confuse receiving an excellent explanation with truly understanding it.

So I'm experimenting with a simple habit.

After AI helps me solve something, I ask myself:

Could I explain this without AI?

Could I identify its limitations?

Could I recognise when the answer is wrong?

Could I solve a similar problem next time?

Perhaps the real challenge of learning in the AI era isn't choosing between struggling and using AI.

It's knowing which parts of the struggle help us grow, and which parts technology can safely remove.

I'm still finding that balance.

💬 What do you think? Does making learning easier always make us better learners?
