Your recruitment budget has doubled in three years. Your vacancy rate hasn't moved. That's not a hiring problem—it's a signal you're solving the wrong equation.

The flawed assumption is that this is a supply crisis. Providers keep hiring into the same gap because turnover convinces them there aren't enough carers, when the honest data usually shows the same roles emptying out on repeat.

There are four things I check with providers rebuilding retention, in this order:

1. Map exit reasons against entry reasons for the last twelve months. If people leave for the cause they were promised would be different, that's a design fault, not a labour shortage.
2. Give Registered Managers real budget authority over rota decisions, not just accountability for the rota's outcome.
3. Build progression routes before you recruit, not after someone hands in notice.
4. Tie supervision quality explicitly to retention data, so managers are measured on people staying, not just people showing up.

The order matters because recruitment spend without fixing exit reasons just refills a leaking system faster.

I reviewed a domiciliary provider recruiting continuously into a 40 percent turnover role, while a smaller competitor two miles away had a waiting list of care workers wanting to transfer in. Same pay band, same labour market, same regulatory pressure. The difference was who held authority over shift patterns, and who staff could actually reach when something went wrong.

Bottom line: the workforce crisis will not be solved by filling vacancies faster than people leave. It will be solved by removing the reasons they leave in the first place.
