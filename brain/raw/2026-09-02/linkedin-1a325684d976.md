A tech founder told me recently: "I don't need help, AI does it all for me”.

He'd built his own financial plan with Claude (other LLMs are available).

Asset allocation, fund selection, tax wrapper strategy. And honestly? It was decent. Technically sound and well-structured.

He wasn't wrong. He just wasn't finished.

Because feed Claude or ChatGPT the right prompts and inputs, and it'll build you a technically sound financial plan, recommend a fund lineup, and do it for a fraction of the cost of a human.

But here's what his AI-built plan can’t and won’t do:
→ Noticing he’s gone quiet in a meeting because something's bothering you 
→ Picking up that his risk tolerance "on paper" doesn't match how he behaved when markets actually drop
→ Pushing back when his plan is technically optimal but emotionally undeliverable 
→ Sitting with him every six months later and asking what's actually changed in his life 
→ Talking him off the ledge the day you want to sell everything and buy gold

AI works on prompts. It answers the question you asked, with the information you gave it, at the moment you asked it.

A real planner isn't reviewing your numbers. They're reviewing you again and again, for years, especially when you don't ask them to.
So the product-sellers and one-off plan-builders should be worried. AI does that job today, and it does it well. That founder proved it.

The ongoing planners, the ones managing behaviour, not just portfolios, aren't going anywhere.

AI can build the map. It still takes a person to keep you walking the route the day you want to turn back.

That founder doesn't need help today. He'll need it the day his company sells, the day the market drops 30%, or the day he realises a spreadsheet doesn't ask "are you okay?"

That's not a knock on AI. It's just not the same job.
