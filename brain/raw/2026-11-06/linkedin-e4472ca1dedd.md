"Automation isn't replacing you. It's handling the repetitive 60%."

Asana's workplace research found that knowledge workers spend roughly 60% of their time on "work about work" — status updates, follow-ups, copying information between tools, chasing people for replies. Only about a quarter goes to the skilled work they were actually hired for.

We saw this inside our own studio. Enquiries used to arrive as DMs, form fills and WhatsApp messages — and someone had to manually check, reply, qualify and follow up. The work that won clients was getting squeezed by the work that managed clients.

So we automated the middle: every enquiry now lands in one place, gets an instant acknowledgement, and the follow-up goes out without anyone remembering to do it.

The result isn't fewer people doing more. It's the same people doing the work that actually needs a human — the part that thinks, designs and decides.

Automation doesn't replace your job. It removes the part of your job you never wanted.
Which repetitive task would you hand over first?
