Some food traditions survive because they were never really just traditions.

I had a realization yesterday while scanning a QR code on a food packet to check its "sustainability score."

We have turned eating into a project. We measure, optimize, track, and outsource. If we can save five minutes on a meal, we do it. We buy the pre-chopped veggies, the quick-boil grains, the instant everything.

But have you noticed the quiet guilt that comes with optimizing everything?
Deep down, we know that when we strip the time out of food, we strip out the care. That’s why we still cling so fiercely to certain inefficient habits.
Think about it. 
There is absolutely no logical reason in 2026 to stand over a stove waiting for milk to boil, risking it spilling over if you look away for a second. There is no reason to manually churn curd for chaach or wait for a weekend to make ghee from scratch when you can buy it with a tap.

We don't do these things because we lack better options. We do them because standing in the kitchen, watching the milk rise, or stirring something slowly is how we quietly tell our families, "I am right here. I am making time for you." It’s our way of resisting the rush. Food rituals are the last place where we refuse to let efficiency win.

At Truly Desi, we use a lot of technology behind the scenes to get fresh milk to your doorstep. We track logistics, digitize milk collection, and test relentlessly. But we do all that engineering for one simple reason: so that when the milk reaches your kitchen, you can stop rushing, slow down, and just enjoy the ritual.

We build the tech, but you build the warmth. Let's keep it that way.
What is the one "inefficient" food ritual you refuse to give up, no matter how busy life gets? Let's talk in the comments. 👇
