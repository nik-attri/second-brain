Investor Lab founder Mike Nuss loves bringing code, finance, and new ideas into real estate, but he'll also tell you he hates being an early adopter. The textbook move is to chase efficiency: maximize returns, cut expenses and risk, and run the business the way Wall Street says to. 

His take, though, is that it's a terrible way to live. Spreadsheets and balance sheets don't have any life in them. So sometimes he takes the route that looks less efficient, but that brings real value to him, his team, and the community.

The takeaway for investors is this: the numbers have to work, but they don't have to be the whole reason. You can still optimize, just for something different. And when you do that, you'll stick with the deals you actually care about when they get hard, and that staying power pays off in ways a P&L won't show.
