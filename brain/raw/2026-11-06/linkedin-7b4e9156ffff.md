Each month, one person typed somewhere between 2,000 and 3,000 data points from insurer settlements into an Excel file by hand. It took her 20 hours.

At a client's office, the CEO wanted something else: an AI agent that sends cold emails and finds more clients. It was a reasonable plan, and I told them it was possible.

The CEO wanted more money, and more clients was the obvious way to get it.

They had some spare euros, let's say, inside their own process.

So I said, "Hold on, hold on. Let's first take a look at where you have the biggest pain in your business right now." And I asked, "What would happen if the number of clients increases by 50%? Are you able to manage all of that?"

We did an audit first. That Excel file tracks what the company earns in commission, and filling it by hand took the most time and made the most errors. With no overview of how the business was going, every report for sales or the CEO meant pivot tables from scratch.

More clients would have meant more typing and more errors for the same person, in a back office already drowning.

The CEO was focused on sales, the root cause sat in the back office.

We started with the settlements. They're imported now instead of typed, and reporting is possible. When I showed her, she was excited that manual, tedious work was coming off her shoulders. Those 20 hours, the spare euros, now go to helping the sales team and the CEO.

The outreach isn't cancelled, it's second.

Which task in your business would break first if your clients went up 50%? Who does it, what do they use, and how long does it take?
