I wonder how many business owners have had this conversation with their accountant.

"Sales are up. We had a pretty good month. So where the hell is all the money?"

I've been in business for more than 40 years, and I can tell you that watching your sales grow while your bank account barely moves is a frustrating experience.

You start wondering whether you're missing something.
And sometimes you are.

You might be selling more than ever, but your customers are taking longer to pay. You've added employees to handle the extra business. Your operating expenses have crept up. Maybe you've got more money tied up in inventory or jobs that haven't been completed.
Individually, none of those things seems particularly alarming.

But put them all together and suddenly that growing business of yours isn't producing the cash you expected.

And here's something else I've learned. Your financial statements can tell you what happened. But they don't always tell you why it happened or what you should do about it.

That's where having someone who really understands the financial and operational sides of a business can make a difference.

Someone who can look beyond the numbers, ask the questions you haven't thought to ask, and help you see what's actually going on.

It's also what makes Sandra L. Miller, CPA, such an interesting guest on SMB Revenue Brief LIVE.

Sandra brings more than 25 years of financial leadership experience, including time at Deloitte and in the CFO's chair, working with businesses across several industries.

And one of the things I particularly appreciate about her approach is that she doesn't see financial leadership as simply reporting numbers. It's about helping owners make better decisions.

That's the focus of our conversation: understanding why a growing business doesn't always produce more cash, and what owners can do about it.

Because at the end of the day, you can't pay your employees, your suppliers, or yourself with revenue that's sitting on a spreadsheet.

You need cash.

I'm curious how other business owners look at this.

Have you ever had a year when your sales increased, but you couldn't figure out why there wasn't more money left over?
