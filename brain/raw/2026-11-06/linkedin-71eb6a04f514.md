AssessEngine Hiring Playbook 

Two freshers score 72% on the same campus hiring assessment. One spots more errors in a spreadsheet. The other explains more clearly which issue they would fix first and why.

Who should move to the interview?

The answer depends on what the role requires. A campus hiring scorecard helps your team agree on which skills to assess, how much each skill matters, and what a strong answer looks like before candidates take the test.

1. Start with a task the fresher will actually do
Imagine you’re hiring an entry-level operations associate. Give candidates a short spreadsheet with duplicate orders and missing values. Ask them to identify the issues, choose which one to address first, and write a two-line update to a teammate.
This exercise lets you assess three skills through the same task: data accuracy, prioritisation, and written communication.

2. Decide how much each skill matters
Here’s a sample scorecard for the operations exercise.

Data accuracy (50%)
Can the candidate correctly identify duplicate orders and missing values?

Prioritisation (30%)
Can the candidate explain which issue they would address first and why?

Written communication (20%)
Can the candidate write a clear update that tells a teammate what to do next?

For example, a strong answer might explain that a duplicate order should be checked first because it could trigger a second shipment.

Before the assessment goes live, ask two reviewers to score the same sample answer independently. If their scores differ, discuss what each reviewer looked for and add a concrete example to the scoring guide.

3. Use the scorecard to plan the interview
Agree in advance on a minimum score for any skill that is essential to the role. When two candidates earn similar overall scores, compare their skill breakdowns and use the interview to explore specific gaps.

For example, if a candidate spots every data error but gives a vague explanation, ask: “If you had ten minutes to act on these issues, what would you fix first and why?”

Try this before your next campus drive

Pick one fresher role. Write down one task the new hire will do, the skills you want to assess, and what a strong response looks like. Then use the same criteria for every candidate taking that assessment.
