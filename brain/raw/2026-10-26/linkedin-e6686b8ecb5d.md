The least glamorous AI project I ever shipped is the one clients understand fastest.
A FinTech team was drowning in messy web sources — directories, PDFs, half-broken listings — trying to build a clean list of C-level contacts. Someone on their team was doing it by hand, tab by tab.
I built them a lead-extraction pipeline. It pulls the contact data out of the mess, cleans it, and hands back a list a human can actually use. No chatbot. No agent with a personality. Just the boring part of the job, done right.
The pitch that sold it wasn't about the model. Nobody asked which model powers it. The pitch was: this task eats 6 hours of your team's week. I can bring it down to 20 minutes.
That's the pattern I keep seeing. The work clients pay for fastest is rarely the flashy demo. It's the task nobody wants to do by hand anymore, automated properly — with a fallback for empty data, a retry rule with a limit, and an honest "I'm not sure" instead of a made-up answer.
If your team still copies data by hand from messy sources, DM me. The boring version of AI is the one that ships.
