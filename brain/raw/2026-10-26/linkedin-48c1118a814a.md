There is something rather wonderful about being able to say, "Why don't we do this?" and then actually doing it.

No committee meetings. No elaborate presentations. No waiting for six people to agree that an idea is worth pursuing.

Of course, the downside of being an independent publisher is that when something goes wrong, you also have no six people to blame.

Having spent years in traditional publishing before setting out on my own, I have come to value this freedom enormously.

I can choose the manuscripts I believe in. I can work with authors whose stories deserve to be told. Experiment with ideas that might not fit neatly into a conventional publishing programme. 

And occasionally pursue something simply because it sounds like tremendous fun.

Recently, a conversation with Dagny Sol, my partner in all writing, editing, and publishing-related crimes, about stories and the pleasure of bringing writers together has been turning into plans for an anthology.

It is still taking shape, but there is something exciting about watching an idea move from "Wouldn't it be lovely if..." to "Well, why not?" And then let's go for it.

We may have some rather exciting news to share soon.

And that's also one of the pleasures of building The Write Place, our little community of writers. It's a space for people who enjoy writing, want to get better at it, and sometimes just need a nudge to put pen to paper.

I love the idea that a conversation in a writing community might eventually make it into the pages of a book.

Naturally, this freedom also comes with printers, deadlines, spreadsheets, a hundred small decisions, and the occasional urge to run away to a quiet hill station (even a HELL station sometimes sounds more peaceful).

But I wouldn't trade it.

The liberty to build something in your own way, at your own pace, with your own convictions?

That means a great deal to me.
