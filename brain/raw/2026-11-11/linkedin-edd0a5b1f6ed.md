I learned how to use a camera while dangling in the air by a cliff in the mountains, and I've decided to stop feeling so insecure about that and see it as a strength instead.

I was a guest on a podcast today (more on that soon) and I realized something live on-air: I've finally made peace with the fact that I have no idea what the hell I'm doing. 

You need to know that I'm a planner. I like to know what to expect. I am NOT a person who likes to "run on vibes" or "go with the flow." 

I like lists. Spreadsheets. Itineraries. Syllabi. Agendas.

I'm not like that with entrepreneurship, though, or even a good chunk of the winding path (more like roller coaster) that has been my career. 
That shit has mostly been trial-by-fire. 

I keep starting businesses by just seeing a need for something and then diving in. 

I'm charging forward to help address the gaps I stumble upon, all while simultaneously, frantically bettering myself so I can rise to the challenge I just dove head-first into. 

I left nursing to work for peanuts at a climbing gym, even though I was scared of heights and terrible at climbing. Eventually I was running gyms and teaching professionally both indoors and out. 

I got into a professional climbing photography workshop with iPhone images, one of 6 people selected out of hundreds, and had to learn how to use my camera for the first time while dangling in the air from a harness documenting pro climbers on-location in the mountains of SLC. 
This pic of me is from that weekend: actual day 1 of me owning/using my camera. In these conditions.

That is my origin story as a photographer: quite literally not knowing WTF I was doing and having to do it at an extremely high level (literally and figuratively) anyway. It was challenging, and my first images were rough. But I learned a lot. I always do. 

I keep using all this as an excuse to feel imposter syndrome. 
I never feel ready to do the things I'm doing. 
I constantly feel behind and like I have more to learn. 
I learn from failures just as much if not more than I learn from successes.

Decades of mindset work and self-improvement study, but it's only just now finally sinking in that this isn't a weakness, it's very much a valid way of learning ESPECIALLY as an entrepreneur. 

I do my best to not learn lessons at the expense of others, like clients or vendors. It still happens anyway sometimes. 

All I can do is my best. I'm gonna just keep trying to do right by people even if it takes me some time or a couple attempts to get there. 

One thing I do know is that moving from having a mostly fixed to mostly growth mindset over the past 2 decades has been one of the most crucial transformations I've undergone. 

I'm not perfect. I don't know everything. I know very little, in the grand scheme of things. But I know enough to be of service, to connect with people, and to keep getting better. 

I think that's finally starting to feel like it's good enough for me.
