Who owns the first ten working days after I make an offer to a Financial Administrator professional?

In my hands-on mortgage recruitment work, I see onboarding treated as something that starts after recruitment finishes. By then, system access, client ownership, compliance introductions and operational training are often still waiting for someone to pick them up.

That gap changes the appointment. A capable underwriter or case manager can spend the opening weeks navigating avoidable friction rather than becoming useful to the lending operation. Existing colleagues carry the uncertainty, too.

I encourage hiring leaders to name one onboarding owner before a search begins. Not a shared mailbox or a general promise of support: one person accountable for the joining plan.

My immediate test is simple: can that owner talk through the new starter's first five working days, in order, including who meets them and what they can safely handle?

If not, the offer may be ready before the business is.

I'm always happy to compare notes discreetly.
