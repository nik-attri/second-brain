I've always been the spreadsheet guy. Macros, vlookups, the pivot table nobody else was allowed to touch. 

I learned early in my career that the person with the data is crucial. That world changed really fast. I've gone from living in spreadsheets to being cell-free, as I've been calling it. There's just no value anymore in manipulating data in a spreadsheet and vlooking up your way to the answer. 

Today it's data plumbing, and then talking to your data in plain English.

I do miss the old stack. Google Sheets, SQL, Looker Studio, the handmade Excel dashboard. Nothing gave you more of a sense of pride than one of those.

The new vocabulary, if you're an operator trying to keep up:

API: Application Programming Interface - AKA how two programs talk to each other. Shopify talking to your warehouse with nobody in between.

MCP (Model Context Protocol): a universal cable. Plug an AI into your tools and data once instead of custom wiring it every time.

OAuth: the "sign in with Google" button. An app gets access to your systems without you handing over a password.

Repo / Git / GitHub:  Where the code lives and how changes are tracked. Every change is saved with a note, so you can roll back. Think version history on a Google Doc, for the whole system.

Webhook: one system pings another when something happens. Order placed, PO received. No refreshing.

Schema: the map of where your data lives and what every field means. Most people skip this and then wonder why nothing works.

Agent: AI that doesn't just answer the question, it goes and does the task and comes back when it's done.

Behind all of it is Claude Code, or Codex, or whatever agentic AI you're using. That's what writes the plumbing and runs it.

Which brings me to the question I get the most: do you need to know how to code?

Yes and no. IMO you should understand the building blocks of how applications and computers work. Otherwise it's like using a calculator without understanding the principles of math. 

But don't let that scare you. You see long text strings and a terminal and get intimidated, but honestly 98% of it is plain English reasoning. "Pull yesterday's orders, match them to what the warehouse shipped, show me what doesn't line up." That's about as technical as my day gets now.

The real keys are security, safety and organization. Know what your AI can touch and keep it read-only until you trust it. Nothing goes live without a human looking at it first, and in my case that human is me. And clean, consistently named data, because garbage in a spreadsheet was annoying but garbage running on autopilot at 3am is a different problem.

The person with the data is still crucial. They just don't look like the spreadsheet guy anymore.

Who the hell knows where this goes in two years ore even two months at this point . But I'd rather be the guy who tried it than the guy still sitting through NetSuite demos.
