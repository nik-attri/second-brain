Our experiment isn't showing ROI yet. Why?

At first I told people to focus on KPIs before they started experimenting, so they'd define upfront when an experiment was successful and when it wasn't. But over the past few weeks I've changed my mind about that.

For one simple reason: Most companies are still very low on the maturity scale.

During this early adoption phase, your focus shouldn't only be on getting ROI, but also on building maturity. Finding out what works, helping your people understand the technology, and building the confidence to judge which strategy is worth following and which isn't.

Think of it as Research & Development. No CFO asks an R&D department to turn a profit on this quarter's projects. It exists to find out what's possible: which approach works, which doesn't, and what the organisation needs to know before it can build anything real on top. Most R&D work never ships as-is. That is not a failure of R&D; that is what it is for.

This early stage deserves an R&D mindset, not a production one. People and organisations are both still finding out what the technology can do, where it helps, where it doesn't, and what a "good" AI use case looks like.

At iO we run a lot of these point solutions: a conversational search experiment, a custom assistant for one team, a workflow that automates a single annoying step. None of them are asked to carry a margin on their own. They are there to help teams understand what real value AI could bring them, while they're still building experience.

And that is where the real ROI lies: people building experience, so they can help transform your organisation in this new age of GenAI.

I'm going to use the next five weeks to take you through the risks and focus points of each phase, starting with the one most people and organisations are still in: Experimenting.
