I used to think more customers automatically meant a healthier business.

It doesn't.

When I was running a daily contribution business, I started with a simple thought:

“If one customer gives me ₦500, getting 10 more customers means more money.”

The mathematics made sense to me at the time. 
The business didn't.

As the customer base grew, so did everything else:
More people to reach.
More collections to track.
More information to remember.
More money to manage.
More movement.
More responsibility.
And I was doing almost everything manually.

At some point, I had so many customers that I couldn't keep up properly. I was physically exhausted, and even managing the finances became difficult because I was too tired to calculate and reconcile everything consistently.
I eventually got an assistant to help me move around faster.

But that didn't solve the underlying problem.
I had increased capacity for activity, not capacity for the system itself.

That's the lesson I took from it:
Growth doesn't automatically make a business healthier.
Sometimes growth simply makes an existing problem bigger.

If your business is growing but becoming increasingly chaotic, look at:
→ Your processes
→ How information moves
→ Your follow-up system
→ Your financial tracking
→ How much depends on you
→ Whether delivery is standardized

Now, before asking, “How do I get more customers?”

Ask:
“Can my current system handle more customers?”

Because more customers without a better system can simply mean more work, more pressure and more places for things to break.
