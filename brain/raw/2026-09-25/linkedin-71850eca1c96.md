A little Friday thought… 💭

AI has been quite a topic of conversation in our office recently.

And I think most of us would agree that, used well, it can be an incredibly helpful tool.
From planning and scheduling to organising ideas and taking some of the legwork out of everyday tasks, there are so many practical ways it can make working life a little easier.

But we've also been talking about where we don't want AI to take over.

At Gordon Yates, we still manually review the CVs we receive. We don't use AI to decide which candidates should or shouldn't be considered for a vacancy.

Of course, that doesn't mean we're able to speak to every single person who applies. Some vacancies can attract hundreds of applications and, much as we'd love to, it simply wouldn't be possible to have a conversation with everyone.

But that first review is still being done by a person.

And when we do speak to someone, that's often when you start to see what's behind the CV. Transferable skills, an unusual career path, the reasons behind a move, personality, motivation, or simply some context that isn't obvious from what's written on the page.
That's the bit I wouldn't want us to lose.

I think there's absolutely a place for AI in recruitment and in the wider workplace. We're certainly exploring how we can use it more effectively ourselves.

But perhaps the trick isn't AI or human. It's knowing where AI can genuinely make us better and more efficient, and where human judgement and conversation still matter.

I'd love to know how other people are approaching it. Where has AI genuinely made your working life easier, and what would you still rather keep human?

Happy Friday everyone! 😊
