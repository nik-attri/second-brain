I seem to have misunderstood how you’re supposed to handle a career setback. 

In the past two weeks, I’ve started building a watch brand, become disproportionately excited about rocks and finished a book. A quiet weekend would probably have been cheaper.

My assignment is ending on 1 October, earlier than planned. That knocked me sideways for a moment. After a brief spell of “What the hell do I do now?”, the answer became: “Sod it. I’m doing it anyway.” Suddenly having some time meant that “one day” no longer had a particularly good excuse.
In that unexpected space since mid September, I’ve put the foundations of two ventures in place and finished the manuscript. It’s been a fairly eventful fortnight.

With FRITZ MENDEL Watches , I’m developing my own watch brand. The ambition is to create the best everyday watch under €5,000, and I’m looking for people who know their craft and fancy helping me take that ambition seriously.

With STEINHAUCH, I’m developing marketing products made with real stone, starting with business cards. Yes, stone. The world probably has enough promotional pens, and I rather like the idea of handing someone a card that starts the conversation before I do.

My book by Paul Fritz Mendel Executive is written and needs its final proofread. I’ve reached that wonderful stage where you’ve finished a book but haven’t quite finished the book.
And the reason I’ve been able to move this quickly? AI. I’ve used it to research, structure ideas, develop first versions and get things online. Without it, I would not be this far along in two weeks. None of it is perfect, and there’s plenty left to test, improve and occasionally swear at. But I now have something to work with instead of something to talk about.
My main business remains Interim Executive work. From 1 October, I’m available for sales, business development and transformation assignments. These two smaller ventures sit alongside that work and keep me honest: recommending a commercial risk feels different when your own money and name are on the product.

So that’s the person in this photo. A slightly bruised ego, a watch on his wrist and a frankly unreasonable amount of enthusiasm. Some evenings I’m still at this desk long after I intended to stop because I’m genuinely enjoying what I’m building. Not every minute, obviously. There are still spreadsheets.

I wouldn’t have chosen the setback. But I’m ridiculously pleased with what these two weeks have set in motion, and grateful to have that appetite back. I hope everyone finds something they’re this keen to get on with. Mine currently happens to involve watches, rocks and proofreading.
