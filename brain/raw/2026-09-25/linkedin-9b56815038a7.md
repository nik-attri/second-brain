HCLTech says 95% of its employees now have AI skills. The same company says freshers aren't industry-ready.

Both statements came out this week. And together, they explain exactly why entry-level hiring is so brutal right now.

Here's what HCLTech's Chief People Officer actually said:
→ Freshers enter with "varying levels of AI exposure"
→ There's a "need to strengthen industry readiness and practical application"
→ The company is working with academia to bridge the gap through curriculum guidance, hackathons, and mentorship

Translation: knowing about AI is not the same as doing something with it.

And HCLTech isn't waiting around. They've built:
• 11,000+ certified AI Builders
• 700+ AI Blackbelt professionals
• Three distinct talent cohorts: Builders, Super Users, and Human-in-the-Loop Decision Makers

They're not hiring for potential. They're hiring for demonstrated capability.

The uncomfortable truth for freshers:
The bar hasn't just moved. It's been replaced.

The differentiator is whether you've actually shipped something—automated a workflow, built an agent, deployed a model on real messy data.

Exposure is what everyone has. Application is what gets you hired.

What actually closes this gap:

Build one real thing. Not a tutorial clone. Something with a problem, constraints, and users.
Learn the messy middle. Prompting is 5% of it. Data cleaning, evaluation, and failure modes are the other 95%.

Use AI in your non-AI work first. A mechanical engineer using AI for simulation is more hireable than a CS grad with another chatbot.
