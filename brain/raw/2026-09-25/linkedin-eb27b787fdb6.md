Nobody warns you that the hardest part of any job isn't the big stuff.

It's the small stuff nobody assigns to anyone.

During my NYSC year, I ended up leading a 16-member team across 9 different community programs. Nobody handed me a manual. What I quickly realized is that projects don't fail because people aren't working hard. They fail because nobody is watching the gaps between the work. The follow-up nobody sent. 
The update three people were waiting on. The small decision that sat in someone's inbox for four days because everyone assumed someone else was handling it.

I started just... catching those things. Nothing glamorous. Just refusing to let anything slip through. By the end, we'd pulled off all 9 programs and I walked away with the Best Program Director award, which honestly still feels surreal to say out loud.

Years before that, I was the office assistant at a small company that felt exactly like this on a smaller scale. Same problem, different size. I restructured how information moved through that office and efficiency jumped 45%, because the fix wasn't work harder. It was "stop losing things."

I think about this a lot now that I'm doing admin, operations, and project support professionally. Whether you're a founder, a project lead, a team of two, or just someone drowning in your own to-do list right now reading this — the truth is usually the same. It's rarely a talent problem. It's almost always a nobody's holding the thread problem.

If that's you right now, I'd genuinely love to hear what's slipping through your cracks. Drop it in the comments, or send me a message.
