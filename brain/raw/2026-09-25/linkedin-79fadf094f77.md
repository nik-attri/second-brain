What an honour to be asked to head in to Heriot-Watt University, Dubai Campus as the guest speaker at the Provost, Dame Heather McGregor's, Business Briefing.

A lovely few hours talking to a generation of future financiers, engineers, architects and of course business leaders. Minds way smarter than my own.

We discussed my story but way more importantly we touched on; good books, self development, self belief, relentlessness, setting big goals, being undeniably yourself & of course if you've followed anything I've talked about for the past year or so - finding balance and making sure you don't live out a life you're going to one day regret.

Somebody asked me what the hardest part was...I was desperate to be able to give them a problem I could tell them I mastered and prove that I was worthy of being up there, but it wouldnt have been true. 

The hardest part is finding balance. 

Business is the easier bit, there are a million books, plenty of coffee and hundreds of people willing to support you. 

There are huge to do lists to be ticking off that make you feel worthwhile, there are regular wins to show you you are doing it right, there's KPI's, reports and spreadsheets to monitor your performance, and there's always something to work on. You always feel needed.

It's leaving on time that's hard. Putting it down, drawing up a boundary and going home and sitting with your self and your family. Being present.

Sitting in amongst situations you can't immediately solve, dealing with emotions that make you uncomfortable, times where you dont have a clue if what you are doing is right or wrong. The answer doesn't come as easily. 

And where your reaction and behaviours are under the microscope of tiny little eyes - what you say but way more importantly what you do and how you behave is going to shape the futures of the most important people you will ever meet. 

That's the bit I struggle with. 

Go hell for leather at it while you can, soak up every minute, put everything else aside. Go into extreme monk mode. Its brilliant and rewarding beyond almost anything else. 

But recognise when its time to prioritise something else. 

When your time is up what do you want to look back on?  

I think thats the key question we should all be resolving.
