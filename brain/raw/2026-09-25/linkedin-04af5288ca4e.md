GenAI isn't replacing admissions officers. It's drowning them in better data.

Here's where GenAI and enterprise workflows actually intersect: not in replacing decision-makers, but in augmenting the data they work with.

In this engagement, we explored GenAI use cases in student services and academic processes. Most hypotheticals failed the "reality check":

"GenAI could write recommendation letters" → No. Recommendations carry institutional liability.

"GenAI could flag at-risk students" → Maybe. But based on what? Training data bias? Institutional sensitivity around certain demographics?

What did work: Using GenAI to summarize and extract from unstructured data.
Example:
Admissions receives 500 student applications per cycle
Each application has essays, recommendations, transcripts, test scores, and supplemental materials
An admissions officer needs to prioritize which applications to deep-dive on first

Without GenAI: Manual scan and flagging (labor-intensive, prone to fatigue)

With GenAI: Structured summaries of each application (strengths, gaps, flag points) so humans spend review time on decision-making, not information gathering.

We used gen AI to:
- Summarize application narratives
- Flag prerequisite mismatches
- Identify credential discrepancies
- Highlight academic strength areas
-The admissions team's throughput went up. But more importantly, their decision quality improved because they weren't cognitively overloaded by unstructured text.

The delivery insight: GenAI in enterprise workflows should serve human judgment, not replace it.

GenAI delivers ROI in enterprise workflows when it augments decision-makers with structured data, not when it attempts to make decisions.
