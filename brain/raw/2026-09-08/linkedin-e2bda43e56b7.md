Sat in on ISPE Australasia Affiliate's "Life Sciences AI Reality Check" webinar this afternoon. Thank you Charlie Wakeham, Benjamin K. for organising this event.

It is interesting that, the biggest misconception about AI in our industry, according to the panel: that it's a magic wand. Chuck your data in, get the right answer out. It isn't. It still gets things wrong, and it still needs scrutiny every time.

The most interesting technical idea came from a speaker that now works at Google, who shared a conceptual architecture for validating AI systems that don't behave like traditional deterministic software. Three agents, each doing a different job:

- A rule-keeper agent that turns written GxP requirements into machine-readable logic, reviewed and approved by a human before it ever runs.
- An adversarial agent that stress-tests the system with thousands of edge cases and boundary inputs before it goes anywhere near production.
- A sentinel agent that watches live production, read-only, flagging anything that breaks the rules in real time.

The point isn't to remove human judgment. It's to make sure the humans are reviewing the right things, at the right point in the lifecycle, instead of drowning in 40-page manual test scripts every time a cloud platform pushes an update.

Worth thinking about how this applies to how we validate any AI tool we bring into a GxP environment, not just the big ones.
