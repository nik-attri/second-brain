“£1 million a year... pissed against the wall.”

I’ll never forget those words.

I’d just shown the CEO that one issue in the business was costing around £20,000 every week.

Nobody had spotted it.

Not because people weren’t working hard.

Not because the business didn’t have enough data.

They had plenty.

Reports.
Spreadsheets.
Variance analysis.
Commentary.
Pages and pages of numbers.

The issue was that nobody was looking at those numbers in the right way.

The fix itself wasn’t particularly complicated.

Finding it was.

And that’s something I’ve seen repeatedly throughout my career.

A business can produce its management accounts perfectly on time every month and still not be able to answer some very basic questions.

What’s actually making us money?

What’s costing us money?

Where is the cash going?

What needs attention first?

Years ago, a Managing Director put it much more simply.

“I don’t actually want the report, Phil. I just want to know what the hell is going on.”

He was right.

The business didn’t need another report.

It needed clarity.

In 26 years of finance, I’ve never seen a business transformed because somebody produced more pages.

I’ve seen businesses transformed when somebody cut through the noise, challenged what didn’t make sense and focused attention on the handful of numbers that actually mattered.

Because good finance isn’t really about reporting what happened.

It’s about helping people understand what’s happening now and make better decisions about what happens next.

Sometimes the biggest opportunity isn’t finding another £1 million of revenue.

It’s finding the £1 million you’re already earning...

before it disappears down the drain.
