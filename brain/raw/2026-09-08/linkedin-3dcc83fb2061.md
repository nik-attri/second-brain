Maybe accessibility needs more people who don’t come from the accessibility industry. There. I said it.
 
I’ve been called “the UX guy” coming in and disrupting accessibility. They’re right about one thing. I am the UX guy. I’ve spent 22 years designing digital products.

I’ve worked with accessibility, but I’m not going to pretend I know everything about it. I don’t.
There are accessibility practitioners who know WCAG, assistive technology and disability experience far better than I ever will.
 
That’s not the problem I’m trying to solve.
 
The problem I see is an industry that has gotten incredibly good at finding accessibility issues…and organizations that are still incredibly bad at fixing them.
 
Another scan won’t fix that.
Another 300-row audit won’t fix that.
Another AI tool generating remediation suggestions won’t fix that.
Because eventually somebody still has to decide:
 
What actually matters?
Who owns it?
Where does it affect the customer experience?
Did it get fixed?
Was the fix verified?
Did it stay fixed?
 
Accessibility doesn’t have a findings problem.
It has a follow-through problem.
 
And that looks a hell of a lot like a product and operations problem to me.
So I’m not building Limena to out-expert accessibility experts.
 
I’m building it because their expertise deserves better than dying in a spreadsheet.
and if that makes me “the UX guy disrupting accessibility”… good.
