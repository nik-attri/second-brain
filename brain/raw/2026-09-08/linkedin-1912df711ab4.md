I've been talking to a lot of people lately who are confused by how checked out they've become at work.

They're doing things like taking extra breaks even when they don't need them.

Or they've got LinkedIn open on their computer searching for jobs, and when someone walks by, they quickly switch the screen back to the spreadsheet they're supposed to be working on.

Does any of this sound familiar? If so, I can understand why you'd eventually start wondering what the hell is going on.

Especially if you've generally been someone who works hard, solves problems, and can get yourself to do what needs to get done most of the time.

I've also noticed that a lot of people get really self-critical when they start behaving this way. They start having thoughts like:

“Maybe I'm lazy.”

“I'm so ungrateful. I have a good job, I make good money, other people would love to 
have what I have.”

“I'm the problem.”

I don't think that's what's happening though.

There's a good chance these behaviors are signs that something about your work doesn't fit you anymore.

And if that's what's going on, having a good job or making good money doesn't make the mismatch disappear.

Neither does trying to force yourself to power through.

I'm not suggesting you quit your job or anything. Especially if you don't know what's actually driving what you're experiencing yet.

But I also don't think you should ignore it.

Feeling checked out, bored, restless, disengaged. That's information that's worth paying attention to.

So instead of assuming something is wrong with you, or trying to ignore the feeling and hoping it will go away on its own, I think the better place to start is recognizing that the feeling is real.

Then get curious about it and try to understand what it's actually telling you.
