I noticed something walking around Darlington this weekend for the NASCAR Cup Series. 

Almost every single fan was wearing a shirt or hat affiliated with a driver.

But look closer...

They weren't just wearing the driver.

They were wearing the sponsors.

Brand names across their chest. Logos on their hats. Companies they may have never had any connection with if it wasn't for racing.

And I don't think you get that in many other sports.

Of course, the brands are activating at the track.

They have booths. Displays. Giveaways. Product samples. Hospitality.

They're on the race cars.

They're on live TV.

They're in social media content.

They're running commercials.

But then you look around at the crowd and realize...

The FANS are advertising the brands too.

And it doesn't stop when the race ends.

That same fan wears that shirt to the grocery store on Wednesday.

They wear the hat while they're out walking the dog.

They wear it to a party.

A birthday.

To work.

Out to dinner.

And eventually somebody asks about it.

"What's that?"

"Who's that driver?"

"What's that company?"

And now you've started a conversation.

That's the part of motorsports marketing that I think is incredibly difficult to put into a spreadsheet.

We talk so much about views.

Impressions.

Clicks.

Conversions.

Sales.

ROI.

And obviously all of those things matter. I want every partnership we build to be measurable and to produce a return.

But sometimes brands become so focused on what they made from ONE campaign that they miss the bigger picture.

What happens when your brand actually becomes part of the culture?

Because that's what I saw at Darlington.

I didn't see thousands of people walking around wearing advertisements.

I saw thousands of people proudly wearing brands they associate with drivers, memories, races and a sport they love.

That's different.

You're not just putting your company in front of a customer anymore.

You're building relevance.

You're building familiarity.

You're building conversations.

You're building an emotional connection.

And you're building something that can last a hell of a lot longer than the 30 days of an ad campaign.

Of course we should measure ROI.

But there is also something incredibly valuable about building a customer base that genuinely wants your brand to be part of their world.

That's what motorsports does so well.

It doesn't just give brands an audience.

It gives them the opportunity to become part of the culture.
