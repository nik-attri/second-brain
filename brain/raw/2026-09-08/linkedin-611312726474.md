AI - Outage Paralysis 🛑💳
My credits ran out in a single day, and it forced me to confront a terrifying reality about modern software engineering.

The Incident:
Last week, I hit a massive token consumption wall. My AI credits were completely exhausted in just a few hours. After investigating the logs, I realized the Auto selector had misfired and routed every single one of my requests even basic questions , directly to a heavy, expensive flagship model (like Opus 5 & Fable). 

But the glitch wasn't the scary part. The scary part was what happened next.

The Paralysis:
With my Copilot disabled, I stared at my editor. I knew exactly what needed to be coded. I understood the architecture, the logic, and the exact steps to progress my work.

Yet, I felt completely sluggish and helpless. The sheer friction of manually typing out boilerplate code, closing brackets, and writing standard loops felt like I was suddenly asked to chop down a tree with a butter knife.

I have only been using AI coding assistants for about two years. In just 24 months, it has completely rewired my brain's baseline for productivity.

The Realization: Is this concerning?
My initial reaction was panic. If an engineer cannot work effectively without an AI assistant, are we losing our edge?
But then I took a step back.

Imagine how paralyzed we would feel today if someone turned off the Internet, blocked Stack Overflow, or disabled basic IDE IntelliSense. We wouldn't be able to work either.

We are experiencing a generational shift in the baseline of human tooling. Just like Google became an inseparable part of life, AI agents are no longer just "assistants." They are the new foundational infrastructure of engineering thought. They aren't pulling us away from the work; they are elevating the abstraction layer we operate on.

Have you experienced this  AI outage paralysis yet? Are you worried about the dependency, or do you accept it as the new normal? 🤔
