It took me four years to do this.
 
Two Saturdays ago, I spent the night alone in the New Forest. Just me, my bike, my tent, a ridiculous amount of kit, and the resident ponies. Once I convinced myself that every rustle in the woods wasn't an escaped lunatic, it was rather wonderful.
 
I’d been preparing for this single night for four years.
 
I had watched an obscene number of YouTube videos about bikepacking. Researched the hell out of tents, stoves, trowels (don't ask) and even what to do if I encountered a bear… in Hampshire. Two years ago, I had everything I needed.
 
And still, I waited. Sleeping in a tent less than 20 miles from home was hardly an Antarctic expedition.
 
Then a few weeks ago, I had a stern word with myself: "If you don't go now, Jamie, another year will disappear while you watch some bloke on YouTube live your life for you."
 
So I loaded the bike, waved goodbye to Sarah and the dog, and pedalled off. Four years of prepping for one night of doing. A chronic case of being "overprepared and underlived."
 
It made me think about how often I see this in conversations with people in midlife.
 
They’re not miserable, but things don't fit quite the same as they used to. Maybe they want to leave corporate life, work differently, or start something of their own. But first, they need to be "ready." A bit more savings. A clearer plan. One more bonus cycle.
 
Oliver Burkeman makes a good point in Four Thousand Weeks: the decks are never going to be clear.
 
Those of us with corporate backgrounds are trained to analyse risk and eliminate uncertainty. Useful skills, until preparation becomes a very respectable form of procrastination.
 
There comes a point when another spreadsheet or YouTube video isn't making you any more ready. It's just delaying the moment you begin.
 
Somewhat annoyingly, after four years of preparation, all I really needed to do was get on my bike.
 
What are you still preparing for that you might already be ready enough to begin?
