What a hell of a week in archaeology (but wi deh ya).

On Thursday, October 1, while traveling with my four-year-old for a family birthday weekend, I learned Chronicle Heritage was shutting down. By my birthday on October 2, I was out of a job. Field crews, clients, and years of commitments were suddenly left in an impossible position.

The birthday plans involved a few extra phone calls.

With Ray Pasquariello, RPA helping lead the way, our small Caribbean team got to work finding a home together. Between calls and complicated logistics, we kept our field staff informed, stayed close to our clients, and worked to protect what we had built.

By Monday, October 5, I was working alongside the same team at Gray & Pape, Inc. I’m proud to continue leading our Caribbean program as Caribbean Branch Manager, with Ray, Maxwell Lopez, Carla de la Rosa, and a growing locally based field team.

These relationships run deep. Ray and I began talking Caribbean CRM in 2020, while I was teaching at UWI in Jamaica. He has been a mentor, loyal confidant, and reliable source of calm ever since. Max and I have worked together since 2022. Our trusted technical lead, he keeps us on the right path and has saved my ass countless times. Carla came into my life through a project with roots in 2015 and has become the glue holding so much together.

Gray & Pape brings nearly 40 years of experience and a commitment to quality that makes me excited about what comes next. Thank you to Cinder Miller and everyone helping us get established.

I’m grateful for the opportunity Chronicle gave me to build something beyond my own research interests, and for exceptional colleagues across its offices and disciplines. Many are now facing a frightening scramble. Please keep reaching out, making introductions, and hiring these talented people.

This collapse raises serious questions about private equity’s place in our profession. When growth and margins outrank professional judgment, honest communication, and responsibility to people, we put the foundations of this work at risk. Culture, preservation, and identity require more than spreadsheets and templates.

Archaeology asks a lot of us. Working with people and partners you trust makes those long days worthwhile. Our Caribbean work has plenty of life ahead.

If I’ve missed your message this week, my apologies. I’m catching up, grateful for the support, and looking forward to reconnecting.

Same team. New chapter. Plenty of work ahead.

One love.
