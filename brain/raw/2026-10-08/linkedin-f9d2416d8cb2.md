Why Are We Making Users Work So Hard for Something So Simple?

As a UI/UX designer, I’ve been thinking more about how often a simple user goal becomes a complicated product journey.

A user may only want to:
🧩 Book an appointment  
🧩 Buy a product  
🧩 Change an address  
🧩 Download an invoice  

But the product may ask them to create an account, verify an email, repeat information, choose unnecessary preferences, and move through multiple screens before reaching the actual goal.

From the product team’s side, every step may have a reason.

From the user’s side, the feeling is much simpler:
“Why is this taking so long?”

For example, someone who wants to book a haircut probably thinks:
Choose service → Choose time → Book

But the experience may become:
Choose service → Sign up → Verify email → Log in → Find service again → Choose location → Choose stylist → Choose time → Enter details → Confirm → Book

The user doesn’t care about our database structure or internal process.
They care about completing their task.

Another thing I watch for is repeated information.

If the user already entered their address, why ask for it again?
If they already selected their city, why make them choose it again?
If the system already knows something, the user shouldn’t have to do the work twice.

I think of this unnecessary effort as a kind of UX tax.
📌 Re-entering information  
📌 Creating accounts too early  
📌 Making obvious choices manually  
📌 Searching for hidden actions  
📌 Confirming things that are easily reversible  
📌 Going through five screens for a two-step task  

One small UX tax may not make someone leave.
But they add up.
And eventually, the product starts to feel frustrating even when users can’t explain exactly why.

I also try not to measure simplicity only by click count.
A three-click journey can still be confusing.
A five-click journey can feel effortless when every step is clear.

What matters more is how much thinking, remembering, repeating, and unnecessary deciding we ask users to do.

When I review a flow, I like asking one simple question after every step:
Why does the user need to do this?”

If the step protects them or genuinely helps them, it probably belongs.
If the answer is only:
“Because our system needs it,”

then maybe the system should carry more of that work.
📌 Good UX doesn’t just help users complete a task. It removes unnecessary work between them and the outcome they came for.
