Most businesses have plenty of data. But how many are actually making intelligent decisions with it?

For years, CRM systems have helped organisations track leads, customers, sales activities and performance.

That was an important first step. But I believe the next evolution of business management goes much further.

Consider these four questions:

1. What happened?
    We use data to understand past performance.
2. What is likely to happen?
    We use predictive intelligence to anticipate future outcomes.
3. What should we do?
    We use prescriptive intelligence to identify the next best action.
4. Did our action work?
    We measure the impact, learn from the outcome and improve future decisions.

The fourth question is where things become really interesting.

Imagine an education business.

Instead of simply reporting how many enquiries came in, an intelligent system could identify which prospective students are most likely to enrol, recommend the right follow-up action, measure whether that action improved conversion, and learn which interventions work best.

The same thinking can transform admissions, sales, marketing, customer retention and operational performance across industries.

The real opportunity of AI is not just predicting the future. It is helping organisations act on those predictions and learn from the results.

This is the shift I find most exciting:

From managing reports to managing decisions.
From tracking activity to improving outcomes.
From collecting data to building organisational intelligence.

And one important distinction: technology should not replace managerial judgement. It should give leaders better evidence, earlier signals and more effective choices.

My question to business leaders:

If your CRM disappeared tomorrow, would your organisation still know what action to take next—or does your decision-making still depend on manually interpreting reports?
