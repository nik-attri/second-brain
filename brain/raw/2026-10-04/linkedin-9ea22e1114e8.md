If the Indian tech engine is going to win the next decade, we have to stop using AI to just "cut headcount" or bill fewer hours.

That’s a race to the bottom.

Think about where most of the time actually goes in the services industry right now. It’s not core engineering or deep problem solving.

It’s the Coordination Tax.

The status syncs. The spec translations across four management layers. The manual ticket handoffs, the edge-case patching, the context switching across fragmented tools.

It’s endless operational friction dressed up as enterprise delivery.
If you just slap AI on top of that mess to write code a bit faster, you haven't fixed anything. You’ve just automated chaos.

The real shift happens when you use AI to collapse that coordination tax down to absolute zero.

When that happens, the individual in the services industry isn't an executor drowning in tickets anymore. They become a system architect.

Instead of spending weeks porting logic or syncing state, they spend 30 minutes defining hard constraints, domain rules, and system boundaries. AI handles the non-deterministic heavy lifting. The human owns the intent, the taste, and the accountability.

This is how services become genuinely deflationary.

Not by making human labor cheap, but by making enterprise-grade execution so insanely leveraged that world-class architecture becomes accessible to everyone.

India’s next growth curve won't come from selling hours of execution.
It’s going to come from individual engineers operating as 10x orchestrators—cutting out the corporate bloat and delivering pure, high-leverage systems.

Stop automating bad processes. Start stripping out the friction.
