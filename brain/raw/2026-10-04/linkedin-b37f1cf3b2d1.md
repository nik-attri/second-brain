Ransomware gets talked about as a technical problem: an unpatched vulnerability, an attacker moving across the network, files getting encrypted, a backup that may or may not work, an EDR alert someone saw too late.
The people who pay for it see something else. Operations stop. Services run late. Revenue disappears for days or weeks. Regulators start asking questions, and customers wonder whether they should stay.

Two recent stories show where things are going. A suspected China-linked group called Warlock has reportedly been exploiting SharePoint flaws to switch off security tools before deploying ransomware. Its targets include critical infrastructure, government agencies and schools in several regions. Separately, law enforcement action against KillSec showed how a single ransomware operation can work across borders and reach thousands of victims.
Stopping encryption matters, but it isn't the whole job. The question every leadership team should be able to answer is: if this happens to us on Monday, how do we keep running?

Here's where I'd start:
Know which business processes matter most and which systems they depend on.
Keep backups offline and immutable, and actually test restoring them. A backup you've never restored is a guess.
Segment the network so one compromised machine can't reach everything.
Use MFA everywhere and give accounts only the access they need.
Patch internet-facing and high-value systems first, and quickly.
Watch for odd admin activity, changes to security tools and sudden mass file operations. Warlock's playbook of disabling defenses first is exactly what this catches.
Run incident response drills based on realistic ransomware scenarios, not tabletop exercises nobody takes seriously.
Decide ahead of time how the business will run manually if key systems are down.

Tools help. But the organizations that come through an attack in one piece are usually the ones that prepared, stayed disciplined and planned for continuity before they needed it.
Prevention is the goal. Being able to survive an attack is the requirement.
