ENRI from MNB Networks: How Much of Your Network Risk Is Human-Driven?
  
Technology is becoming smarter. Networks are becoming more automated. Yet human intervention remains at the heart of many enterprise network operations. Engineers configure devices. Teams implement changes. Administrators manage policies. Specialists troubleshoot incidents.
  
Human expertise is essential—but every manual interaction can introduce an element of risk.
  
A single incorrect configuration can impact availability. A missed change can create configuration drift. An outdated policy can introduce a security gap. A simple mistake made under pressure can potentially affect thousands of users or critical business applications.
  
The challenge isn't that people make mistakes. It's that modern networks have become so complex that the consequences of small mistakes can be much larger than before.
  
The Enterprise Network Risk Index (ENRI) from MNB Networks encourages organisations to consider the human factor when assessing their overall network risk;
  
 ·       How many network changes are still performed manually?
 ·       How much time do engineers spend on repetitive tasks?
 ·       How consistently are configurations applied?
 ·       How quickly can an error be detected and corrected?
  
At MNB Networks, we help customers identify where manual processes may be increasing operational risk and explore opportunities to introduce greater automation, visibility, and assurance.
  
The objective isn't to remove people from network operations. It's to remove unnecessary risk from the process.
  
By combining human expertise with intelligent automation, organisations can improve consistency, reduce repetitive workloads, and allow engineers to focus on higher-value activities.
  
The question isn't whether your network is managed by talented people. It's whether your processes are designed to help those people succeed.
