The CEOs of two of the world's leading AI companies are now saying the industry needs to slow down.

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has called for deliberately pacing frontier AI development. 

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman publicly agreed, while OpenAI has also ruled out a 2026 IPO as it focuses on safety and alignment work. 

Think about that.

The people building the most capable AI systems are telling us that capability is moving faster than our ability to control it.

Yet inside enterprises, we're still racing to deploy agents, connect tools, grant permissions and automate workflows.

For me, the takeaway isn't "stop using AI."

It's stop deploying autonomy without architecture and without proper governance. 

Before an agent touches production, we need to know:

What can it access?

What actions can it take?

How is it observed?

How are actions verified?

What's the blast radius if it behaves unexpectedly?

And can we stop it?

This is exactly why I keep coming back to architecture-first governance.

The faster AI gets, the more important the controls around it become.

If the frontier labs are applying the brakes, enterprises should at least check whether their brakes work.
