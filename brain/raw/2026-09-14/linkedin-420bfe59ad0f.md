Your month-end close takes 16 days.

But that's not the real problem.

The real problem is this: Your CFO sees the numbers on day 16. Your board makes decisions on day 20. That means 4 days every month where you're managing the business without current information.

4 days of flying blind.

I was with a CFO yesterday and she walked me through her entire month-end process. Every step was manual. Data collection from subsidiaries. Reconciliation between companies. Spreadsheet consolidation. Finance team manually coding invoices. Waiting for operational numbers. Investigating exceptions.

16 days of coordination work that could be happening continuously.

I asked her: Why does it take this long?

She said it's just how things work.

But here's what I found: It's not how things HAVE to work anymore.

Every single step in that process can be redesigned. Some automated. Some integrated. Some eliminated entirely.

The finance function she built 10 years ago was designed for manual data movement. That design is outdated. But she's still operating inside it.

I've seen what happens when finance functions get redesigned: Close cycles cut in half. Real-time visibility instead of historical reports. Teams doing strategy instead of chasing numbers.

If your close is taking 15+ days, you have a design problem. Not a people problem.

I wrote about what I found and what changes when you fix it in this week's CFO Notes.
