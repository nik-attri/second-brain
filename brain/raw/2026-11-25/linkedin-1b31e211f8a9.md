Peter Lynch said to know what you own and why you own it. Easy advice when $NVDA is ripping. A hell of a lot harder on a Friday at $125 when you don’t know if Jensen is coming back.

I bought my first shares in 2016. I’ve spent a decade studying this company, from the data center floor all the way up to the Nemotron models. I held. I added on the dips. But these last two weeks forced me to answer the same question CNBC asked me on air: What happens to NVIDIA without Jensen?

My answer was pretty blunt. He’s the lifeblood of this company. Customers plan multibillion-dollar data centers around the roadmap he lays out. Engineers who’ve spent their careers working for him have to deliver on it. If you think you can swap out the guy who built those relationships over 33 years and leave every assumption in your spreadsheet untouched, good luck with that.

So yes, if Jensen was out for good, the investment story changed for me. That’s why I pushed the Stand With Jensen petition, went on CNBC and wrote directly to NVIDIA’s board members. I wanted him back, and I told them exactly why.

Then I watched him walk back into NVIDIA. His employees were cheering. He thanked them for a second chance and told them he’d be a better CEO the second time around. After 33 years, the guy still thinks he can get better. That’s the Jensen I bet on.

Last week I was on CNBC being asked what NVIDIA looks like without him. Next week I’ll be at GTC DC hearing from Jensen himself about what he’s building next.

I fought for his comeback. Here we are. See you in DC, Jensen.
