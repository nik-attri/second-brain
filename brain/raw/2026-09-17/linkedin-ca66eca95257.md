Early-stage startups aren't for everyone. 

That's the hard truth nobody wants to say out loud.

Everyone's chasing the "early stage" dream. But you actually need to ask yourself: is this what I need right now, in this season of my life? 

Not what sounds cool on LinkedIn - what you actually need.

I've been employee #1. I've been employee #20. I've been employee #100. 

Same word - "startup" - three completely different lives.

At #1, there's no manager. There's no process. There's barely a product half the time. You ARE the manager, the process, and the plan B, often on the same day, before anyone's even hired anyone else.

At #20-something, the chaos doesn't go away - it just changes shape. Now there's a little structure, but you're also building it while still doing your actual job. You're writing the process AND following it, in real time, with no manual.

At #100-something, there's a team, there's a Slack channel for everything, and someone already made the mistake you were about to make - so you get to skip it. Less "figure it out," more "here's the playbook, now make it better."

None of these stages is more "startup" than the others. 

They're just different water, with different currents.

The people who burn out aren't the ones who picked the wrong stage. 

They're the ones who never asked which water they were actually built to swim in - until they were already drowning in it.
