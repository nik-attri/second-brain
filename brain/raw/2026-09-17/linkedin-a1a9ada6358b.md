Apart from the mistake of wearing new shoes and then spending most of the day standing at Accountex (definite noob mistake😅), the show didn’t disappoint.

It was good to catch up with familiar faces, discover some new brands and, probably most importantly, listen to some genuinely useful talks.

One that really stood out for me was “AML Essentials: The Fundamentals That Matter”, presented by Aranjeet Gill from ACCA, Jane Mellor from The Association of Taxation Technicians and David Potts from Association of International Accountants (AIA).

Judging by the fact it was standing room only, I wasn’t the only one who found it useful.
A few of the points really stuck with me.

There are still accounting firms that don’t have a clear firm-wide risk assessment policy in place.

And even where Client Risk Assessments are being completed, some of the weaknesses being seen include:

⚠️Risk scores that aren’t properly justified
⚠️Assessments becoming a tick-box exercise
⚠️Important risk factors being missed
⚠️Adverse media, PEP and sanctions information being overlooked
⚠️Weak or incomplete audit trails
⚠️Little evidence of effective ongoing monitoring

What struck me is that none of those problems are really about simply “doing an AML check”.

They’re about having a consistent, repeatable and explainable risk assessment process.
You need to be able to show what factors were considered, why a particular risk level was reached, what evidence supported it and what happens when something changes later.

That was particularly relevant for us because it is exactly the problem we have been working on with Braid, which we launched at Accountex.

The idea behind Braid is quite simple.

Give firms a way of translating their firm-wide risk assessment policy into a structured Client Risk Assessment process, and then use AI to do much of the heavy lifting.

But rather than simply asking AI to “assess this client”, the process is controlled by defined risk factors, rules and guardrails so the resulting assessment is structured, explainable and repeatable.

It means firms can complete assessments in seconds rather than manually piecing together information, while still retaining the reasoning, evidence and audit trail behind the outcome.
And importantly, the assessment shouldn’t just disappear into a folder once onboarding is complete.

⚠️Risk changes.
⚠️Clients change.
⚠️Directors change.
⚠️Countries, activities and ownership structures change.

So ongoing monitoring has to be part of the process too.

It was reassuring to sit in a room listening to AML specialists talk about the practical weaknesses firms are seeing and realise that they were describing almost exactly the problems we had set out to solve.

Sometimes the best validation of a product idea isn’t someone telling you they like your software.

It’s hearing the industry describe the problem without mentioning your product at all.
