AI has an attention mechanism. You have an attention budget.

Donald Thompson's version: "Attention is all I have."

At AI Infra Summit, Raymond Lee spoke with Donald Thompson, Distinguished Engineer at Microsoft, about his long-range work on the future of work.

The goal isn't to do yesterday's busywork faster. It's to make room for work we couldn't do before.

Three ideas worth taking back to your team:

• Use AI as a thought partner, not just a task-taker. Ask it to challenge assumptions, research alternatives and help frame the problem before you prescribe the steps.

• Think beyond one-off agents. Don explores digital teammates with ongoing responsibilities, memory and organizational context. That's a direction he's investigating, not a claim that autonomous companies are already solved.

• Automate the drudgery, not the meaning. Employees who helped build the business bring experience worth applying to its next problems, not just costs to remove.

Don describes giving an agent responsibility for spotting where his attention goes and helping him stay focused on higher-value work.

Finally, an AI workflow that doesn't begin with "please attend another meeting."

For founders and leaders: what could your team now attempt that was previously impossible, rather than merely cheaper?

Discussion: Which part of your job would you gladly delegate to AI, and which part would you actively protect because you enjoy it or bring unique judgment?

Inviting perspectives from Microsoft's Satya Nadella, Kevin Scott, Brad Smith, Judson Althoff and Amy Hood, plus cofounder Bill Gates. A nod, too, to the legacy of cofounder Paul Allen.
