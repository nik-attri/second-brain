🎧 Day 12 of the Podcast-a-Day Challenge 

Today’s episode comes from A History of Marketing, featuring Tod Johnson, a market research pioneer who spent more than 50 years in the industry and was one of the first people to measure the internet.

So, we have a man who used to capture market research MANUALLY. 
Consumers were given 20-page purchase diaries and a pencil. They filled in what they bought, mailed the booklets back every month, and the data was coded and analysed. This was how marketers were measuring consumer behaviour before dashboards, GA4, cookies, pixels and the other measuring sticks we now have at our disposal.

Fast forward to 1994, Tod’s team had 300 people download software onto their PCs to measure usage. Three of them had some very strange data, which took a couple of months to figure out. It was their internet usage. 

That was the moment and measurement when Tod figured that the internet was going to grow. By January 1996, they were publishing their first internet ratings data.

These days we've got access to GA4, CRM data, social analytics, search data, pixels, attribution models and now AI, all giving us increasingly sophisticated ways of watching what people do (thank heavens!).

One thing that hasn't changed in all of this: We've never stopped asking the consumer; "What are you buying, and why are you buying it?"
