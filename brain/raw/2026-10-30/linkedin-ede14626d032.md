A sales rep used to spend his mornings sorting leads.
Now he spends them reviewing.

That's not a hypothetical. Anthropic's Japan team published exactly how one of their business development reps runs his day.

Here's what changed, and what he still won't hand off:

He's a sales rep, not a developer.
So he didn't build "an AI."
He wrote job descriptions.

One agent, one job:

1️⃣ The Sorter
Best for: teams drowning in inbound
How it works: Read the queue → route each lead to the right rep → post a report
Example: Every lead has an owner before anyone starts their day

2️⃣ The Qualifier
Best for: reps who triage by hand
How it works: Check against set criteria → take the next step → draft the first email
Example: Self-serve leads get pointed to self-serve. Real ones get a custom first touch.

3️⃣ The Replier
Best for: busy inboxes (in two languages, in his case)
How it works: Scan hourly → draft the reply → he edits and sends
Example: Every draft opens with a short summary, so he doesn't reread the thread

4️⃣ The CRM Keeper
Best for: pipelines that are always a day behind
How it works: Read recent conversations → update the deal records
Example: No more typing notes into the CRM after calls

5️⃣ The Loop Closer
Best for: meetings that evaporate
How it works: End of day → read transcripts → summarize → draft follow-ups
Example: Nothing "I meant to follow up on" survives the night

The part most people skip:
Pricing questions, sensitive accounts and ambiguous asks still route to him.
And every agent learns from the edits he makes to its drafts.

By his own estimate, the setup saves him around 60 hours of manual work.

The lesson for every sales team:
Don't give AI your whole job.
Give it five small ones. Keep the judgment.

Which of the 5 would you hand off first?
