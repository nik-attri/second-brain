Most B2B deals don't close in a week. One of ours took eight months.

Here's how we used LinkedIn — both organic and paid — to stay relevant the entire time without a single cold follow-up call.

The client was a mid-sized company evaluating a long-term digital partner. Their buying committee had four people involved at different stages. None of them were ready to talk in month one.

**What organic did:**
We published consistently on LinkedIn — technical posts about specific campaign structures, honest breakdowns of what doesn't work in paid media, and behind-the-scenes observations from client projects. Nothing promotional. Just content that demonstrated how we think.

Two decision-makers from that company started engaging with posts over the first couple of months. No DMs, no sales pitch. They were quietly evaluating us.

**What paid did:**
Once we identified the company as a warm prospect, we ran a targeted LinkedIn campaign aimed at their sector and job titles — specifically content that addressed the exact problems their industry faces. Think: "Why your lead quality drops when you scale paid search" rather than "We offer digital marketing services."

Paid kept us visible to the people who weren't on our follower list yet. Organic gave credibility to anyone who clicked through to our profile.

**The handoff:**
By month five, one of their team members reached out — referencing a post they'd seen organically three months earlier. The conversation moved fast from there because the trust was already built.

The deal closed in month eight. We'd had two formal calls total.

Organic builds the credibility. Paid maintains the visibility. Together they do the work a sales team would otherwise have to do manually — and they do it at scale.
