What does a 0.5-meter conveyor actually do on an SMT line?

Everyone talks about the pick-and-place machine, the reflow oven, and the printer. But the unsung part of every running SMT line is the humble PCB conveyor.

Here is what it handles and why it matters:

1.It bridges the gap between machines
A printer outputs a bare PCB. The mounter needs to receive it at the right height and edge. The conveyor sits in between and transfers the board — without it, operators manually load boards, which is slow and causes misalignment.
2.It becomes an inspection or buffer station
A short 0.5-meter conveyor doubles as a manual inspection point. You can check solder paste after printing, or verify placement before the board goes into reflow. It also acts as a small buffer when the next machine is temporarily busy.
3.SMEMA compatibility is non-negotiable
Every conveyor on a real SMT line must speak SMEMA. The downstream machine signals "ready" and the conveyor releases the board. If your conveyor is not SMEMA-compatible, it becomes a manual bottleneck in an otherwise automated line.
4.Adjustable rail width and ESD-safe surface
PCBs come in different sizes. The rail width adjusts to match the board. The belt surface is ESD-safe — that green coating protects sensitive components from static discharge.
5.Height must match the line exactly
Standard SMT line height is 900mm ± 20mm. The conveyor has leveling feet to align precisely with your printer, mounter, and oven. A mismatch of even a few millimeters causes board jams and downtime.
