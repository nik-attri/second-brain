I missed the budget announcement on Wednesday, and I had a hectic Thursday - so I was playing catch up last night solely with my AI and Data recruitment goggles on and how it effects our clients, their workforce and our candidates...

My first reaction: there is probably very little overnight change, specifically around AI. But there's some very interesting stuff in there.

There are of course some meaningful commitments i.e. additional investment in UK AI companies, more funding for AI procurement, cybersecurity support and local adoption partnerships.

For AI start-ups or start-ups building solutions in those areas, that could create opportunities to secure investment or win customers. For the average business 'using AI', the immediate benefit is less obvious.

Given what I do... what interests me more is what happens between buying the AI technology and getting value from it...  i.e. someone (as in a physical person for the avoidance of doubt...) needs to understand the workflow, connect the systems, check the outputs and bring the team on the journey of transformation & change i.e. AI adoption - and I suspect teams would be needed for this!

That makes me think the most popular hiring needs will move beyond Forward Deploy & AI engineers... but to PEOPLE who combine industry knowledge with practical AI skills could - I think this could become increasingly more valuable across operations, finance, sales and customer service.

A big thinking point for me, as a business leader and a father, is it raises a question about junior talent. If we automate the tasks people traditionally learn through, how do we help them develop the judgement they’ll need later? Or how do they get their foot in the door if junior / "automatable" tasks, are all being eaten by AI & Automation? I'm sure there's some logical answers out there...

I’d be cautious to believe the budget is driving businesses to adopt AI to reduce headcount (I've seen quite a bit of rhetoric on this). Personally, I’d use it as a prompt to review which skills the business needs next. Where can you get maximum value and ROI?

I think the AI funding element is worth watching. But the questions I’d encourage businesses to ask are:
“Where can AI improve our business?" 
"Do our people have the skills, support and ownership to make it work?”

And then pragmatic questions like:
"If AI gave everyone in your business five hours back each week, would they know what you wanted them to do with it?”

If the answers are unclear, that’s a broader leadership decision to make before expecting a return on the technology and specifically AI!

That's my two-cents anyway
