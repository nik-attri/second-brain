When we analysed the results of our Eight Advisory PMI study, in which we interviewed finance leaders and M&A executives, one finding stood out. The phase of an acquisition that demands the most manual effort gets the least technology support. And the people doing the work come last on the agenda. That is a paradox, and it says a lot about how we think about integration.

Here are the results:

 1️⃣  The adoption rate of AI for PMI purposes is below 20%, whereas more than 75% utilize AI in a pre-deal phase (doing target research, scanning / summarizing data rooms etc.).
 2️⃣ The workload explodes post-deal. The first 100 days are about aligning reporting, budgeting and forecasting, getting cash and working capital under control, and harmonising accounting policies. Almost all of this work is still done manually, with little or no AI support.
 3️⃣ But the people who do it rank last. Talent retention comes 5th out of 5 on the priorities of the finance agenda according to finance executives, even though losing key finance people is among the biggest risks after closing.
 4️⃣ The clock keeps ticking. 81% of finance executives plan for full finance integration to take between 100 and 365 days.
 
If we now combine these findings, the picture that emerges is not a pleasant one:
 🔷 The phase with the most manual work gets the least tooling.
 🔷 The phase that depends most on knowledge gets the least attention for the people who hold it.
 🔷 The phase that takes up to a year is poorly staffed.

And this is the phase where deals are won or lost. We spend weeks making sure we buy the right asset. Then we leave the integration to whoever is still there.
  
How much of your integration plan is about systems, and how much about the people who run them?
