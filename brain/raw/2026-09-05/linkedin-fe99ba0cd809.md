This week, I had the pleasure of visiting France’s Champagne region—and I left with an even greater appreciation for the craftsmanship behind every bottle.

I learned how much manual work and patience the process requires: tending the vines, harvesting and carefully selecting grapes by hand, pressing and fermentation, aging for at least 24 months, and manually turning thousands of bottles so the yeast sediment gradually moves toward the neck.

During the tour, I asked the owner of a boutique, family-run Champagne house: “What about AI and automation?”

He laughed and replied, “How could a machine possibly do work rooted in traditions passed down for generations?”

His response stayed with me.

With all the excitement surrounding AI, perhaps some things should never be fully automated. Technology can improve efficiency, but craftsmanship, tradition, human judgment, and pride in one’s work carry a value that cannot always be replicated by a machine.

What do you think? Which traditional crafts and trades should we protect and preserve for future generations?
