Week six of due diligence. The competing bidder already had a signed LOI.

Same deal. Same deadline. Completely different position.

This wasn't a story I heard secondhand. It was the actual state of a real client's legal team, a major European renewable energy group, mid-way through what should have been a routine acquisition.

Six weeks in, they were still assembling the picture. Contracts in one format, permits in another, land registry records that needed manual cross-referencing against filings that were sometimes years old. Every document that came back raised two more questions.

The other side of the table wasn't smarter. They just weren't drowning in the same paperwork. While this team was still reconciling version three of a permit against version one of a contract, the competing bidder had already moved to exclusivity.

Nobody on that legal team was bad at their job. They were doing the mechanical work of thirty people with the headcount of eight, and the actual legal thinking kept getting pushed to whatever hour was left over.

That's the moment that stuck with me building this. Speed in due diligence was never really about working faster. It was about not spending six weeks doing work that was never legal work to begin with.

Have you ever watched a deal get lost, not to a better offer, but to whoever finished reading first?
