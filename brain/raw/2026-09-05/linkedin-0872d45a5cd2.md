We placed a candidate in October.
Operations role. Solid hire. Clean process.
Six weeks later - she called us.
Not to say thank you. To ask for help.

"I think I made a mistake joining. The role isn't what I was told."
We asked her to walk us through it.

In the interview - the manager had described a role with ownership, visibility, cross-functional work.
Week one - she was doing data entry. Week two - she was fixing someone else's backlog. Week three - she raised it with her manager.
The manager said: "Everyone starts here. You'll get to the interesting work eventually."

No timeline. No definition of "eventually." Just - wait.
She felt misled. She probably was.
But here's the harder truth we had to share with her:
There were signals during the process she didn't read.
The interview was entirely about her. Nobody described what the first 90 days actually looked like. She didn't ask.

The role had been open for 4 months. She didn't ask why.
The team she'd be joining had three new joiners in 6 months. She didn't ask about tenure.

The offer came fast — faster than most processes move. She didn't ask what the urgency was.

Every one of those signals was available to her before she signed.
She was so focused on getting the offer — she forgot to evaluate whether she actually wanted it.

This is one of the most common and most painful candidate mistakes we see.
The job search becomes so consuming - so focused on clearing each stage - that the actual decision gets made without enough information.

Here's what evaluating an offer actually looks like:

Ask to speak with someone on the team - not the hiring manager - before you accept.

Ask specifically: "What does the first 30 days look like? What will I be working on in week one?"

Ask why the role is open. Ask how long the previous person was in it. Ask what made people successful here - and what made people leave.
These questions don't make you difficult. They make you serious.
Companies that won't answer them have answered them.

Here's the tip we give every candidate on EarlyJobs:

The offer is not the finish line. It's the beginning of a decision. Give yourself 48 hours before accepting anything. Use that time to ask the questions you were too nervous to ask during the process. The company already wants you - that leverage disappears the moment you sign. Use it to get clarity first.
Getting the offer is not the goal.
Getting the right offer is.

Most candidates confuse the two - until they're sitting in a role that doesn't match the interview and wondering how they got there.

Are you evaluating companies as seriously as they're evaluating you?
