Canada should take a lesson from Brazil here. They preserved Brazil's control over critical minerals by rejecting a purely financial move by Vale, the massive mining conglomerate. Vale split the company in two with the goal of selling the critical minerals division to the public markets through an IPO. 

The move offered no benefit to anyone - the assets would remain the same, developed in the same way. It was a purely financial idea, no doubt developed on some banker's spreadsheet. It's the kind of idea that's become so common, where assets get moved around for no real purpose other than to play a financial game. By stopping the IPO, Brazil loses absolutely nothing, but keeps control over key resources in the country. From the reporting in The Globe and Mail:

"Brazil’s concerns about the potential hollowing out of its resource economy is occurring at a time when many countries around the world are getting more aggressive about protecting their domestic supply chains from supply shocks in critical minerals."

As the world's biggest investors come to Canada in a couple weeks, these are the things to watch out for. Are we doing deals that build things? That bring in money that was otherwise too expensive or unavailable? Or are we doing deals that are financial, creating no value, but in the process losing control of our resources and assets? For example, the way we let Vale buy Inco, a proud Canadian company, 20 years ago, in a way that provided no benefit to the Canadian economy? 

We need to be on guard, as the song says, because the people coming here have one hell of a lot of spreadsheets, and they know how to use them.
