I've known Rajiv Ramanan for about 10 years now. I had seen him juggle multiple responsibilities at #Freshworks but when I met him after he became a Co-founder and CRO at Spendflo it felt I like I met someone totally different (in a good way  😃). He was Smarter. Sharper...and... Fitter than I had ever seen him before :) 

I finally had a chance to interview him for our inaugural edition of JazzHQ #FirstTake - where in about 50mins he condensed years of experience building, selling and scaling into some incredibly practical lessons for first time founders. Excerpts from the interview below. Link to full video in comments  😊 

1. How do you know if your startup idea is actually validated?
Rajiv: Validation is when money changes hands.
It’s not when someone says, “Great idea.”
It’s not a survey response.
Talk to 20–30 potential customers. Look for patterns. And ideally, close a couple of paid pilots.
If people aren’t willing to pay, you haven’t validated the problem yet.

2. How do you get your first 10 customers when you have no brand, references or finished product?
Rajiv: Your network is way more powerful than you think.
Your first customers will probably come from friends, former colleagues and more importantly, their networks.
Use your first-degree connections to reach the right people in your second and third degree.
And as a founder, you have to become a little shameless.
The first rule of sales is simple:
Show up.
If you don’t show up, you have zero chance of winning. If you show up, at least you have a chance.

3. You finally get 30 minutes with a CFO. How do you use it?
Rajiv: First 20 minutes, listen.
Understand their current state, where they want to get to, what is stopping them and who else is involved.
Next 5 minutes, explain how you can take them from their current state to their desired state.
Last 5 minutes, agree on the next step.
Demo. Pilot. POC. Another meeting.
Never leave without a clearly defined next step.

4. What if your product isn't fully ready when customers want to buy?
Rajiv: The customer doesn't care about your product.
The customer cares about the output.
When we started Spendflo, our “product” was essentially a dashboard sitting on top of an Excel sheet. A lot of the work happened manually behind the scenes.
But we delivered the outcome we had promised.
Don't lie about capabilities you don't have. But don't confuse a polished product with delivering customer value either.
Trust is what you're really selling.

5. Why should founders do sales themselves instead of hiring a VP Sales early?
Rajiv: Founder-led sales isn't really a sales process.
It's a validation process.
You are figuring out:
What problem are we solving?
Who has it?
What triggers them to buy?
How much will they pay?
What language resonates?
Can we repeat it?
