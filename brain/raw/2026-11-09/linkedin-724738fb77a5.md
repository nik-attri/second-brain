Sunday evening is that magical time when the weekend packs its bags, steals your happiness, and leaves Monday standing on your porch like a debt collector with a Teams invitation. 

The air smells like reheated coffee, unfinished spreadsheets, and somebody saying, “Just a quick call.” You spend Saturday believing you’re a free human being. By Sunday night you’re negotiating with the clock like a hostage trying to buy one more hour of daylight. “If I go to bed now, I’ll get eight hours.” Then you open TikTok, watch a man build a cabin out of mud, and wake up emotionally invested in a raccoon stealing grapes. Congratulations. You’ve traded sleep for wildlife content and three new anxieties.

The Sunday Scaries are ridiculous. Nobody has emailed you yet, but somehow you can already hear Karen typing “Gentle reminder” with the force of a courtroom subpoena. Your inbox is stretching before the big game. Somewhere a manager is scheduling a 7:30 a.m. meeting titled “Monday Motivation.” Sir, if motivation requires an alarm before sunrise, that’s not leadership. That’s a misdemeanor with a slideshow.

Tomorrow we’ll all log in pretending we’ve been reborn. “Happy Monday!” chirps Brad, who apparently spent the weekend charging himself in a docking station. Brad has posted “Rise and grind” next to a photo of his protein shake. Brad, respectfully, shut the hell up. Some of us are still grieving Sunday and looking for pants. Even the coffee wants a day off.

Then the meeting starts. Someone asks, “Any wins from last week?” I successfully avoided replying to an email that began “As discussed.” That’s a win. Another person says, “Let’s hit the ground running.” Why? Did the building catch fire? We have chairs. Let’s hit the ground sitting and see how the quarter develops.

And to my job hunting friends, Sunday has its own special horror soundtrack. While everyone else dreads Monday’s meetings, you’re staring at seventeen application portals wondering whether you really need to upload your resume AND manually type every job you’ve had since the invention of electricity. Then a robot rejects you at 2:13 a.m. Even the rejection department has better work life balance than you.

But underneath the jokes, I know Sunday can feel heavy. Maybe you’re exhausted by work. Maybe you’re looking for work. Maybe you’re trying to hold your family, finances, and confidence together with leftover pizza and a prayer. You’re allowed to be tired. You don’t have to conquer the universe before breakfast to prove you’re doing enough.

So tonight, eat the snack. Watch the episode. Put the phone down before your thumb applies for a job in another state. Take a breath, laugh at the chaos, and remember that one rough Monday doesn’t get to write your whole story.

And if someone sends “New week, new opportunities!” at 5:01 a.m., I hope their motivational quote gets stuck in the printer. Sleep well, legends. Corporate court reconvenes tomorrow, and my inner raccoon has pleaded not guilty.
