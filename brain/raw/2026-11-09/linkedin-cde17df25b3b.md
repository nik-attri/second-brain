Congratulations. Your law firm has a new practice area.

The partners have agreed.

The submission is being prepared.

The evidence, unfortunately, has yet to be located.

Somewhere in Business Development, a spreadsheet is opened. Matter descriptions are scattered across old pitches, inboxes and documents nobody remembers writing.

One partner has forwarded a 94-page agreement with the reassuring message: “everything is there.”

Wonderful.

But before deciding whether a firm should compete for recognition in a new practice area, there are a few questions worth answering.

What work does the firm actually do? Does it fit the directory's definition? Is the evidence sufficiently strong? And how does the practice compare with the competition?

Then comes the question that probably should have been asked first:
Why are we pursuing this ranking at all?

Strategic growth? Market positioning? Recognition of an established practice?
Or simply because the competitors are already there?

In the latest edition of Laurel & Hell, Congratulations on Your New Practice Area, we explore what happens when ranking ambitions move considerably faster than the information needed to support them.

Because wanting a ranking is not the same as having a rankings strategy.
