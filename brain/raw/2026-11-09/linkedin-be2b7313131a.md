I swear I'm doing about 40 hours of work in every 8-hour day at the moment. 
⠀
I run operations at Editorialist , and Claude is open from my first coffee to my last Slack message. It does transcripts, research, data analysis, and a frankly embarrassing number of little scripts. Some of what I do is operationally sensitive, so a few examples below are fictionalized. The shape and the stakes are real.
⠀
A normal couple of weeks:
⠀
• Every meeting gets transcribed. Claude writes the notes, files them with the right person, and puts only my action items on my to-do list. Your action items are your problem, and mine are mine.
⠀
• Before I've finished breakfast, it has built prep docs for every 1x1 on my calendar.
⠀
• A daily "war room" script reads the team's Slack updates, our support desk and our checkout dashboard, fills in a spreadsheet row and yells at me about whatever's spiking.
⠀
• I dragged two sprawling Google Sheets, nearly 4,000 sourcing requests going back to 2024, into a proper Airtable base. In a few afternoons.
⠀⠀
• A six-figure card fraud. Claude pulled thousands of transactions out of a payment dashboard, and helped create the dispute documentation. 
⠀
Each of those used to be a day or a week of grinding. Now they're a morning.
⠀
Now the other side.
⠀
A few weeks ago I asked Claude how many payment disputes our team had let lapse. It came back fast: about half, with a dollar figure attached. The export had a "submission count" column, and half the rows said zero. Zero submissions, nobody responded. I'd have read it exactly the same way.
⠀
So I posted the number to the team, and I wasn't particularly polite about it.
⠀
My Special Ops lead replied, politely, that they had responded to every single one. She was right. A count of zero also means you accepted the dispute on purpose. Every "missed" dispute had been accepted deliberately, before the deadline.
⠀
I was wrong, in public, about my own team, at machine speed. That one stung like hell - It was unfair on them, and embarrassing for me. It would ahve been easy to blame Claude - but honestly, I could very easily have screwed that up myself, by hand. Claude just did it faster and with great confidence. I hit send before the friction of doing it by hand could save me from myself. 

The corollary: When you do 40 hours of work in 8 and you also get to make 40 hours of mistakes in 8.
⠀
Honestly? I'd still make that trade every damn day.
⠀
I did the math: Forty hours of output, minus a few hours of cleaning up after myself and one mortifying apology in Slack, still leaves me roughly 30 hours ahead. The speed is the point. The mistakes are the tax, and the tax is relatively cheap.
