When a business system isn’t doing what we need it to do, it can be tempting to start looking for a replacement. Sometimes that will be the right answer. Quite often, though, the business already owns technology capable of doing much more.

The real problem may be that:
- The original setup was never completed.
- Different people use the system in different ways.
- It doesn’t connect properly with other systems.
- Useful features remain switched off.
- Workarounds have gradually become part of the normal process.

Adding something new can then leave the business with one more subscription, one more place to check, one more system to try and integrate and another set of information to maintain.

Before buying anything, it is worth taking a closer look at what is already there. Ask these questions -

- What is the existing system supposed to do?
- Is it configured around the current process?
- Does everybody use it in the same way?
- Where is information being copied or entered again?

You may find that the answer is better configuration, clearer processes or stronger connections between the systems you already have.
