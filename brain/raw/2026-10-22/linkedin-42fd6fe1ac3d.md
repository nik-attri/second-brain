If you're still managing your kitchen operations entirely on Excel, WhatsApp, and guesswork, you're losing valuable time every single day.

As a Sous Chef or Executive Chef, our job isn't just to cook good food. It's to manage people, control costs, maintain consistency, reduce wastage, and ensure smooth operations.

And AI can help us do all of this smarter. 👨‍🍳🤖

Here's how we can use AI in everyday kitchen operations:

🔹 1. Food Cost & COGS Control:

Use AI to analyze recipe costing, raw material prices, yield percentages, and food cost variations. Identify where costs are increasing before they affect profitability.

🔹 2. Production Planning:

Analyze historical sales, outlet requirements, and consumption patterns to plan daily production more accurately and reduce overproduction.

🔹 3. Inventory & Wastage Management:

Track stock movement, identify slow-moving ingredients, monitor expiry dates, and find opportunities to minimize food waste.

🔹 4. Recipe Standardization:

Convert recipes into batch-wise or 1 kg ingredient quantities, calculate yields, standardize portions, and maintain consistency across multiple outlets.

🔹 5. Manpower Planning:

Use production volumes, workload, and dispatch schedules to identify staffing gaps and distribute responsibilities more effectively.

🔹 6. SOPs & Staff Training:

Create clear SOPs, hygiene checklists, food safety training materials, and practical assessments for kitchen teams.

🔹 7. Data Analysis & Reporting:

Turn daily production reports, purchase data, and wastage records into meaningful insights that help chefs make better decisions.

🔹 8. Smarter Purchase Order (PO) Raising:

Why spend hours manually calculating purchase requirements?

AI can help convert production plans and standardized recipes into raw material requirements, compare them against available stock, account for pending orders, and calculate the quantities that need to be purchased.

It can also organize item names, quantities, and units of measurement into PO-ready spreadsheets, helping reduce manual calculations, avoid duplicate ordering, and save valuable time.

Of course, physical stock, supplier details, pack sizes, and final quantities must be verified before raising the PO in the procurement system.


But here's the most important point: AI cannot replace a chef's experience, food safety judgment, leadership, or understanding of the kitchen floor.

AI can analyze the data, suggest improvements and save hours of repetitive work.But the chef must validate the results and make the final decision.

The future of kitchen management isn't about replacing chefs with AI. It's about empowering chefs to become better leaders, smarter planners, and more effective decision-makers.

My question to fellow chefs: 👇

If you could automate just ONE part of your kitchen operations using AI, what would it be?
