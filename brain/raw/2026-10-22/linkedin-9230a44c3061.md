you can have 5 or 50 tools. if they don't share context, your agents won't work.

no context, no AI adoption - it's that simple.

imagine you (the human) get a task and five people tell you how to solve it in different ways. the answer is in the CRM. no, it's in billing. maybe it's in a spreadsheet...

could you solve it? hell no. and of course your agent can't either.

that's what happens when you ask an agent "how many customers did we lose last month?". billing marks a customer as churned the day a card fails. the CRM still shows the customer as active. the dashboard has no clue.

so you can't really separate AI adoption from context quality.

it's about how a company defines its own metrics and how the context is shared. if a metric has two definitions, the agent gives you two answers, or picks one without telling you there was a choice.

so if you want real AI productivity and ROI, you need to know what you measure, how you measure it, why you measure it and the results you expect from each part of AI work.

and you can't do that if your own processes and definitions are poor. this is why only a handful of the companies that try to implement AI can actually prove ROI.
