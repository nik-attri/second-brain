Think about it.

Your company has invested in software. Teams have dashboards. Reports are automated. 

Information is supposed to be available at the click of a button.

Yet, when someone needs to make an important decision, the message still comes:

“Can you send me the latest Excel file? I’m not sure the dashboard is updated.”

Sound familiar?

The interesting part is that the spreadsheet may not be the real problem.

Maybe the same information is being entered into three different systems.

Maybe finance and operations are looking at different numbers.

Maybe a process that was supposed to be automated still depends on someone manually checking, updating, and forwarding information.

Over time, these workarounds become part of the way a business operates.

People stop questioning them. New employees learn them. And the company keeps investing in technology while some of its everyday problems remain unchanged.

That’s the part worth paying attention to.

When people repeatedly work around a system, it may be a sign that something deeper needs attention:

→ Systems that don't share information properly.

→ Processes that don't reflect how teams actually work.

→ Data that people don't fully trust.

→ Automation that handles individual tasks but misses the bigger workflow.

The solution isn't necessarily another software subscription.

Sometimes, the first step is to understand where information gets stuck, why the workaround exists, and what needs to change behind the scenes.

At Novo Slack, we believe that’s an important part of making technology genuinely useful to a business.

Because successful digital transformation isn't just about implementing systems. It's about helping those systems work together in a way people can rely on.

Let me ask you something:

When a company already has dedicated software but still depends on spreadsheets to get things done, what do you think is usually the underlying issue - disconnected systems, poorly designed processes, or a lack of trust in the data?

I'd be interested to hear your perspective.
