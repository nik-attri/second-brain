Day 1 at #HLTH26 in #LasVegas:

I landed expecting the usual conference noise. What I got was a pattern that repeated in room after room.

Different organizations sizes. Different budgets. Same bottleneck: #access.

Here's what I heard across the day:
🔹 Patient access is still too manual. Benefits verification, prior authorization, and status follow-ups are still handled by people chasing portals, phone calls, and faxes. Everyone agrees it's broken, but few have a clear path to fix it.
🔹 Everyone is building with AI, and that's a good sign. One large pharma team told me they're already developing in-house capabilities. I respect that. It confirms the problem is urgent, and the real questions are speed, scale, and the cost of building alone.
🔹 Dependence on IT is slowing teams down. Several teams told me their best ideas wait in a queue behind internal IT priorities. Solutions that plug in without a heavy lift are what they're looking for.
🔹 Budget doesn't define the need. Community-driven organizations and large enterprises are asking for the same thing: fewer delays and less friction for the patient.
🔹 Technology services and platforms are converging. One conversation moved from a patient access platform to custom Salesforce-style builds for internal teams. Buyers want partners who understand both the product and the engineering.

The knowledge tracks that stayed with me:

📍 AI moving from pilots to production: the conversation has shifted from "can it work?" to "can it work safely, at scale, inside our workflows?"
📍 Affordability and access: the sessions kept returning to the gap between therapy approval and therapy start.
📍 Agentic AI in healthcare operations: automating the repetitive work so humans focus on the patients who need judgment and empathy.

This is exactly why we built AccessOS at NeutrinoTech: an AI-driven patient access platform that takes on the heavy lifting of benefits verification and prior authorization, so patients start therapy sooner and teams stop drowning in follow-ups.

After roughly 8 years in the patient access and market access ecosystem, my takeaway from Day 1 is simple: 
“The industry doesn't lack ideas. It lacks time. Every day a patient waits is a day too long”.

I'm looking forward to it. On to Day 2.
