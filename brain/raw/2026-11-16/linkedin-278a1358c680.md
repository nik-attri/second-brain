There has been a lot of discussion about whether AI will replace Customer Success. What I see emerging is CS becoming more of a commercial operator, with AI changing how the work gets done.

Bain’s recent article, “The Rebirth of Customer Success”, highlights declining median net revenue retention among public software companies. In that environment, how well we keep the promises that made customers buy becomes even more important.

For me, CS teams are promise keepers. Reflecting on Bain & Company's article and conversations at a recent Planhat  event, three shifts stand out:

1. Commercial ownership means connecting expertise.

At GlobalData, account managers, analysts and CS all contribute to the post-sale customer journey. Their combined value comes from understanding what the customer needs and bringing the right expertise into the conversation. CS increasingly needs the commercial judgement to connect the original promise with delivery and identify where specialist intervention can make a difference.

2. The customer journey is becoming more responsive.

As distribution shifts towards MCP and intelligence reaches customers directly within their own AI tools and workflows, standard touchpoints are gradually giving way to on-demand, high-value conversations.

That changes how we engage and demonstrate value. We need to understand the decisions our intelligence supports, recognise when customers need help and make those interactions count.

3. AI agents need to see beyond our org charts.

Agents are helping automate administrative work, but many remain organised around individual functions: sales, CS or RevOps. Each sees only a slice of the customer.

The next step is shared context across the journey: what was promised, what has been delivered and what still needs to happen. Otherwise, we risk automating the same silos we are trying to overcome.

My view: as AI changes how customers access our products and how we serve them, discovery, commercial judgement and coordination become more valuable.

The question remains simple: can the customer explain what is better in their business because they work with us?

That is the promise we need to keep.
