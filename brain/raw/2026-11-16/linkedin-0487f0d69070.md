I could write a novel about the lessons we’ve learnt in running a farm and building a business over the past 15 months. But perhaps the biggest lesson of all is that there is no silver bullet when it comes to farming.
 
Yesterday, we loaded our first B-Double. A milestone that was both exciting and bittersweet, and one that gave me an even greater appreciation for the realities of running a farming enterprise.
 
In our first 15 months of trading, we’ve endured five interest rate rises, adding another 1.25% to our borrowing costs. Fertiliser, fuel, insurance and just about every other operating expense have increased along the way.
 
These aren’t just numbers on a spreadsheet. They are real costs that farmers have to absorb, manage or find a way to offset. And when you’re operating in an industry where you have very little control over the price you receive for your product, making the numbers stack up is an ongoing challenge.
 
Farmers are constantly having to reassess their business models, looking for efficiencies, questioning where they can cut back and finding ways to protect margins without compromising the long-term sustainability of their operations.
 
As John Dutton famously says in Yellowstone, “Ranching is a terrible business. We can’t control the price of beef, or hay, or the diesel it takes to take the cattle to auction, or the hay out to the cattle.”
 
It’s a simple but powerful reminder of the commercial realities of farming. So much of what determines your bottom line sits outside your control. What you can control is how you adapt, manage risk and make decisions to keep your business moving forward.
 
We are only 15 months into our journey, and I have an enormous amount still to learn. But one thing is certain: despite the challenges, the long days and the commercial realities, there’s something pretty special about being part of an industry that feeds the world.
 
As John puts it, “It’s one hell of a life.” 🐮
