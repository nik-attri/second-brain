“This AI agent will replace your entire marketing, finance and delivery team.” And then thousands of people in the comments asking for the blueprint. What a f*cking joke. 😅

I've spent 15+ years building and running multiple successful businesses.

And if there's one thing I've learned, it's how valuable good people with real experience are.

Someone who understands your customers, knows when the numbers don't make sense, spots a problem before it becomes expensive and can make the right decision when things don't go according to plan.

That kind of judgment takes years to develop.

And now people want to replace an entire team with a couple of AI agents?

If I were making a company AI-first today, here's exactly how I'd approach it:

1. Start with the people doing the work.

I'd sit down with sales, marketing, finance, delivery and operations.
Find out what they're spending days on.

Where do they lose time? Which tasks come back every week? 
Your employees usually know exactly where the problems are.

2. Separate the work from the expertise.

Your best salesperson should be talking to customers, negotiating and closing deals. Why are they spending hours researching prospects, updating CRM records and preparing follow-ups?

Your finance manager should understand the numbers and help make better decisions.

Why are they manually collecting information from 5 different places?

That's the work I'd start removing.

3. Redesign the workflows around AI.

Now we start building:
→ Sales agents that research prospects, prepare meetings and update the CRM.
→ Marketing agents that handle research, reporting and repetitive production work.
→ Finance agents that collect documents, match information and flag inconsistencies.
→ Delivery agents that prepare onboarding, track projects and handle routine updates.

All connected to the systems the company already uses. With the right permissions and human approval where needed.

4. Let the people with experience make the decisions.

I want the salesperson focused on customers.
The marketer thinking about what actually makes people buy.
The finance manager spotting opportunities and risks.
The delivery team doing great work for clients.

And the agents handling as much of the repetitive execution around them as possible.

5. Measure how much more the existing team can handle.

We've already seen what's possible at ITRE.

For BRONX, we connected ERP, email, WhatsApp and wholesale information, helping reduce customer service and document handling time by 90%.

I think experience and judgment are becoming incredibly valuable as AI gets better at execution.

And I would much rather build a company around making great people significantly more capable than trying to replace everyone.

I'm putting together a full blueprint of how I'd transform a business into an AI-first company, including the agents, workflows and where I'd keep people involved.
