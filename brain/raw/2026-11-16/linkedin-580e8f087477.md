The AI race is no longer about who has the smartest chatbot.

It's about who can make AI work across an entire organisation.

And IBM and Microsoft's latest announcement is a strong signal of where things are heading.

On November 10, the two companies announced Imagine Enterprise, bringing together more than 6,000 pre-built AI agents and supporting assets from IBM Consulting to help organisations build and govern AI-powered workflows.

But here's what caught our attention.

The real breakthrough isn't 6,000 AI agents and assets.

It's the growing recognition that AI needs to move beyond isolated tasks and become part of how organisations operate.

Think about a typical business process.

A customer enquiry arrives.

Someone enters the details into a CRM.

Another employee prepares a follow-up.

A manager reviews the information.

And someone updates a spreadsheet.

Five steps. Multiple handoffs. Plenty of opportunities for delays.

Now imagine an AI-enabled workflow that connects these activities, supports execution and keeps humans involved where judgment is needed.

That's the promise of agentic AI.

But here's the challenge for business leaders:

Buying AI tools is easy. Redesigning workflows is the hard part.

At AgilityAI, this is why we focus on both AI enablement and practical automation.

Because the organisations that benefit most from AI won't necessarily be those adopting the most tools.

They'll be the ones asking better questions about how work gets done.
