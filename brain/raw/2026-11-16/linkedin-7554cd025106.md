All the breakfast slots were occupied.
Murphy’s solution? Crowd-surf UNDER the other puppies until she hit the jackpot.
 
If you’re responsible for an AS9100 or ISO 13485 QMS, keep that picture in your head:
When the intended route doesn’t work, people find another one.
 
Murphy was the runt.
 
Tiny little black nugget in a pile of 7 other puppies.
 
And when breakfast opened?
 
Every easily-accessible spot at the milk bar was already taken.
 
One of Murphy’s sisters sat next to the pile crying.
 
Pfft.
 
Not my Murphy.
 
Apparently, “all spots occupied” was not a show-stopper for her.
 
She started working her way UNDER the other puppies.
 
Wiggling.
Shoving.
Scooting.
Tunneling through the puppy pile.
 
Only her back paws were visible, wiggling those little white tips as she progressed.
 
Until…
Jackpot.
 
Tummy filling in progress. 🤣
 
It seems Murphy understood something very early in life:
If the obvious route doesn’t work, try something else.
 
Which brings us to:
 
What would Murphy do?
 
In this case:
Top nipples filled? Check out the bottom row.
 
Find a different route that works.
 
And humans do their own version of this every day.
 
Give someone a procedure or work instruction that is technically correct but awkward as hell to actually use?
 
They still have a job to finish.
 
So they find another route.
 
A shortcut. 
A side note.
A spreadsheet.
A different sequence.
 
The person who knows the easier way to get it done.
 
Maybe that alternate route works perfectly.
 
Maybe it introduces risk nobody intended.
 
Either way:
The procedure existing does not mean the procedure is the path people are actually using.
 
And that’s the part I care about.
 
Not just:
“Is the procedure technically correct?”
 
But:
“Does the documented process match the actual process?”
 
And if they don’t?
Which one needs to change?
 
Because if the documented route doesn’t work in practice?
Don’t be surprised when people find another way through the puppy pile.
 
#MurphyMoments takeaway:
What ridiculous workaround has your pet invented because the obvious route wasn’t working?
