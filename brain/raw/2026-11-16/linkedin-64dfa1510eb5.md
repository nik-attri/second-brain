Over the past few days, I've had about five people reach out to me asking how to get started with AI in HR. And I've realized that for someone who's completely new to AI, the amount of information out there can be overwhelming.

ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, AI agents, automation, prompt engineering... where exactly do you begin?

You don't have to learn everything at once, and you certainly don't need to know how to code before you start.

So, I spent some time putting together a few beginner-friendly YouTube videos I'd recommend watching, preferably in this order.

1. Meet ChatGPT: Ask Your First Question — OpenAI Academy
https://lnkd.in/d4FHcuTu:  If you've never used ChatGPT or you're still unsure how to interact with it, start here. It introduces the basics of asking questions, following up, and improving responses.

2. AI for HR: How to Use AI for Human Resources Management — Edureka
https://lnkd.in/drBP9G3n:  A beginner-friendly introduction to AI applications across recruitment, employee experience, HR planning, and other HR functions. Watch this to start identifying opportunities within your own work.

3. Google AI Tools for Recruiters — PromptMates Live #005 https://lnkd.in/dwde4TeZ: This is where things become practical. Mike Brown joins PromptMates to demonstrate how Gemini and Google Workspace can support recruiting workflows, candidate communication, and automation.

4. AI Outreach & Personalisation — PromptMates Live #008 https://lnkd.in/dSa3Bc_Z:  This session explores how recruiters can use AI for candidate outreach and personalized communication. I particularly recommend paying attention to the discussion about providing context, refining outputs, and building repeatable workflows.

One thing I'd encourage you to do: don't just watch these videos. Practise.
Pick one repetitive task you currently handle in HR.
It could be drafting interview questions, preparing onboarding materials, organizing recruitment communications, or creating training outlines.

Try using AI to improve that one task.
Evaluate the result. Identify what worked, what didn't, and what you still needed to do manually. And please use fictional information while experimenting, rather than uploading confidential employee or candidate data into unapproved tools.

You don't need to build an AI agent on your first day.

You just need to start understanding how these tools work and where they can genuinely make your job easier.

Start small. Build from there.

I'll share more resources as we go. ❤️
