“If he’s so good at real estate, why does he need to sell a course?”

I find this to be some of the laziest thinking in the real estate industry.

The idea being that if you’re so good at operating real estate, you should make plenty of money doing that rather than teaching it.

Breaking news: Apple is good at making phones and still sells laptops.

You can do more than one thing.

I don’t sell a course (not that I never would), but I get some version of this criticism from time to time with my writing and podcasting.

Like if you’re not in a spreadsheet 24/7, you’re not an “operator.”

Now, there are absolutely people who make more money teaching real estate than actually doing it, while pretending the opposite is true. That’s worth scrutinizing.

But like any other business, there are good courses and bad ones. Valuable masterminds and worthless ones.

I’m personally part of a group that could be called some version of a mastermind, and I pay a lot of money for it every year. It’s been worth it to me.

The existence of bad actors doesn’t invalidate an entire business model.

Selling a course doesn’t make you a bad operator. And not selling one sure as hell doesn’t make you a good one.
