🎤 In a session recently, a bid team told me their best case studies were stuck inside the heads of their frontline staff. Lovely place for a story to live but not an overly convenient place to go looking for one at 4pm on deadline day...

They'd tried the obvious thing - politely requesting a case study. And the people doing the delivery work (who are brilliant at it, by the way) went very quiet. 😬

Fair enough, too. Take Kev. Kev has spent years actually helping people, and over a coffee he'll tell you all about the customer who rang 14 times in one week and now sends the office a Christmas card. But email Kev the words "can you write us a case study by Friday" and you get two lines back. One of them is "see attached". There is no attachment. Good stuff.

What worked for this team was really simple - they stopped asking for writing. Frontline staff now fill in a short form. Who was it, what was going wrong, what did you do, what changed? The answers drop into a spreadsheet, and the bid team (the ones who actually DO like writing) turn each row into the case study.

The other option, for the Kevs who'd rather talk than type - and they do like to talk, in my experience - skip the form, have a chat with him on Teams, and let AutogenAI work through the transcript and structure it into a reusable piece of material.

Either way, if your case study library is the same three favourites on rotation, it might be worth asking who's really holding the good stories, and whether asking them to do extra homework on top of their normal job is going to get you what you really need...

Kev will regale you with stories all day - just don't ask him to write them up!
