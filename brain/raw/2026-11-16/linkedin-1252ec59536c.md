AI can generate an image in seconds. But can it create a professional product visual that actually sells?
During my work with Otath, a Saudi-based brand, I had the opportunity to create lifestyle visuals and product photoshoots for more than 1,000 products.
This experience taught me something important:
A beautiful AI-generated image doesn't necessarily make a successful product visual.
While working on these products, I noticed a real difference in how customers responded to different product presentations. The visuals that were more appealing, professionally styled, and thoughtfully designed helped attract more customer attention and contributed to better sales performance.
This is especially important in the Gulf market, where visual presentation plays a significant role in how customers perceive furniture and home products.
So, what's the difference?
It's not just about generating an attractive image. It's about giving every product its own visual story.

Here are a few things I learned along the way:

Understand the product: Its materials, colors, dimensions, details, and intended use.
Choose the right environment: A furniture piece should be presented in a setting that complements its design and highlights its functionality.
Add your creative direction: Don't just accept the first result AI gives you. Bring your own ideas, imagination, and artistic decisions into the process.
Maintain product accuracy: Pay close attention to details and fix AI-generated errors, either with the right AI tools or manually in Photoshop.
Focus on quality: Use suitable AI models, precise prompts, and high-resolution outputs while maintaining product fidelity.
There's something I've noticed with tools like Nano Banana and other AI image generators.
If you rely entirely on their default visual style without adding your own creative direction, your results can start looking very similar to everyone else's.
And that's exactly what we should avoid.
AI should be your creative assistant, not your creative director.
The real value comes from knowing what to create, why to create it, and how to make each product stand out.
Of course, product visuals aren't the only factor behind sales, but they can make a real difference in attracting customers, building trust, and influencing purchasing decisions.
I'm curious: Do you think AI has made product visualization easier, or has it made creative direction even more important?
