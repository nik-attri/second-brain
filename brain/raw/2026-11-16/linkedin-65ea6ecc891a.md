🚀 Excited to share my latest Power BI project: Zomato Restaurants Data Analysis! 🍕📊

​As an aspiring Data Analyst looking to break into the industry, I wanted to build a portfolio project that moves beyond basic charts and delivers real, actionable insights.
I chose the Zomato restaurant dataset to explore what drives success in the food and dining industry—from pricing trends and customer ratings to cuisine popularity.

​Here is a quick breakdown of what I built and the key insights I uncovered:

​🔍 What’s Inside the Dashboard?
​Executive KPI Cards: Tracking total restaurants, cumulative ratings, average ratings, and average cost for two.

​Interactive Slicers: Dynamic filtering by restaurant types (Cafes, Casual Dining, Quick Bites, Bars, Bakeries, Lounges, etc.) to instantly update all cross-filtered visuals.

​Geographic & Category Breakdown: Visualizing restaurant distribution and comparing online ordering adoption against customer satisfaction.

​Top Cuisines Analysis: Highlighting market demand across popular categories like North Indian, South Indian, Chinese, Continental, and Desserts.

​💡 Key Data Insights
​Rating vs. Format: While Quick Bites and Delivery-only models dominate volume, niche and premium formats like Irani Cafes (4.40 avg rating), Lounges (4.04), and Bars (3.99) command significantly higher customer satisfaction scores.

​The Pricing Spectrum: Average dining cost for two varies drastically—ranging from budget-friendly options like Sweet Shops (₹235) and Beverage Shops (₹266) to premium experiences in Lounges (₹1,540).

​Online Ordering Impact: Restaurants supporting online ordering maintain strong, competitive ratings while capturing a vastly larger volume of customer feedback compared to offline-only spots.

​🛠️ Tools & Tech Stack
​Microsoft Power BI: Data modeling, custom theme formatting, and DAX measures.
​Power Query: Data cleaning, handling missing null values, and structuring multi-value cuisine strings.
