🛑  Last week I lost a client. Well… not really.

💸 A sales-qualified lead did not go all the way through the funnel… or at least not yet.
While I get that the business buying decision process is red-tape filled and full of ebbs and flows, I also know that most companies are stuck in financial-quarter-thinking, and the idea of a long-term strategy seem utopic.
 
 💭 The thing that bugs me the most is that I know how quickly I could have transformed the way their business runs and how much time and money they would have saved. Sometimes we’re too close to a problem to see the solution. Here’s what happened.
 
 🔍 During my assessment, I noted that their main process required more than 15 hours of manual data input per week - think printouts, screenshots, internal emails as raw data that had to be manually input across systems with inadequate audit trails.
 
 🫸 I understand that ways of working and processes change incrementally as the business evolves but sometimes the biggest opportunities for improvement are hidden in processes that have simply become “the way we do things.”
 
 🛠️ The conundrum is that those pains are not equally felt across the business. Skilled employees spending time on repetitive task, middle managers trying to enhance processes locally, information travelling too slowly from one team to another, while decision makers or budget holders are insulated from how those problems feel day-to-day… Until something goes wrong…
 
 💻 Ultimately, these issues are common across industries and sectors and the key resources of modern businesses and the main value drivers are people and data.
 
 📈 Digital transformation does not always require a major system replacement or a large capital project. Sometimes, connecting existing systems, automating routine reporting, and removing unnecessary manual steps can deliver substantial improvements in speed, accuracy, and capacity. Streamlining your data across the raw-process-report flow and saving time for your skilled employees to dedicate it to adding value to the business and innovating on the job is they key to long-term thinking. And that’s your starting point for your data strategy
 
❓ How much time could your business recover by reviewing the manual processes that have become part of everyday working?
