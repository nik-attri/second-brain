Three years ago, using AI in my Cambridge research would have been considered cheating.

Today, I am being encouraged to use it.

I am currently undertaking my Business Doctorate at the University of Cambridge Judge Business School, where my research explores what drives leadership across religious, regional and professional groups.

As part of my research, I have conducted thousands of pages of interviews from around the world.

When I began this work, analysing those interviews would have meant manually reviewing the material, identifying keywords and looking for themes across the research.

Today, AI allows me to do that much faster.

It can process large amounts of information which would have taken months manually, in minutes, helping me identify patterns and organise my research more efficiently.

But the most important part remains unchanged.

AI does not replace the thinking.

I still need to interpret the findings, analyse what they mean and apply my own judgement.

This experience has reinforced something I believe strongly: technology is at its most powerful when it enhances human capability.

The future will belong to those who understand how to combine new tools with curiosity, creativity, critical thinking and experience.

Universities and businesses have an important role to play in helping people use AI responsibly and effectively.

The pace of change is extraordinary, and our ability to learn, adapt and embrace new opportunities will shape how we succeed in this new era.
