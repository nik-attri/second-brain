I wouldn’t automate everything just because I can.

As an Executive Assistant, I’d actually ask:

“Does this need a human?”

For example:

A calendar reminder?

Automate it.

A recurring report that pulls information from the same places?

Automate it.

Sorting routine inbox notifications?

Potentially automate it.

But a sensitive client email?

A frustrated team member?

A relationship that needs a thoughtful response?

A decision that depends on context?

I’d keep the human involved.

Because automation is great at handling repetition.

It’s not a replacement for judgment.

My approach would be:

1. Identify the repetitive task.
What happens again and again?

2. Map the process.
What triggers it? What happens next?

3. Automate the predictable parts.
Remove unnecessary manual steps.

4. Keep human checkpoints.
Anything requiring judgment, nuance, or relationship management stays with a person.

5. Review the result.
Did we actually make the process better?

That’s how I think about AI and automation as an EA.

Not:

“Where can I use AI?”

But:

“Where can technology remove unnecessary work without removing good judgment?”

That’s a much more useful question.

What is one repetitive task in your business you’d happily never do manually again?
