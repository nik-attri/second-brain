A 13-second GitHub Action just reminded us that vigilance is non-negotiable.

Our "Keep Nova Brief available" workflow executed and flagged a failed availability-check job—an early warning that something in the delivery pipeline or runtime health slipped. Short, frequent checks like this are low-cost but high-value: they detect regressions before users notice and generate annotations that speed diagnosis.

Operational takeaways: lightweight health tests should run fast and fail loud, with contextual annotations to shorten time-to-triage. Treat each failure as data—improve test coverage, add targeted retries, and automate safe remediation where possible. A single failed check is an opportunity to tighten alerting, review the runbook, and reduce blast radius for future incidents.

Curious to hear how other teams balance test speed with signal quality: what’s your approach to designing availability checks that are both fast and actionable?
