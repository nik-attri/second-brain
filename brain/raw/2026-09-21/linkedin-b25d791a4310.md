My son couldn't read his own tennis racket.

We were hitting this weekend and he kept talking about his "wisdom." How it felt, how much he liked it. I had no idea what he was talking about. Took a full minute of me nodding along before he said "wisdom racket" and I actually looked at the thing.

WILSON. Written in cursive.

They don't really teach cursive anymore, so to him it may as well have been Greek. And this is a kid who reads well above his grade level — I can't remember the last time he asked me how to pronounce a word.

He wasn't stuck because he isn't smart. He was stuck because nobody taught him that particular script.

I thought about that again after a call last week with an engineer at SpaceX. Somewhere in the conversation he said something I hear frequently:
➡️ "I feel like I should be able to do this myself."
➡️ And almost in the same breath: "But I know there's a lot I don't know. That's why I'm calling you."

This is a guy who puts rockets into orbit. He's not stuck because he isn't smart.

Eight figures concentrated in SPCX. ISOs and AMT, ESPP, 6+ years of RSU vesting. Trying to figure out when to sell, how much to sell, and how to argue with the part of your brain that says "it's gone up every year, just hold."

None of that is harder than his day job. It's just a script nobody taught him.

The word we kept coming back to was "should."

Nobody expects to read a language they were never taught. But somewhere along the way, a lot of very capable people decided money was supposed to be the exception — that if you're smart enough to do hard things, you should be smart enough to do this one too.

The problem with "should" is what it does to your timeline. It makes you wait.

You start out meaning to do the work yourself. Read a few articles, half-build a spreadsheet, tell yourself you'll get to it after the next lockup release. Meanwhile it's almost Q4, the ISO/AMT and loss-harvesting windows are getting narrower by the week, and the stock can move 40% against you in a month — because it already has multiple times ($135 --> $200 --> $105 --> $150)

Bad pun, but personal finance isn't rocket science. But it is its own language, and it takes hundreds of hours to learn the nuances and the oddities.

With enough time and enough interest, you can absolutely do this yourself. But when you're a kick-ass engineer with a demanding job, navigating life-changing money that is liquid right now, doing it alone is a high-stakes gamble.

The people I've seen handle this IPO best — the equity, the taxes, the psychology, all of it — aren't the ones who figured it out alone. They're the ones who got comfortable saying "I don't read this script," and went and found someone who does.

He'll keep doing things I could never do. I'll handle the cursive.
