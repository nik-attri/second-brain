If I started a large CRM migration today, I would not ask: “Where can we add AI?”

I would ask:
“Which parts of the migration still require people to do work that AI can accelerate?”

In one of our enterprise transformation programs, we migrated core Sales and Service processes from Microsoft Dynamics CRM to Salesforce.
The scope was much broader than moving data:
- Leads and opportunities.
- Accounts and contracts.
- Products, pricing and discounts.
- Territories and segmentation.
- Cases and service processes.
- Integrations, security, reports and automation.
And, as with most CRM transformations, a significant part of the effort happened before anything reached production.

- Understanding the legacy system.
- Comparing existing processes with the target platform.
- Mapping data.
- Analyzing customizations.
- Preparing test scenarios.
- Validating migrated data.
- Supporting users during UAT.

Looking at the same type of program today, I see several areas where AI could change the delivery model.
Discovery.
AI could analyze legacy documentation, requirements, and configuration artifacts to identify processes, dependencies, and potential gaps.

Data migration.
AI-assisted analysis could help identify duplicates, inconsistent classifications, suspicious mappings, and data-quality anomalies before migration.

Solution design.
Instead of manually reviewing hundreds of legacy customizations, teams could use AI to help classify them: retain, redesign, replace with standard capability, or retire.

Testing.
AI could help generate test scenarios from requirements and business processes, identify coverage gaps, and accelerate regression preparation.

Adoption.
After go-live, AI assistants could provide contextual guidance to users moving from one CRM operating model to another.

The objective would not be to let AI make every transformation decision.
Quite the opposite.

Architecture decisions, business ownership, security, migration acceptance, and go-live readiness still require clear human accountability.
But AI can reduce the amount of time experienced people spend finding, comparing, documenting, and checking information.
That changes the economics of transformation.
The next generation of enterprise migration will not simply be system-to-system.
It will be human expertise amplified by AI throughout the transformation lifecycle.
