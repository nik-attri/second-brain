Everyone is talking about AI replacing jobs.

I think we're asking the wrong question.

The latest MEOS Vietnam findings from Manpower Vietnam suggest that cost pressures and automation are contributing to a decline in entry-level hiring. But the bigger issue may be the growing gap between what employers need and how talent enters the workforce.

For decades, entry-level jobs served as training grounds. Today, many of those tasks can be automated.

As a result, employers expect new hires to contribute faster, think critically, and adapt quickly.

This shifts responsibility across the ecosystem:
✔️ Employers must rethink how they develop emerging talent.
✔️ Educational institutions must align learning with real workplace needs.
✔️ Young professionals must actively build practical skills before entering the job market.

The real opportunity is not choosing between people and automation.
It's helping people work alongside automation and thrive because of it.
