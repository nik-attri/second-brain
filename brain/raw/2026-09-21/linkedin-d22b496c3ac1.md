I genuinely want Al to make marketers lazier.

And before someone goes:

"But then people will stop thinking!!!"

About WHAT though?

Because I absolutely want marketers to stop thinking about some things.

I do not need a human being spending 45 minutes turning meeting notes into a summary.

I do not need someone manually cleaning a transcript.

Or rebuilding the same report every Friday.

Or rewriting one email into six slightly different versions.

Or "strategically thinking" about the webinar checklist for the 14th time.

Please use Al.

Actually, use it aggressively.

Because none of that is where I desperately need the human brain.

I need the human brain when we're asking:

- Is this campaign actually a good idea?

-Does this positioning make any sense?

- Why are people clicking but not buying?

- Is the data telling us something real or just something convenient?

- something convenient?

Does this sound like us?

- Are we copying a competitor because it's smart, or because nobody wants to take a risk?

- Should this even exist?

THAT is the expensive part.

Judgment.

And I think we've spent years confusing effort with value.

If something used to take three hours and Al gets you 80% of the way there in 20 minutes...

good???

That is literally what tools are for.

Nobody gets extra marketing points because they suffered more while making the deck.

The interesting question is what you do with the time you got back.

Because yes, Al can also produce absolute garbage.

I use it constantly and still regularly look at an answer and go:

what the freaking hell are you talking about

Which is why "use Al" cannot mean "stop paying attention."

Al reduces labour.

It does not reduce responsibility.

You still need to know when the output is wrong.

When it's generic.

When it missed the point.

When it confidently invented something.

When the technically correct answer is still a terrible marketing decision.

And weirdly, that makes experienced judgment MORE valuable to me, not less.

The better you are at the job, the more work you can safely hand off because you know what deserves your brain and what absolutely does not.

That's kind of my ideal use of Al.

Automate the obvious.

Systemise the repetitive.

Build playbooks for the boring stuff.

Then use the saved brainpower on the decisions where there genuinely isn't an answer key.

So yes.

I want Al to make marketers lazier.

Just very selectively lazy.

Because if Al removes 70% of the effort from something and the result is still good...

that isn't cheating.

That's the damn point.
