From EY to the NYSE

Something remarkable happened this past Friday night.

John Summit made history by becoming the first artist to perform live on the trading floor of the New York Stock Exchange. For those into EDM he also dropped an unreleased Flume remix during the night.

So if you didn't know, before John Summit was selling out arenas and headlining festivals, he studied accounting at the University of Illinois Chicago and became a CPA, including spending time at EY.

If you thought busy seasons were bad for you as a public accountant try doing that and DJing and producing music in between. The sweet part is that in the end John's passion of music won the balance of power.

Fast forward a few years and he's become one of the biggest names in electronic music, with more than a billion global streams and sold-out shows around the world. My favorite is "All The Time" with the Chainsmokers on his new album, CTRL ESCAPE.

It's pretty obvious that he hasn't exactly tried to hide from his accounting past but instead he's leaned all the way into it.

The album released this year on tax day on 4/15 and control escape is reference of escaping corporate life.

Like dad jokes, CPA humor apparently never dies. (What does CPA stand for? Can't Pass Again 😅)

But that's probably why I enjoy his story so much and I bet so does Leilany Moran.

I've spent my career in accounting and finance, ultimately becoming a CFO, but music has always represented something completely different for me. 

There's the structured side of us that likes numbers, spreadsheets, controls and getting things to balance but I always started as a creative and the love for building and looking at things differently was just part of who I am.

John Summit is obviously an extreme example of what can happen when those two worlds collide, but I love the reminder that your profession doesn't have to define the entirety of who you are.

Call it ironic, but the accountant who escaped the firms eventually came back and turned the New York Stock Exchange into a damn nightclub. That's one hell of a career pivot, LFG!
