Are we really becoming more productive with AI, or are we just generating more noise at a faster rate?

Artificial intelligence was supposed to be the ultimate time-saver. We draft emails in seconds and generate reports with a single prompt. Yet, if you look closely at modern workplaces, something strange is happening.

Instead of saving time, we’ve shifted the burden from writing to reading.

Inboxes overflow with multi-page memos saying very little, and teams spend hours decoding content created in seconds. This highlights the fine line between true leverage and digital clutter—a growing phenomenon known as work slop.

From Workflows to Work Slop:

To understand why this happens, it helps to distinguish between two ways AI enters the workplace:

 * AI Workflows: Intentional, structured systems where AI processes verified data, human expertise guides the prompt, and rigorous review ensures quality.

 * Work Slop: Unedited, low-signal content generated in seconds and dumped onto colleagues—packed with fluff, generic advice, and unverified claims.

When someone outputs slop, they save five minutes of drafting time, but force five teammates to waste twenty minutes trying to extract the core message.

4 Practical Rules to Keep AI Productive

If you want your team to harness AI without drowning in slop, consider these guardrails:

 1. The "Human-in-the-Loop" Standard: Treat AI as a first-draft assistant, not a finished product. The human sender owns 100% of the accountability for every fact, stat, and sentence.

 2. Ground the Inputs: Never ask an LLM to write a report from scratch. Provide specific data, bullet points, or meeting notes so the AI acts as a processor rather than a fiction writer.

 3. Reward Brevity Over Volume: AI defaults to wordiness. Normalize cutting fluff—if a 500-word draft can be edited down to 3 sharp bullet points, cut the rest before hitting send.

 4. Mandate Fact Verification: Require a quick manual check of all links, numbers, and cited sources before distribution.

AI is designed to lower cognitive load, not offload drafting effort onto your team's reading list. By building deliberate workflows around human review, organizations can keep the productivity gains while keeping the noise out.
