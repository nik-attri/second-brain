The longer I look at the current state of game development and at what AIs can do with a properly set-up pipeline, the less I believe traditional game development will survive anywhere except AAA, because they are too inertial to transform radically.

I'm more and more starting to believe in a different production model: small groups of people with taste, experience, and strong vision, building products collectively through agentic AI workflows.

Let’s call them "skill-jockeys" or SJs for short.

A SJ is not someone who merely throws prompts at a generic model or uses it to "talk to". It is a T-shaped specialist who brings both his expertise in various fields and a personal agentic toolset built around that expertise.

The SJ sets the direction and quality bar while the agents turn that into results fast, which the SJ is polishing manually.
It is the combination — not either part in isolation — that makes SJs effective.

With my Codex agents and my production background, I can now cover a surprising range of expertise: game design, level design, level art, concept art, narrative, monetization, LiveOps, and more.

Is that enough for a full production? Well, to the point.

Pair that with a great engineering SJ who uses Claude to cover his side. Add a 3D graphics SJ with their own agentic toolset. Bring in narrow specialists only for work we still cannot handle in-house — or to review what the agents produced.

And you have a complete AI-native production unit.

Three skill jockeys. Targeted outsourcing. Small-to-medium products.
That's how I would build a studio today, and I expect we will see this setup as a trend in the next 5 years.
