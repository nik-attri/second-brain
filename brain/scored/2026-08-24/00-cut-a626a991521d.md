The $3,000 she almost spent
An agent I work with was about to wire $3,000 to a lead vendor.


She wasn't reckless. 

Her pipeline felt thin a couple of deals had fallen through

And buying leads is what you do when you're staring at a quiet month. 

It's the reflex the whole industry trains into you: 

low on business → buy more leads.

Before she hit send I asked her one question. 

How many old leads are in your CRM right now?

She didn't know. 
So we looked. 811.

Eight hundred and eleven people who had at some point in the previous two years filled out a form

Called about a listing or come through an open house and left their number. 

Every one of them already cost her money to acquire. 

And every one of them had gone untouched for months because her words  

I just never had time to get back to all of them.

So here's what we did instead of spending the $3,000. 

We took that list of 811 and ran it through a proper reactivation sequence. 

Not a single blast email. 

An actual sequence: a voice call to open the conversation and gauge interest

An SMS follow-up for the ones who didn't answer and an email to give the warm ones a reason to re-engage.

The first booked appointment came in under 48 hours. 

From a lead she'd forgotten existed. 

By the end of the week she had a handful of real conversations on the calendar  from people 

She had already paid for once and was about to ignore in favor of new strangers she'd pay for again.


She never sent the $3,000.


Here's the lesson, and it's not really about my software. 

It's this: most agents don't have a lead-generation problem. 

They have a lead-abandonment problem. You are generating plenty of leads. 

You're just letting most of them decay because follow-up at that volume is genuinely impossible by hand.

That last part is the only reason a tool needs to exist. 

Reactivate.cc is what ran that sequence for her 

AI voice, SMS, email, and automatic booking  so the 811 got worked without her doing it man
