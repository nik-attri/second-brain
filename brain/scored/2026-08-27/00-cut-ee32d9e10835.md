Two weeks ago, I was running a practice project that mixed two things people rarely put together: bookkeeping and project management.

The setup: a small e-commerce client (simulated) with a backlog of unreconciled transactions AND a pile of vendor tasks nobody was tracking — payments due, invoices to chase, receipts to file.

Most data entry work stops at "type the numbers in." I wanted to see if I could go further.
So I built a system:
I moved every unpaid vendor invoice into a Trello board — one column for "Awaiting Payment," one for "Paid, Needs Reconciling," one for "Reconciled & Closed." Each card carried the due date, amount, and vendor contact.

Then I built the Google Sheet that talked to it — a live tracker where every transaction from the bank statement got matched against a Trello card using INDEX/MATCH. If a card sat in "Awaiting Payment" past its due date, it turned red on the sheet automatically.

Three days in, the system caught something I almost missed myself — a vendor payment that had been made twice. Same amount, two different dates, no note explaining why. If I'd only been doing the bookkeeping side, I might have reconciled both and moved on. But because I was also tracking it as a task on the board, I could see the vendor card had already been marked "Paid" once. That's what made me stop and dig.

Turned out to be a duplicate payment worth catching before it became a real loss.

That's the moment I understood what I actually offer: I'm not just a bookkeeper, and I'm not just a VA. I'm the person who builds the systems where your numbers and your tasks talk to each other, so nothing slips through the crack between "the books" and "the to-do list."

If you're hiring for someone who can hold both sides ; the financial accuracy and the operational follow-through , that's the gap I fill.
