AI is not hurting companies because it is too weak.

It hurts them because too many organizations stop thinking when it appears strong.

Generative AI can accelerate research, streamline workflows, and remove repetitive work. But when teams use it as a substitute for knowledge, judgment, creativity, and accountability, the hidden costs begin to compound.

Employees lose the practice that builds expertise. Leaders accept polished outputs without challenging the assumptions behind them. Organizations measure speed while overlooking accuracy, trust, resilience, and long-term capability.

The result may look like short-term productivity.

But a company that cannot explain, validate, or operate without its AI tools has not created intelligence. It has created dependency.

The answer is not to reject AI. It is to use it deliberately:

-  Keep humans accountable for consequential decisions.  
-  Measure quality alongside efficiency.  
-  Protect the skills that allow teams to challenge machine output.  
-  Establish clear boundaries for responsible AI use.

The strongest companies will not be those that automate the most. They will be those that know when AI should accelerate work and when human judgment must lead.
