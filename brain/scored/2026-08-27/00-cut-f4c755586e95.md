AI AI AI ..
Something I’ve been noticing a lot on LinkedIn lately
Everyone seems to be adding “AI” to their job title.AI-powered analytics. AI strategy. AI transformation. AI specialist. AI associate. AI engineer.

I understand why. AI is changing the job market. But I think we’re starting to confuse using AI with actually building AI.

Using ChatGPT to summarize something is useful. Using AI to help write code is useful. But that’s very different from building RAG systems, developing AI agents, designing evaluation frameworks, integrating AI with real systems, or building an AI solution from scratch.

Yet everything is increasingly being called simply “AI experience.”

Then there’s the hiring problem.

You’ll see an “entry-level” position asking for 2–3 years of experience.

They want Python, SQL, cloud, analytics, machine learning, LLMs, AI agents, automation, APIs, dashboards and production experience.

And it’s still called entry-level.

If an entry-level candidate needs three years of experience, where are they supposed to get it?

And if an “AI Associate” is expected to build agents that automate real business processes, shouldn’t that be recognized as an engineering skill rather than simply another analyst responsibility?

At the same time, building software has become much easier.

A few years ago, building an application could require frontend, backend, databases, APIs, authentication, cloud infrastructure and weeks or months of development.

Today, AI coding tools can turn an idea into a working prototype incredibly fast.

That’s amazing.

But building a prototype is not the same as engineering a production system.

AI can help write the code. It doesn’t automatically solve architecture, security, data privacy, hallucinations, evaluation, scalability or failure handling.
