There is a big issue facing Big 4 early-career tax professionals because of AI. They used to spend about four years preparing returns, one after another. The repetition built the judgment.

The hope is, do enough of them, and you stop seeing forms and start seeing the business underneath.

That is the moment someone becomes an advisor rather than a preparer.

The issue is that AI is now very good at preparing returns.

So the reps disappear for the early career professionals, and the judgment they used to produce has to come from somewhere else.

Where will they learn that judgement?

Well, that's where TaxSIM comes into play from Centaurian AI, real life work simulations to get the reps in and build experience much quicker.

We already have sims for Formula 1 drivers and surgeons to train them up quicker.

But for some reason, knowledge workers were expected to get those reps in over 4 years of repetitive tasks instead of 4 months.


So TaxSIM runs the scenarios instead. High volume, high speed, and the outcome changes based on what you decide.

Years of client situations compressed into something you can practice against, the way a driving simulator teaches you a corner you have never driven.


It's why a first-year KPMG analyst told us he wanted to build his first valuation model by hand. His reasoning: doing one or two reps manually is worth it. Four years of them is not.


It's also why in May, Business Insider covered our TaxSIM, short for Strategic Imagination Machine. We built it with KPMG's tax team and it reaches around 10,000 tax professionals this year so they can start getting reps in!


PS. Genuine question for anyone who came up through a profession like this one.

How many of those reps did you actually need? I suspect the honest answer is far fewer than four years' worth of repetitive tasks. I would love to hear perspectives from other folks.
