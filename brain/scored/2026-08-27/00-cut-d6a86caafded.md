The more consequential a decision becomes, the more visible human authority should become.

At Kinetic, we use a simple Human Authority Test when designing AI-supported workflows:

CONSEQUENCE — Would a wrong decision materially affect a person, customer, safety, money, or reputation?

AMBIGUITY — Does the decision require interpreting incomplete or conflicting context, or handling an exception?

COMMITMENT — Does it create a promise, approval, obligation, or irreversible action?

If any of those signals is present, attach a named human owner to the decision point.

The workflow should make five things visible:

1. What the system prepares,
2. Where the work pauses,
3. Who decides,
4. How exceptions return, and
5. Who owns the result.

Human authority does not mean people must perform every step manually.

AI-supported systems can gather context, prepare drafts, monitor agreed signals, repeat defined steps, and route work.

The responsibility for consequential judgment and commitments remains visible.

Choose one workflow. Where do consequence, ambiguity, or commitment require a named human owner?
