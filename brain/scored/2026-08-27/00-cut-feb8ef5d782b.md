The numbers in Google Analytics, the CRM and ad platforms don't match. Nobody trusts the reporting and so the search for a new tool begins…sounds familiar? 🤔

Before you all come at me, I do recognise that some differences between these platforms are completely normal. After all, they may use different attribution models, lookback windows and counting methods. The bigger problem is when those differences sit alongside inconsistent campaign tags, missing source context and conflicting definitions across sales and marketing.

A new platform may produce cleaner dashboards, but it cannot recover information that was never captured in the first place. What tends to happen is that the team ends up building a more expensive version of the same confusion.

👋 Marketers, before paying for another tool, I’d want clear answers to four questions:
👉 Can we name and tag every campaign consistently?
👉 Can we preserve the relevant source and campaign context when someone becomes a known lead?
👉 Can sales and marketing agree on what counts as an inquiry, qualified lead, opportunity and sourced revenue?
👉 Can we reconcile a sample customer journey, from the campaign touchpoint through the form submission to the CRM record?

If the answer to any of these is no, software alone will not solve the underlying problem. It can consolidate data and assign credit according to a chosen model, but it can't repair missing data or settle definitions the business has never agreed on.

Start by:
 
1️⃣ Standardising campaign names
2️⃣ Preserving source context
3️⃣ Aligning sales and marketing definitions
4️⃣ Manually reconciling a sample of real customer journeys

Once that foundation is in place, you can properly judge whether another tool is needed. Until then, don’t spend a dime.
