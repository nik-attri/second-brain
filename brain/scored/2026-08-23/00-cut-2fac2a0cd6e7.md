【A Visit to Handan, Hebei 】

During a recent LegCo recess, I took a trip to Handan, Hebei. I visited my long‑time partner of 12 years, toured the Midea corporate park in the local development zone, and also stopped by a new‑energy glass factory—my old stomping ground.

What really struck me was my partner's automated foundry. I've been in the industry for nearly two decades. In the old days, workshop floors were caked in dust, and the moment you pushed the door open, you'd be hit by a blast of 1,500°C heat. Many workers had little to no protective gear; some even worked right next to the furnaces in flip‑flops. Accidents were far too common, and what we produced back then would be considered pretty crude by today's standards.

Northern China has always been the country's ironmaking heartland, but that era of sheer grit and brute force is finally over. The new plant I saw is immaculate and state‑of‑the‑art, co‑developed with the Chinese Academy of Sciences. With full automation, injuries have plummeted, and quality has been lifted to international standards. The foundry attracts European buyers, and I'm now working to help them break into the Middle East. Seeing the owner pass the baton to his son while I continue to work alongside both generations really means a lot to me.

The evening, though, was the real highlight. Local friends took me to Ubrew, a homegrown craft brewery. The owner imported German wheat malt and a full set of Krones equipment (the Rolls-Royce of brewing) to build a garden‑like, EU‑standard brewery, which later went on to win Asia's Best Brewery.

They started with traditional German styles but have since come up with dozens of Chinese‑infused flavours, including mung bean and osmanthus blossom. Hongkongers like to joke that beer is "foreigner's herbal tea," and according to TCM, mung beans are great for cooling and detoxifying.
