The bell curve of the people I follow/content I see on LinkedIn related to ai is 45% never ai people 45% do everything better with ai people and 10% something in the middle

That’s to say the middle is small

I use ai, I also use my brain. The main benefit I have discovered in my own work using ai is that explaining to something (in lieu of someone) what I am trying to do creates enough distance for me to actually see what it is that I am trying to do and how to best do it

It’s easy to get lost in busy work and too much exposition with ai, especially gpt, which is why I write in it a lot and then limit their output by giving it a character limit

I also very much use Claude and gpt at the same time as challengers of each other’s output and of my own logic and process

The more contrarian your set up, the more likely to get something interesting

Anyhow, the biggest benefit I have been getting lately is large data base structuring and processing, creating apps and artifacts and visualizing ideas to an mvp so I can decide to build or kill

And for all the talk against ai I know way too many companies drowning in the most trivial busywork that yeah you are not getting any smarter by doing that work manually instead of with ai, it’s ok to use for that

Don’t really have a point other than to say, don’t use for writing or thinking, do use it for busywork, as a challenger and an interlocutor to whom you must explain your idea

I am extremely distrustful of the ai companies, I am not of the actual technology but I do think the hype is overblown

Bread still takes 12 hours to rise, just like it did millennia ago, so we still got a while to go here
