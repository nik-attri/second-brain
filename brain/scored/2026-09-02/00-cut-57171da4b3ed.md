From weekly spreadsheets to real-time visibility — a manufacturing quality control case study
 
One of our clients, a manufacturer running indoor and outdoor assembly lines, was doing something a lot of factories still do: pulling defect data manually every week to figure out what went wrong on the floor.
 
That meant:
 — Quality issues discovered days after they happened
 — No easy way to see which station or model was driving defects
 — Hours spent every week compiling numbers instead of acting on them
 
We built them a live First Time Right (FTR) dashboard that tracks:
 ✅ Defect rate and FTR by line, station, and model — updated in real time
 ✅ Pareto breakdowns showing exactly which stations and defect types are driving the biggest losses
 ✅ Automatic flagging of the worst-performing station and model, no manual digging required
 
The result — with production up 82.8% period-over-period, defects dropped 8.1%, and overall FTR climbed to 97.69%, with one line hitting 99.09%.
 
No more waiting for Monday's report to find out what went wrong last Tuesday. The floor manager sees it live, the same day it happens.
 
This is what business systemization actually looks like — not dashboards for the sake of dashboards, but visibility that changes how fast a business can respond.
