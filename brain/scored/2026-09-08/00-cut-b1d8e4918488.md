I have a bit of a morning ritual. I get my coffee and I like to scroll through LinkedIn. 

Lately, my algorithm has either got stuck or it’s defaulted to repetition: human in the loop, human in the loop, human in the loop.

It seems if we treat our human presence within AI as a measure of good AI governance, we’ve achieved it. But wait, did we check, what is ‘the human’ actually doing in the loop?

And I wonder whether we really understand what “human in the loop” means.

I try to liken it to my own experiences. Something relatively simple needs changing say, a set of documents. Five people get involved. Someone needs access. Someone else can open it but can’t edit it. Someone downloads it. Someone changes it and checks it. Then, we missed some!

Eventually, and it’s usually days later, it’s done. Yet it’s exactly the kind of task an AI agent could have completed in minutes. So I’m not convinced we’re protecting human accountability here, I think we’re protecting administration.

If AI writes an email, AI summarises it for the recipient, AI drafts a response then after, summarises it for the original sender. We’ve managed to automate an entire conversation but automating communication isn’t advancing work.

I use AI constantly in my own workflow. Sometimes to capture my thoughts and turn them into actions. Sometimes to assess information and surface what needs my attention.

After a day of back-to-back meetings, one of my simplest uses of AI is one of my most useful. It looks across my inbox. What needs my focus? What needs a escalation? Is there a meeting I need to accept or send?

I can then use it to turn those actions into a prioritised task list. AI gives me the map but I still decide where I’m going. We’ve been doing versions of this for years.

I don’t unfold a road map every time my satnav tells me to turn left. I tell it where I want to go and let it calculate the route.
