AI is making information cheaper...

And I think that makes judgment more valuable!!!

A buyer no longer needs a salesperson to explain the basics. (Those times are gone fs!)

They can compare vendors, summarize products, identify alternatives and build a shortlist before a seller even knows they exist.

That shift changes something bigger than the sales process: it changes where commercial value actually sits.

Gartner's 2026 research captures the contradiction really well.

67% of B2B buyers said they prefer a rep-free experience.

Yet 69% said they prefer to validate AI-generated insights with a sales rep.

At first, those numbers almost look like they disagree with each other...

I think they actually describe the same buyer.

The modern buyer wants independence while gathering information.

But when the decision becomes consequential, they still want confidence, context and validation. (And that's a VERY different job for the seller!)

This is where I think a lot of commercial teams could misread AI.

If AI makes research faster, the answer isn't necessarily to produce more content, more messages, more sequences and more information.

Buyers already have more information than they can comfortably process.

The harder commercial question becomes:

Where does uncertainty still exist?
Is this actually the right solution for our situation?
Are we evaluating the right problem?
What are the trade-offs?
Who else internally needs to believe this is the right decision?
What happens if we choose incorrectly?

That last part matters more than it looks.

Edelman and LinkedIn's research on hidden buyers shows that B2B decisions are often influenced by stakeholders a seller may never directly interact with.

So identifying one "decision-maker" isn't enough...

A commercial system has to understand the buying environment around that person too.
And McKinsey's latest B2B research points in a similar direction.
