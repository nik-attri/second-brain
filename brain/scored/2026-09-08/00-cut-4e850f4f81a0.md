We all know two wrongs don’t make a right. But at work, I’m starting to think two rights can make a wrong.

I’m a millennial boss. Technically an elder millennial, which feels needlessly aggressive before coffee. I sit between two very different views of work.

My generation is right about some things. Careers are built through effort, resilience and work that is sometimes deeply unsexy. The spreadsheet is the bloody spreadsheet and it still needs to be done.

Many women my age were told we could have it all and heard “do it all”: career, kids, home, life admin, late night emails. We became one woman relay teams and called it progress, until burnout or peri audited the operating model. 🙃

Gen Z has a point too. Many watched their mums work like dogs. They respect the hell out of them, but don’t want the sequel. Add COVID, house deposit maths that no longer maths, and AI changing the career ladder as they step onto it. Sacrifice now for security later is a harder sell.

Are they right to protect their time and live more now? Or are we right that hard work, discomfort and resilience still build confidence and careers?

Annoyingly, probably both.

Maybe this isn’t a values mismatch at all. Both generations value success, contribution and a good life. We disagree about what success looks like and what it should cost.Two rights can make a wrong, especially when one has the org chart on its side….

In a healthy culture, tension creates useful debate. In a rigid hierarchy, managers read boundaries as bad attitude and younger employees read accountability as toxicity. Trust disappears and it ends in burnout, quiet quitting or an exit interview, usually followed by an engagement survey asking why everyone is unhappy.
