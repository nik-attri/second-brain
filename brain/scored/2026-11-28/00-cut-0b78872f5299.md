A confirmed appointment doesn't necessarily mean a customer feels informed.

It's a distinction I think more trade business owners should pay attention to.

Consider a glazing company that's just booked a homeowner in for a window survey.

The appointment is in the calendar.

The surveyor knows where they're going.

The business considers the booking complete.

But the homeowner may still be wondering:

What time will someone arrive?

Do I need to be at the property?

Should I prepare access to the windows?

How long should I allow for the appointment?

Who do I contact if I need to rearrange?

The booking is organised internally, but the customer still doesn't have the full picture.

And that gap matters.

From the customer's perspective, professionalism isn't only demonstrated through the quality of the installation.

It's also reflected in how clearly the business communicates, how well appointments are managed and whether customers know what to expect.

Having spent 25 years on the tools, I've seen how much easier a working day can be when everyone has the right information beforehand.

It's one of the reasons we developed Elite Connector around the concept of a Digital Admin Manager.

For glazing and window installation businesses, the system can help establish a more consistent process.

1. Booking confirmation
Send the customer their appointment details once the booking is made.

2. Pre-appointment information
Include relevant instructions, such as access requirements or whether someone needs to be present.

3. Appointment reminders
Automatically remind the customer ahead of their survey or installation.

4. Customer communication
Keep replies and appointment-related conversations organised in one place.

5. Follow-up
Maintain a clear record of the customer and the next stage of their journey.

These aren't particularly complicated processes.

But when they're handled inconsistently, they create unnecessary administration for both the business and the custome
