OT Reality Check: Who the Hell Owns the Risk You Can't Patch?

On November 24, the Alliance for Critical Infrastructure asked CEOs to answer four questions within 30 days:

Which systems can't you afford to lose?
How quickly can you respond to a serious threat?
Have you verified that you can recover?
Who is responsible for managing the risk on systems that can't be patched?

Excellent questions. My only concern is whether anyone can produce an inventory before the first meeting ends.

Imagine sitting in a boardroom discussing the risks associated with unpatchable industrial equipment when nobody can tell you exactly which equipment can't be patched, what firmware it's running, which process depends on it or who owns it.

But don't worry. I'm sure someone has a dashboard.

What frustrates me is that we're still asking these incredibly fundamental questions after years of spending money on OT cybersecurity. How the hell can you prioritize vulnerabilities, implement compensating controls or assign accountability when you don't have a verified understanding of your own infrastructure?

And please don't tell me it's all documented in a spreadsheet that was last updated in 2019 by an engineer who no longer works there.

You cannot meaningfully assign responsibility for a risk you haven't established. And you certainly shouldn't start isolating equipment without understanding the operational consequences of doing so.

This is precisely why OTbase correlates installed software and firmware with published vulnerabilities, identifies known exploited vulnerabilities, tracks equipment lifecycle and end-of-life information, and allows your team to assign asset ownership and operational criticality.

This isn't some revolutionary cybersecurity concept. It's basic asset management, and it should have been done before you started spending money on everything else.

You can't fix what you don't understand.
