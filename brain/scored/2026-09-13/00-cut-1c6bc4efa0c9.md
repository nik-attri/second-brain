Junior coding jobs are disappearing — and UK data just proved it.

The proportion of computer science graduates landing professional coding or programming jobs dropped from ~40% to just 28% in a single year.

Overall graduate-level employment for CS majors? Down from 60% to 50%.

This isn't a prediction anymore. It's happening right now.

Here's what the data is telling us:

🔴 AI coding tools are replacing entry-level developers faster than anyone expected.
🔴 Junior devs and fresh graduates are the first casualties — not senior engineers.
🔴 CS and economics grads are among the hardest hit, as AI agents can now produce software and analysis quickly and efficiently.

But here's the nuance most people are missing:

Companies aren't eliminating jobs outright. They're transforming them.

Finance and tech employers are still hiring — but they expect new hires to walk in the door with AI fluency. The job description hasn't disappeared. The required skill set has completely changed.

The graduates who are adapting? They're pivoting into cybersecurity, network engineering, and AI-adjacent roles — areas where human judgment still leads.

The ones struggling? Those who learned to code but didn't learn how to think strategically around AI.

The hard truth: AI won't replace all developers. But it will replace developers who treat coding as their only skill.

The new baseline isn't "can you write code?" It's "can you direct, audit, and build on top of AI-generated code?"

If you're in tech — or hiring in tech — this shift is no longer coming. It's already here.
