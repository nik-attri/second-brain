The reason your company's internal directory is completely wrong has almost nothing to do with lazy people.

I learned this the hard way years ago. We had a brilliant new hire sit at their desk on Monday morning without an email account, while payroll had them listed under the wrong tax code. My immediate instinct back then was to ask HR what went wrong. But it wasn't an HR failure. It was a systems architecture failure.

The core insight is simple: bad HR data isn't born from carelessness; it is mathematically guaranteed by re-entry.

According to Sierra-Cedar, 63% of HR teams manually re-enter new hire data into at least two separate platforms. PwC Saratoga workforce insights found that downstream corrections cost between £300 to £500 per employee record. That adds up shockingly fast.

Think about what actually happens during onboarding:
• The new hire submits their personal details via a form or email.
• HR manually keys those details into a spreadsheet or onboarding tracker.
• HR re-types that same data into the HRIS, like Zoho People or BambooHR.
• IT manually adds the user into Active Directory or Microsoft 365.
• Payroll manually keys the exact same bank and tax details into payroll.

Every single handoff is an open invitation for a typo, a wrong start date, or a broken department mapping. That corrupted record then poisons payroll, benefits enrollment, and compliance reporting.

If you want clean data, stop asking people to type faster. Fix it at the source:
• Collect data once directly from the hire via structured, validated fields.
• Sync to your HRIS with automatic field and department mapping.
• Provision IT credentials in Microsoft 365 simultaneously via APIs upon completion.
• Maintain a single audit log so every downstream update is traceable.

When was the last time you mapped every manual step in your onboarding flow?
