I started Pearl Lemon in 2016 from a laptop in London.

No funding.

No team.

No fancy office.

Ten years later, the Pearl Lemon Group is six operating businesses, 150+ people and more software subscriptions than any human should reasonably have to track.

Which is how I ended up staring at a $2,000 ChatGPT API bill thinking:
How the hell did this happen?

Shared keys across projects.

No spending caps.

No proper visibility.

Nobody noticed until the invoice landed.

That should probably have been enough of a wake-up call.
It wasn’t 😂

That same quarter, we lost two websites because the domain renewals slipped through.

Years of SEO work.

Gone overnight.

By the time we realised, competitors had registered the domains.

That one hurt.

So we stopped relying on spreadsheets, inboxes and “someone will remember” and audited the whole software stack.

We found roughly $25,000 a year going on software we didn’t need anymore.

Tools for projects that had finished.

Analytics platforms for campaigns that had ended months earlier.
Overlapping subscriptions.

Software nobody could remember using.

Then there were the lifetime deals.

Bought with excellent intentions.

Barely adopted.

Refund window gone before we’d worked out whether they actually fitted into the business.

The strange part is that none of this happened because we didn’t care about costs.

We built Pearl Lemon without outside funding.

The group grew largely through word of mouth and 1,000+ public 5-star reviews.

I’m used to watching where money goes.

But software is sneaky.

Nobody approves “waste $25,000 this year.”

It happens one small decision at a time.

Someone needs a tool.

A project starts.

A campaign ends.

Someone leaves.

The subscription stays.

That was the point where I realised spreadsheets and “I’ll remember to check” weren’t going to cut it anymore.
