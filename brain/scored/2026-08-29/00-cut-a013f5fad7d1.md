The most expensive mistake businesses make with AI is automating dysfunction.

AI doesn't fix your process.

It speeds up whatever you already have.

Good systems get faster.

Broken ones get more broken faster.

I work with clients who come to me excited about AI tools that will save them time and money.

But when I ask them to walk me through their current process, they can't.

They don't have a clear process.

They have a series of workarounds that different people do differently depending on the day.

And they want to automate that.

Here's what happens when you automate a broken process.

The confusion scales.

The errors multiply.

The rework becomes automatic.

You end up spending more time fixing what the AI did than you would have spent doing it manually.

This is the sequence I use with every client before we touch AI.

1) Map what you actually do right now.

Not what you think you do. Not what the manual says. What actually happens when someone completes this task today.

2) Identify where it breaks.

Where do people get stuck? Where do they have to ask someone else? Where do they improvise?

3) Clean the process first.

Remove the workarounds. Clarify the steps. Make it repeatable. Get it to the point where two different people would do it the same way.

4) Then apply AI.

Once the process is clean, AI becomes a multiplier.

It takes a good system and makes it faster, more consistent, and more scalable.

But if you skip steps 1 through 3, you're just automating the mess.

And that's the most expensive version of the mess you can create.
