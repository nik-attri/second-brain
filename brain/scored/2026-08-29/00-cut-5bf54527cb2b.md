We ran a pilot: give engineers one week to fully hand off a recurring task to an AI agent. Not "get help with it." Hand it off completely.

Most delegated their lowest-stakes work — log monitoring, routine status updates, the stuff nobody would notice if it disappeared.

That instinct is backwards. The tasks worth delegating are the ones that consume disproportionate time relative to their decision complexity. Incident triage summaries. First-pass code review on standard patterns. Dependency update assessments. High-frequency, structured, low-ambiguity work that engineers are genuinely overqualified to do manually.

Engineers protected that work instead, because handing off something visible felt risky.

The teams that move fastest are the ones who delegate up the value chain, not down it.
