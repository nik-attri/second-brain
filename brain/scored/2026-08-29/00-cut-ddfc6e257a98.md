Where does AI actually belong in the claims lifecycle — and where should it stay out?

I put together a training case study for our ops team that traces one fictional claim (a routine rear-end collision) through all 10 stages — FNOL, assignment, coverage verification, investigation, damage assessment, reserves, decision, settlement, subrogation, closure.

At each stage we mapped two columns side by side: what AI can reliably do, and what stays with the adjuster.

A few things that stood out while building it:

→ AI earns its keep in the data-heavy stages — intake, photo-based damage estimates, reserve prediction from historical claims. These are pattern-matching tasks, and they're also the biggest source of TAT drag today.

→ Coverage and liability decisions stay human. Every claim, every time. AI can surface a confidence score and cite the relevant policy language, but the adjuster signs off — especially on anything the policyholder can dispute.

→ The real design problem isn't "where does AI go" — it's "what triggers escalation." Low confidence scores, disputed coverage, fraud signals, high-value losses. If those triggers aren't crisp, you either bottleneck everything or automate past things you shouldn't.

→ Faster processing raises the documentation and compliance bar. It doesn't lower it.

If you're building out AI-assisted workflows in claims (or any regulated, judgment-heavy process), curious how your team draws the human/AI line — especially at the coverage decision stage.
