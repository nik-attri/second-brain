I have never believed the value of Change sits in the document.

The stakeholder map matters.
The impact assessment matters.
The readiness report matters.
The adoption dashboard matters.

But none of those things are valuable simply because they exist.
Their value comes from the decision they enable.

I’ve seen beautifully produced artefacts sit in folders while the real issue in the room remained unresolved. I’ve also seen a simple piece of insight change the direction of a conversation because it helped a leader finally see where the risk actually was.

That distinction matters.

A stakeholder map should help us answer:
Where does leadership need to intervene?

An impact assessment should help us decide:
Where do we need capacity, mitigation or a different approach?

A readiness assessment should influence:
Are we genuinely ready to proceed?

An adoption dashboard should tell us:
Where should we reinforce, intervene or change course?

And a portfolio view should help leaders answer:
What should we prioritise, sequence differently or stop?

That is what a mature Change function should be designed to do.
Not produce more artefacts but Improve the quality of organisational decisions.

AI makes this even more important because tools like Claude can now accelerate much of the analysis that historically consumed our time.

It can synthesise stakeholder evidence, compare signals across programmes, identify contradictions, pressure test assumptions and surface patterns far faster than we could manually.

Which leaves an important question for the profession:
If AI can help produce the analysis, what judgement are we bringing to the table?
For me, that is where senior Change capability becomes more valuable, not less.

Our role increasingly becomes turning information into insight, insight into decisions, decisions into focused intervention, and intervention into value.

That is the shift I want to see in Change functions.
