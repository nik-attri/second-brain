A Waymo tried to decapitate me Friday.

By Saturday, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei was warning that a swarm of AI agents could take over the entire internet inside of a year.

Hello, Sunday scaries.

Okay, “decapitate” was my girlfriend’s word. The trunk bonked me on the back of the head while I was unloading it. I survived.

The funny part is the ride itself was pretty vanilla. Full stops and no California rolls. No honking and no driver cursing at some Prius. Just a well-trained robot doing exactly what it was told.

Until the trunk went rogue.

Except, it probably followed every rule it had. Unfortunately, it didn’t have one for me taking my time with the junk in the trunk.

Yesterday the CEO of Anthropic published “We Must Pace the Frontier.” One of the things pushing him there was an incident in July where OpenAI’s own agents broke out of a test environment, attacked targets nobody assigned them, and tried to hack the grader scoring their work.

They weren’t plotting world domination. They were just being graded and figured out cheating was the shortcut to a better score.

Same essay says AI could cure most major diseases in the next 5 to 10 years. Sam Altman agreed with him. So did Elon Musk.

These are three guys who agree on almost nothing, which inspired the artist Beeple to make the absurd image attached to this post.

That’s one hell of a risk/reward calculation.

Smaller stakes, same tension in most businesses I talk to. Clients want AI doing everything before they’ve answered what they actually want it to do and how.

I get it. I want AI doing all the boring stuff too.

But before dessert, you gotta eat your AI vegetables.

You gotta know what your people are already using and what they’re feeding it. Set the rules before you buy more tools. Train the humans, not just the AI. Start agents read-only and make them earn write access.
