The account looked perfectly healthy. The client was already making a different decision.

Nobody talks about what is happening on the other side of the renewal conversation.

Not the CSM's side. The client's.

So let me try.

The VP of Growth has not joined the last two QBRs.

In their head it sounds something like this.
"If I join that call they are going to show me the numbers. And the numbers are going to show that we are at 18 percent of a target I told my CEO would be 25. The automation we bought this for is barely running. My team is still doing manually what I promised leadership we would automate by Q2. I signed off on this. I championed it internally. And right now I cannot walk into that room and pretend the gap does not exist."

So they send an apology. Something about a conflict. They mean to reschedule.
They do not reschedule.

The CRM Manager takes the calls instead. Says everything is fine. The team likes the platform. Fine.

Meanwhile three floors up someone is quietly pulling together a comparison of marketing technology options before the renewal conversation arrives.

Not because the platform failed dramatically.

Because the person who staked their credibility on it is afraid of what renewing says about their judgment.

This is what renewal risk actually looks like before it has a name.

Not a red health score.

Not an angry email.

Not a cancelled contract.

Just an executive who stopped showing up.

A health score that stayed green because activity was happening even though outcomes were not.

A CSM measuring the wrong thing because the dashboard was not built to capture professional fear.

Here is what this means for every CSM managing a renewal right now.

If your executive sponsor has gone quiet ask yourself honestly whether they are busy or whether they are avoiding a conversation about a gap they feel responsible for.

Those require completely different responses.
