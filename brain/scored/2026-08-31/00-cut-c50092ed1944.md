Every now and then I look at what we’re doing with fiber and think about the railroad builders of the 1800s.

Different technology. Pretty much the same damn problem.

Those guys were trying to connect a growing country with steel, timber, horses, dynamite and a whole lot of hard labor. We’re doing it with fiber, boring machines, bucket trucks, fusion splicers and enough paperwork to keep an army of office people employed.

They had mountains, rivers, mud, weather, terrible roads and landowners who probably weren't thrilled about a railroad running through their property.

We've got all that, plus permits, environmental reviews, pole owners, make-ready, easements, 811, county road departments, state agencies, federal rules, utility coordination and three different people sometimes arguing about who actually owns the damn thing we're trying to build around.

Now before somebody gets carried away with the railroad comparison, no, I'm not suggesting we put 19th-century working conditions back in place.

I'm not advocating slave labor, twelve-hour days with no safety program, or sending some poor bastard up a mountain with a pickaxe and a sandwich and telling him to be back Friday.

We've learned a few things in 150 years. OSHA, safety programs, decent wages, equipment that does the heavy lifting and treating people like human beings are all pretty damn good ideas.

But there is something worth remembering.

The railroad builders didn't confuse planning the railroad with building the railroad.

Neither should we.

And we're getting pretty damn good at confusing the two.

We award money. We hold meetings. We write plans. We build spreadsheets. We create compliance programs. We have conferences about broadband deployment.

And somewhere along the way, somebody occasionally remembers that we're supposed to actually build the network.
