I Took One Sip… and Knew Something Was Wrong.

I’ve heard about bottled water being refilled manually by traders, but I honestly didn’t know it was THIS rampant until I became a victim.

A few days ago, I bought a bottle of water at my CDS. As I was about to open it, I noticed that the torque of the cap was unusually low (The cap was a bit loose), but I didn’t pay much attention to it.

Until I took a sip. I had to take another sip just to confirm what I had tasted.

Because I’m very familiar with that particular brand of bottled water, I immediately knew something was wrong. This did not taste like the water produced by the company. I even gave a friend to taste, and she noticed the difference too.

I checked the cap again.

It wasn’t embossed, It was plain!

That was when it hit me 🥹I had just fallen victim to food fraud.

And it made me pause and think…

How deep has food fraud become in our society?

We often talk about food adulteration in terms of chemicals added to food, substitution of ingredients, or misleading labels. But food fraud can also happen in much simpler ways like taking an original bottle, refilling it with water from an unknown source, and selling it to an unsuspecting consumer.

The frightening part?

The consumer may never know what they are actually drinking.

Where did the water come from?
Was it treated?
Was the container properly cleaned?
Was it handled hygienically?
What microorganisms could be present?

And this brings me to the question:

Is food safety really being taken as seriously as it should be?

Food fraud is not just an economic issue. It is a public health concern. It is something we encounter every single day.

So, what more can we do as consumers, food scientists, regulators, manufacturers, and stakeholders to ensure that the food and water we consume are truly safe?
