THINGS EVERY MOTHER SHOULD TELL HER CHILDREN.

“30 years old and unemployed?”

SO WHAT?!

Whose Aunty from hell wrote this rule?

At 30, you must have a career.
At 30, you must have money.
At 30, you must own a house.
At 30, you must have a family.
At 30, you must know exactly where your life is going.

And if you don't?

Congratulations!

HR stamps your résumé:

EMPLOYMENT GAP.

REJECTED.

What a brilliant system.

A person spends three years trying to put his life back together, and some HR manager behind a laptop decides:

“Hmm... unstable candidate.”

Really?

You didn't even ask what happened.

You just saw a gap.

That's the problem.

Corporations love talking about “mental health”.

Put up a poster.
Send an email.
Hold a wellness webinar.

Then someone actually struggles?

Suddenly:

“Not a good cultural fit.”

WHAT THE HELL IS THAT?

If you have the power to decide whether someone gets a job, a salary, a promotion or a second chance, then that power comes with responsibility.

HR isn't just paperwork.

You're dealing with people's livelihoods, dignity and sometimes their entire sense of self-worth.

So maybe HR needs to grow up.

Professional standards.
Mandatory training.
Ethical rules.
Proper accreditation.
Independent complaints mechanisms.
And serious consideration of professional licensing and accountability.

Not because every HR person is evil.

But because power without accountability eventually becomes abuse.

And to every mother:

Stop telling your children that they are behind.

Stop asking your 30-year-old son, “When are you going to get a proper job?”

Ask instead:

“Are you okay?”

“Do you need help?”

“Have you eaten?”

And if he's struggling, tell him this:

You lost your job.

You did not lose your value.

Your career is delayed.

Your life isn't.

You are struggling.

You are not a failure.

Don't let some fucking spreadsheet tell you what your life is worth.

Take one day at a time.

Get help.
Stay alive.
Recover.
Try again.
