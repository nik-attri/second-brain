Busiest teams automate fastest. Quiet teams overthink it to death.

There's a common belief that automation is a luxury for teams with breathing room—something you tackle when workload lightens. In reality, the opposite dynamic tends to happen.

Teams drowning in manual work often move faster on automation because they're desperate. They can't afford perfect knowledge before starting. They learn by doing, iterate quickly, and accept that version one won't be flawless.

Teams with lighter schedules? They debate endlessly. They want to understand every edge case before implementation. They delay launch waiting for consensus. The capacity to think about automation often becomes the enemy of actually building it.

The principle here is straightforward: urgency forces pragmatism. Comfort enables perfectionism.

If your team is stretched thin, that's not a reason to postpone automation—it's the strongest signal you should start now. Begin small, learn as you go, refine as you discover real problems. Waiting for the perfect moment usually means waiting forever.
