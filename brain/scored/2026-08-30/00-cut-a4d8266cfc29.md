An emotion’s biological lifespan in your bloodstream is only 90 seconds.
   
The rest is a story your mind keeps replaying.
   
Neuroanatomist Dr. Jill Bolte Taylor discovered that when we experience an emotional trigger, the physiological response—a surge of adrenaline, cortisol, and increased heart rate—takes less than 90 seconds to flood, peak, and completely clear out of our vascular system.
    
Biologically, that is all anger, panic, or frustration needs to pass.
   
So why do we stay angry or anxious for hours, days, or even weeks?
    
Because our cognitive loops take over.
    
Every time you replay the meeting, rehearse the argument, or fixate on the "what-ifs," your brain perceives a fresh threat and triggers another 90-second chemical wave. You aren't experiencing one prolonged emotion; you are manually hitting the replay button over and over.
   
The next time a trigger hits, run this 90-Second Physical Pause Protocol:
    
1.      Breathe to Anchor: Take slow, deliberate exhales longer than your inhales to signal the parasympathetic nervous system.
    
2.      Track the Sensation: Shift focus from your thoughts to pure physiology. Where is the tension? Your chest, jaw, or stomach? Observe it without judgment as raw physical energy.
    
3.      Mute the Narration: Refuse to assign blame, draft a response, or build a storyline until the clock runs out.
    
Give your neurochemistry the 90 seconds it needs to flush the system before you decide how to respond.
    
Mastering emotional resilience isn't about suppressing what you feel—it's about letting the biology run its course so your logic can take the wheel.
