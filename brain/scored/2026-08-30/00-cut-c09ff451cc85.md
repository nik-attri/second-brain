One of the biggest barriers to ESG has always been relatively simple. We talk a lot about sustainability, but how often can we tie it directly to financial performance?
That’s where artificial intelligence becomes interesting.

A lot of ESG analysis is still about looking at sustainability reports, annual reports, financial statements and other disclosures to understand what the issues that really matter to the business are. And then the analysis says what those issues could mean for revenue, costs, assets, liabilities, cash flows and ultimately the value of the company. To accomplish this task in the past with the highest degree of accuracy and expertise, it took long time and specialized knowledge.

AI could be a game changer.

In a recent analysis, the use of AI brought down a task that would have taken approx. 100 hours to complete manually to approx. one hour. The time saved is not the only interesting thing. That's what's possible with the speed and availability of this kind of analysis.

Instead of relying only on ESG scores or general sustainability ratings, companies and investors can start asking much more useful questions: Which ESG issues are material to financial performance? What is the probability of their occurrence in the financial statements? What is the effect on the business if these risks escalate or if the company manages them effectively?

And that, in my view, is a substantially more significant perspective on ESG. But there is a significant caveat. It can sift through huge amounts of data quickly, detect trends, and develop scenarios. It has the potential to allow the connection of information that a human analyst would need days or weeks to process. But it can’t replace the need for judgment.
