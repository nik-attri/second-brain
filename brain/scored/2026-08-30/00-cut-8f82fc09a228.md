I was looking at a job posting and got that annoying feeling: I’d seen this one before.

Same company. Similar title. Different URL.

So I stopped and checked.

Had I already reviewed it? Dismissed it? Applied to it? Which version had I actually looked at?

Two minutes, maybe.

Except this kept happening.

Find a posting. Figure out if it’s actually new. Decide whether it’s worth pursuing. Remember what I already decided. Keep the right CV with the right application. Try not to let the whole thing turn into 40 open tabs, a half-updated spreadsheet, and me vaguely remembering that I’d “seen this somewhere.”

So I started automating the boring parts.

I thought I was automating a checklist.

I wasn’t.

The actual problem was much more basic: I needed the system to remember what I had already seen and what I had already decided.

Not just find jobs.

Remember them.

That sounds trivial until you try to make it reliable.

And then it gets more interesting, because sometimes it isn’t only your memory that’s unreliable.

Sometimes the web itself can’t seem to agree on whether a job still exists.
That one sent me down a much deeper rabbit hole.

Different story.

Anyone else’s job search eventually turn into some improvised system for remembering what the hell you already did?
