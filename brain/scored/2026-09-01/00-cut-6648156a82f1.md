Over the next few weeks, I’ll be sharing excerpts from my book, What the Hell Is an RMO (and why should I care)?!
If you’ve ever watched talented people get pulled in five directions, projects compete for the same resources, priorities change overnight, and leadership ask, “Why can’t we get this done faster?”—you already understand why an RMO matters.
A Resource Management Office isn’t just another layer of process. Done well, it creates clarity around capacity, priorities, skills, accountability, and the real tradeoffs behind every commitment your organization makes.
This book is for leaders, project managers, operations professionals, and anyone tired of trying to manage resources through spreadsheets, assumptions, and last-minute heroics.
I’ll be sharing practical insights, real-world lessons, and a few honest “what the hell are we doing?” moments along the way.

Here is this week's excerpt:
If you’ve ever introduced yourself as “I work in resource management” and watched someone’s eyes glaze over, this book is for you.
In most organizations, the Resource Management Office (RMO) lives in a strange twilight zone: everyone sort of knows it exists, nobody is entirely sure what it does, and yet somehow it gets blamed whenever utilization is low, projects are late, or someone’s favorite analyst is not available “right now.”
Ask ten leaders what an RMO does and you’ll get answers like:
·        “They… schedule people?”
·        “They own the PSA, I think?”
·        “Aren’t they the ones who send those reports we never read?”
·        “They’re the folks we call when we’ve already promised three clients the same person.”
In the average org chart, the RMO is represented by a neat little box with a title that sounds official and important. In reality, that box might as well say, “Here be dragons.” This is where the mysterious resource people live, allegedly doing something with spreadsheets and systems, while everyone else quietly negotiates their own side deals for talent
