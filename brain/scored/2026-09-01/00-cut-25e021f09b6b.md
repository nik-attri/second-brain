Spent my morning reading through the latest AI agent research and something stood out: 62% of organisations are experimenting with AI agents, but only 23% have actually scaled them into real operations.

That gap tells you everything.

Experimentation is safe. You spin up a pilot, it works on a controlled workflow, everyone feels clever, then... nothing. It sits in isolation. The rest of the business keeps running the old way.

Scaling agents requires something most organisations aren't set up for: rethinking how work actually flows. You can't just bolt an agent onto a broken process and expect magic. You have to redesign the process around what the agent can do safely.

I've watched teams get this wrong. They automate the task without automating the decision framework around it. Then they panic when the agent does exactly what they told it to do in a scenario they didn't anticipate.

The teams actually pulling this off are the ones treating it like infrastructure work, not a feature add. They're mapping where decisions get escalated. They're auditing what data the agent needs. They're building governance from the start.

It's unsexy. It's not a headline. But it's what separates the "nice pilot" from the "this actually runs our business" implementations.
