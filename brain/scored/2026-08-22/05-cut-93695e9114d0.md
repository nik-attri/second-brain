Every ERP pitch says the same thing. Rip out what you have. This one will finally run everything.

Frank Granara has heard that pitch before. About ten years ago, General Insulation tried it. New ERP, more advanced, going to fix the back office for good.

It didn't work. He'll tell you that straight. "We were one of those failed ERP integrations," he said. (Ask any distributor who's been through one. They all have a story like this.)

So we asked him how GIC actually runs things today.

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝗘𝗥𝗣 𝗱𝗼𝗲𝘀𝗻'𝘁 𝗵𝗮𝘃𝗲 𝘁𝗼 𝗿𝘂𝗻 𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗿𝘆𝘁𝗵𝗶𝗻𝗴.

There's a version of this conversation where the answer is "we finally migrated and now it's all in one system." That's not what he said.

What he said was messier. Keep the ERP for what it's actually good at. Build in front of it or behind it for everything else. Decide, on purpose, what has to live inside that system and what doesn't.

Maybe that's a less satisfying answer than "we solved it." I don't know. But it's the honest one.

And yes, a lot of reporting still happens in spreadsheets. He said it almost like a confession: legacy ERP, spreadsheets, tribal knowledge. That's what's running the business right now. A hundred percent.
