Lloyds Bank is doing something counterintuitive: as it automates more back-office work, it's hiring more people.

The bank is growing its relationship manager network by 50% by end of 2027 — 500 additional RMs, while simultaneously investing in AI to eliminate manual data entry, speed up credit decisions, and give managers instant visibility into a client's full lending position.

The logic, per CEO Amanda Murphy: automation doesn't reduce the need for judgment, it relocates it. Once AI handles the forms, the processing, the routine risk-scoring, what's left for humans is the harder stuff, reading a seasonal cash flow dip correctly instead of letting an algorithm flag it as risk, understanding why a founder needs a finance package structured a specific way, walking a factory floor instead of reading a spreadsheet.

Two details stood out to me:

First: #Lloyds RMs have explicit authority to override automated credit outcomes. That's a real governance choice, not just a talking point, it says the model informs the decision, it doesn't make it. 

Second: their #AI-powered "single view" tool doesn't replace the RM's job, it removes the admin that was preventing them from doing it well.

Here's the business lesson that generalizes way beyond banking: #automation done right doesn't eliminate relationship roles, it funds them, by freeing up the time and attention those roles actually require. If your #AIstrategy is only measured in headcount reduction, you're optimizing for the wrong outcome. The companies that win will use AI to make their best people more available, not less necessary.

Worth asking of your own operation: what routine work is currently eating the time your senior people should be spending with clients?
