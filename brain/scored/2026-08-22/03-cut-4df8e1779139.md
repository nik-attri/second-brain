Loop Engineering?
ループエンジニアリング？（日本語版は英語版に続きます）
How you use AI depends on your skills, professional background, urgency, priorities, and even your personality.
Just as I was getting used to prompt engineering, I came across another term: Loop Engineering.
And then I heard another concept: the transition from Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) to Human-on-the-Loop (HOTL).
AI is moving quickly. I had better catch up.
Let me give you a simple example from my everyday HR work.
When I receive an employee's question, I first think about the issue myself. I may draft a response based on my experience. Then I use our secure Copilot environment, giving it my draft together with relevant context, such as company policies or other HR information.
I review Copilot's response, refine it, and then make the final decision about what to send to the employee.
Looking at this process, I can now see the beginning of an AI loop:
My judgment → prompt → context → AI response → human review → refinement → final decision
But this is still very manual. I am sitting in the middle of the process.
In that sense, I am Human-in-the-Loop.
If I eventually automate more of this workflow with an AI agent, allowing the agent to gather information, work through several steps, check its own output, and come back to me only when human judgment is needed, the human could move from being in the loop to being on the loop.
The AI would do more of the work.
I would supervise the process, review exceptions, and make the decisions that require human judgment.
I find this fascinating.
But here is the reality: I am still learning how to use AI effectively at the Human-in-the-Loop stage.
And that's okay.
I don't need to catch every new AI concept the moment it appears. I need to understand enough to apply the technology to my actual work, and keep learning.
AI technology may be advancing faster than I can learn.
No hurry. I’m still in the loop.
