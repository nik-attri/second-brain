What happens to the efficiency?

Give a nurse an hour back through automation and we have created capacity.

What we do with that capacity tells me everything.

Give it back to the patient and we may have just restored something healthcare has been starving for years: time.

Turn it immediately into another staffing reduction and we haven’t transformed nursing at all. We have simply found a more sophisticated way to extract from it, and YES, quantify nursing.

Show me what nurses catch before deterioration becomes an event, the unnecessary care that never happened, the complication avoided, the trajectory changed, and the frightened family who finally understood what was happening.

Put nursing on the spreadsheet.

I am not afraid of the measurement.

I’m interested in who gets to decide what happens once the value becomes visible.

That is why nurses cannot arrive after the technology has been built, the metrics chosen, the efficiencies promised, and the business case approved.

By then we aren’t designing anything.

We’re being handed the consequences.

So challenge the stupid stuff.

Automate the stupid stuff.

Measure what matters.

Then fight like hell to make sure the capacity we create gets used to make care better instead of nursing smaller.
