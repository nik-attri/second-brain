Outlook was down. Unfortunately, SharePoint was not.

So yes, I was still deep in my spreadsheets. But this time, in excellent company.

Yesterday was the second edition of the portfolio careers coworking session Maggie Blackburn 🩵 started. 

While we missed her, Ivona and Winnie were brilliant hosts.

There’s something lovely about getting your own work done alongside people who are figuring things out too.

Between spreadsheets and calls, our conversations wandered through finding your feet in freelancing, creating opportunities, and networking (ah, my favourite).

Which brought me to a question I get quite regularly:
“How the hell do you go to 4–5 events a week?”
Yeah, I do. I know 😂

The honest answer is that I’m obsessed with human connection. I love finding out what people are building, what they’re stuck on, and what gets them excited enough to keep going.

And I firmly believe you can create more chances for luck to find you.

You show up to something.
You have a conversation.
Someone mentions an idea.
You know someone they should meet.

You don’t know where any of it will lead. But the more people you meet, the more opportunities you have to make those connections. That’s what “increasing your surface area for luck” means to me.

It’s how I’ve come to know so many people in London’s startup and tech community.

The slightly less inspirational explanation is that my social life and my work life have become almost the same thing. Honestly, I love what I do, and a lot of the people I want to spend time with happen to be part of it.

That day, I also had three back-to-back calls with founders about their ideas. 

Every conversation gave me something different to think about, and I finished the last one with more energy than I’d started with.

Freelancing can involve a lot of time in your own head. Having a room to work in, people to bounce things off, and space for a conversation between tasks makes a real difference.
