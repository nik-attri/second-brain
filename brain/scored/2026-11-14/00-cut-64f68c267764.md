The most “prestigious” job I ever had was also by far the least glamorous.

I spent two years working in management consulting at a major firm, advising Fortune 50 companies on massive corporate projects.

Sounds cool…right?

In practice, I was working 60–80-hour weeks, living out of hotels Monday through Thursday, and spending most of my waking hours in an office.

My routine was pretty simple:

Wake up. Go to the client site. Work all day then maybe hit the gym if I had enough energy. Go back to the hotel. Repeat. Wither. 

Then fly home on Thursday, spend Friday in the office, and do it all over again the following week.

And the actual work?

A ton of spreadsheets, PowerPoint decks, taking minutes, and making sure the right numbers got in front of the right executives.

Not exactly the fancy corporate strategy work I'd imagined.

I learned an incredible amount during those two years, particularly about working with executives and operating under pressure.

But I also learned that a prestigious career doesn't necessarily translate to a particularly enjoyable one.

Now I run a Google Ads agency.

Probably sounds less impressive at a dinner party than management consulting.

But I have a hell of a lot more control over my time, which is worth way more than any job title.
