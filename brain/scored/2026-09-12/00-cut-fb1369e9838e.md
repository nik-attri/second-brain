(Part Three) Now, we can create a roadmap of Tom Cruise's "journey to the couch," or any other thought leader's journey to influence, even so granularly as to know “hey, this executive, along with three influential people in this industry, all mentioned the matcha latte at this one coffee shop in Austin within three months of appearing on XYZ podcast, and that podcast was mentioned by this reporter as a favorite in a roundup six years ago, and now that reporter is, seemingly ‘out of the blue’ including the no-name company of one of those matcha-loving execs in a front-page story.” 

And, the best part is, using AI to do research that simply was not humanly or even technologically possible before doesn’t outsource “the good stuff”-- the creativity and human relationship-building PR folks live for will always require a flesh-to-flesh, eyeball-to-eyeball, connection. It does not make us irrelevant, it makes us a hell of a lot more informed and all the more valuable. 

And, fixing a gripe and AI-churned comms programs, it doesn’t invent something out of nothing (or, more correctly, “make sh*t up”) like the telltale, “turn this one-sentence concept into a 600 word blog post” or “put a photo of people who have never existed on this flier and invent icons for three different ways of saying ‘we’re having a company picnic.'” 

It’s a technology, in all its various forms, that can help you uncover and track citable, material  evidence of influence across all earned touchpoints. It takes the diligent process most of us have always used to build media or influencer strategies and turns what used to take a great PR person half-an-hour and a career's worth of background per contact to build into 10 seconds to get to twice as many vetted opportunities and 90% there.
