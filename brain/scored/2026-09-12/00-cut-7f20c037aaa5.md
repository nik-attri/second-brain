Is there anything more fraught than future casting?

I strive to adopt a Stoic view not to borrow tomorrow's troubles. No sense in carrying the burden of worry about the future, which I cannot control. From a business perspective, it's September, and our 2027 plan is due in October. A beautiful tension.

A 2027 plan based on Moonshot's 2026 results is quite easy. It's a conversation among our financials, our CFO, me and Joe Villa, and our reasonable plans for growth. But what about those things we are working on that don't fit neatly into a spreadsheet. 

Our industry is far from static. And the economic environment is incredibly fluid. There's a war / not war, that we've won / not won, with a strait we've renamed / not renamed and control / don't control. Tell all of that to diesel prices. 

There's this great scene from season 1 of Ted Lasso, when Ted has trapped Rupert into a game of darts. The odds appear stacked against Ted, and he's bargained for the future of another who he cares for deeply. He's behind, and needs a double 20 and a bullseye, with two throws left; nothing about the game to that point suggests he'll get what he needs. If you know, you know.

His message to Rupert and the gathered crowd: he's always been misjudged, and his message to those judgers, is to be curious. My internal critics are circling. They have lots of experience suggesting that our next moves look tough.

But my true self, the one who's balancing the observations of the Stoic, the planner, and the internal critics, feels calm and poised. We've made it seven years. The future's never looked brighter. Can't wait to nail the next two throws.
