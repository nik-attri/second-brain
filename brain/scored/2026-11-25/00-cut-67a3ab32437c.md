Satya Nadella says A.I. is finding bugs at breakneck pace. Hackers don’t take Thanksgiving off.
 
Microsoft’s chief executive spoke at the DealBook Summit on Wednesday.
 
Less than a week earlier, a Microsoft outage knocked millions of people off Outlook for about a day. A summer software update had hidden a bug. It surfaced when traffic surged. At its worst, more than 80% of Microsoft’s servers stopped responding.
 
Nadella’s warning was blunt. A.I. now finds security flaws so fast that attackers exploit them straight away. Companies must patch almost instantly. That leaves less time to test. Rushed patches then cause outages of their own.
 
“Y2K was one bug, and one day,” he said. He can’t say when this ends.
 
Nadella says physical systems such as heating and cooling are the hardest to update. Britain has just shown why.
 
Since late October, the National Cyber Security Centre has helped supermarkets secure tens of thousands of fridges and freezers. Staff pulled out cables by hand and checked temperatures manually. One chain took hundreds of devices offline every day for a week. No UK fridges were hacked.
 
Here's the lesson for anyone running SAP. You now have to patch fast and patch safely. Customisation makes both harder. Every bespoke change must be found and tested before a patch goes live. The more you have, the longer that takes, and the more can break.
 
Standard SAP isn’t just cheaper to upgrade. It’s faster and safer to fix.
 
Before the holiday, ask your team three questions. Do we know every system we run? How long would an urgent patch take us? Who’s on call over Thanksgiving?

For everyone else? Happy Thanksgiving!
