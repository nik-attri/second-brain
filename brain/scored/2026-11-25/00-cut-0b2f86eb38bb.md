SaaS is having its COVID moment.

Unlike gyms and restaurants closing down during the lockdown, SaaS companies both public and private are seeing record churn and softening demand as customers shift more to their favourite LLM.

So what can a small to mid-sized SaaS company do?

Well, we're all trying to figure it out. Hell, Brian Halligan and Dharmesh Shah are "refounding" Hubspot. Nobody is safe.

But what I do know is that SaaS companies need to get more CUSTOMER-centric than ever before. 

Part of why so many people hate their SaaS vendors is the general air of superiority they've all had for so many years. 

SaaS collectively spent a decade operating on our own terms; selling access to a tool, leaving it to customers to do the work, and locking them in. If you were lucky, you'd hear from your customer success rep right around renewal time to check in before your rate went up 10%.

It's not unlike taxi companies pre-Uber, which offered a terrible customer experience and refused to change it. Then Uber came in and removed friction, so people flocked to them and never looked back.

SaaS used to sit on its high horse. 

The only alternative was expensive custom software which was more expensive and worse, or use spreadsheets to get the job done.
 
Now with AI, the customer HAS a viable alternative that's faster, cheaper (for now) and does the work for them.

I'm seeing it on my own team as we build out dashboards in Claude and then ask ourselves why we're paying fancy reporting tools $20K/year to visualize data we had to track ourselves.

This is why I believe the SaaS companies who survive and thrive in this new environment have to put the SERVICE back in Software as a Service.

 🔷 We need to help our customers achieve outcomes, not just sell access to a tool.

🔷 We need to make it so easy and painless for customers to adopt our product that the subscription is a no-brainer compared to trying to hack it themselves with AI.

🔷 We need to offer real human success. N
