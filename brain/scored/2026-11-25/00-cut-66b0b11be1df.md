It's been 6 months since I started my founder journey, so it's time for a self-reflection. Guess what has cost me the most financially running a tech consulting business? (Answer at the end.)

If you asked me:
- Did you make a lot of revenue? Hell NO!
- Do you regret the move? Hell NO! (Well, a little. If I'd waited to be made redundant, a severance pack would have given me a longer runway.)
- Did you learn a lot, and do you love it? Hell YES and YES!!!

These 6 months felt like 6 years, and I've loved every bit of it: the things I've tried, the lessons I've learned, the people I've met and worked with, and how much my personality has changed. I only wish I'd started 10 years ago, before I had family responsibilities or financial pressure.

Some uncomfortable numbers I'm not ashamed to share:
- Total revenue: $7,816 (plus $600 still owed for a completed project)
- Clients served: 8
- Organic clients: 7 of 8 (87.5%)
- Projects completed: 9
- First client and first revenue: month 3
- Next committed project: $5,000, starting mid-December

For a service-based tech consulting business, that's really not much. A few FOMO moments:
- My 6-month revenue isn't even half of what I used to earn in a single month.
- A mate I met this week said: "I made $25k from one side project with a Kiwi company."
- Another mate I met a few weeks ago has delivered heaps of client projects and made crazy money. He's even helping BYD Australia run marketing campaigns.
- A founder friend asked: "How do you even make a living from this?" 🫠 

As of now, I can't. But who knows, maybe next year? 😆 I've tried so many random things that consulting only got about 60% of my capacity.
